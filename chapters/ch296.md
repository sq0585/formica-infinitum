# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Ninety-Six — The Workshop District

The friction started, as friction usually did in the Strata these days, over something small enough to seem trivial and specific enough to reveal exactly where two working cultures didn't yet agree.

Lin Mushi arrived at the half-built workshop district to find a human carpenter and a Formican structural technician standing on opposite sides of an unfinished bench frame, neither one visibly angry but both radiating the particular stubbornness of people who believed, with equal conviction, that they were correct.

"Explain it to me," he said, setting down the tools he'd brought.

"He wants to reinforce the joints with Qi-lattice binding before the wood's fully cured," the carpenter said. "That's backwards. You cure the wood first. Otherwise you lock moisture into the joint and it warps within a season."

*The binding technique compensates for moisture retention,* the technician signaled, the translation software Fang Ruxue had helped install rendering it in flat, accurate text across the shared display. *Formican construction has used this sequence for eleven thousand years. It is proven.*

"Proven for Formican wood analogues. This is actual pine. It behaves differently."

Lin Mushi looked between them, recognizing the shape of the disagreement immediately — not a conflict of will, but a conflict of expertise, each side correct within the boundaries of what they actually knew, and neither side yet aware how narrow those boundaries were.

"Has anyone tried it your way," he asked the carpenter, "on a joint using the Formican binding, just to see what actually happens?"

"No. Because it's clearly wrong."

"Has anyone tried it your way," he asked the technician, "using cured human timber specifically, rather than assuming the general principle transfers?"

*No. Because the general principle has always held.*

"Then neither of you actually knows," he said, not unkindly. "You both know a version of this that's true somewhere else. Build one joint your way, one joint his way, let them sit for a month, and we'll have an actual answer instead of two confident guesses."

The carpenter and the technician looked at each other with matching expressions of mild, grudging recognition — the specific look of two people who'd rather have been right immediately but could live with being right eventually, once the evidence came in.

---

He didn't stay to referee further. That wasn't the point of what he'd suggested, and staying to supervise would have undercut the actual lesson — that the workshop district didn't need him settling every disagreement between human and Formican method. It needed a structure that let disagreements resolve themselves through evidence rather than authority.

He found Wei Canglan on the far side of the district instead, hunched over a spread of diagrams with the particular tightness in his shoulders that Lin Mushi had learned, across many months, to recognize as trouble that hadn't yet become a crisis but was working steadily in that direction.

"Compression Ratio Four," Wei Canglan said, before Lin Mushi had even fully sat down, the words carrying the flat exhaustion of someone who'd said them, internally, a great many times already today.

"Still stuck."

"Three weeks stuck." Wei Canglan pushed the diagrams across the table, spatial notation dense enough that Lin Mushi recognized the general shape of the problem without immediately following every step of the attempted derivation. "I can extend the compression technique through three ratios cleanly. The fourth collapses every time — not dramatically, just quietly wrong, the geometry drifting out of alignment about two-thirds through the fold and never correcting."

"Show me where it starts drifting."

Wei Canglan walked him through it, his voice tightening incrementally as he retraced ground he'd clearly retraced dozens of times already, arriving each time at the same dead end.

Lin Mushi watched the pattern rather than the specific mathematics, recognizing something familiar in the shape of the failure — not an error in execution, but a problem sitting one layer beneath where Wei Canglan was looking for it.

"You're trying to hold the fourth ratio as an extension of the third," he said slowly. "Same structural logic, just scaled up."

"That's how the first three worked."

"The first three were extensions of each other. What if the fourth isn't an extension. What if it's a different kind of fold entirely, and you've been trying to force continuity where the technique actually wants a break."

Wei Canglan went still, staring at the diagram with an expression Lin Mushi recognized — not the relief of an answer arriving, but the harder, more valuable moment just before one did, where a wrong assumption finally became visible as an assumption rather than a fact.

"I don't know if that's right," Wei Canglan said slowly. "But it's a different question than the one I've been asking for three weeks."

"That might be enough for tonight."

---

He left Wei Canglan working through the reframed problem with visibly renewed focus, the exhaustion in his shoulders replaced by something sharper and more purposeful, and walked back through the workshop district in the day's fading light, past the carpenter and the technician now working, side by side and mostly in silence, on their two competing joints.

He stopped at the district's threshold and looked back at all of it — human and Formican method colliding, testing, occasionally failing, gradually, unglamorously, finding the shape of something neither tradition had built alone.

It wasn't dramatic. It wasn't the kind of progress that made for a good story told quickly.

But it was, he thought, exactly the kind of progress that lasted.
