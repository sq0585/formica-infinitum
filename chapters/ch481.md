# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 481 — The Extension — Day One

They began at dawn, three hundred and twenty-eight people distributed across the planet and, in a handful of cases, already extended into pocket dimensions of their own, each one settling into the same careful readiness Lin Mushi remembered from every threshold before this one, only multiplied now by a scale he was still, honestly, learning to hold.

He anchored the process from the pocket dimension laboratory, his five Anchors present in the room the way they'd been present for every crossing before this, while Dr. Shen Yue's instruments tracked, in real time, the extension's opening moments across a network considerably larger than anything her monitoring framework had ever attempted before.

"Beginning the outward sequence," he said, more for the room's benefit than his own, the discipline of narrating significant moments aloud something he'd built specifically for the people steadying him through them. "Starting from Earth's own Colony Link, extending node by node, following the Lattice's own self-repair signals rather than imposing a structure of my own design."

That last part mattered more than he'd initially expected it to. The Archive's repair theory had been clear on the principle — the Lattice needed a coherent pattern to reinstantiate against, but it also, he'd discovered in the weeks of careful preparation since Veth-Karun's account, showed a faint, patient preference for where that pattern actually anchored, subtle currents in the dissolution zone's own residual structure that suggested, if followed rather than overridden, a considerably more efficient path toward genuine repair.

He worked outward carefully, extending the Colony Link's coherent architecture past the solar system's own familiar boundary, past the stellar neighborhood he'd only recently gained the capacity to perceive at all, following the Lattice's faint guiding currents the way a river follows the shape of the land beneath it rather than cutting its own course through solid rock.

Each node, as it extended, anchored to one of the three hundred and twenty-three, the specific coherent presence of a scientist in Shanghai, a Formican elder whose name predated the Strata itself, the vegetable vendor from the eastern market, all of them holding their individual portion of the growing structure steady, exactly as the screening and orientation had prepared them to.

Liang Jingzhi's own extension ran alongside the general network rather than inside it, a separate, deeper thread Meen-4 monitored personally rather than folding into Dr. Shen Yue's broader instrumentation. He felt it distinctly from the other three hundred and twenty-three the moment it began — not one voice among many, but something being built to last, patient and singular, reaching for the zone's actual center rather than merely touching its edge.

*Observation 483,* he wrote, working the notebook even as the extension continued, the discipline of documentation somehow easier to maintain now than it had been during any solitary crossing. *The distributed structure is holding. I can feel each anchor point distinctly — not merged, not diminished, three hundred and twenty-three separate, coherent presences woven into a single sustained pattern the way the original Colony Link once wove twenty million individual lives into something coherent without erasing any single one of them.*

By midday, the network had extended a meaningful fraction of the total distance the dissolution zone's outer boundary required, and Dr. Shen Yue's instruments confirmed what he could already feel directly — stability, sustained coherence, no sign of the fraying he'd half-feared given the sheer scale of what they were attempting.

"How long," Chen Yufei asked, watching the projection Amber-0 had built to visualize the extension's slow, steady progress.

"Six weeks," he said, checking the calculation Amber-0 had confirmed against his own careful sense of the remaining distance. "If the current pace holds. Longer if the zone's own structure resists in ways we haven't yet encountered."

"And you're going to hold this the entire time? Actively?"

"For as long as it requires active maintenance," he said. "The theory suggests it becomes self-sustaining eventually, once the pattern's presence is fully established. Until then, yes. I hold it, and three hundred and twenty-three people hold their own portions alongside me, the same way you've all held me through everything else."

He worked until evening, the extension's steady progress a quiet, patient companion to the day's passing hours, and found, closing the notebook at last, that the specific weight of it — enormous, genuinely enormous, considerably larger than anything he'd personally attempted before — felt, for the first time in longer than he expected, entirely survivable.

Not because the scale had shrunk. Because he was no longer the only person holding it.

He sent a single message through the relay before he finally let himself rest, addressed to all three hundred and twenty-three at once, the same plain honesty he'd tried to bring to every part of this project since the first careful draft of the original call.

*Day one complete. Structure holding. Thank you for trusting me with the largest thing I have ever asked anyone to carry.*

The replies came back slowly, through the night, dozens of them, each one brief, each one, in its own way, some version of the same steady answer: *We're still here.*
