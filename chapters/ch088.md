# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eighty-Eight — The Compression Training

Two days before Lady Shen Baoling arrived in Chengdu, in the middle of the crisis it hadn't fully become yet, Chen Yufei arrived at his apartment and watched him train.

He hadn't invited her for this specifically. She had come with a report — she'd filed her assessment with the Iron Meridian Court that morning and wanted to brief him on its contents and the Court's initial response. But she arrived early, before he'd finished his morning practice, and he was mid-session when she knocked, and letting her in mid-session seemed more efficient than stopping.

So she sat at the desk with her notebook and watched.

---

His Compression training at this point was primarily stabilization work: holding Ratio 2 for progressively longer durations, then practicing controlled transitions between Ratio 1.5 and 2, then working on what the Codex called *environmental awareness under compression* — the ability to maintain spatial perception of the room around him at the same time as the compressed Qi perception of its internal structure.

This last was the difficult one. At Ratio 2, the environmental information doubled: the normal visual field plus the Qi field at molecular resolution. Managing both simultaneously without the resolution of one degrading the other was the skill that separated functional Compression from impressive but impractical Compression.

He had been working on it for two weeks. He could sustain it for approximately forty seconds before the visual field began to blur.

"You're doing it wrong," Chen Yufei said.

He came out of Ratio 2. "What?"

"The posture. You're holding your shoulders up." She demonstrated, which was slightly ironic given that she had not yet achieved stable Ratio 2 herself. "When you raise your shoulders, you're applying tension to the upper meridian channels, which restricts the flow to the perception channels. It's the same error beginning Iron Meridian students make in the third meditation form."

He considered this. He checked his shoulders. She was right — not dramatically, but the tension was there.

"How did you notice that from across the room?"

"I've been watching practitioners meditate for nine years." She went back to her notebook. "Try it with your shoulders down."

He tried it.

The duration before visual blur: sixty-two seconds.

He noted this.

"That's better," he said.

"By how much?"

"About a third."

"The Iron Meridian has twenty-seven documented postural corrections for the standard meditation forms. I suspect most of them correct the same underlying mechanical errors your protocol has." She looked up. "When you have time, I should teach them to you. You've built your technique from theory. The postural knowledge is empirical."

He thought about this. The Standard Protocol described the geometry of Compression without describing the physical posture of the practitioner performing it — an omission that made sense if the Formicans had transmitted the practice through apprenticeship, where posture was corrected in person, rather than through text, where it had to be described.

The Iron Meridian had preserved the empirical corrections without the theoretical framework. He had the theoretical framework without the empirical corrections.

"That's," he said, "actually a very efficient basis for collaboration."

"I noticed that." She made a note in her own notebook. "The Court's response to my report, by the way, was interesting."

He sat down. "Tell me."

---

The Iron Meridian Court had received her assessment at 9 AM. By 11 AM they had consulted with Elder Zhao, their senior Chengdu practitioner. By 1 PM, the response had come down from the Court's administrative center in Beijing.

The response was, in Chen Yufei's summary: *proceed with information gathering, maintain relationship, do not take independent action.*

Which was, as she pointed out, an instruction to do exactly what she was already doing.

"They didn't ask you to report further detail?" he said.

"They asked me to report if the situation developed or if direct contact with the Northern Peak Court became necessary." She paused. "They don't know what to do with the information yet. The Iron Meridian Court is not the Northern Peak Court — they don't have a standing acquisition protocol. What I gave them is outside their existing categories."

"How long before they develop a position?"

"Days. Maybe a week. The Elder Council meets every two weeks normally; they'll call an emergency session once the Northern Peak Court's presence in Chengdu becomes known." She looked at him over her notebook. "You're working on the assumption that having the Iron Meridian in a complicated relationship with you is better than not having them at all."

"Yes."

"It is," she said. "But complicated relationships with institutions are *complicated.* I want you to understand that I'm not the Court. I can't guarantee what the Court will do."

"I know." He had never assumed otherwise. "You're a person who is also a Court member, and those are different things."

She looked at him with the recalibrating expression.

"How do you know that?" she said.

"You said it yourself. Nine years in, progressing faster than the institution was comfortable with, no lineage, no politics." He met her eyes. "You've been operating independently because the Court and Chen Yufei have different interests. That's been true for three years. I just happen to be the context that's made it explicit."

She was quiet for a moment.

"You're more perceptive than you look," she said.

"I have more data points than most people in this conversation," he said. "Notebooks."

She laughed once, the brief sound she made when something was genuinely funny. Then she went back to her notebook.

"Show me the posture correction again," she said. "I want to map it to the Protocol geometry and see which phase it corresponds to."

He showed her.

They worked for another two hours, her empirical corrections and his theoretical framework producing, between them, a version of Compression technique that was better than either tradition had managed alone.

He noted this in the observation notebook.

*Two traditions. One working, one complete. Together: more than the sum.*

It was the Colony Link principle, applied to people.
