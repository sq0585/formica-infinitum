# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty-Three — Axis-7's Role

He found Axis-7 in her garden the following morning, the same quiet alcove where he'd first learned, months earlier, how carefully she guarded her own private history. He'd debated, through a mostly sleepless night, whether this conversation was his to initiate, and had decided, finally, that Meen-4's revelation the day before left him no honest choice but to ask directly.

"I know about the Amber Lattice School," he said, without preamble, understanding that whatever came next deserved directness rather than careful lead-up. "I know Ren Wuji taught Veth-Karun. I know the Isolationists destroyed the School as disciplinary action."

Axis-7 went very still, her hands pausing over the pale, unfamiliar leaves she'd been tending, and for a long moment she said nothing at all.

*Meen-4 told you,* she signaled finally.

"I found the connection myself, in the Archive. I told Meen-4, and she told me what she knew about the disciplinary action." He held her gaze steadily, though something in him wanted to look away from whatever came next. "I think there's more you know. I think that's why you filed the workshop district objection so formally months ago, and why you've been part of my life in ways I haven't fully understood the reason for."

She was quiet for a long moment, her attention drifting somewhere past him, into a distance he couldn't follow.

*I was part of the team that executed the disciplinary action on the Amber Lattice School,* she signaled finally, the admission arriving with a flatness that felt, he understood immediately, like the only way she could say it at all. *I was two hundred years old. Young, by Formican measure, but old enough to have been trusted with the assignment.*

He felt the floor of the conversation shift beneath him, the weight of what she was telling him arriving faster and heavier than he'd braced for.

"You were there. When it was destroyed."

*I was one of the executing team,* she signaled. *I did not make the decision. I carried it out. I believed, at the time, that it was correct — that the School's failure to prevent Veth-Karun's dissolution constituted a genuine, catastrophic breach of responsibility, and that the Isolationist Council's judgment, however severe, was proportionate to the scale of what had been allowed to happen.*

She paused, the stillness stretching long enough that he understood she was choosing her next words with unusual, deliberate care.

*I no longer believe it was correct.*

---

He sat with the admission, understanding, watching her, exactly how much it had cost her to say it plainly.

"Is that why you've been helping me?" he asked gently. "All this time. The workshop district objection. Watching from the edges of the city's opening. Everything you've quietly done since I first arrived here."

*Partly,* she signaled. *Yes.*

"What's the other part?"

She was quiet for a long moment, her attention finally settling on him fully. *I have spent three thousand years since the Amber Lattice School's destruction largely alone, by my own choice, believing that involvement — genuine, close involvement — with anyone's development was a risk I no longer trusted myself to carry. I watched what happened when a teacher failed to prevent a student's catastrophic overreach. I decided, afterward, that distance was safer. For everyone.*

"And now?"

*And now I have watched you, for two years, build something careful and deliberate and honest, at every threshold, with a caution I did not expect and have not fully understood the source of until this week.* She held his gaze steadily. *I understand now. Ren Wuji taught you that caution directly, out of his own unhealed grief over exactly what I helped destroy. I find that I am — grateful, is perhaps the closest word — that his caution reached you, when mine could not reach Veth-Karun in time.*

He felt something in his chest ache at that, the specific weight of three thousand years of guarded distance finally, carefully, beginning to loosen.

"What you did cannot be undone," he said quietly, choosing the words with as much care as she'd chosen hers. "What you're doing now — that can be part of what you are, if you let it."

She didn't respond immediately, her attention returning to the pale leaves in front of her, the silence stretching long enough that he wondered whether he'd said too much, too soon.

He left her to her stillness, understanding that whatever came next in her own reckoning with this wasn't his to rush, and walked back through the Strata's quiet morning corridors carrying the specific, heavy privilege of having been trusted with something she'd apparently carried alone for three thousand years.
