# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Twenty — What the Array Was

He spent two days reading.

Not in the archive — in his apartment, with the Scale Walking manual, the relevant Codex sections, and everything he could access from the Secondary Archive's materials on the subject of *Veritonal network monitoring infrastructure.*

What he found:

The Strata beneath every major settlement that the Formicans had occupied or studied was instrumented with a health-monitoring array. Not just Chengdu — the network was global, with monitoring stations distributed according to the Under-Web's nodal geography. The Chengdu station was one of approximately three hundred and forty globally, of which perhaps forty were known to be operational (meaning they had received acknowledgment pings within the past five hundred years).

The Chengdu station had not been in the active-operational category.

Until now.

The ping Axis-7 had sent by activating the array would travel through the Veritonal network at a propagation speed the Codex described as significantly faster than electromagnetic transmission — not instantaneous, but rapid enough that distant monitoring stations would receive the update within days rather than months.

He did not know how many monitoring stations were still operational. He did not know who was monitoring them. He did not know whether the Formican civilization's distributed network still had active nodes outside of Earth, or whether the withdrawal the Codex described — a retreat from the surface civilization that preceded the current human period — had left anyone in a position to receive and respond to status pings.

What he knew: something had been designed to receive these pings, maintained monitoring stations to log them, and had protocols for responding to occupancy status changes.

Whether those protocols were still operational was the question he couldn't answer.

He wrote:

> *The array's function: global Strata health monitoring, part of a distributed Formican network. Chengdu station ping sent. Recipients: monitoring stations still operational in the broader Formican network. Location of those stations: unknown, possibly off-planet.*
>
> *Best case: the network has no active recipients. The ping propagates and finds no one home. Zero consequence.*
>
> *Middle case: the network has some active recipients but the recipients are occupied with other priorities. The ping is logged. Acknowledgment comes slowly or not at all. A long lead time before consequence.*
>
> *Concerning case: the network has active recipients who are actively monitoring for exactly this type of activation from a believed-dormant station. Response time: unknown. Content of response: unknown.*
>
> *Why Axis-7 activated it now: I think I understand this. The station's occupancy status was dormant because no Seed-carrier had been present. Now one is. The dormant-station classification had been — hiding us. Keeping the Chengdu station off the network's awareness. Now that I'm here, Axis-7 has decided the correct approach is to be visible rather than concealed.*
>
> *She made a decision about the station's relationship to the broader network. She did not consult me. She wanted me to see the decision being made.*
>
> *This is not the same as consulting me. It is more like informing me.*
>
> *I respect the distinction. I need to understand what it means for the Formican community here — that this decision was Axis-7's to make, not the Keepers', not the archive worker who gave me the notes. She has authority here.*

He looked at his notes on Axis-7: veteran integration stage, reading the cultivation protocol containers, three weeks of observation before speaking. She was not a Keeper. She was not archive staff.

She was something else.

He needed to find out what, specifically. The communication register was at eighty percent now. He could ask.

He had one other item of business first.

He called Chen Yufei.

"The array," he said. "Has your network heard anything about a signal from Chengdu's direction?"

A pause. "What kind of signal?"

"Veritonal network. Not electromagnetic. Something that went out two days ago."

Another pause. "The Celestial Compass School monitors Veritonal propagation anomalies. It's part of their astronomical cultivation practice." Her voice had changed quality. "I received a message this morning from my contact there. She mentioned an unusual propagation pattern originating from Chengdu. She was asking if I knew anything about it."

"What did you tell her?"

"That I was looking into the anomaly." A pause. "Is this the Strata?"

"It's connected to the Strata. Something in the monitoring infrastructure that was reactivated." He paused. "The Celestial Compass School has instruments that can detect Veritonal propagation at network scale?"

"Apparently. They've been developing their detection capability for years." She paused. "This is going to matter for the distribution list."

He had been thinking the same thing. "Two of the forty-seven are Celestial Compass School. They're going to receive the translation document in the context of already having questions about the Chengdu propagation anomaly."

"They're going to connect the dots within the first day."

"Yes." He thought. "That's probably not a problem. If anything, it means the translation document arrives at exactly the moment when the questions are most vivid." He paused. "Keep the release timing as planned. The propagation anomaly is context that makes the document more urgent, not less."

She was quiet for a moment.

"You're calm about this," she said.

"The signal has already propagated. What I can do about it is understand it and prepare. Not be calm — be useful." He paused. "I'm going to ask Axis-7 what she knows about the network's response timeline."

"Who is Axis-7?"

He had been referring to her by the designation he'd pulled from the archive index. "The Formican who corrected my derivation errors. I don't have a fuller identification yet." He paused. "That conversation is next."

"Today?"

"As soon as I get to the Strata."

He went.
