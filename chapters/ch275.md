# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Seventy-Five — Wei Canglan's Progress

Eight months of framework-first cultivation had changed Wei Canglan in ways Lin Mushi found himself genuinely admiring, watching the younger man work through his evening practice in the training space they'd carved out of the Strata's reconstruction months ago.

He'd built his understanding slowly, methodically, refusing every shortcut Lin Mushi might have offered him in a weaker moment — insisting, instead, on deriving each layer of theory independently before attempting the technique it implied, the same patient discipline Lin Mushi had once insisted on and Wei Canglan had, somewhere along the way, made entirely his own.

"I want to show you something," Wei Canglan said, finding him in the outer hall on an ordinary evening. "I've been working on Ratio 3 derivation. I think I found something."

Lin Mushi followed him to the training space, curious despite himself, and watched Wei Canglan settle into the careful entry posture for a Compression attempt.

The signature deepened steadily, the familiar climb he'd watched countless times now, except the geometry underneath it felt subtly different from his own original path to Ratio 3 — a different primary axis, a different timing relationship between the channels, the kind of variation that suggested independent derivation rather than simple replication of a memorized pattern.

Ratio 3 held, stable, clean.

"How long can you hold it?" Lin Mushi asked.

"Eighteen minutes, last test. Still improving."

Lin Mushi extended his own perception carefully toward the geometry Wei Canglan had built, studying the architecture with genuine professional curiosity rather than simple verification.

"This is different from how I reached Ratio 3," he said slowly, working through the variation. "You've routed the interface through a different relationship between the primary channels — more efficient, actually, in terms of the energy cost per unit of stability. This is better than what I did originally."

Wei Canglan released the Compression carefully, settling back into ordinary baseline, something quietly satisfied crossing his usually composed expression.

"I had time to think about it properly," he said. "You were in a hurry, the first time you reached this stage — racing against the Northern Peak coalition, trying to understand Document 7 fast enough to be useful before the political situation collapsed around you. I didn't have that pressure. I had eight months to derive this carefully, testing each piece before building the next, the way you originally taught me to work."

Lin Mushi sat with this, the particular satisfaction of watching a student surpass a teacher's original path settling into something he genuinely hadn't expected to feel this early in Wei Canglan's development.

"This is better than what I did for Ratio 3," he said plainly, without hedging the assessment.

"I know," Wei Canglan said, something almost amused entering his voice. "I checked it three different ways before I was confident enough to show you. I wasn't going to claim an improvement I couldn't actually defend."

"That's exactly the discipline I was trying to teach you."

"I know that too. It's why I bothered checking three times instead of simply being satisfied the first time it worked."

Lin Mushi studied the younger man for a moment — the careful, patient composure that had grown, over eight months and considerably longer before that, into something genuinely his own, distinct now from the angry, displaced young man who'd shown up at Chen Yufei's door with a single bag and no expression.

"I'm proud of this," he said. "Not in a way that implies the credit belongs to me. You built this yourself, on your own timeline, through your own careful work. I just gave you the framework and got out of the way."

Wei Canglan considered this for a moment, something settling in his expression that looked, briefly, less guarded than his usual composed bearing allowed.

"The framework mattered," he said. "I want to be honest about that too. I couldn't have derived this without the foundation you insisted on building first, even when I was frustrated that you wouldn't just hand me the technique directly."

"Both things can be true," Lin Mushi said. "I gave you the foundation. You built something better on top of it than I'd built myself. That's not a contradiction. That's exactly what good teaching is supposed to produce."

Wei Canglan nodded, and returned to his practice, working now through the careful refinement of a technique that had become, unmistakably, entirely his own — the student's path diverging cleanly from the teacher's, in exactly the way Lin Mushi had always hoped it eventually would.
