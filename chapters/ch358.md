# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifty-Eight — Fang Ruxue's Health

He noticed it first through the Colony Link, a faint, persistent texture of discomfort in Fang Ruxue's field that he'd registered, over recent weeks, without fully attending to it — the specific, low-grade quality of chronic pain someone had learned to carry without complaint, present often enough that he'd begun, uncomfortably, to recognize it as background rather than anomaly.

He raised it with her directly, choosing a quiet afternoon rather than letting the observation sit unaddressed any longer.

"I need to ask you something honestly," he said, "and I want you to know I'm not asking because I think you've been hiding anything deliberately. I've noticed something through the Colony Link, and I think it deserves a direct conversation rather than me just quietly worrying about it."

Fang Ruxue's expression shifted, something guarded briefly crossing her usually composed features before she settled back into her characteristic directness. "My Qi-channel injury. From Arc Three."

"I didn't know you were still carrying it."

"Mostly healed," she said, though something in her tone suggested the qualifier carried more weight than the reassurance intended. "Mostly. At fifty-eight, mostly healed doesn't quite mean what it meant when I was younger. There's a persistent weakness in the channel that doesn't seem inclined to fully resolve, no matter how much rest or careful cultivation I give it."

"Is it getting worse?"

"Not dramatically. Slowly, perhaps. Enough that I've started thinking about it more seriously than I once did." She met his eyes directly, her usual practical honesty unwavering even on a subject clearly difficult for her. "I've been considering Formican biological extension. I haven't made any decision. I wanted to think it through fully before raising it with anyone, including you."

---

He sat with the admission, understanding, with a specific weight, exactly how significant a consideration this actually represented.

"What would that involve?" he asked.

"I don't fully know the details yet. I've asked Meen-4 for preliminary information, nothing more. The general shape, as I understand it, involves Formican techniques for extending functional health and longevity beyond what human biology alone typically manages — not immortality, nothing so dramatic. Simply a meaningful extension of capable, healthy years, applied to an injury that isn't likely to heal further through purely human recovery."

"Do you want it?"

"I don't know yet," she admitted, something rawer entering her usually measured tone. "Part of me resists the idea on principle — I've spent my whole life as fully, unmodified human, and there's something in me that flinches at the idea of becoming something else, even partially, even for good reason. Part of me recognizes that principle might simply be pride, dressed up as conviction, standing in the way of a genuinely sensible choice."

He considered his own honest reaction carefully before responding, understanding that she'd come to him wanting real perspective rather than easy reassurance.

"Can I be direct?" he asked.

"Please."

"I think the Collective will need you for what's coming," he said slowly, choosing the words with care. "Not just the review. Whatever comes after it, however it resolves. You've built something here that I don't think anyone else could have built quite the way you have — the governance framework, the patient, careful structure that's let this city become an actual community rather than just my personal project. I'd be lying if I said I didn't want you healthy and capable for as long as that can reasonably be arranged."

---

She was quiet for a long moment, absorbing his honesty with the same careful weighing she brought to every serious decision.

"That's helpful," she said finally. "Not as permission — I don't need your permission, and I don't think you're offering it as such. But as honest input from someone whose judgment I trust, weighing something I've been struggling to weigh clearly on my own."

"I don't want to push you toward anything you're not genuinely certain about."

"I know. I don't think you are pushing. I think you're being honest, the way I asked you to be." She managed a small, tired smile. "I'll keep thinking about it. I don't think I'm ready to decide today. But I'm glad we talked about it directly, rather than letting you quietly worry through the Colony Link while I quietly said nothing."

He nodded, understanding that the conversation's real value lay less in any resolution and more in the honesty itself — one more piece, he thought, of the careful, deliberate transparency this world had slowly taught him mattered more than almost anything else.

He wrote about it that evening, brief and thoughtful.

> *Fang Ruxue is considering Formican biological extension. She hasn't decided. I told her honestly that the Collective will need her for what's coming. I meant it. I hope, whatever she decides, it's a choice made from genuine clarity rather than obligation to any of us.*
