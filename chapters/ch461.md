# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 461 — A New Sunday Ritual

The Sunday calls continued exactly as they always had, and also, quietly, entirely differently.

He called at the same time he always called, sitting at the same small table above the noodle shop where he'd taken every important measurement of the past five years, and she answered the way she always answered, mid-task, slightly out of breath, some ordinary domestic project interrupted by the phone's ring.

"I'm elbow-deep in the eastern bed," she said, by way of greeting. "Meen-4's fungal network theory is either brilliant or has ruined three years of soil for good. I'll know which by autumn."

"How are the knees holding up?"

"Perfectly," she said, with the particular satisfaction of someone reporting a fact she still found mildly delightful to state plainly. "I dug for four hours yesterday and woke up this morning without a single complaint from either of them. I'd forgotten what that felt like. It's been a very long time."

They talked for the better part of an hour, the same unhurried rhythm they'd always had — the garden, the noodle shop's ongoing feud with the parking committee, a question she'd been saving about whether Formican agricultural technique had anything useful to say about companion planting. Ordinary conversation, warm and specific and entirely unconcerned with stellar mechanics or dissolution zones or anything else waiting, patiently, in the larger shape of his life.

What had changed, he understood, listening to her describe the eastern bed's slow transformation, wasn't the content of the calls at all. It was the weight underneath them.

For four years, every Sunday conversation had carried a second, silent conversation running beneath it — everything he wasn't saying, every careful omission dressed up as protection, the specific effort of performing an ordinary life for someone he loved while an extraordinary one continued, unmentioned, just out of her sight. He hadn't fully understood, until it was gone, how much of his attention that performance had actually required.

Now there was simply the call itself. No second conversation running underneath. No careful management of which details were safe to mention and which needed softening or omitting entirely. When she asked what he'd been working on, he could simply tell her — not the full technical weight of it, not more than she wanted, but honestly, without the reflexive filtering that had governed every previous exchange.

"I've been thinking about the dissolution problem again," he told her, near the end of the call. "The one I mentioned — the ancient wound in the Lattice, from the researcher who died trying to reach a scale I'm still working toward myself."

"Veth-Karun," she said, recalling the name with the same careful attention she'd brought to every new piece of vocabulary across the past months.

"Veth-Karun," he confirmed, something settling warmly in his chest at hearing her say it correctly, unprompted. "I set it aside for a while, to focus on you, on the extension. I think I'm ready to pick it back up properly now."

"Good," she said. "That sounds like exactly the kind of enormous, patient problem you're best at. Go solve it properly. Just don't disappear for two months without calling this time."

"I promise," he said, and meant it entirely.

He ended the call the way he always ended it, and sat for a while afterward in the quiet of his apartment, feeling lighter than he had in longer than he could easily place — not because the Veth-Karun problem had gotten any smaller in his absence, but because he was returning to it now without the specific, accumulated weight of a secret still waiting to be told.

He thought, briefly, of the version of himself who had first opened this notebook five years ago, terrified of a Codex he couldn't read, certain that whatever waited beyond his ordinary life would eventually cost him the people who mattered most. That version of him, he understood now, had been wrong about the shape of the danger. The extraordinary had never been the threat. The silence he'd built around it, one careful omission at a time, had been the only genuinely corrosive thing in the whole five years — and he had, finally, set it down.

He opened the notebook, found a clean page, and wrote a single line before turning, finally, back to the Void Lattice's ancient, patient wound.

*Everything I was protecting her from is behind me now. Whatever comes next, at least I get to face it honestly, on both sides of every Sunday call.*

He closed the notebook, made a fresh pot of tea, and began, with his full attention finally, entirely available, the careful work of understanding exactly what forty million years of quiet dissolution actually required to heal.
