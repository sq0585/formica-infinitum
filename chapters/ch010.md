# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ten — What the Diagram Shows

He decided, on the twelfth day, to approach the notation as a language problem.

Not a mathematics problem. Not a physics problem. A _language_ problem, which meant something specific: it had structure that was internally consistent, units that recurred in predictable contexts, relationships that implied grammar rather than formula. Languages could be decoded without external reference if you had enough instances and enough patience. That was the premise. That was where he would start.

He spread four notebooks across the desk.

The first contained his Codex screenshots, printed cheaply at a copy shop, annotated in red pen. The second was his running observation log. The third was blank, designated now for linguistic analysis only. The fourth was a reference collection — linguistics papers he'd downloaded and skimmed, a borrowed book on the decipherment of Linear B that he'd read twice in university and found again on his shelf, and a printout of a methodology article titled _Non-Linguistic Symbol Systems and Their Internal Logic_ that had looked useful and turned out to be very boring and also useful.

He made tea. He aligned the materials. He began.

---

The notation had three obvious features that he catalogued first, because the obvious was always where you started.

_Feature one: recurrence._ Specific symbols appeared many times across the diagram, at different scales and in different configurations. This meant they were not unique — they were units, the way letters were units. How many distinct units? He spent forty minutes counting, losing count twice, arriving at a working estimate of somewhere between sixty and eighty, with the caveat that some he was probably splitting when they were variations of the same sign, and some he was probably lumping when they were distinct.

_Feature two: combination rules._ The units did not appear randomly next to each other. Certain combinations recurred; certain others never appeared at all. This was the structural fingerprint of grammar — not all arrangements were permitted, which meant the system had rules, which meant it had logic, which meant it could in principle be reverse-engineered.

_Feature three: the weight differential._ He had noticed this on day one, the nodes that felt heavier than others, but he hadn't articulated it as a feature of the notation itself until now. Some symbols appeared at these heavy nodes, and some appeared only in the connecting lines, and some appeared in both but seemed to behave differently depending on which context they were in. Context-sensitive symbols. That was a specific property. He drew a line in the third notebook and wrote it down.

He worked for three hours without stopping, which he registered only when he stood to refill his tea and noticed that his legs had gone slightly numb from sitting.

He had covered eleven pages.

He had understood nothing concretely yet. But the shape of the not-understanding had gotten clearer, which was progress — the particular progress of reducing a large unknown into a collection of smaller ones.

---

On the thirteenth page, something shifted.

He had been trying to count the distinct symbols for the second time, working from a fresh printout, when he noticed that a cluster of three symbols near the center of the diagram always appeared together, always in the same sequence, and always adjacent to what he had been calling a heavy node.

Not sometimes. Every time. Across every scale at which he could find heavy nodes — and there were several, nested — the same three-symbol cluster appeared.

He stared at it.

A signature. Or a label. Or a classification — the way certain prefixes in chemistry indicated a class of compound before the specific name. Not the name of the node itself. The _type_.

He drew the three symbols carefully in the third notebook, his best reproduction, and wrote below them:

> _Recurring cluster at heavy nodes. Not a name — a category marker. Hypothesis: these three symbols mean something equivalent to "point of significance" or "anchor" or "junction." The heavy nodes are a type, and this cluster is their type-label._

He held this thought for a moment before continuing.

> _If that's right, then the diagram has a taxonomy. And a taxonomy implies a system of classification. And a system of classification implies whoever built this had a theory of what there was to classify._

He looked up at the Codex diagram on his phone, open beside the notebooks, doing its slow patient breathing.

The man who had built this had spent four hundred years on it.

Four hundred years of classification. Four hundred years of taxonomy. Four hundred years of understanding — or attempting to understand — whatever lay beneath the surface of the world.

And he had transmitted the whole thing in 0.4 seconds to a convenience store clerk at 3 AM in Wuhou District.

Lin Mushi looked at his eleven pages of notes and the third notebook and the Linear B book and the linguistics methodology printout, and felt the particular quality of vertigo that came not from confusion but from scale — the sudden awareness that the problem in front of him was not going to resolve in a week, or a month, or perhaps a year, and that this was alright, and that the only thing to do about it was to continue working.

He turned to the next blank page.

> _Working model (Day 12):_
>
> _The Codex notation is a formal system. Approx. 60–80 distinct units. Context-sensitive grammar. Category markers for node types._
>
> _What the diagram shows: I don't know yet. But I know now that it shows something specific rather than something general. It is not a map of the world. It is a map of a particular structure within the world._
>
> _Next step: identify a second recurring cluster and determine its distributional relationship to the first. If I can find two category markers, I can begin to understand what categories the system has._
>
> _Rule: one cluster at a time. Treat this the same way you'd treat a dig site. Remove one layer. Note what's there. Then remove the next._

He capped the pen. He looked out the window. The sky was doing the Chengdu gray thing, the non-committal gray, the gray that neither threatened nor promised anything.

He thought about the watch-repair shop, and the air that felt different inside it, and a man who kept records standing across the street watching the demolition and saying _something was in there that shouldn't have been in the rubble._

Something had been in there.

It had a notation system with sixty to eighty distinct units and category markers and context-sensitive grammar.

He added a final note, more question than observation:

> _If I decode enough of this to understand what the heavy nodes are — what I've been perceiving as depth beneath floors and inside construction frames and obliquely through the ground — what do I do with that information?_
>
> _Unknown. Keep going. Decide when you know more._

He closed the notebook, made a third cup of tea, and went back to work.
