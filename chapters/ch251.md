# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-One — The Decision on Violence

He sat with the notebook open late that night, the conference room's wreckage still vivid behind his eyes, and wrote the sentence he'd been circling since the moment he'd knelt beside the injured scholar.

> *Gou Ping's faction will not stop. I need to be able to stop them — not defeat, stop. The difference matters.*

He read the sentence back, testing it against everything he believed about what he was willing to become in order to protect the people who'd chosen to stand beside him.

Defeat implied force matched against force, harm exchanged for harm, the kind of escalating confrontation where the eventual winner simply hurt the loser badly enough that they stopped trying. He'd avoided that path every time it had presented itself — the maze against Gou Ping's first assault, the careful, bloodless containment he'd always reached for first. Today's chaos hadn't been a failure of that principle. It had been a failure of preparation, a crude, rushed intervention that hadn't had time to be anything more careful than it was.

He needed something better. Not a weapon — he was certain of that much, certain enough that he wrote it down plainly, the way he wrote down every principle he wasn't willing to compromise on later under pressure.

> *Not a weapon. A weapon is built to hurt people who oppose you, calibrated for damage. I don't want that, not even against people who've shown they're willing to hurt uninvolved bystanders. I want something that removes the capacity for harm without requiring me to cause harm in the process. Containment, not defeat.*

He spent the rest of the night in the Archive, pulling every defensive technique record he could find, searching with a specific, narrow question he hadn't needed to ask this precisely before: what immobilizes rather than destroys?

Most of what the Codex described as defensive technique assumed a Formican context that didn't map cleanly onto his current problem — defenses built for micro-scale conflict, or for environmental hazards rather than hostile cultivators. But buried in Document 8's later sections, he found something closer to what he was looking for: a category of spatial technique the notation translated, loosely, as *containment without contact* — geometry designed to neutralize an opponent's ability to act within a bounded area, without ever directly touching or harming the person contained.

It was theoretical. Underdeveloped. The Codex described the principle without providing a complete, ready-to-use technique, the way it so often did with anything genuinely advanced — handing him the architecture and trusting him to build the specific application himself.

He worked through the night, sketching geometry, testing small-scale versions in the pocket dimension laboratory, failing and adjusting with the same patient, methodical attention he'd brought to every breakthrough this strange world had eventually granted him.

By morning, he had the beginning of something — not finished, not yet reliable enough to deploy against a real threat, but the clear shape of a principle he believed could actually work.

Chen Yufei found him still working, the morning light filtering faintly through the pocket dimension's single window-shaped aperture, his eyes red with exhaustion and his notebook filled with three separate failed iterations of the same core geometry.

"You've been here all night," she said.

"I needed to find something better than what happened yesterday. Crude improvisation isn't good enough anymore. Someone's arm broke because I wasn't fast enough or precise enough to prevent it."

She sat down across from him, studying the sketched geometry with the careful attention she brought to anything that mattered.

"What is it?"

"The beginning of an answer," he said. "Not finished. But I think, given enough work, this could actually let me stop people like Gou Ping without ever needing to hurt them to do it."

"How long until it's finished?"

He looked at the notebook, the three failed iterations, the rough shape of a fourth attempt already forming in his mind.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "But I'm not stopping until it is. Whatever comes next from Gou Ping's faction, I want to be ready with something better than what I managed yesterday."

Chen Yufei nodded, and without further discussion, pulled her own notebook from her bag and settled in beside him, ready to spend however many more hours the work required.
