# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 502 — Scale One Through Four — Simultaneous

The first four scales came the way the Archive had promised they would, and not at all the way he had privately expected.

He had assumed, without ever quite examining the assumption, that holding Micro Scale, Compression, Dimensional Weaver, and Planetary Architect perception simultaneously would feel like holding four separate weights at once — a matter of strength, of capacity, the same kind of effort as carrying more than his hands were built for. He had trained for exactly that kind of effort for five years. He was ready for it.

It was not that.

Micro Scale arrived first, the way it always had — the molecular hush of the room around him, the slow chemical conversation of the cushion fibers under his hands, the specific vibration of Chen Yufei's tea, three cups old now and long since cold, sitting untouched on the table Wei Canglan had insisted on bringing in "for practicality," a phrase that had, over five years, come to mean *because I am worried and this is how I show it.*

Compression joined it without displacing it. He felt his own cellular structure the way he had once, years ago, spent eleven minutes staring at a single stubborn notation symbol — a slowness that used to require closing everything else out and now simply sat alongside the molecular hush, a second channel rather than a competing one.

Dimensional Weaver followed, the pocket dimension laboratory's own careful architecture becoming visible to him not as a place he occupied but as a structure he could feel the edges of from the inside, the same way a person feels the walls of a room in the dark without needing to see them.

And then Planetary Architect — Earth's whole weather of lives, twelve hundred connections and more besides, the Strata's four thousand two hundred residents, Chengdu's twenty million, arriving not as flood, not even as background anymore, but as one voice among four, equally present, equally clear.

He held all of it. He did not switch between them. This was the part the Archive's language had failed to fully prepare him for, however carefully he'd read it — the specific, disorienting fact that this was not four separate acts of attention performed in rapid sequence, the way a skilled reader's eye might jump between four open books fast enough to feel continuous. It was one act. Four books, and he was reading all of them, and understanding all of them, at once.

*Observation 502,* he wrote, his hand steady in a way that surprised him. *This is not harder than holding each scale separately. It is a different kind of experience entirely — closer to gaining a new sense than to exercising an old one more strenuously. I do not yet have language for what kind of sense.*

Wei Canglan's connection through the Anchor Protocol sharpened at that observation, a small pulse of attention Lin Mushi had learned, over years, to read as the particular shape of Wei Canglan's worry — not fear exactly, but the worry of someone whose entire discipline was built on knowing precisely where every boundary sat, watching a boundary he could not himself perceive begin to move.

*You're all right,* Lin Mushi sent back, unprompted, before Wei Canglan could ask.

*I did not ask,* Wei Canglan sent, and there was something almost like humor folded into the formality of it.

*You were about to.*

*I was composing the question with appropriate restraint. You have interrupted a perfectly good sentence.*

Chen Yufei's presence warmed, briefly, at that — the specific quiet amusement of someone who had watched this exact dynamic play out a hundred times before and never tired of it.

He let himself hold the moment the way he was holding everything else now — not separately, not as a break from the work, but simply as one more true thing occupying the same field as the molecular hush and the planetary weather and the walls he could feel without seeing.

Four scales. One perception. He sat with it a while, testing its edges the careful way he tested everything, and found, to his quiet surprise, that it did not strain him at all.

The fifth scale was still waiting. He could feel its shape already, distant and enormous, at the edge of what the four together made room for.

Not yet, he told himself. In order. Always in order.

He breathed once, deliberately, the last ordinary breath he suspected he would take for some time, and reached toward it anyway.
