# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Seventy-Seven — Chen Yufei's Acceleration

While the political situation developed around them, Chen Yufei's own cultivation had been advancing at a pace that, by the time anyone formally noticed it, had already become remarkable.

She achieved Compression Ratio 3.5 on a Tuesday — past full Ratio 3 stability, into the unstable transitional zone toward Ratio 4 — during what had started as an ordinary practice session in the Strata library. Lin Mushi, working nearby on his own Dimensional Lock practice, felt the shift in her Qi signature before she announced it: a sudden increase in density and complexity that registered through his peripheral Colony Link awareness as something qualitatively different from her usual practice state.

He looked up. She was sitting very still, eyes closed, her field of perception clearly extended well beyond her normal range.

He didn't interrupt. He waited.

When she came out of it, eleven minutes later, she opened her eyes with the specific disorientation of someone who has just seen further than they expected to.

"I think I just touched the edge of Dimensional Weaving," she said.

He set down his work entirely. "Describe it."

"Not the technique itself — I don't have the theoretical framework yet, the way you've been building toward it with Axis-7's lessons. But the *quality* of it. For a moment, the wall I was sitting near stopped being something I needed to compress myself to pass through. It felt — adjustable. Like I could have folded the space instead." She paused, working to find the words. "I don't know if that makes sense."

"It makes complete sense," he said. "That's exactly the distinction Axis-7 described to me — discipline of self versus discipline of the world. You touched the edge of the second one."

She looked startled by this, in the way of someone who has just been told their private, half-formed experience has a name and a context far larger than they realized.

"Nine years of Iron Meridian practice," she said slowly, "and then ten months of corrected technique, and now this."

"Your foundation was always solid," he said. "It just had inefficiencies built in. Once those were corrected, the underlying capability you'd built over nine years was free to develop at its actual rate, instead of the suppressed rate the flawed technique imposed."

She sat with this.

"This is going to draw attention," she said.

It did, within the week.

The Iron Meridian Court — despite her formal expulsion still in effect — sent a delegation. Not to discipline her; her expulsion meant they had no formal standing to discipline her further. They came to ask, with the careful diplomatic language of an institution that had lost the right to demand answers but hadn't lost the desire for them, where she had obtained this rate of advancement.

She told them the truth, the same plain truth she'd been telling everyone since the document's first release: corrected technique, properly taught, applied consistently. She did not mention the Strata specifically. She did not need to — the delegation seemed less interested in mechanism than in the simple, uncomfortable fact that an expelled practitioner was now advancing faster than anyone currently inside their structure.

"They're going to keep watching," Lin Mushi said, after the delegation left.

"I know," Chen Yufei said. "I've gotten used to being watched. It used to bother me more than it does now."

He thought about this — the trajectory of her past ten months, institutional standing lost, capability gained, the strange inverse relationship between the two that neither of them had planned but that had become, increasingly, the central proof of everything the document had argued. The institutions had been holding their practitioners back. Correction released what had always been there.

"What does it feel like?" he asked. "The acceleration."

She considered this seriously.

"Like catching up to something that was always waiting for me," she said. "Not like becoming someone new. Like finally becoming who I should have been able to become nine years ago, if anyone had told me the truth from the start."

He nodded.

He understood that feeling better than almost anything else she could have said.
