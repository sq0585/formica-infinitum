# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Five — The Pacific Ring Discovery

The 140-node Circuit network had reached, somewhere over the preceding months, a scale he hadn't fully appreciated until he attempted something he'd never had the coverage or the processing capability to attempt before: a planetary-scale Qi density survey, run quietly across an ordinary evening, more out of methodical curiosity than any specific expectation.

He set the survey running and went to make tea.

He came back to a result he sat with for a long time before he trusted it.

Deep beneath the Pacific Ocean, running in a pattern too regular to be natural geology, the survey had picked up structures he'd never encountered any reference to in two years of Archive study. Chitin-matter formations, vast in scale, threaded through the ocean floor in a configuration that, when he overlaid it against a map of the Pacific basin's tectonic boundaries, traced something disturbingly close to the entire Ring of Fire.

He checked the survey's calibration three times, certain he'd made an error.

The structures held.

Not ruins. He understood this within the first careful hour of analysis — the Qi signature wasn't the cold, dormant residue he'd learned to associate with abandoned Formican infrastructure. It was active, low-level but unmistakably functioning, the particular signature of a system still doing whatever it had originally been built to do.

He thought about every Formican ruin he'd explored, every dormant structure Meen-4 had shown him, every careful, patient lesson about a civilization in retreat, its great works abandoned and slowly decaying across forty million years.

This wasn't decaying. This was still running.

He worked through the night, refining the survey's resolution, mapping the structure's full extent as carefully as his current network's reach allowed. By morning, he had a picture he didn't fully understand but couldn't responsibly ignore — a planetary-scale system, ancient, active, connected somehow to the same broader anchor network that tethered the Chengdu Strata and presumably every other Strata across the globe.

He wrote in his notebook, the careful, methodical hand he used for anything genuinely significant.

> *Deep Chitin-matter structures detected beneath the Pacific Ocean, configuration tracing the Ring of Fire almost exactly. Active, not dormant. Connected to the broader anchor network.*
>
> *This is not a ruin. This is infrastructure, still functioning, on a scale I have no framework yet for understanding the purpose of.*
>
> *I need to bring this to Meen-4. I suspect she already knows what this is. I suspect, given the pattern of everything I've learned so far in this world, that knowing will come with an obligation attached to it I haven't yet had time to brace for.*

He closed the notebook and sat for a moment with the particular, familiar weight of a discovery that had just quietly, irrevocably expanded the size of the world he was responsible for.

Two years ago, his responsibility had begun and ended with a notation he couldn't read and a dying man's final gesture. It had grown, slowly, chapter by chapter, crisis by crisis, until it encompassed a Strata, a community, a Collective spanning six traditions and sixty-seven careful, hard-won members.

He looked at the survey data again, the vast structure tracing the entire Pacific Rim beneath layers of ocean and rock he'd never have imagined his attention reaching.

He understood, sitting with it in the quiet early morning light, that whatever came next was going to be considerably larger than anything that had come before.

He finished his cold tea, and went to find Meen-4.
