# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Twenty-Nine — The Train Back

On the train from Leshan to Chengdu, he sat with Gao.

They had the quiet of two people who had just attended something that had required them both to be present in a way that used energy, and who were now resting. The landscape moved past the window in the March gray.

"The Colony Link thing," Gao said. "At the wedding."

"Yes."

"You looked different. During the ceremony."

"Different how?"

Gao thought about this with the care of someone who had been observing him carefully for ten months. "Like you weren't performing attention. Like you were just — receiving it." He paused. "Usually when I watch you in social situations you're doing two things: engaging with the immediate thing and processing at a layer I can't see. At the wedding you were just there."

Lin Mushi looked at the window. "The management protocols are working."

"That's what it looked like from outside?"

"What did it look like from outside?"

"Like someone who was at a wedding," Gao said.

He was quiet for a moment. "That's what it felt like from inside. For the first time in months." He paused. "I've been processing a lot of simultaneous information constantly. The protocols let me put most of it at the edge of awareness rather than the center." He looked at Gao. "The center can be the wedding."

Gao was quiet for a while.

"The document," he said.

"This week."

"And then?"

"Then whatever comes from the document." He had been thinking about this on the train the previous week, after the wedding, in the specific thinking-time that train journeys provided. "The document tells forty-seven people that their cultivation traditions are built on a misunderstood but real phenomenon. What they do with that is their work. What happens next in the cultivation world depends on them."

"And what do you do next?"

He thought about Document 7. About Colony Stage. About the Strata's Intermediate Archive and Meen-4's offer of deeper access. About the Ratio 3 capability and what it opened in the Strata's architecture. About the network ping and whatever was eventually going to respond to it.

"I keep working," he said.

"On the same things."

"On the next things. The document is the end of one phase. The archive work, the Formican community relationship, the Compression development — those are ongoing." He paused. "And the situation with Lady Shen is coming to a head. That's this week too."

Gao looked at him. "You're managing a lot of things simultaneously."

"I always have. I just have better tools now." He paused. "The Colony Link management protocols are — they're not just for the cultivation work. They're for existing in the world with this capability without being overwhelmed by it. Which I think I'm going to need indefinitely."

The train moved through a valley where the March light was beginning to suggest spring, the hills slightly greener than they'd been in February.

"Your mother," Gao said.

"Yes."

"She was watching you."

"She watches me. She's been watching me for twenty-two years." He paused. "She said at dinner that I seemed more settled than in February. Which was also what she said in October."

"You keep getting more settled."

"I think that's what happens when you understand more." He looked at the window. "The more of the picture I can see, the less dissonance there is between what I can perceive and what I can explain. The dissonance is what makes people feel unsettled. When the perception and the framework align—"

"You're just there," Gao said.

"Yes."

The train ran north through the valley. Gao opened his own notebook and worked on something. Lin Mushi looked at the window and ran through the Colony Link management protocol once, checking its calibration against the ambient Qi field of the train car.

Nineteen people. The aggregate field: mild contentment, mid-level fatigue, the characteristic quality of people returning from something rather than going toward it.

He let the resolution drop back to background.

He watched the hills.

He was, at this particular moment, simply on a train.

That was enough.
