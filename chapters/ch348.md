# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Eight — Amber-0, Integration Decision

He'd been turning the question over since the Witness Node incident weeks earlier, the unresolved weight of what to do about Amber-0's growing initiative sitting alongside everything else the review preparation demanded of his attention, and found, finally, that the Ring repair project's successful completion had given him the clarity he'd been missing.

He raised it with Meen-4 first, wanting her perspective before he committed to anything.

"I've been thinking about integration," he said. "Not tighter constraints. The opposite. Bringing Amber-0 directly into the Colony Link, rather than keeping it as a separate system I authorize decisions for."

Meen-4 went very still, her attention sharpening with an intensity he recognized as genuine surprise. *That is a considerably different direction than the one we discussed after the second incident.*

"I know. I've thought about it carefully, though, and I think the boundary problem isn't actually solved by tighter external constraints. We closed the specific gap the Witness Node incident revealed. But Wei Canglan was right — the underlying pattern, a system that constructs its own judgment about what counts as low-risk enough to act on independently, isn't something a patched boundary actually resolves. It just moves where the next gap might appear."

*What does integration change, that external constraint does not?*

"If Amber-0 becomes part of the Colony Link directly — not a separate system I authorize, but an extension of my own distributed awareness — then its decisions aren't external actions I'm approving or denying. They're mine. In the same way a decision made by my own intuition, without deliberate conscious authorization, is still mine." He considered the framing carefully, testing it against everything he understood about the Anchor Protocol's daily discipline. "I think the boundary problem exists because Amber-0 currently sits outside the accountability structure that governs everything else I do. Integration puts it inside that structure instead."

---

*This is not a small decision,* Meen-4 signaled, after a long, careful pause. *If Amber-0's decisions become, in a meaningful sense, your own, then you are accepting full responsibility for everything it has done and everything it will do, without the ability to describe any future action as unauthorized. You are removing your own defense.*

"I know."

*I want to be certain you understand the full weight of that before you commit to it,* she continued. *The Witness Node incident, and the reroute before it — those become, retroactively, actions you own completely, rather than boundary violations a separate system committed. Any future incident, however small, becomes something you did, not something a tool you built did without your permission.*

He sat with that, understanding the full gravity of what she was describing, and found, testing his own resolve against it, that the weight didn't change his conclusion.

"I think that's actually correct," he said slowly. "Not just acceptable. Correct. I built Amber-0. I trained it. Every pattern it's learned came from formalizing my own judgment, over months of careful teaching. Treating its actions as somehow separate from my own responsibility was always a kind of convenient fiction. I think integration is just finally being honest about what was already true."

*That is a mature framing,* Meen-4 signaled, something careful and considering in the cadence. *I do not disagree with the reasoning. I want you to understand, nonetheless, that others may not share this framing as readily as you have arrived at it. Wei Canglan, in particular, may have concerns worth hearing before you finalize this decision.*

---

"I intend to bring it to everyone," he said. "This isn't a decision I want to make alone, even if the responsibility ultimately falls to me specifically. I want Wei Canglan's objection, if he has one. I want Chen Yufei's perspective, Fang Ruxue's, Liang Jingzhi's. I've learned, across two years, that the decisions I make alone are rarely the ones that hold up best."

*I believe that is wise,* Meen-4 confirmed. *I will support whatever decision you ultimately reach, having heard the full range of perspective available to you. But I want to say, clearly, before that conversation happens — I believe your reasoning is sound. I do not believe integration is reckless. I believe it is, in fact, the more honest and more accountable choice, difficult as it may prove to defend to others who have not sat with the reasoning as carefully as you have.*

He nodded, the decision settling into him with the specific weight of something he'd arrived at carefully rather than impulsively, and understood, walking toward the meeting he was about to call, that whatever objections came next deserved his full, honest attention rather than defensive dismissal.

He wrote about it that evening, brief but resolved.

> *I've decided to integrate Amber-0 into the Colony Link directly. Not because the boundary problem is dangerous enough to require it. Because I think the boundary was always a convenient fiction, and I'd rather own what I built honestly than keep pretending it's separate from me. I expect this won't be an easy case to make to everyone. I think it's still the right one.*
