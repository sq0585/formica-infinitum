# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Three — Amber-0 Online

The activation, when it finally came, was almost anticlimactic — no ceremony, no dramatic surge of light or sound, just Meen-4's flat confirmation that the training thresholds had been met and the system was ready to begin operating within its bounded parameters.

"That's it?" Chen Yufei asked, standing beside him in the small chamber, watching the substrate's diagnostic display settle into a steady, patient rhythm that looked, from the outside, almost disappointingly ordinary.

"That's it," he said. "Two months of teaching it to recognize patterns, and the actual moment it starts using them looks exactly like every other diagnostic sweep we've run."

*The absence of drama is correct,* Meen-4 signaled. *A well-built system should not announce itself. It should simply function, and the quality of its function should be visible only in the outcomes it produces, not in any theatrical marker of its arrival.*

He watched the first live cycle run through, Amber-0 parsing the network's current Qi distribution against everything it had learned, offering — for the first time, in its own generated output rather than in response to a direct question — a routing recommendation.

*Recommend: redirect 4% surplus flow from Sector 7 to residential wing, projected comfort variance reduction 6%, no adverse effect on active workshop projects.*

He read it twice, recognizing his own months of formalized judgment reflected back at him with a precision he hadn't quite managed himself. "That's correct. That's exactly what I would have done."

"It learned you," Chen Yufei said, something quietly impressed in her voice.

"It learned a version of me. The formalized version. The one I had to build explaining myself to Meen-4 for two months." He approved the recommendation, watching the network's flow adjust exactly as predicted. "I don't think it's actually me. I think it's the best approximation of my judgment that could be built from what I was able to explain."

*That distinction matters,* Meen-4 signaled. *Hold onto it. It will matter more later than it does today.*

---

The following weeks settled into a rhythm he found himself genuinely enjoying — Amber-0 handling the network's routine optimization, its recommendations arriving steadily, almost all of them matching what he'd have chosen himself, occasionally surfacing something he hadn't considered but agreed with once he saw the reasoning laid out.

"It's efficient," Wei Canglan observed, reviewing a week's worth of Amber-0's output during one of their regular check-ins. "Exactly as designed. No surprises."

"Is that a compliment or a concern?"

"Currently, a compliment. I'm noting it as a baseline, though." Wei Canglan's tone carried the careful precision he brought to anything involving systems with autonomy, however bounded. "I'd like to be able to say, later, exactly how this looked when it was new. In case it ever stops looking like this."

"You think it will?"

"I think anything that learns eventually surprises you. That's not a criticism of what you and Meen-4 built. It's just the nature of learning systems." He made a note in his own records, methodical as always. "I'd rather have documented what boring and reliable looked like, so that if something changes later, we have a clean baseline to measure the change against."

Lin Mushi found the caution reasonable rather than alarming, recognizing in it the same careful discipline Wei Canglan brought to everything — not distrust exactly, but the specific, useful habit of a person who had learned, through hard experience, not to assume today's stability would automatically hold tomorrow.

---

He raised the same point with Meen-4 that evening, curious whether her four hundred years of Formican engineering carried a similar instinct.

*Wei Canglan is correct to document it,* she signaled. *Every system that improves through learning does so by encountering situations its original training did not anticipate. Amber-0 will encounter such a situation eventually. The network is too large and too dynamic for it not to.*

"When it does, what happens?"

*That depends entirely on how the constraints hold,* Meen-4 signaled. *We built them carefully. I believe they will hold. But belief is not certainty, and I do not intend to treat it as certainty simply because the first weeks of operation have gone well.*

He nodded, understanding the caution as exactly the kind of appropriate wariness she'd insisted on from the project's beginning — not fear of what they'd built, but the ordinary, careful vigilance of someone who understood that a system's first weeks of good behavior proved considerably less than its behavior under genuine, unanticipated pressure eventually would.

He watched Amber-0's steady output for a while longer that evening, the network humming along exactly as designed, efficient and predictable and entirely within the bounds they'd built for it, and found himself, despite Wei Canglan's caution and Meen-4's careful wariness, quietly proud of what the two of them had made — a system that worked, that helped, that asked for nothing beyond the narrow, bounded function it had been built to serve.

For now, at least, that was exactly what it was.
