# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Seven — After Dormancy

The Seed reactivated a little past the six-hour mark, the return of his perception arriving the way a held breath finally releases — not gradual, but a single, complete restoration, the ambient Qi field simply present again where moments before there had been nothing but ordinary air.

He sat with it for a moment before he moved, making certain it was real and not some fragile half-state that would slip away again if he reached for it too quickly.

It held.

He found Chen Yufei still at the anchor's edge, visibly exhausted but steady, the reinforcement work she'd carried alone for the past six hours finally easing as his own perception returned and he could feel, for the first time since the dormancy hit, the actual current state of the structure he'd spent fourteen hours protecting.

"It held," he said, the relief in his voice arriving before he'd fully processed the words himself.

"It held," she confirmed. "Degraded, but stable. The demolition crew finished their work above ground around the tenth hour — they got their parking structure demolished, whatever that was worth to them. The anchor's coherence never broke."

He extended his perception carefully toward the structure, mapping what remained of it with the same methodical attention he brought to everything, and found something he hadn't expected in the data.

The anchor's connection to the surface — the specific, fixed point where the parking structure's foundation had tethered the fold to physical geography — was gone. Not damaged. Severed, cleanly, the demolition's violence having finally accomplished exactly what it set out to accomplish at the most literal level: the physical anchor point no longer existed.

But the fold itself hadn't collapsed.

He sat with this, working through the implications carefully before he said anything.

"The surface tether's gone," he said slowly. "Completely. There's no longer a fixed physical point connecting this section of the network to ground-level geography."

"Is that bad?"

"I don't think so. I think it's the opposite." He extended his perception further, confirming what he was beginning to understand. "The fold's coherence held because the reinforcement I built over fourteen hours wasn't dependent on the surface tether the way the original Formican architecture was. It's anchored now to the dimensional structure itself, self-sustaining, the way the pocket dimensions I've been building independently have always worked."

Chen Yufei considered this, the exhaustion in her expression giving way, slowly, to something closer to cautious understanding.

"They tried to destroy it by attacking the surface connection," she said. "And the surface connection is gone now. But the thing they were actually trying to destroy is more stable than it's ever been."

"Yes."

"That's not a small irony."

"No," he agreed. "It's the kind of irony I'd have preferred not to learn at the cost of fourteen hours and a six-hour dormancy. But it's also, structurally, an improvement. This section of the network can't be attacked from the surface again — there's no longer a physical point to attack. Anyone who wants to reach it now has to do it the way I do everything else: through dimensional technique, earned the hard way, not through demolition permits and shell companies."

Chen Yufei looked at the place where the parking structure's foundation used to be, the demolition's aftermath still settling somewhere above them, and something in her expression shifted toward the particular weary satisfaction of someone watching a genuine threat collapse into an accidental advantage.

"A problem became an advantage," she said.

"This time," he said. "I don't think I should count on that pattern holding indefinitely. But this time, yes."

He sat with the weight of the past fourteen hours settling into his body — the dormancy, the exhaustion, the genuine fear he'd carried for Chen Yufei and the others while he'd sat helpless on the cold ground — and found, despite all of it, that he was grateful for exactly the strange, complicated shape the night had finally resolved into.
