# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 385 — Chen Yufei Holds

It happened again two days later, smaller this time but unmistakably the same failure mode — the line between his own feelings and the Strata's borrowed weight thinning at the edges, threatening to blur before it fully collapsed. This time Chen Yufei caught it earlier, before it reached the point of true disorientation, and made a decision she explained to him only afterward, once it was already done.

"I'm not letting go this time until it's fully stable," she said. "Not for an hour. However long it takes."

It took four.

He remembered very little of the middle of it with any clarity — the way exhaustion sometimes erased its own record even while it was happening — but he remembered the beginning, sitting together on the floor of the apartment above the noodle shop because neither of them had the patience to arrange anything more formal, her hand wrapped around his, her attention held with a focus he had rarely seen from her outside of genuine crisis. And he remembered the end, four hours later, the light outside gone from afternoon to full evening, his own sense of himself finally, fully, distinctly his own again, no borrowed grief or borrowed anxiety bleeding in at the seams.

"How did you do that," he asked, once he could speak again without the words feeling like they belonged partly to someone else. "Four hours. Without a break."

She was quiet for a moment, flexing her hand slowly, the muscles visibly stiff from having stayed in the same position far longer than was comfortable. "It's not like holding something still," she said finally. "That was my first instinct, the first time this happened — grip tight, don't let go, keep you anchored by sheer force. It doesn't work that way. If I hold too tightly, you stop being able to find your own edges at all. You just become an extension of whatever I'm holding onto instead."

"Then what is it."

"Like holding something very heavy," she said slowly, working the words out as she found them, "while being careful not to hold so tight it can't be itself. You have to give it enough support that it doesn't fall, and enough room that it's still actually the thing you're supporting, rather than something you've reshaped just by holding it. I don't know how else to describe it. It's less like gripping and more like — standing very still, and letting you lean exactly as much as you need to, no more."

He sat with that description for a long moment, turning it over the way he turned over any piece of language that had managed to capture something true and difficult in very few words.

"That's the most precise description of what an Anchor actually does that I've ever heard," he said. "Including from Meen-4. Including from the Archive's own documentation."

"I'm not trying to be precise. I'm just telling you what it felt like."

"That's usually when people are most precise," he said. "When they're not trying to be."

She managed something close to a tired laugh at that, and leaned back against the low table behind her, finally letting her shoulders drop from the rigid focus she'd held for four hours straight. "I'm exhausted," she admitted. "Not exactly the kind of exhausted sleep fixes. The kind where I think I need a day of doing absolutely nothing before I trust myself to do anything difficult again."

"Take it," he said immediately. "Whatever you need. I mean that."

"I know you do." She reached over and squeezed his hand once, briefly, before finally releasing it — the first time her hand had left his in four hours, and he felt the absence of it more than he expected to. "This is what the bond costs sometimes, Lin Mushi. Not just for you. For me too. I don't say that to make you feel guilty. I say it because I think you forget, sometimes, that holding you steady isn't free just because I choose to do it willingly."

He nodded, filing the truth of that away somewhere it wouldn't be forgotten, and reached for the notebook one last time before the evening fully closed around them both.

*Anchor Protocol has a cost on both sides of the bond. I have been treating it as something she gives without paying for it herself. That was wrong, and I need to stop making that mistake.*
