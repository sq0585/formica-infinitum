# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Seventy-Four — Lady Shen's Move — Political

While Wei Canglan began his training in the Strata, Lady Shen Baoling was making a different kind of move on the surface.

Chen Yufei's network caught it within forty-eight hours — a private approach Lady Shen had made to the Iron Meridian Court's two dissenting Council members, the same two who had voted to acknowledge the document rather than suppress it.

The proposal, as relayed through Chen Yufei's contact: a coordinated narrative in which the corrected understanding would be publicly attributed not to an unaffiliated outside practitioner, but to a joint Northern Peak–Iron Meridian research initiative — a face-saving fiction that would let both Courts claim credit for the correction rather than acknowledging that it had come from entirely outside their structures, from a twenty-two-year-old with no institutional affiliation at all.

"It's clever," Chen Yufei said, when she brought it to Lin Mushi. "It solves the problem Lady Shen identified in her own analysis — the power vacuum — by having the existing institutions simply absorb the correction and rebrand it as their own work. The information stays accurate. The institutional authority gets preserved, repackaged as the source of its own correction rather than the subject of it."

Lin Mushi considered this carefully.

"It's also dishonest," he said. "Not about the technical content — about the history. It erases Ren Wuji, the archive, two hundred years of a predecessor's documentation, and replaces it with an institutional narrative that didn't happen."

"Yes."

"Would it work? Politically?"

Chen Yufei thought about this with the specific care she brought to questions about institutional dynamics. "It might, for a while. People want stable narratives. 'The Courts corrected themselves' is a more comfortable story than 'an unaffiliated former convenience store clerk found a buried alien archive and exposed eight hundred years of institutional error.' The comfortable story tends to win, in the short term."

"And in the long term?"

"In the long term, the truth tends to surface anyway, because too many people already know the real version — Wei Canglan, Lin Wenqing, the Iron Meridian practitioners who verified it independently, everyone on the original distribution list. A fabricated origin story has too many holes in it now."

Lin Mushi thought about this.

"I don't think I need to fight this with anger," he said. "I think I need to fight it with better documentation. The same thing I did with the technical content."

"What do you mean?"

"A document that establishes the historical record clearly — that the corrected information comes from the Amber Lattice School and from Ren Wuji specifically, by name, with documentation from the archive itself confirming the source predates any Court by millions of years. Not an argument. A record."

Chen Yufei considered this.

"That would directly counter Lady Shen's narrative," she said. "It would also, more importantly, give Ren Wuji the credit he's owed. He spent four hundred years on this. He deserves to be named, not erased into an institutional rebrand."

Lin Mushi nodded. He had been thinking, increasingly, about Ren Wuji's record in the Intermediate Archive — thirty years of careful work, building transmissible frameworks for someone he'd never meet. The idea of that work being quietly absorbed into a fabricated Northern Peak–Iron Meridian joint initiative struck him as a specific kind of wrong that went beyond institutional politics.

"I want to draft this carefully," he said. "Full historical documentation, archive-sourced, naming Ren Wuji explicitly, establishing the Amber Lattice School's existence and his thirty years of study there."

"How long will it take?"

"A few days, with Meen-4's help accessing the relevant historical records." He paused. "Lady Shen's coalition needs time to consolidate before it becomes politically difficult to dislodge. I'd like to move before that happens."

Chen Yufei nodded.

"I'll help with the drafting," she said. "And I'll make sure Lin Wenqing sees it early — her credibility with the document so far makes her an important early reader."

They began that evening.
