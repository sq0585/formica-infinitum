# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ninety-Six — One More Month

December became January without ceremony.

He had been working in Chengdu for seven months when the year changed, and the year changing was something the city noticed more than he did — fireworks across the district, the smell of cooking from every direction, his phone accumulating well-wishes from contacts he hadn't spoken to in months.

He sent his mother a message. She sent back a photograph of the apartment's rooftop garden with the first signs of the new planting. He told her the geometric pattern of the pot arrangement looked good. She replied: *I moved them. They weren't getting enough sun.* He looked at the photo more carefully and revised his assessment. She was right.

He called his father in Kunming. The call lasted six minutes. His father said the weather there was mild, his business was steady, and he hoped Lin Mushi was eating properly. Lin Mushi said he was, which was more accurate than it would have been three months ago. His father said that was good. They said goodbye with the specific warmth of people who were comfortable with the distance between them.

He did not tell either of them about Lady Shen Baoling.

---

What he did in the month of January:

He met Elder Zhao in the tea house on Baiyun Street with the Chitin Circuit installed in a potted plant near the window. The meeting lasted two and a half hours. Elder Zhao asked twelve questions, each precise and each following directly from the previous answer. She did not express surprise at any point, which he interpreted as either exceptional self-control or a genuine depth of experience that had calibrated her surprise threshold very high.

At the end of the meeting she looked at the ambient readings from the circuit on his phone and was quiet for approximately four minutes.

Then she said: "The array in our building is running at thirty-five percent efficiency."

"Approximately," he said.

"Could it be improved?"

"Significantly. Not without changes to the physical installation, but the design modifications are straightforward once you understand the underlying mechanism." He paused. "I can show you the design."

She looked at him. "You would share this."

"Yes."

"Why?"

He had thought carefully about how to answer this. "Because you've been maintaining something real for thirty years that deserves to work properly. And because the Under-Web in this district has been under-supported by that array for forty years, and the support would help."

She was quiet again.

"What do you want from us?" she said. Not suspiciously. Practically.

"The same thing I told Chen Yufei. Honest information about the cultivation world. Advance notice if the Court plans any action that involves me. And—" he paused — "I would like to meet the other senior practitioners. Not officially. Informally. I have things to learn from people who have been practicing for decades."

Elder Zhao looked at him for a moment with an expression that was, he thought, the closest she was going to come to showing what she was actually thinking.

"You have things to learn," she said.

"Yes."

"At your level."

"I have a complete framework and limited application experience. Everyone I've described as significantly more advanced has significantly more application experience." He looked at her directly. "The Codex tells me what's true. Nine years of practice tells you what it feels like. These are different things."

She made a sound that was not quite a laugh. "Come to the monthly practice session," she said. "Observe. Don't demonstrate. Not yet."

He accepted this.

---

He also, in January, decoded sixty percent of the Keeper communication register.

This took three weeks of daily Strata visits, each time spending an hour in the circular room letting the older Keeper signal at him. His comprehension rose from thirty to forty-five to sixty percent not through decoding sessions at his desk but through immersion — the same way language learning accelerated when you stopped translating and started receiving.

At sixty percent he understood something he hadn't understood at forty: the older Keeper's first communication had not been a general introduction. It had been a specific statement about authority.

He went back to his notes from that first session and re-read them with sixty-percent comprehension applied.

The signal had said, approximately:

*You are the first Carrier to cross this boundary in this station's current maintenance period. The station records two prior Carriers: one approximately four hundred years ago, one approximately two hundred years before that. The station's maintenance responsibility includes facilitating Carrier access, documenting Carrier activity, and maintaining the archive integrity until direct transfer is possible.*

He sat with this for a long time.

*Facilitating Carrier access.* The thin section's compression. The library door's access criteria. The floor's recognition protocol.

*The station's maintenance responsibility.*

The Keepers were not residents of the Strata in the way he'd been thinking. They were staff. Maintenance staff, assigned to this station by whatever authority the Formican community in the deeper layers constituted, responsible for managing the interface between the surface and the archive.

They had been doing this for at least four hundred years.

They had been waiting for someone to come through.

He wrote:

> *Re-interpreted first Keeper signal at 60% comprehension. Key revision: Keepers are assigned maintenance staff, not independent residents. Responsibility: facilitate Carrier access, document activity, maintain archive until direct transfer.*
>
> *'Until direct transfer is possible' — transfer of what? To whom? The archive? From the Codex's isolated state to some wider distribution? Or something else?*
>
> *'Direct transfer' is a function I'm not yet cleared for, apparently. Or that requires something I don't yet have.*
>
> *The Strata has been waiting for four hundred years for a Carrier to come through. And now one has.*
>
> *What does it want from me specifically? That's Document 7. Document 7 is still locked.*

He looked at Document 7's lock status on the Codex interface.

The comprehension threshold was higher than anything he'd unlocked before.

He needed more time, more decoding, and — he suspected — more visits to the circular room.

He had January for this. Lady Shen was in the city but had not yet made direct contact. The directional suppression was holding. He had a month of working time, probably.

He used it.
