# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Ninety-One — The Pelagic Archive

The reference appeared in a footnote, which was, he was learning, how the Deep Archive tended to bury the things it considered most dangerous — not sealed behind obvious warnings, but tucked into the margins of records about something else entirely, waiting for someone patient enough to read all the way to the bottom of the page.

He found it three days after the Veth-Karun record, working through the Archive's broader documentation of the Formless Scale event, trying to understand what the Empire's response had been in the aftermath — how a civilization rebuilds its sense of scale after watching one of its own dissolve into something it couldn't name.

The footnote was brief.

*Complete documentation of the Formless Scale Event, including full monitoring array data and subsequent Isolationist Council deliberations, is preserved separately. See: Pelagic Archive.*

He read it twice, the phrase landing with the particular weight of a door he hadn't known existed suddenly becoming visible in a wall he'd thought was solid.

He brought it to Meen-4 that afternoon, finding her in the circular chamber, and set the reference in front of her without preamble.

"Pelagic Archive," he said. "What is it?"

*A separate Strata,* she signaled, *anchored beneath the Pacific Ocean floor. Approximately 4,700 meters below the surface, in a location the Empire selected specifically for its isolation.*

"There are other Strata."

*There are several. This one is not like the others. It was built for a single purpose — to hold the complete record of the Formless Scale Event in a location no one could reach without significant deliberate effort, and to keep it there.*

He waited, sensing more underneath the flat statement than she'd yet given him.

*The Isolationists sealed it,* Meen-4 continued. *Forty million years ago, by your calendar. Their reasoning, as recorded, was direct: the full record — every detail of what Veth-Karun's dissolution actually looked like, moment by moment, everything the summary account in the general Deep Archive deliberately excludes — was judged too dangerous to remain accessible. Not because the knowledge itself was forbidden. Because the temptation to study it too closely, to look for what could be learned rather than only what should be feared, was judged inevitable in anyone with sufficient capability and insufficient caution.*

"So they buried it instead of destroying it."

*Destruction was considered and rejected. The Formican relationship to recorded history does not permit erasure, even of the most dangerous material. Burial was the compromise — preserved, but placed somewhere that access itself would function as a filter. Only someone who had already reached significant capability, who understood the ocean floor's geology well enough to locate and reach the site, who had demonstrated the judgment necessary to seek it deliberately rather than stumble into it, would ever find their way there.*

He thought about that — an archive built not around a lock, but around distance, the sheer physical difficulty of reaching it doing the work that a door and a key might have done less effectively.

"Has anyone opened it? In forty million years?"

*Not to my knowledge. The Pelagic Archive's location is documented. Its contents are known, in general terms, to the Isolationist Council and its successors. But entering it — actually crossing the threshold and reading what's inside — has not, as far as any record I have access to indicates, occurred since the seal was placed.*

"Why not? If someone reached the capability the Isolationists thought was required —"

*Because reaching the capability is not the same as having a reason,* Meen-4 signaled, with a precision that felt, for the first time in the conversation, almost like a warning aimed directly at him. *The Pelagic Archive does not answer a question anyone has needed answered in forty million years. It answers a question about exactly how Veth-Karun's dissolution proceeded, moment by moment, detail by detail — knowledge with no practical application except satisfying the specific, dangerous curiosity of someone who wants to understand precisely how close to that edge a person can walk before they fall.*

He sat with that for a long moment, the shape of the warning settling into him fully.

"You're telling me not to go looking for it."

*I am telling you it exists, because you asked, and because I do not withhold facts from you once you have demonstrated the judgment to be trusted with them.* Her signal carried the particular steadiness she reserved for the moments that mattered most. *I am not telling you not to go looking for it. I am telling you that when you eventually do — and I believe, given who you are, that you eventually will — I want you to have thought carefully, first, about what you are actually looking for, and why.*

---

He wrote the name into his notebook that evening, alongside the coordinates Meen-4 had given him without being asked twice — a location, 4,700 meters down, marked and unvisited, sitting now in his awareness the way Veth-Karun's three untranslated symbols sat there, a door he knew the shape of and had chosen, deliberately, not yet to open.

> *Pelagic Archive. Located. Not entered. I don't yet know what I'm looking for down there, or whether the honest answer is that I'm not looking for anything — that some doors are worth knowing about specifically so you can choose, every day you don't open them, that today isn't the day.*

He closed the notebook and went to find Chen Yufei, the residential wing's quiet lamplight guiding him home through corridors that, four thousand seven hundred meters above an ocean floor he had no immediate plans to visit, felt exactly as safe and exactly as ordinary as they had the morning before he'd learned what waited beneath them.
