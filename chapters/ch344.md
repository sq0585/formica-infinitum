# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Four — The Collective's Values

The insight arrived during the seventh session, one Meen-4 hadn't originally planned but had added once she judged him ready for it — the specific, distilled question underlying everything they'd covered so far.

"If I strip away all the structural detail," he said, working through his notes aloud, "what does the Collective actually want? Not procedurally. Fundamentally."

Meen-4 was quiet for a moment, the particular stillness that meant she was choosing her answer with unusual care.

*Two things, as far as my understanding of Formican historical dealings with the Collective extends,* she signaled finally. *The stability of the Void Lattice. And novel contributions to understanding it.*

He sat with the phrase, unfamiliar and strange, the same specific quality he'd felt encountering Veth-Karun's name for the first time — a term arriving fully formed, carrying weight he didn't yet have context to fully hold.

"The Void Lattice," he repeated. "What is that?"

*A cosmological structure,* Meen-4 signaled, her explanation careful and deliberately bounded. *I do not have complete understanding of its nature myself — this is knowledge that exists at the edges of what even the Formican Empire, at the height of its comprehension, ever fully grasped. What I can tell you is that the Collective treats its stability as a matter of fundamental, civilization-spanning importance. Worlds and practices that threaten that stability are treated with extreme caution. Worlds and practices that contribute genuine understanding of it are, by contrast, treated with something closer to genuine welcome.*

"Is that connected to Formless Scale? To what happened to Veth-Karun?"

*I believe it may be,* Meen-4 signaled, something careful in the admission. *The precise mechanism is not something I fully understand, and I would rather tell you honestly that a gap exists in my knowledge than construct a confident answer I cannot actually support. What I can tell you is that an uncontrolled Formless Scale event is, as far as older Formican records suggest, considered a genuine risk to the Lattice's stability — which would explain, more completely than anything I have previously offered you, exactly why the Collective classifies and monitors worlds capable of producing one.*

---

He turned the framework over carefully, understanding, with growing clarity, that Meen-4 had just handed him something considerably more useful than an abstract cosmological curiosity.

"So the review isn't really about whether Earth is dangerous," he said slowly. "It's about whether Earth's development threatens the Lattice's stability, or contributes to understanding it."

*That is my working interpretation, yes,* Meen-4 confirmed. *I would encourage you to hold it as an interpretation, not a certainty. But if it is accurate, it reframes the entire question of preparation. The case is not simply demonstrating that this world is careful and well-governed, though that remains genuinely valuable. The stronger case demonstrates that this world's development is actively contributing something the Collective values understanding — not threatening the Lattice's stability, but adding to the body of knowledge surrounding it.*

He thought immediately of Dr. Shen Yue's research, the anomalous readings her instruments had captured, the careful physics she was patiently building a case around.

"Dr. Shen Yue's paper," he said. "It's not just evidence of scientific rigor. If the Void Lattice connects to whatever her instruments are actually measuring — "

*Then her research may represent precisely the kind of novel contribution the Collective would find genuinely valuable,* Meen-4 finished. *I do not want to overstate this connection before we have examined it more carefully. But I believe it is worth pursuing directly, with her, as part of the broader case we are building.*

---

He brought the reframing to the full group that same evening, watching Dr. Shen Yue's expression shift as the implications settled into place.

"You're saying my research might be directly relevant," she said slowly, "not just as evidence of careful science, but as an actual contribution to something the review specifically weighs."

"I don't know for certain," he said. "Meen-4's honest about the gaps in her own understanding here. But if there's any chance your work connects to something the Collective genuinely values understanding, I think it's worth exploring deliberately, alongside everything else we're already preparing."

She was quiet for a moment, the careful, rigorous part of her mind visibly already working through the implications. "I'd need to understand the Void Lattice considerably better before I could meaningfully connect it to my own data. But if Meen-4's willing to teach me what she knows, the same way she's been teaching you, I'd be very interested in exploring exactly how far that connection actually goes."

*I would be glad to teach you,* Meen-4 signaled, and Lin Mushi watched the group's strategy quietly, deliberately reorient itself around this new, sharper understanding of exactly what the eighteen-month case actually needed to prove.

He wrote about it that night, the entry carrying the specific weight of a genuine turning point.

> *The Void Lattice. Something vast and cosmological, something even Meen-4 doesn't fully understand, sitting at the center of what the Collective actually values. Not just caution about danger — genuine hunger for understanding. If Dr. Shen Yue's research touches it, even distantly, we might be building toward something considerably more valuable than careful self-defense. We might be building toward an actual contribution.*
