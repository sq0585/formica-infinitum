# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Seventy-Six — Dr. Shen Yue, First Contact

The email arrived in the inbox he used for his geological consulting cover identity, the address he'd set up years ago and checked irregularly, mostly out of habit rather than any expectation it would ever produce something genuinely significant.

He nearly missed it among the usual accumulation of newsletter subscriptions and routine correspondence, the subject line plain enough that it took a second look before the sender's name registered as something he half-remembered from a context he hadn't thought about in years.

*Dr. Shen Yue, Sichuan University Department of Physics*

He opened it.

*You are the person from the physics forum three years ago — the one who posted Qi flow visualizations and asked what the structures were. I have been working with your data since then. I have found something in the dark energy readings that I cannot explain through any current theoretical framework, and I believe, based on the structural similarity to what you posted, that you might be able to explain it.*

*I understand this is an unusual message to receive from a stranger. I would appreciate the opportunity to discuss this in person, at your convenience.*

He sat with the message for a long moment, the particular vertigo of a forgotten thread suddenly, unexpectedly reconnecting after years of dormancy.

He remembered the post — barely, the way one remembers something done late at night, half out of boredom, in the early disorienting weeks after the Codex app had first appeared on his phone. He'd photographed the Qi flow visualizations his modified perception was beginning to register, posted them to a physics forum with the kind of idle curiosity that came from not yet understanding how significant any of it actually was, and received, as far as he'd remembered, almost no meaningful response.

He'd never followed up. He hadn't had the context, then, to understand what he might have actually shared.

He pulled up the old forum thread, dormant in his browser history, and found, scrolling through the sparse reply chain, a single response he'd apparently registered and then immediately forgotten: a user with a university-affiliated username, asking careful, technical follow-up questions he'd never answered, having moved on within days to the considerably larger discoveries that had consumed every subsequent month of his attention.

Three years. She'd apparently been working with the data quietly, persistently, for three years.

He thought about the Pacific Ring network, still settling into stable operation. About Liang Jingzhi's careful thesis, deliberately incomplete at the exact boundary where honest science ran out of room to claim what she actually knew. About the slow, careful work of bringing genuinely capable, genuinely honest people into a world most of them had no framework to imagine existed.

He composed his reply the next morning, careful and direct, the same honest caution he'd brought to every similar decision across the past two years.

*I remember the post, though it's been a long time. I'd be glad to discuss what you've found. I should be upfront that the explanation, if there is one, is likely to be considerably stranger than anything in your current theoretical framework — strange enough that I'd want to discuss it in person, carefully, rather than over email.*

He sent it, and sat afterward with the particular weight of a thread he'd planted years ago without understanding what it might eventually grow into — a forum post made out of idle 4 AM curiosity, dormant for three years, now reaching back toward him at exactly the moment he'd grown sophisticated enough, and patient enough, to actually answer it properly.

He thought about how much of this strange world had arrived this way — not through any deliberate plan, but through small, half-forgotten actions taken long before he understood their weight, finally circling back to matter precisely when he'd finally grown into the capacity to meet them.

He closed his laptop and went to tell Chen Yufei. This, he suspected, was going to be significant.
