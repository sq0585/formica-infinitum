# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Forty-Five — His Father — Brief

His father called on a Tuesday.

Not a Sunday — his father did not have a calling schedule the way his mother did, he called when something prompted him to, which made the calls rarer and harder to predict. The prompt this time appeared to be a news article about Chengdu's geological survey industry that his father had read and that mentioned, in passing, the name of one of the consulting firms Lin Mushi had done contract work for.

"I saw a reference to the Fang firm," his father said.

"I've done work for them."

"Geological assessment?"

"Foundation and infrastructure work." He paused. "It suits me."

His father was quiet for a moment in the way of a man assessing how much a sentence reveals and deciding what to ask next.

"You seem to have found a direction," he said finally.

"I have."

"The research you mentioned — in the last call, you said things had developed."

"Yes."

A pause. "Can you say anything about it?"

He thought about this. Not with hesitation — with care. The question of what to tell his father was a different question from what to tell his mother. His mother operated from warmth and wanted to know he was all right. His father operated from something more lateral — a respect for capability and direction that expressed itself as interest in what the direction was.

"I've found a body of work that nobody else has had access to," he said. "Technical and scientific in nature. I'm translating it, studying it, applying it. The consulting work lets me apply parts of it practically."

"What kind of technical work?"

"Systems biology with an unusual application framework." He paused. "That's the most accurate short description."

His father was quiet again.

"You sound different," he said.

"Different how?"

"More settled," his father said. "Not in a settled-down way. In a—" He paused, looking for it. "In the way of someone who is doing what they're supposed to be doing."

He thought about this.

"Yes," he said. "That's accurate."

"Good." His father's voice had the quality of someone who had been wanting to say something simple for a long time and had found the words. "I thought, when you left the university — I thought you were making a mistake. Not a catastrophic one. But a mistake." A pause. "I was wrong."

He didn't say *you were right.* That wasn't quite what was happening. He said: "The path from there to here was not obvious. It required a lot of finding out."

"I know," his father said. "That's what finding out looks like from the outside." A pause. "Your mother says you're thin."

"She sends money for noodles."

"I know. She told me." A brief pause. "I would also like to send money for noodles."

He looked at his phone for a moment.

"You don't have to," he said.

"I know I don't have to," his father said, with the mild exasperation of someone whose point has been missed. "That's not what I asked."

"Then yes," he said. "Thank you."

They talked for another six minutes — about Kunming, about his father's business, about a cousin's job change that his father found puzzling and that Lin Mushi, with the Colony Link's emotional-field reading available even through phone signal, understood in about thirty seconds as anxiety-driven but probably not a mistake.

He did not share this analysis. It was not his to share.

After the call he sat for a moment with the specific feeling that phone calls from his father produced: the warmth of something that had always been there, expressed awkwardly and sincerely.

He wrote in the notebook:

> *Father called. He said I seem more settled — same word my mother uses. This is either a family linguistic inheritance or an accurate observation that reaches both of them through different channels.*
>
> *He apologized for thinking I was making a mistake. He was not wrong at the time. He is not wrong now. Both can be true.*
>
> *He's going to send noodle money. Two parents sending noodle money simultaneously suggests I should buy very good noodles.*

He put the pen down.

He thought about the gap — the widening gap between what he was doing and what the people who cared about him knew about it. The Colony Link field in phone calls now gave him a different kind of information about his parents than he'd had before: more specific, more textured, closer to what they were actually feeling underneath what they were saying.

He could feel more about them.

They knew less about him.

The gap was getting heavier, as he'd noted in October. He was still not sure what to do about it.

He filed it under *ongoing.*

He went to bed at a reasonable hour, which was easier to do than it used to be.
