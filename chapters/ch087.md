# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eighty-Seven — The Descent Plan

He stayed in the Strata for four hours.

The older Keeper communicated again twice after the initial signal, each time a structured vibration through the ambient Qi field — each time he understood a different percentage, the comprehension varying with how much the signal overlapped with notation sequences he'd already decoded. The first time: forty percent. The second: fifty-five.

He was learning, in the room itself, by exposure to the actual communication system.

Chen Yufei had said almost nothing during the four hours. She had stood beside him, made careful notes in her own notebook (he had lent her a spare; this was apparently a thing he was going to need to supply to people), and paid attention in the way of a person who understood that she was receiving information she didn't yet have the framework to interpret and was therefore prioritizing collection over analysis.

He respected this.

On the way out, walking back through the main hall, she said: "What did you understand?"

"Approximately forty percent of the total. More of the second and third communications than the first."

"What was the forty percent?"

He read from his notebook, walking: "The Strata is not unoccupied — there are Formican communities in the lower layers, specifically Layers 3 and 4 from my earlier schematic. The surface layer and Layer 2 are maintained by Keeper-class individuals, who function as interface managers between the deeper layers and whatever comes through from above. They have been observing the surface Qi network for approximately two hundred years since the last human practitioner they registered — not Ren Wuji, someone earlier."

"Two hundred years," she said.

"Yes. Ren Wuji's entries weren't registered by their system — he entered at a different point and apparently at a compression level that was below the Keeper threshold of notice. Or he was careful to avoid notice. I'm not sure which yet."

"And the parts you didn't understand?"

"There are thirty to forty minutes of signal I have notation clusters for but can't parse at the grammatical level yet." He closed the notebook. "I need to decode more of Document 6. The communication system is related to but distinct from the written notation — it's like the relationship between spoken Mandarin and written Classical Chinese. Same roots, different register."

She was quiet for a few steps.

"So we need to go back," she said.

"We need to go back."

"With more comprehension."

"Yes."

"And in the meantime, Lady Shen Baoling is arriving in Chengdu."

"Probably tomorrow or the day after." He came out through the threshold back into the store — the fluorescent lights doing their usual thing, the night clerk not at the counter, a sound from the stockroom suggesting the inventory was being managed. He walked past the counter and out through the front door.

The December night outside was the coldest it had been.

He stood on the pavement and thought about what he needed to do in the next forty-eight hours.

"The plan," Chen Yufei said beside him.

"Yes." He had been building it since the circular room. "Three things. First: I need to decode the relevant sections of Document 6 that will give me the communication register. That's eight to twelve hours of work, best estimate."

"Second?"

"I need to establish a documented presence in the Strata — not just access, evidence of relationship with the Keepers that predates Lady Shen's arrival. If she tries to acquire me, the Strata's authority structure is a prior claim she can't easily override."

"You're going to use the Keepers as diplomatic cover."

"I'm going to use the Strata's status as a Formican-occupied space as relevant context. The Courts operate under a framework that acknowledges Formican treaty precedents — it's one of the things the Codex mentions that the Courts have preserved without understanding it. If the Strata has occupied Formican residents and those residents have acknowledged my presence, I have a status the acquisition protocol doesn't account for."

She was quiet for a moment. "Where did you learn about Formican treaty precedents?"

"Document 5, Section 8. The Echo Courts' founding documents reference it. They preserved the acknowledgment without knowing what it meant." He looked at her. "Third: I need you to inform the Iron Meridian Court of my existence before Lady Shen can brief them. The Northern Peak Court will approach the Iron Meridian for cooperation. If the Iron Meridian already has a documented relationship with me, their answer to that approach is more complicated."

"You want me to report."

"I want you to report accurately, at a time of your choosing rather than the Northern Peak Court's, using your professional assessment rather than Elder Kuo's."

She stood with this for several seconds.

"You planned all of this tonight," she said.

"I've been thinking about it since the node died," he said. "Tonight gave me the pieces I was missing."

"The treaty precedents."

"And the Keepers' communication. Forty percent was enough."

She looked at him in the manner she'd developed — the specific quality of attention she gave him when he had done something she hadn't predicted. Then she looked away at the December street.

"I'll report in the morning," she said. "Professional assessment. Undisclosed location. Prior relationship established." She paused. "You'll owe me one."

"I'll owe you the continued honest accounting."

"That works," she said.

She walked north. He walked home.

He had forty-eight hours and three tasks and a communication system to learn.

He started with Document 6 as soon as he got through the door.
