# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Seventeen — Earth's Quarantine Status

He asked for the fuller explanation the following day, after a night spent turning the disclosure over without arriving anywhere close to enough understanding to feel settled about it.

Meen-4 met him in the Deep Archive itself, apparently having anticipated the question, a set of records already surfaced and waiting on the reading table.

"Explain it fully," he said. "Everything you know. I don't want the careful, incremental version anymore. I want the whole shape of it."

*That is fair, given what you now carry,* she signaled, and began.

*Earth's restricted status began at the moment of Veth-Karun's dissolution. The event was significant enough, and violent enough in its underlying implications, that the Collective's monitoring bodies flagged this world for classification. Not as a threat requiring immediate intervention — the dissolution itself resolved without any lasting external harm, as far as any record indicates. But as a world that had demonstrated the capacity to produce an uncontrolled Formless Scale event, and therefore warranted continued observation.*

"Forty million years of observation, for one event."

*Forty million years is not an unusual timescale, for a civilization whose institutional memory extends considerably further than your own species' entire recorded history.* She continued without pause. *The classification has held consistently since. No contact. No interference. No extraction of resources or population, as far as any Formican record documents. Simply observation, maintained through monitoring points like the Witness Node, sustained at a level of attention that has, until three years ago, remained essentially passive.*

He turned the framework over, three distinct categories settling into place in his understanding — contact, interference, extraction, each one explicitly absent from forty million years of documented history.

"You listed three things the Collective hasn't done," he said slowly. "Contact. Interference. Extraction. That's a specific list. It implies there's something else, something you haven't named, that might explain what the classification actually permits, beyond simple watching."

Meen-4 went still, the particular stillness he'd learned to recognize as her deciding, carefully, how much further to go.

*There is a fourth possibility referenced in older Formican records,* she signaled finally. *The records describe it only obliquely — restricted status maintained, in certain documented cases across the Collective's broader history, through means other than simple observation. The phrase used is enforced differently.*

"What does that mean?"

*I do not know,* she signaled, and the admission carried a weight that made the uncertainty itself feel more unsettling than a clear, if frightening, answer might have. *The Formican Empire's own historical relationship to the Collective did not require us to understand the full mechanics of how restricted status is maintained across cases we were not directly involved in. The phrase exists in the record. Its full meaning does not.*

---

He sat with the unanswered question for a long moment, understanding that pressing further would produce nothing but repetition of the same honest gap.

"So the possibilities are: nothing changes, the Collective continues passive observation the way it has for forty million years. Or: something changes, contact of some kind, now that the Witness Node has gone active. Or: something enforced differently, whatever that actually means, that neither of us fully understands."

*That is an accurate summary of what I know and do not know,* Meen-4 confirmed.

"That's not a comfortable set of possibilities."

*No,* she agreed. *I will not pretend otherwise for your comfort. I have found, across our working relationship, that you handle honest uncertainty considerably better than false reassurance, and I intend to continue treating you accordingly.*

He appreciated the honesty even as its weight settled uncomfortably into his chest, understanding, sitting with it, that he'd rather carry this clearly than have it softened into something easier and less true.

"What do we do," he asked, "with three possibilities and no way to know which one is coming?"

*We prepare for the possibility that requires the most from us,* Meen-4 signaled, *while hoping, honestly, for the possibility that requires the least. I do not believe waiting passively is wise, given what the Witness Node's reactivation suggests. But I also do not believe panic serves you, or anyone connected to you, in any useful way.*

He nodded slowly, the fear and the methodical planning arriving together, the way they usually did now, neither one canceling the other out but both, somehow, coexisting in the same steady, working attention he'd learned to bring to every threshold before this one.

"Then let's prepare," he said. "Starting with understanding the Colony Link's full planetary reach. If something is watching, I want to at least understand, as clearly as I can, exactly what's being watched."

*A reasonable place to begin,* Meen-4 signaled, and the two of them turned, together, toward the considerable work of understanding a world that had just quietly, irrevocably, become considerably larger than either of them had fully accounted for.
