# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Nineteen — The Cold Room

His rented room had been cold since March.

Not cold like drafty — cold like wrong. The temperature in the main room was acceptable. The temperature near the window was whatever the window was. But there was a spot in the northeast corner, roughly where the desk sat, where the cold was a consistent fact regardless of season, undeterred by the space heater he'd run last winter and indifferent to the weather outside.

He had asked the landlord about it in May. The landlord had suggested the corner faced north. Lin Mushi had noted that every northeast corner faced north by definition and the landlord had looked at him with the expression of a man who found this observation unhelpful, and the subject had been dropped.

He had stopped noticing the cold the way you stop noticing a sound that is always there. But now he could see what was causing it, and seeing it made not-addressing-it impossible.

---

The filament he'd mapped in his apartment ran differently from the store's network. Less organized — more natural, if that was the right word, uncorrupted by the direct flow of a major node. The threads moved gently in most of the apartment, an unremarkable ambient presence, consistent with what he'd started thinking of as the background Qi of ordinary urban space.

In the northeast corner, one of the threads was broken.

Not absent — interrupted. It ran normally through the wall from the east, hit a point approximately level with the top of the desk, and diverged: one branch went flat, running sideways along the wall, losing coherence as it spread. The other bent sharply downward and pooled at floor level, going nowhere.

He had noted this on his second day of apartment mapping and understood immediately what it meant in spatial terms: a flow that should have continued was blocked at a point. Energy that was not moving was energy doing nothing. And energy doing nothing, in this context, apparently felt cold.

He had been sleeping in the thermal signature of a broken filament for however long the filament had been broken.

---

The repair took two hours.

He hadn't planned for two hours. He had planned for an experiment: test whether the filament could be redirected using the same mechanism he'd used to direct the warmth to his wrist. Not healing this time — shaping. Moving something that was already there along a path he could specify.

The attempt was humbling in the way first attempts at anything are humbling.

He stood in the northeast corner with his hand at the interruption point — he could feel it now with his palm, the specific quality of a flow that had hit something and lost its direction — and tried to do with the filament what he'd done with his wrist: focus his attention and let the warmth follow.

For the first forty minutes, nothing happened.

He tried varying his distance. He tried varying his angle. He tried focusing on the blocked branch versus the diverted branch versus the interruption point itself. He moved the desk. He sat on the floor. He stood on a chair briefly and decided that was undignified and came back down.

The filament did not move.

At the hour mark, sitting cross-legged on the floor with his back against the wall, he stopped trying to direct it and simply watched it instead. Watched the flow up to the interruption point — smooth, consistent, unremarkable. Watched the blockage. Watched the two divergent branches losing their coherence.

He looked at where the interruption was in relation to the physical structure of the room.

The corner itself. The vertical seam where the east wall met the north wall — a physical joint, plaster and cinder block, the seam probably not quite straight given the building's age and the slight unevenness of every surface in the apartment. A seam. An interface between two surfaces.

Like the crack in the ceiling. Like the boundary at the drinks case wall.

The filament wasn't blocked. It was trying to pass through an interface that wasn't quite aligned.

He stood up and pressed both hands flat at the seam where the walls met, as high as he could reach, and paid attention not to the blockage but to the seam itself. Its shape. Its direction. The specific angle of the misalignment.

He thought about the Codex diagram — the way paths between nodes had angles that were never arbitrary, always determined by the underlying geometry. The filament's natural path had an angle. The room's seam had a different angle. He needed the filament to find the path through.

He repositioned the desk first, moving it a meter north along the wall. Then the bookshelf — angled slightly, one end pushed out from the wall in a way that looked accidental and was not. He moved them along lines he'd worked out from the notation, from the geometry of the hexagonal network, triangulating by feel until the placement felt — the only word he had was *right*, which was not precise but was accurate.

He stepped back.

The cold receded from the corner.

Not dramatically — the room didn't warm visibly. But the quality changed. The filament, when he checked, was moving through the seam now, finding the path the repositioned furniture had suggested, continuing its course toward the north wall and beyond. The broken pool at the floor was gone.

He stood in the northeast corner for a long time.

It was warm.

---

He wrote:

> *Environmental Qi manipulation, first successful attempt.*
>
> *The mechanism: not forcing the energy. Suggesting the path. Physical objects positioned along geometric lines derived from the notation created a spatial argument that the filament followed.*
>
> *The broken flow was an alignment problem. The room's seam was off-axis relative to the network's natural geometry. Furniture repositioned to bridge the angle differential.*
>
> *Result: cold spot eliminated. Filament running normally through the corner.*
>
> *What this means: I can work with the environment. Not just perceive it — adjust it. Small adjustments. Physical objects moved to geometric positions. Low intervention, local effect.*
>
> *What I should be careful about: I don't know the second-order effects of redirection. The diverted branch was going somewhere, even if it was going sideways and losing coherence. I have changed the local network's behavior. I don't know if anything downstream notices.*

He paused. Added:

> *The cold room is warm.*
>
> *I have been sitting in someone else's mistake for eight months.*
>
> *Whoever installed this building's interior walls was not working with geometric awareness. The misalignment is consistent with ordinary construction error — not deliberate, just inattentive.*
>
> *Note for later: the quality of construction in a space has Qi consequences. Something to understand further.*

---

The next afternoon, he passed his downstairs neighbor on the stairwell — a woman in her thirties who kept a row of plants on her windowsill that he could see from outside. She looked up at him and said, with some surprise:

"My plants are doing better."

"Oh?" He kept walking up the stairs.

"I don't know what I did differently." She frowned pleasantly. "The one on the left has been sad all summer. This morning it was fine."

"Good soil, maybe," he said.

She went downstairs. He went upstairs and sat at his desk in the warm corner and looked at the bookshelf, which was angled slightly wrong to the eye and exactly right to everything else, and felt something that was not quite satisfaction — satisfaction implied completion, and nothing was complete — but was in the same family.

He had fixed the room.

The plant downstairs was doing better.

He had not planned for the plant, which was either a consequence he should have anticipated or a reminder that adjustments in one place had effects in adjacent places, and that keeping track of those effects was now part of his responsibility.

He opened the observation notebook and added:

> *Note: neighbor's plant improved after filament realignment. Not a coincidence. Effect radius extends beyond the intervention point.*
>
> *I need to map more carefully before I adjust anything else.*

He underlined the last line and kept it underlined.
