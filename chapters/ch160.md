# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixty — Month Fourteen — The Decision

He gave the Northern Peak Court his answer two weeks to the day after the tea house meeting, in writing, addressed to Elder Kuo for transmission to the Council.

He had spent the two weeks talking to everyone he'd planned to talk to. Chen Yufei had been characteristically direct: *they're offering you something real, but you don't need it, and needing it is the only reason to accept something that compromises what you actually believe.* Axis-7 had been characteristically indirect, offering observation rather than advice: *the previous Carriers who integrated with surface institutions did so before understanding had been corrected. You would be the first Carrier to receive institutional recognition after correction rather than before it. The precedent does not exist yet. You would be setting it.* Elder Zhao had told him, plainly, that she would respect either choice and that the Iron Meridian's own relationship with him didn't depend on what the Northern Peak decided.

He had written and rewritten the letter four times.

The final version was shorter than any of the drafts.

---

*To the Northern Peak Court's Elder Council, by way of Elder Kuo Tianming:*

*Thank you for the meeting, and for the offer of formal recognition extended through Elder Sun and Elder Bai. I have considered it carefully, with the seriousness the offer deserves.*

*I am declining it.*

*Not because the offer was insincere — I believe it was made in good faith, and I am grateful for that. Not because I doubt the Court's willingness to engage constructively going forward — the past months have demonstrated that willingness clearly, through Lady Shen's revised approach, through Senior Practitioner Liu's honest reporting, through Elder Sun's candor at our meeting.*

*I am declining because I cannot accept recognition from institutions whose authority is based on partial and, until very recently, incorrect understanding of the system they claim to manage. This is not a judgment of the individuals within those institutions, many of whom have engaged with the corrected information honestly and at real personal cost. It is a judgment about what accepting institutional legitimacy implies, at a moment when the institution's own foundational authority is still being actively re-examined in light of new and more complete information.*

*I would rather the relationship between us be built on what is true than on what is convenient, even though true is harder and slower.*

*What I would welcome instead: continued honest exchange. Continued access for practitioners who approach the work with genuine curiosity, as Lady Shen has. Continued cooperation on correcting technique where correction is wanted. None of this requires recognition or formal status. It only requires good faith, which the Court has, in recent months, shown it is capable of.*

*I would also suggest that the Courts — Northern Peak and the other four — begin preparing to receive additional documentation. I have been continuing the translation work since the first release. There is more to share, and I believe the time to share it is approaching.*

*With respect and without rancor,*
*Lin Mushi*

---

He sent it on a Tuesday morning.

Elder Kuo's reply came within the hour: *Received. I expected this answer, though I'm not certain the Council did. I will deliver it accurately, including the parts the Council may not enjoy hearing. Thank you for the additional notice about further documentation — I'll make sure the relevant people are prepared.*

Lin Mushi sat at his desk after sending the letter and felt the particular quality of relief that came from having said a difficult true thing rather than an easy convenient one.

He wrote in his notebook:

> *Month 14. Declined the Northern Peak Court's recognition offer.*
>
> *This was the right decision. I am confident of that in a way I was not confident of much, a year ago.*
>
> *What I told them about additional documentation: this commits me to something. The translation document's second phase — the Codex-Court comparison expanded to all five Courts' founding texts in full — needs to be ready soon. Chen Yufei and I have been drafting it for weeks. It needs to be finished.*
>
> *Catastrophe or correction. We'll find out which.*

He closed the notebook.

He picked up his pen again and opened a fresh page — the draft document, the one he and Chen Yufei had been building toward for the past month.

He had work to do.
