# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Eight — What She Wants

She came back two days later, which he hadn't expected, and asked a question he'd expected even less.

"What would you actually have me build?"

They sat in the outer hall's reading alcove, the one Chen Yufei had claimed as her usual workspace, currently empty. Lady Shen had taken the chair without being offered it, the kind of small assumption he was beginning to recognize as the only remaining trace of the authority she used to wear without thinking.

"I don't have a finished answer," he said. "I've been more focused on surviving the politics than designing what comes after them."

"That's an honest limitation." She studied him. "But you have a direction. People who've thought about nothing don't say things like *something that actually lasts* the way you said it two days ago. That phrase had weight behind it."

He considered how much to give her — not out of distrust exactly, but out of the same caution he'd apply to any structure he hadn't finished testing.

"The Courts exist because cultivators needed somewhere to learn, somewhere to belong, somewhere with enough authority that disputes had a place to be settled instead of escalating," he said. "That need doesn't go away just because the foundation under it turned out to be a misunderstanding. If nothing replaces it, you don't get peace. You get five separate fights with no referee."

"You're describing an institution."

"I'm describing a need. The institution is whatever shape ends up meeting it." He paused. "Fang Ruxue's already moving toward something — a research body, independent of the Courts, that compiles and shares what's verified instead of hoarding it for advantage. That's one piece. It's not the whole structure."

Lady Shen was quiet for a moment, turning this over with the same careful precision she brought to everything.

"And Northern Peak's piece, in your vision," she said. "What would that be?"

"I don't know yet. That's not false modesty — I genuinely haven't worked it out, and I don't think it's mine to work out alone. But thirty years of institutional infrastructure doesn't have to be wasted just because the original justification for it collapsed. The maintenance protocols, the dispute resolution, the training pipelines — those things still have value independent of whether the founding mythology was accurate."

"You're saying Northern Peak could become the part of the new structure that does the unglamorous work."

"I'm saying that's one possibility. Infrastructure. Maintenance. The parts that don't make for compelling founding myths but that everything else depends on functioning." He met her eyes. "You'd have to decide if that's beneath you."

Something flickered across her face — not offense, he noted, but something closer to genuine consideration, the look of someone weighing an option she hadn't expected to find tolerable.

"I have spent thirty years," she said slowly, "being the person other people came to when something needed to actually function instead of simply being discussed. I am not certain that work was ever beneath me. I simply believed it earned more glory than it apparently did."

"It earns less glory and more trust. Eventually trust outlasts glory."

"That's a very patient philosophy for someone who's been alive for less than two years in this world."

"I've had good teachers," he said. "Most of them dead. One of them an ant."

She didn't quite laugh, but something in her posture loosened, fractionally, in a way he suspected very few people had ever seen.

"I want to participate," she said. "Not as Northern Peak's representative — they haven't authorized this conversation and wouldn't, if asked. As myself. I want to help build the replacement structure, in whatever capacity makes use of what I actually know how to do."

"Then I'll need to introduce you to Fang Ruxue properly," he said. "I suspect you two have very different instincts about almost everything. That might be exactly what this needs."

Lady Shen considered this, and something in her expression — careful, controlled, but undeniably present — suggested she'd already arrived at the same conclusion, and simply hadn't expected to find herself looking forward to it.
