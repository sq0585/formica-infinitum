# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Three — The Collective at Month Twenty

He took stock on a quiet Sunday morning, the particular ritual he'd developed over two years of needing to understand, periodically, exactly how far things had actually come rather than simply feeling the accumulated weight of effort without measuring its shape.

> *Month 20 since Ren Wuji's death. Assessment:*
>
> *The Collective: 67 formal members, across six traditions and a growing number of independents who never belonged to any Court at all. Working groups producing genuine comparative scholarship. Fang Ruxue's governance structure holding under real pressure — tested now by violence, by institutional collapse, by Lady Shen's defection, and surviving each test more or less intact.*
>
> *The Strata: approximately 200 residents, a number that would have seemed impossible at the start of this arc, when the community was barely a handful of names and an outer hall I was still learning to navigate.*
>
> *Node maintenance: functioning. Eight committed rotation members has become twenty-two, the burden finally distributed widely enough that no single person carries an unsustainable share of it. The western cluster crisis that nearly cost us everything is, for now, fully resolved.*
>
> *The Gou Ping faction: reduced to twelve practitioners, operating entirely outside any institutional structure, formally barred from every legitimate framework the Collective recognizes. Still dangerous. Considerably less dangerous than the coalition that once organized fourteen cultivators for a coordinated assault.*
>
> *Chitin Circuit nodes within the Strata: 140, the network dense enough now that I rarely miss anything happening within its coverage before it happens.*

He set the pen down and read the summary back, the particular satisfaction of seeing accumulated effort rendered into honest numbers settling slowly into something he allowed himself to simply feel for a moment, without immediately complicating it with the next worry.

Things were better. Not fixed — he understood clearly that nothing in this world ever resolved into something he could call finished, the Codex's own philosophy having taught him that lesson patiently across two years of breakthroughs that each opened onto a larger, more complicated question. But better, measurably, in numbers he could write down and trust.

Chen Yufei found him still sitting with the notebook, mid-morning light filtering through the Strata's amber ambient glow, and read the summary over his shoulder without asking.

"You do this every few months," she said. "The full accounting."

"I find it helps. It's easy to feel like nothing's actually changing when you're inside the day-to-day work of it. Numbers don't lie the way exhaustion does."

"What does exhaustion lie about?"

He considered the question seriously.

"It tells you you're not making progress, even when you are. It makes every remaining problem feel as large as the first one did, even after you've solved a dozen problems considerably harder than whatever's currently in front of you." He gestured at the notebook. "This reminds me that twenty months ago, none of this existed. Sixty-seven Collective members. Two hundred Strata residents. A maintenance rotation that doesn't depend on five people working themselves into the ground. That's not nothing, even on the days it feels like nothing."

She sat down beside him, reading through the full assessment more carefully now.

"Twelve Gou Ping loyalists left," she said. "Down from fourteen at the original assault, plus whoever joined from Iron Meridian's hard remnant."

"Down from roughly twenty to thirty at the coalition's peak, by Chen Yufei's network's last estimate. The institutional collapse and the dispute resolution ruling both took a toll. People without an institution behind them eventually have to decide whether continued violence is actually worth what it's costing them personally."

"Do you think the remaining twelve will decide that?"

He thought about Gou Ping's voice in the alley, sharp with frustrated intensity, carrying none of the calculation that might suggest reconsideration was anywhere close.

"I don't think Gou Ping will," he said honestly. "I think some of the twelve might, eventually, the way Lady Shen eventually did. But I don't think we've seen the last of this particular conflict yet."

Chen Yufei nodded, accepting the honest assessment without needing it softened.

"Then we keep building," she said, "and stay ready for whatever comes next while we do it."

"Yes," he said, closing the notebook with the particular satisfaction of a problem honestly measured rather than simply endured. "Exactly that."
