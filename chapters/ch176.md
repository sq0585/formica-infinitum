# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Seventy-Six — The Celestial Compass Response

The Celestial Compass School's formal statement, released the following morning through their official scholarly register, was more extensive than the brief acknowledgment Lin Wenqing had sent privately.

Chen Yufei printed it — an old habit she hadn't fully shed, the physical document feeling more substantial to her than a screen — and read it aloud to Lin Mushi over breakfast.

*The Celestial Compass School has, for four centuries, preserved fragments of notation believed to be symbolic representations of cosmological principle. Independent scholarly verification, conducted by Senior Scholar Lin Wenqing over a period of three weeks, has established that these fragments are not symbolic but literal — excerpts from a complete technical system whose source has now been documented as the Amber Lattice School, a Formican-human joint institution that operated for at least six centuries prior to the Echo Courts' formal founding.*

*The School formally acknowledges the accuracy of the comparative document released by the practitioner known to us as Lin Mushi, working from source material inherited from the scholar Ren Wuji. We credit Ren Wuji and the Amber Lattice School explicitly as the origin of this corrected understanding. We reject, on the basis of clear historical documentation now available, any characterization of this correction as originating from existing Court institutions, individually or jointly.*

*We recognize that this acknowledgment will require the School to revise teaching practices that have stood, in their current form, for approximately two centuries. We undertake this revision not as an admission of failure, but as the natural consequence of receiving more complete information than our founders possessed. We would like to open formal scholarly dialogue with the document's author regarding the fuller cosmological framework, toward the goal of teaching our students the complete and accurate system going forward.*

Chen Yufei set the paper down.

"This is the first formal institutional break," she said. "Not an individual practitioner verifying privately. An entire School, publicly, on the record, accepting the correction and explicitly rejecting the rebrand narrative."

Lin Mushi was quiet for a moment, processing the weight of it.

"It's also generous," he said. "They didn't have to credit Ren Wuji by name. They could have simply acknowledged the technical correction without engaging with the historical question at all. They chose to do both."

"Lin Wenqing chose it," Chen Yufei said. "I'd guess she pushed for that specifically. She's been corresponding with you for weeks. She understands what the document means to you, beyond the technical content."

He thought about this — Lin Wenqing, fifteen years of unexplained gaps in her own astronomical work, finally finding the answer and choosing, in her institution's formal public statement, to make sure the person who'd actually done the four-hundred-year work received the credit rather than letting it be absorbed into politics.

"I should write to her directly," he said. "Not about the technical material. Just — thank you."

"You should," Chen Yufei agreed.

He did, that afternoon, briefly: *Thank you for ensuring Ren Wuji was named. He spent four hundred years on work that almost no one would ever see directly. Having his school formally acknowledge that, by name, matters more than I can easily express.*

Her reply came within the hour: *He earned it. Scholarship that endures four centuries in isolation deserves more than a footnote.*

Lin Mushi sat with this for a long moment.

He thought about Chen Yufei's earlier assessment — that the Celestial Compass's break would not be the last crack in the coalition Lady Shen had been trying to build.

Over the following week, two more factions reached out quietly, indicating they would prefer arrangement to confrontation.

He had not engineered any of it directly. He had simply told the truth, carefully, with documentation, and let the truth do what true things tended to do once enough people were willing to look at them clearly.
