# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Seventy-Five — The Counter

The second document took four days to complete, working with Meen-4's archive access and Chen Yufei's careful cross-referencing.

It was different in character from the first two releases. Not a comparison of technique and notation — a historical record. The Amber Lattice School's founding, as best could be reconstructed from the Intermediate Archive's records. Its six-hundred-year practice of accepting human students alongside Formican ones. The forty-seven documented human practitioners across that span, named where names survived, described where they didn't. And, at the center of it, Ren Wuji's thirty-year tenure — his arrival, his unusual aptitude for Scale Philosophy, his specific and deliberate focus on building frameworks that could be transmitted to practitioners who would never have direct access to the School itself.

The document included a reproduction of the Archive's own notation confirming the dating — material that predated the Northern Peak Court's founding by centuries, that predated the Iron Meridian's founding by longer still, that established beyond any reasonable institutional dispute that the corrected understanding had not originated with any Court, joint or otherwise, but had been carried forward by a single scholar working in isolation across four centuries to deliver it to exactly one person, at exactly the right moment, in a convenience store at 3:17 AM.

It did not mention Lady Shen's proposal directly. It didn't need to. The historical record, laid out plainly with full documentation, made the rebrand narrative untenable on its face.

Lin Mushi reviewed it one final time before sending it to the same broad distribution as the first document — all five Courts, formal channels, no curation.

"This is going to embarrass Lady Shen specifically," Chen Yufei said, reading the final draft. "Not just counter her proposal. Embarrass her, professionally, in front of the entire cultivation world."

"I know," Lin Mushi said. "I thought about whether to soften that. I decided not to, for a specific reason: the document isn't built to embarrass her. It's built to establish the truth. If the truth happens to make her proposal look bad, that's because her proposal was built on erasing the truth. I'm not going to distort the history to protect her from the consequence of what she tried to do with it."

Chen Yufei nodded slowly.

"That's fair," she said. "I just want you to go in understanding what the reaction will be. She's not going to take this quietly."

"I don't expect her to," Lin Mushi said. "But I think there's a difference between a fair counter and a vindictive one, and I've tried to stay on the right side of that line. The document states facts. It doesn't speculate about her motives or attack her character. It just makes the rebrand impossible by establishing what actually happened."

He sent it that evening.

The response was faster than either of the previous documents' had been.

Within six hours, Lin Wenqing — who had been quietly corresponding with him for weeks now, working through the cosmological notation at her own careful pace — sent a brief message: *This is the document that should have come first. Ren Wuji deserves to be known. I will make sure my school understands this properly.*

Within twelve hours, the Celestial Compass School issued the first institutional public statement of the entire saga: formal acknowledgment that their scholar had verified the original document's claims, formal acceptance of its implications for their own foundational understanding, and — the detail that mattered most strategically — an explicit statement crediting the Amber Lattice School and Ren Wuji by name as the source, with no mention of any Court's claimed involvement.

It was, Lin Mushi understood reading it, a direct and public rejection of exactly the narrative Lady Shen had been building.

Chen Yufei's network confirmed within the day: the Iron Meridian's two dissenting Council members had quietly withdrawn from Lady Shen's proposed arrangement.

The rebrand attempt had failed before it had properly begun.

Lin Mushi felt no triumph reading the confirmation. Just the specific, quiet satisfaction of having fought with better documentation rather than better anger, and having it work.
