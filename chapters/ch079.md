# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Seventy-Nine — Old Rules, New Player

Chen Yufei messaged at 11:47 PM: *ready.*

He met her outside the 7-Eleven at midnight.

The store was lit behind them, Peng visible through the glass counting the midnight till. The street was empty in the way of streets that belong to night-shift workers and delivery drivers and people who keep unusual hours for reasons they don't explain.

He explained the entry process, standing on the pavement: the Compression required, the membrane quality, how to approach the threshold with an engaged interface rather than passive presence. She listened with the specific attention of a practitioner receiving technical instruction — focused, slightly skeptical, filing each element against her existing framework.

"You said eight channels minimum," she said.

"The membrane responds to Chitin Seed-compatible interface geometry. Eight channels give you enough structure for the membrane to recognize and yield. Below eight you'd need to push, and pushing is not how the threshold works."

She looked at the store. "And you found this how?"

"I've been inside nine times. I found it by mapping what I could perceive from outside and then testing the threshold's response to different approach conditions."

"You tested a Strata threshold by pressing on it."

"Yes."

"That's—" She stopped. "That's not how practitioners usually do it."

"I know. I didn't have anyone to tell me how practitioners usually do it."

She looked at him for a moment with an expression that was settling into something he'd classify as cautious respect — the kind that acknowledged capability while reserving judgment on sense.

They went inside.

---

He went first. He showed her the Compression by example, pressing his palm to the threshold and letting the geometry reduce, the membrane yielding. He stepped through.

She followed.

Her crossing was rougher than his — eight channels rather than his twenty-three, and an interface geometry built on the Iron Meridian framework rather than the Codex system. The membrane resisted her for a moment. He could feel, from inside the Strata, the quality of the resistance — not rejection, more like a lock encountering a key that wasn't quite the right shape.

She pressed.

The membrane yielded.

She stepped through.

---

The hall.

He watched Chen Yufei's face register the vaulted space. Thirty-two meters to the crown of the vault. The hexagonal geometry. The floor illuminating under their feet — his at the ten-second lag, hers at a fifteen-second lag, the system registering her Chitin Seed-adjacent interface and responding with slightly less certainty than it did for him.

She stood very still.

He gave her the silence she needed.

After approximately two minutes she said: "The Iron Meridian texts describe this as mythological."

"It's not."

"No." She looked at the floor, at the glow fading in the tiles where she'd walked. "The floor tracks us."

"Recognition protocol. The Strata records Chitin Seed-compatible presences."

"Why does it glow for you and barely glow for me?"

"Your interface geometry is approximately compatible but not native to the Codex system. The Strata can read enough of it to record your presence, but it reads mine more clearly." He paused. "The Iron Meridian framework was derived from the original Formican system. It's related but translated."

She was quiet.

Then she turned to look at him directly. "You said the Iron Meridian texts are sixty percent accurate."

"Approximately."

"And the forty percent that's wrong."

"Wrong about mechanism. The techniques work. The explanation for why they work is incorrect. The incorrect explanation doesn't affect the technique's function but limits what you can understand about the technique's limits and extensions."

She processed this. He watched her process it — the specific quality of someone receiving information that required restructuring things they had believed for a long time.

She said: "Show me the library."

He said: "I need to ask you something first."

"Ask."

"The Northern Peak Court has an investigator in the city. He has my name and address and two weeks until backup arrives. He's going to move toward acquisition when that backup lands." He looked at her. "The Iron Meridian Court is not the Northern Peak Court. But they have a relationship, and you have reporting obligations, and I need to understand what those obligations mean before I take you further in."

She was quiet for a long time.

"My obligation," she said carefully, "is to report unauthorized Qi sources and potential threats to the Court's interests." A pause. "I have found an unauthorized Qi source. The source appears to have access to the Amber Lattice Library, which the entire cultivation world has spent two thousand years looking for." Another pause. "My assessment of threat level: none. My assessment of strategic value: significant. My reporting obligation would be served by either reporting immediately or by gathering more information before reporting to ensure the report is accurate and complete."

Lin Mushi waited.

"I'm going to gather more information," she said. "How much more depends on what's in the library."

"That's acceptable," he said.

He walked north. She followed.

The Keeper was waiting at the library door.

Chen Yufei stopped when she saw it.

Lin Mushi kept walking.

She caught up.
