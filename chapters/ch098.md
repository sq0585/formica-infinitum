# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ninety-Eight — The Scale of It

He was in the Secondary Archive for six hours.

Chen Yufei was in there with him for the first three and a half before she messaged him that she needed to surface for the meeting with Elder Zhao she'd forgotten she had scheduled. He acknowledged this without looking up from the shelf he was reading.

She left. He kept reading.

---

The container labels were in the communication register, which he was currently parsing at sixty percent — enough to understand the subject matter, not always enough to understand the nuance. He moved through the shelves systematically, reading labels, noting subjects, building a rough index that he updated in his notebook as he went.

What the previous Carrier had documented:

*Human Cultivation History — Five Courts Origin and Development, 1200 CE to approximately 1820 CE.* A historical record, probably compiled from the cultivators the previous Carrier had encountered. This implied the previous Carrier had also built relationships with the cultivation world.

*Under-Web Survey — Northern Sichuan Basin, 1720–1810.* Ninety years of node-by-node survey. The previous Carrier had mapped the regional Under-Web with an instrument Lin Mushi could now identify from the Codex's technical documents as a second-generation Chitin Circuit array. He had built a device better than the one Lin Mushi had made on his fourth attempt. After ninety years of practice, presumably.

*Biological Integration Records — Personal Documentation.* His own cultivation records. Channels opened, Compression milestones, Colony Link development stages. Lin Mushi lingered here — this was the closest thing to a predecessor's diary he was going to find, and the first item on the list was dated 1720, which meant the previous Carrier had been at work for over ninety years.

*Formican Communication — Register Development and Translation Archive.* The previous Carrier had been working on the communication register too. Had gotten, based on the volume of material, significantly further than sixty percent.

Lin Mushi filed this under *urgent reading* and moved on.

*Node Repair Protocols — Practical Documentation.* The directed release technique, or something equivalent to it, applied to Under-Web node repair. The previous Carrier had worked out what Lin Mushi had worked out from the thermodynamics, and then had spent decades applying it. The documentation was practical: what worked, what didn't, the failure modes, the timeline for recovery.

*Incomplete Project — Strata Reclamation, Outer Layer.* A project the previous Carrier had been working on when they stopped adding to the archive. Reclaiming the outer Strata spaces from the slow geological drift that had been compressing the volume over centuries. Unfinished.

*Notes on Being a Carrier, Volume 1–12.* Twelve volumes.

He stood in front of the twelve volumes for a long time.

He did not open them.

Not because he didn't want to. Because the containers were sealed with a notation he could read clearly: *open when you have worked out the first year yourself.* He had worked out seven months. He needed five more. He would come back.

---

He surfaced at 4 AM.

The parking structure above the thin section's entry point was empty at 4 AM, which was convenient, because what he looked like when he completed the Compression reversal and re-expanded to Ratio 1 was — he'd been told by Gao — somewhat unsettling to watch.

He stood in the empty parking structure and looked at his notebook, which had fifty-three new pages in it.

He thought about the previous Carrier who had spent ninety years working in this city and whose documentation was sitting on shelves forty meters below his feet, organized and methodical and covering terrain that overlapped with his in ways he was only beginning to understand.

He thought about Ren Wuji, who had lived four hundred years and whom the Codex listed as a third Carrier in the station's record.

He thought: there is a lineage here. Not blood, not institutional. Something else. The lineage of people who had stood in a specific place and found the same thing and tried to understand it.

He had been working alone.

He was not, it turned out, working alone in the way he'd thought.

He walked home through the January night.

The city was very quiet at 4 AM, which was an hour he knew well from the night shifts. He walked past the 7-Eleven without stopping — Peng was visible through the glass at the counter, doing whatever Peng did at 4 AM — and kept walking north.

He thought: the previous Carrier documented ninety years of node surveys. I've been at this for seven months and I'm already beginning to understand what they were looking at.

He thought: I should read everything they left. In order.

He thought: the twelve volumes of *Notes on Being a Carrier* can wait five months. The node repair protocols cannot.

He went home. He ate breakfast. He slept four hours.

When he woke, he opened the observation notebook to a fresh section.

He labeled it: *Predecessor's Record — Working Notes.*

He started transferring what he'd read to a format he could actually use.

He had a lot of catching up to do — in the sense of standing on the shoulders of someone who had been here before — and very little time to do it in before Lady Shen Baoling finished her reconnaissance.
