# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-Four — Protecting the Collective

The Array's single-location limitation bothered him for exactly as long as it took him to realize the obvious solution had been sitting in front of him since the first maintenance rotation: he didn't have to be the only person who could maintain it.

He brought the proposal to four senior Collective members — Fang Ruxue, still working around her immobilized arm but insistent on participating; Lady Shen, whose Compression stability made her an obvious candidate; Chen Yufei, already intimately familiar with the technique from helping him test it; and one of the recently resigned Iron Meridian practitioners whose discipline and steady temperament had impressed him during the maintenance rotation's early weeks.

"I can teach this," he told them, the four of them gathered in the pocket dimension laboratory around a careful diagram he'd spent two days preparing. "Not the underlying Dimensional Weaving theory — that takes longer than any of us have time for right now. But the Spatial Anchor Array specifically, as a standalone technique, the way I taught Wei Canglan the corrected meditation framework without handing him every piece of theory underneath it."

"You're proposing to distribute defensive capability instead of centralizing it in yourself," Fang Ruxue said, the careful precision he'd come to expect from her settling into the proposal's shape immediately.

"Yes. If I'm the only person who can deploy this, the Collective only has one protected location at any given time — wherever I happen to be standing. That's not real protection. That's a single point of failure with my name on it."

"And if four of us can deploy it independently?"

"Then we have four protected locations, simultaneously, anywhere the Collective needs them. Working group meetings, Fang Ruxue's office, the rotation's maintenance sites, wherever the next threat happens to be aimed instead of wherever I happen to already be standing."

Lady Shen studied the diagram with the careful attention she brought to anything structural, the same instinct that had once built thirty years of institutional infrastructure now turned toward a problem considerably more personal than anything she'd managed before.

"This is good architecture," she said. "Defensible, distributable, not dependent on any single person's continued presence or survival. I'd have built Northern Peak's defenses this way, if I'd understood the principle thirty years ago instead of relying on hierarchy and concentrated authority."

"That's roughly the point," Lin Mushi said. "Concentrated authority is a liability disguised as strength. Distributed capability is slower to build and harder to centralize credit for, but it's considerably more resilient when something actually goes wrong."

---

The teaching took two weeks, careful and methodical, each of the four working through the geometry at their own pace, failing in their own specific ways before arriving at a stable version of the technique they could trust.

Fang Ruxue, working one-handed around her healing arm, mastered the boundary mechanics faster than anyone expected, her decades of careful astronomical calculation translating surprisingly well into the precise spatial reasoning the Array required.

Lady Shen's version held with a kind of severe, disciplined stability that matched her own bearing exactly — narrower in scope than Lin Mushi's, but unshakeable within whatever boundary she chose to maintain.

By the end of the second week, four independent Arrays stood ready to deploy across the Collective's various meeting sites, each maintained by someone who understood the principle well enough to adjust it under real pressure rather than simply executing a memorized pattern.

Lin Mushi stood in the pocket dimension laboratory on the final evening, watching all four of them practice simultaneous deployment — four separate boundaries, four separate locations, the Collective's vulnerability distributed into something resilient enough that no single point of failure remained.

"This is what I should have built from the beginning," he said to Chen Yufei, watching the practice session wind down. "Not me, alone, trying to protect everyone. Everyone, together, capable of protecting themselves and each other."

"You couldn't have built it from the beginning," she said. "You needed the technique to exist first, and the technique needed everything that came before it — the maze, the failure at the conference room, Fang Ruxue's injury. You built this as fast as the path actually allowed."

He considered this, the particular comfort of her steady, accurate assessment settling into something he could finally let himself accept.

"I suppose that's true," he said.

"It is," she said. "Now stop second-guessing good work and come help me check the boundary calibration on the fourth Array. Lady Shen thinks it's drifting slightly under sustained load, and I'd rather we find out now than during something that actually matters."

He nodded, and turned back to the work, the Collective's defense finally resting on more than one person's exhausted, continuous vigilance.
