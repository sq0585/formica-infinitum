# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Thirty-Five — Meeting Liang Jingzhi

Her reply came at 7:14 the next morning, brief and businesslike, the careful skepticism of someone who'd clearly received strange emails before and had learned not to extend much trust to them without good reason.

*I don't know who you are or what "information relevant to my recent paper" means specifically. If you have a genuine scientific concern with my methodology, I'm happy to discuss it professionally. If this is something else, I'd appreciate you being direct about what it actually is.*

He appreciated the directness enough that he matched it.

*Not a methodology concern — your methodology is sound, which is part of the problem. I think you've detected something real that isn't geological in the way you're currently interpreting it, and I think you deserve an honest conversation about what it actually is before anyone else reaches you with a less honest one. I understand this sounds strange. I'm willing to meet wherever's comfortable for you, in public, on your schedule.*

She agreed to a coffee shop near campus, two days out, a public venue of her own choosing — exactly the kind of careful caution he'd have advised her to take if their positions had been reversed.

---

She was already seated when he arrived, ten minutes early by his own habit, a printed copy of her paper on the table in front of her with several new annotations in red pen that hadn't been there in the published version.

"You're not what I expected from the email," she said, by way of greeting, studying him with the same direct, unhurried attention he remembered from the conference footage. "I'd half-prepared myself for someone trying to sell me something."

"I'm not selling anything." He sat down across from her. "I want to show you one thing. Then you can decide whether you want to hear the rest, or whether you'd rather end the conversation here."

She nodded, cautious but engaged, and he set his phone on the table between them — the Circuit amplifier's readout, a live feed of Qi density flowing beneath the coffee shop's floor, rendered as a shifting field of color and intensity that bore no resemblance to anything a standard piece of geological equipment would produce.

She stared at it for a long moment, her academic composure visibly working to process something that didn't fit any framework she'd brought to the table.

"What am I looking at?" she said finally.

"A real-time reading of the same phenomenon your paper detected. Not geological density variation. Something else entirely, that happens to leave a signature dense enough for conventional seismic equipment to pick up if it's drifting outside its normal parameters."

She leaned closer, studying the shifting field with the focused intensity he'd seen her bring to the skeptical professor's question in the recorded footage, except now there was no skepticism in it, only a careful, hungry attention.

"This is what I've been detecting," she said slowly. "The density variations. This is the actual source."

"Yes."

"And it's not geological."

"No."

She sat back, the printed paper with its red-pen annotations forgotten between them, and looked at him directly, the question underneath her expression arriving before she fully voiced it.

"Then what is it?"

"That's what I need to show you," he said. "Not tell you. Show you, somewhere I can actually demonstrate it instead of describing it across a coffee shop table and asking you to take my word for it."

She was quiet for a long moment, weighing something he could see playing out behind her careful expression — caution against curiosity, the academic instinct to verify against the equally strong instinct that whatever he was offering might be the most important thing she ever had the chance to see.

"There's a meeting I'm supposed to have tomorrow," she said slowly. "A research consultancy, interested in funding my continued work. I was going to take it."

"I know about that meeting," he said carefully. "I think you should be cautious about who's actually behind it, and what they're actually interested in funding you to find."

She studied him for a long moment, something shifting in her expression as the pieces — his unexpected email, the readout on the table, the timing of a sudden funding offer that had appeared right as her paper started gaining attention — settled into a pattern she clearly didn't like the shape of.

"I'm cancelling that meeting," she said, with the decisive clarity of someone who trusted her own judgment once she'd gathered enough information to use it. "Show me what you actually have."
