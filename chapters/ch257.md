# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-Seven — Wei Canglan Notices

Wei Canglan noticed within about thirty seconds, which was, Lin Mushi reflected afterward, considerably faster than either of them had managed to notice it about themselves over the preceding six months.

He'd come to the outer hall to report on the maintenance rotation's latest cycle, found Lin Mushi and Chen Yufei sitting together over a shared pot of tea with a particular ease neither of them had quite carried before, and stopped mid-sentence, his eyes moving between the two of them with the focused, assessing attention he usually reserved for unfamiliar cultivation technique.

He didn't ask. He didn't need to.

"Finally," he said, delivered entirely without expression, the single word carrying more weight than a longer reaction would have managed.

Then he sat down, pulled out his own notebook, and went back to reviewing the rotation schedule he'd come to discuss, as though he hadn't just acknowledged the most significant shift in either of their lives in nearly two years.

Lin Mushi waited for more — a question, a comment, the kind of teasing he might have expected from someone less carefully composed than Wei Canglan usually was.

None came.

"That's it?" Chen Yufei asked, after a long moment of silence that Wei Canglan seemed entirely unbothered by.

"What more is there to say?" Wei Canglan didn't look up from the schedule. "You've been circling each other since before I lost my family's apartment. I've had eighteen months to watch it happen. The only surprising part is that it took this long."

"You could have said something."

"It wasn't mine to say something about." He made a small notation on the schedule, the particular efficiency he brought to administrative work unbothered by the conversation happening around it. "Some things resolve on their own timeline. I've learned not to rush other people's timelines, given how poorly it generally goes when people rushed mine."

Lin Mushi understood the reference immediately — the careful, patient teaching he'd insisted on, the framework Wei Canglan had eventually built for himself rather than simply copying. The same principle, applied now to an entirely different kind of patience.

"That's a generous interpretation of restraint," Chen Yufei said, something amused settling into her voice.

"I contain multitudes," Wei Canglan said, still not looking up. "Now, are we discussing the rotation schedule, or are we going to spend the rest of this meeting on the two of you finally catching up to where everyone else already was?"

"The schedule," Lin Mushi said, suppressing something that might, in a less composed moment, have become an actual laugh.

"Good." Wei Canglan finally looked up, his expression as carefully neutral as it had been from the start, though something underneath it had shifted — a quiet, genuine warmth he rarely let surface this plainly. "I'm glad. Both of you deserve something uncomplicated in the middle of everything else this world keeps handing you."

He returned to the schedule before either of them could respond to that, the moment closing as economically as it had opened, and Lin Mushi found, watching him work, that Wei Canglan's brief, understated acknowledgment had somehow landed with more weight than any longer reaction could have managed.

Some things, he thought, were better said in four words and a return to ordinary work than in any amount of careful elaboration.

He picked up his own copy of the rotation schedule, Chen Yufei settling beside him with her tea still warm, and the three of them worked through the maintenance details together, the new, quiet shift between two of them folded seamlessly into the ordinary rhythm of a life that, against every reasonable expectation, had somehow found room for both impossible danger and uncomplicated happiness at the same time.
