# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 479 — The Anchor Council

They gathered in the pocket dimension laboratory two days later, all five of them together for the first time since the volunteer call had gone out, and Lin Mushi understood, watching them settle into their familiar places around the table, that this particular conversation deserved every bit of the formality he'd given it.

"Liang Jingzhi has asked to propose something," he said, once everyone had tea in front of them, the ordinary ritual doing its usual work of steadying a room before something difficult entered it. "I want her to explain it herself, and I want all of you to ask every hard question it deserves before anyone says a word about what they think she should do."

Liang Jingzhi laid it out plainly, the same way she'd laid it out to him privately — the diffuse network's limitation, the need for a single, deep, permanent point of contact at the zone's actual center, her own willingness to be that point.

Meen-4 spoke first, her stillness carrying the particular formal weight she reserved for questions she considered genuinely consequential. "The mechanism itself is theoretically sound," she said. "A Colony Link signature can be extended permanently, not merely sustained temporarily — Formican practice has precedent for long-duration anchoring, though never at this distance, and never into a dissolution zone specifically. I would not call the technique unprecedented in its mechanics. I would call the application unprecedented in its target."

"What does permanent actually mean, mechanically," Dr. Shen Yue asked, already reaching for a notepad. "I want to understand the full physiological and coherence picture before anyone in this room says yes to anything."

"It means," Meen-4 said, "that a portion of Liang Jingzhi's own signature — not her whole self, not the majority of her, but a genuine, lasting fraction — would exist permanently within the dissolution zone's structure, distinct from the temporary extensions the three hundred and twenty-three are each holding. She would not be diminished by this the way a resource is diminished by spending it. She would be, in the most literal sense available to me, simultaneously present in two places, indefinitely, for as long as the arrangement holds."

"Reversible?" Wei Canglan asked, his voice carrying the particular careful edge Lin Mushi had learned to recognize as his most serious register, the one that showed up only when something actually frightened him.

"Theoretically," Meen-4 said. "I want to be honest rather than reassuring. Theoretically reversible, based on the underlying mechanics. Untested at this specific duration and depth. I cannot promise you reversal would be simple, or complete, or free of cost, if it were ever attempted."

Wei Canglan absorbed that, and turned to Liang Jingzhi directly. "Does she understand what permanently means? Not the mechanism — the actual, lived weight of it. Being two places at once for the rest of her life, whether or not she ever wants to stop."

"I understand it as well as anyone can understand something no one has ever actually done," Liang Jingzhi said, meeting his gaze evenly. "Which is to say: not completely. I understand it as well as informed consent has ever meant anything in this entire project, Wei Canglan. I don't think either of us gets more certainty than that, this far out past the edge of anything precedented."

Chen Yufei had been quiet through most of the technical exchange, watching Liang Jingzhi with the particular attentive stillness she brought to decisions that mattered most. "This is her choice to make," she said finally. "I want to say that plainly, before we spend another hour interrogating the mechanism. We can ask every hard question in the world, and we should, but at the end of all of it, this is Liang Jingzhi's life and Liang Jingzhi's decision, and I don't think any of us actually gets a vote on the outcome. Only on whether she has everything she needs to decide it properly."

"I agree," Lin Mushi said quietly. "I said as much when she first brought it to me. It has to be entirely hers. All of it."

Dr. Shen Yue looked up from her notes, something conflicted moving across her expression. "I want to understand the mechanism completely before she commits to anything," she said. "That's not me withholding support. That's me making sure whatever she decides, she decides with the clearest possible picture of what it actually costs."

"That's fair," Liang Jingzhi said. "Take whatever time you need to build that picture. I'm not deciding tonight. I told Lin Mushi that from the beginning — I want this decided properly, not quickly."

The council continued another two hours, technical questions folding into practical ones, practical questions folding, eventually, into the quieter, harder territory of what any of them would actually feel, watching someone they loved carry something this permanent. By the time they finished, no decision had been reached, and Lin Mushi understood that this was exactly as it should be — the weight properly distributed across everyone who needed to help carry it, and the actual choice left, entirely, patiently, in the one set of hands it had always belonged to.
