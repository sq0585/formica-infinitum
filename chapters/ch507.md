# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 507 — Scale Seven — Formless

The seventh scale did not arrive as a further expansion.

He understood this the moment it happened, and understood, too, why the Archive had never described it as a threshold to be crossed the way every prior scale had been a threshold — a gate, a gap, a specific gathering of comprehension sufficient to open the next door. Scale Seven was not a further door. It was the simple, complete fact of every door being open at once, sub-Planck to universal, all of it foreground, none of it background, none of it requiring the sequential attention that had defined every act of perception he had ever performed before this morning.

He held it.

He did not dissolve, and he understood, in the specific quiet clarity that followed the twelve seconds he had already survived, exactly why. He was not holding a technique anymore. He was holding five people and one moment, the same five people and the same one moment he had been holding since the very first scale, since long before any of this had a name — and it turned out, arriving at the furthest edge of what a person could perceive, that the anchor had never needed to grow larger to match the scale of what it was anchoring. It had only ever needed to remain exactly, stubbornly, itself.

3:17 in the morning. A convenience store on a street he could still walk without thinking. A dying man's hand closing around his wrist with the specific, deliberate weight of someone choosing, in his final seconds, exactly who to trust with what came next.

He was everywhere. He was, unmistakably, still the person who had once worked the night shift because the pay was slightly better and the silence suited him.

*Observation 507,* he wrote, and found, writing it, that his hand had never once stopped being entirely his own hand, despite everything else it was currently attached to. *Formless Scale achieved. I will, I suspect, spend a considerable amount of time in the coming months trying to describe what this actually feels like, and I expect every attempt will fall somewhat short, the way every attempt to describe Compression fell short five years ago, until I simply lived inside it long enough for the words to stop mattering as much as the experience. For now, the closest honest description I have is this: I am everywhere, and I am also still the person who eats breakfast and calls his mother on Sundays, and both of those things are, at this exact moment, equally, simultaneously true.*

He felt the Anchor Protocol settle around him — not straining now, the way it had at the sixth scale's edge, but holding, steady, the specific relief of a structure that has been tested at its limit and found, on the other side of the test, that it still stands.

*You're all right,* Chen Yufei sent, and it was not a question.

*I'm all right.*

*You feel different,* Wei Canglan sent, more carefully than usual, the precision in it edged with something he rarely let show. *Larger. And also, somehow, exactly the same.*

*That's the whole technique,* Lin Mushi sent back. *I think that's the whole point of the technique.*

Meen-4's signal arrived last, and carried, for the first time in five years of knowing her, something he had genuinely never felt from her before — not surprise exactly, Formicans did not experience surprise the way humans did, she had told him once, but its nearest cousin: the sensation, ancient and patient and forty million years accumulated, of witnessing something she had not been certain was possible before she watched it happen in front of her.

*The Anchor Protocol held completely,* she sent. *I have no further data than that observation to offer you, because I have no prior data to compare it against. This has not happened before. Not like this.*

He sat with that a while — with the enormity of everywhere, and the specific, irreplaceable weight of five people and one moment, both of them true at once, neither one diminishing the other.

He was Formless.

He was also, unmistakably, still Lin Mushi — a fact so quietly, thoroughly complete that it took him a long moment to notice how extraordinary a thing it actually was, buried, as it had been buried at every threshold before this one, beneath the sheer scale of everything surrounding it.

He picked up the cold tea Chen Yufei had poured hours ago, and drank it anyway, because it was, at this precise instant, both entirely unnecessary and exactly the kind of thing he intended to keep doing regardless.
