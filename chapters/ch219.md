# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Nineteen — Wei Canglan Asks the Right Question

He gathered everyone in the outer hall that same night — Chen Yufei, Wei Canglan, Su Lan, the two independent cultivators, even Meen-4, summoned through a request relayed by one of the inner-section archivists she occasionally permitted to carry messages.

He told them plainly, without softening it, the same way he'd told Wei Canglan an hour earlier.

"The Colony Link has expanded beyond what I can control. I can currently sense the emotional state of everyone in this hall, continuously, without needing to reach for it. I've been able to do this for eleven days. I should have told you on day one, and I didn't, and that was a mistake I take full responsibility for."

The silence that followed had a texture to it he could feel without needing the Colony Link to confirm — discomfort, mostly, layered over something closer to betrayal in at least one corner of the room he didn't try to identify by name.

"How long has it been growing?" Su Lan asked, her quiet voice carrying further than its volume suggested it should.

"Months, in small increments. The expansion accelerated about two weeks ago, around the time I started the maintenance rotation. I don't know if those events are connected. I don't fully understand the growth mechanism yet."

"Can you stop it?" one of the independents asked.

"I don't know. I've tried the techniques I'd use to narrow Compression, applied in reverse. None of them worked." He met their eyes, one by one, refusing the easier path of addressing the room as an abstraction rather than the specific people in it. "I'm not asking anyone to be comfortable with this. I'm telling you because you deserve to know, and because I need to find a way to make it consensual instead of something happening to you without your knowledge."

Meen-4, who had been still since arriving, finally moved — a small, characteristic shift of her forelimbs that Lin Mushi had come to associate with the beginning of a communication.

*Obviously,* she signaled, the register carrying none of the gravity the room's tension might have led him to expect. *The Seed was always going to do this. It is in the documentation.*

He blinked. "It's documented? This specific expansion?"

*Document 9, supplementary appendix. Colony Link range scales with community size and proximity duration once initial threshold is exceeded. This is described as expected behavior, not malfunction.* A pause, the closest thing to amusement he'd learned to read in her signals. *You did not read the appendix.*

"I didn't know the appendix existed."

*It exists. You have access. You simply did not look.* She turned her attention, briefly, toward the rest of the room, the closest she'd come in months to addressing anyone other than Lin Mushi directly. *This expansion is not a defect to be corrected. It is a feature requiring management protocols, which the documentation also describes. You should have read further before alarming your community.*

Wei Canglan, who had been quiet since the gathering began, let out something that wasn't quite a laugh.

"So it's a known feature," he said, "with a known fix, that you simply hadn't gotten around to reading."

"That appears to be accurate, yes," Lin Mushi said, and found, despite everything, that there was something almost steadying about the absurdity of it.

"That's better than I expected," Wei Canglan said. "And worse, in a different way. You scared everyone in this room because you didn't finish your reading assignment."

"I deserved that."

"You did." Wei Canglan's expression hadn't fully softened, the underlying frustration still present, but something in his posture eased fractionally. "Read the appendix. Tonight. Then tell us what the management protocol actually involves, so we can decide for ourselves whether we're comfortable with it instead of just being told it's handled."

"I will."

"And Lin Mushi." Wei Canglan held his gaze. "Next time something like this starts happening, tell people on day one. Not day eleven. I don't care how unfinished the solution is. We get to know what we're agreeing to live inside of."

Lin Mushi nodded, the weight of it settling somewhere he suspected would stay settled for longer than this single conversation.

"Day one," he said. "I understand."

Around the room, the discomfort hadn't fully dissolved, but something had shifted underneath it — not forgiveness exactly, but the beginning of a different kind of trust, the kind built less on never making mistakes and more on what happened honestly in the space right after one.
