# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Three — The App With No Icon

He woke at 6:23 AM, which was barely sleeping at all.

For a moment the ceiling was just a ceiling. Then he remembered.

He sat up, pulled last night's takeout container from the nightstand — rice, cold, the bottom half still decent — and ate three bites before reaching for the phone. This was, he decided, an acceptable level of self-care before engaging with the impossible.

The hexagonal icon waited on the home screen. In the early gray light coming through the curtains, it looked even fainter than before, as if it were considering whether to be there at all.

He opened the app.

---

The diagram bloomed into view.

He had seen it once before, in the small hours at the store, and thought he understood its general shape. He had been wrong in the way you are wrong about a city before you've lived in it. Amber-gold lines nested inside each other at every scale, hexagonal geometries repeating inward and outward without apparent limit, moving too slowly to watch but differently each time you looked away and looked back.

He held the phone closer.

There were regularities. He could see them now that sleep had cleared the noise from his eyes: clusters that resembled lattices, thickened lines like boundaries, several nodes that sat heavier than others, as if they were points of interest rather than mere intersections.

He read Ren Wuji's message again.

*The world you live in is a surface. Beneath it is everything that has ever mattered.*

He set the phone down and looked out the window at the Chengdu morning. A delivery scooter passed. Someone was already frying something on the street below; the smell reached him even through the glass.

He thought: four hundred years is not a lifespan. It is a project.

He thought: the diagram is a map of something he spent four hundred years making.

He picked up his new notebook, uncapped his pen, and wrote at the top of the first page:

> *Working name: the Codex. (Ren Wuji did not give it a name in the message. I am giving it one for reference purposes.)*
>
> *The Codex is not software. It moved differently last night and it moves differently now. No update was downloaded. It is responding to something — possibly me, possibly time, possibly conditions I haven't identified yet.*
>
> *Primary question: what is the diagram a diagram of?*
>
> *Secondary question: what does 'surface' mean exactly. A metaphor for ordinary reality? A technical term? Both?*

He paused.

> *Note: 'the nearest mind awake enough.' He chose me in four minutes, at 3 AM, in a convenience store. Either his criteria were very broad or they were very specific. I don't know which possibility is more unsettling.*

He put the pen down and looked at the app again.

One of the heavier nodes was at the center of the diagram. Not obviously central by position — the geometry didn't have a clean middle — but central in weight, as if the surrounding lines all oriented slightly toward it, the way iron filings orient toward a magnet.

He pressed it.

The phone warmed under his fingertip. Not across the whole device the way it had at 3:17 AM, but precisely under the pad of his index finger. The warmth was intimate, almost like a pulse.

A thin line brightened, extending from the node toward the diagram's interior. Then a second. The geometry adjusted — so subtly that if he hadn't been watching, he might have blamed his own eyes.

His vision shimmered. Half a second, like heat above summer asphalt.

Lin Mushi blinked hard.

The room was the room.

Except — there was depth under the desk.

Not shadow. Not metaphor. A literal sense that the space beneath the cheap particleboard wasn't merely air down to the floor, but an opening into a larger volume, as if the apartment had been built over a hollow that extended far past the building's foundation.

He stood up fast enough that the chair scraped the floor.

The depth sensation surged and then stabilized. It didn't feel like falling. It felt like standing at an edge and discovering that "edge" was something his brain had been inventing.

He put one hand on the desk. Laminate. Dust. Solid.

Underneath, the world felt like it had a second layer.

He knelt and looked under the desk.

---

Floor. Plain floor. A dead beetle on its back near the wall.

The depth sensation remained regardless.

He stood. Picked up the phone. The brightened lines were still bright, the node still selected. He did not touch the diagram again.

This is calibration, he thought.

Not an app that gives you features. An interface that changes you so you can perceive what it's showing you.

His throat went dry.

He set the phone down like something delicate and sat with his hands flat on the desk and breathed until the edges of his vision steadied. Then he laughed once — a short sound with no humor in it, the body venting pressure through the only valve available.

"Okay," he said to the empty room. "Okay."

He picked up the pen.

> *6:47 AM. Selected central node (heavy — orientation-dominant). Warmth localized to fingertip. Diagram changed: two lines brightened.*
>
> *Immediate effect: persistent perception of "depth" beneath objects and floor. Visual inspection: nothing present. Sensation remains.*
>
> *Hypothesis: the Codex is training — or forcing — my perception to recognize a hidden structure. Like learning to see an optical illusion, except the illusion is default reality.*
>
> *Fear response present but manageable. Curiosity also present. Curiosity currently winning.*

He paused. Added:

> *Rule: do not select more nodes today. One variable at a time.*

He capped the pen. Drank the rest of the cold tea. Outside, Chengdu carried on with the calm indifference of a city that had never once cared what a single convenience store clerk could perceive beneath his furniture.

The depth sensation did not go away.

By noon, he had stopped expecting it to.
