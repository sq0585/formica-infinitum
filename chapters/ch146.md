# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Forty-Six — The Expulsion

The thirty days elapsed on a Thursday.

Chen Yufei did not report.

The Iron Meridian Court's formal expulsion notification arrived that evening — a different senior practitioner this time, someone she didn't recognize, who delivered it at 6 PM with considerably less of Elder Zhou's apology and considerably more of the Court's standard administrative procedure.

She called Lin Mushi at 6:24 PM.

"It's done," she said.

"How are you?"

A pause. Then: "Surprised."

"By what?"

"By how little it changes." Her voice had the quality of someone reading an instrument they expected to show one thing and finding it shows another. "I thought I'd feel — cut loose. Adrift. I've been Iron Meridian for nine years. I thought the expulsion would feel like the category removing itself." Another pause. "It doesn't. The practice is still there. The channels are still there. What I know is still what I know."

"Yes," he said.

"The institutional recognition was always — a layer. Not the thing itself." A pause. "I knew this. I wouldn't have made the choice I made if I didn't know it. But knowing something and feeling it are different."

He thought about the first time he'd looked at the depth beneath the convenience store floor, the year he'd spent deciding whether his perceptions were data or symptoms, the specific quality of understanding something intellectually before understanding it through experience. He knew this pattern.

"What are you feeling?" he said.

"Lighter than I expected," she said. "And — there's something else." A pause. "The Qi-seal. They threatened it for non-compliance. I expected to feel — concerned about that. About whether it was still possible, whether they'd pursue it." She was quiet for a moment. "I'm not, particularly. The seal requires physical proximity and a capability advantage. I'm not planning to stand near anyone with that combination of features."

He thought about the fire meridian correction. The forty percent efficiency gain. The three months of practicing a complete framework rather than a suppressed one.

"The capability advantage," he said carefully.

A pause. "Yes," she said. "I've been thinking about that too. The practitioners who would be capable of sealing me — I'm not sure the gap is what they think it is anymore." She said this not with satisfaction but with the flat accuracy of someone reporting what their instruments show. "I'll need to be careful about demonstrating it."

"Yes."

"But it's there."

"Yes."

Another pause, longer.

"I'm going to go to the archive tonight," she said. "If that's all right."

"The archive is not mine to grant access to," he said, which was the accurate answer and also the one that meant *yes, of course.*

She laughed — the genuine, brief one.

"I'll see you there," she said.

He was already gathering his things.

---

They worked in the archive for four hours that evening. She read Formican agricultural records with the same absorbed attention she'd shown since her first entry. He worked on the Dimensional Lock navigation technique, practicing on the secondary locks Axis-7 had recommended.

At the two-hour mark, without looking up from her reading, she said: "Lin Mushi."

"Yes?"

"Thank you for not saying anything."

"When?"

"Just now. When I said the capability gap isn't what they think it is." A pause. "You didn't tell me I was right. You didn't say anything reassuring. You just — said yes and moved on."

He thought about this. "What would have been wrong with saying something reassuring?"

"It would have made it about whether I was worried or not worried. It wasn't about that." She looked up. "It was just true. You received it as true and moved on. That's — that's exactly right."

He looked at her.

She went back to her reading.

He went back to the lock.

They worked.

Outside, somewhere above them, the Iron Meridian Court's administrative record now contained a formal expulsion entry. The Qi-seal threat was logged against a practitioner who no longer had reason to stand near any of the Court's senior members.

Below them, the Formican community continued its work in the lower layers, aware of everything as always and continuing on their own schedule.

Between those two facts was the library, and two people at work, and a particular quality of silence that neither of them named.

Lin Mushi noted it in his notebook, very briefly, before going back to the lock.

*This is what expulsion from the right institution looks like. She expected worse. She got better.*

*The obligation: maintain what she traded toward. Ongoing.*
