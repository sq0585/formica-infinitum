# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 404 — The Collective's Terms

The formal terms arrived a week after the result, through the Witness Node, in notation dense enough that Meen-4 spent the better part of a morning walking him through every clause before he trusted his own reading of it.

"Monitored Development Zone status is not unconditional freedom," she reminded him, before they began. "I want that understood clearly before you read this. It is a significant improvement over Quarantine status. It is not the same as no longer being watched at all."

The document laid out three primary conditions, each stated with the same load-bearing precision that had characterized every Collective communication since the Surveyor first arrived.

*Quarterly reporting, through the Tibetan Witness Node, documenting all significant developments within Zone QZ-7 relevant to dimensional activity, Veritonal capability, and population-scale changes to the Strata civilization.*

"That's manageable," Lin Mushi said, reading it over. "We're already documenting most of that internally."

"Yes. The reporting requirement is not intended to burden you. It is intended to maintain the transparency that helped win this review, on an ongoing basis, so the Collective is never again in a position of assessing you from incomplete information."

The second condition took longer to parse, its implications heavier than its wording initially suggested.

*Notification, in advance where feasible, before any dimensional activity conducted above Planetary Architect scale.*

"That's the one that concerns me," Meen-4 said, before he could ask. "Not because it is unreasonable — it is, in fact, a remarkably light restriction given what it could have been. But it means any future scale advancement you achieve is no longer a private milestone. It becomes, formally, something the Collective expects to be told about before or as it happens."

He thought about that, turning over the shape of a future in which every threshold he crossed carried an obligation attached to it — not secrecy exactly, he had already chosen against that path, but a specific, ongoing accountability to something vast and watching, indefinitely.

"I can live with that," he said finally. "It's not so different from what I've already been doing. I just need to get used to doing it formally instead of on my own timeline."

The third condition, when he reached it, made him pause longer than the other two combined.

*Formal prohibition on Pelagic Archive access without explicit Collective pre-approval, pending further review of the Archive's classified contents and their relevance to Zone QZ-7's development trajectory.*

"The Archive," he said slowly. "I haven't even requested access yet."

"No. But they anticipated that you would, eventually, and have pre-emptively gated it rather than leaving it open for you to attempt without their knowledge. This is, in its own way, a compliment — an acknowledgment that they consider you likely to eventually seek exactly the kind of information the Archive holds." Meen-4's antennae dipped slightly. "It is also, I suspect, connected to your Veth-Karun analysis. The Archive is widely believed, within Collective records I have limited access to myself, to hold the most complete surviving documentation of the dissolution anywhere in this or any adjacent zone. If your analysis is as significant as this review's outcome suggests, they will want to control, carefully, how and when you're permitted to deepen it."

He sat with that a while, the shape of the restriction settling into something he could accept without fully liking. Access denied for now, not forever — a door left deliberately, formally closed, rather than simply unavailable.

"I'll submit a request anyway," he said finally. "Formally. Let them see the reasoning laid out honestly, the same way I laid out everything else in the Opening Submission. If they say no, at least it's a documented no, not an assumption I never tested."

"That is a reasonable approach," Meen-4 agreed. "I would advise patience with whatever timeline they attach to the decision. This review has already moved faster than any precedent I hold. I would not expect every subsequent Collective process to match that pace."

He nodded, already composing the request in his mind — the same careful, evidence-based reasoning that had carried him through the Opening Submission and the Veth-Karun presentation, applied now to a door he suspected would take considerably longer to open than anything that had come before it.

"Then let's draft it," he said. "Carefully. If this is going to take a while, I'd rather start the clock now than lose more time hesitating over the wording."
