# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 414 — The Archive — Stellar Scale Section

He found it the way he found most things in the Deep Archive now — not by searching directly, but by following a thread of curiosity that led him somewhere he hadn't specifically intended to go.

He had been reviewing the Planetary Architect documentation again, partly out of habit, partly because Meen-4 had suggested, gently, that revisiting foundational material sometimes revealed details a first pass had missed. Buried in a cross-reference he'd skimmed past a dozen times before, a designation caught his attention that he didn't immediately recognize.

"What's this," he asked Meen-4, pointing to the notation. "Stellar Scale curriculum. I don't think I've seen this section before."

She studied it for a long moment, something careful entering her posture. "You haven't," she confirmed. "It has been present in the Archive's structure the entire time, but access has always required demonstrated mastery at Planetary Architect scale first — a gating condition, not a deliberate concealment. You simply hadn't reached the point where the Archive considered you ready to see it."

"And now I have."

"Apparently." She opened the section carefully, and Lin Mushi felt the particular vertigo he'd come to associate with the Archive's largest revelations — the sense of a door opening onto something considerably larger than what had come before it.

The curriculum unfolded across notation denser than anything he'd encountered even in the most technical sections of Planetary Architect training — not simply an extension of skills he already possessed, but something that felt, reading through its early structure, like an entirely different category of perception being described. Where Planetary Architect scale had taught him to hold a world's worth of Circuit nodes and Colony Link connections within a single coherent awareness, this material spoke of something vaster still — perception extending past the boundaries of a single world entirely, into the space between worlds, toward the specific, overwhelming complexity of a star.

He worked through the early sections slowly, Meen-4 translating where his fluency still faltered, and found himself, within the first hour, encountering a prerequisite that stopped his progress entirely.

*Comprehensive direct observation of stellar Qi field behavior is required before Phase Two techniques may be safely attempted. This observation cannot be substituted with indirect measurement or theoretical modeling. Direct perceptual contact with a stellar environment is necessary.*

"I don't have that," he said slowly, reading the requirement three times to make sure he'd understood it correctly. "I've never observed a star's Qi field directly. I don't even know if that's possible from here — from Earth's surface, or even from Planetary Architect scale within Earth's own atmosphere."

"It is not possible from here," Meen-4 confirmed. "This requirement assumes physical or perceptual access to a stellar environment that current human and Formican infrastructure on Earth simply does not provide. You would need reach considerably beyond anything the Colony Link currently extends to."

He sat with that, turning the shape of the obstacle over — not a technical limitation he could solve through more careful study or more patient practice, the way most of his previous obstacles had eventually yielded. A genuine gap in access, requiring something the Strata, for all its accumulated infrastructure, simply did not yet possess.

"What would it take," he asked. "To actually reach something like that."

Meen-4 was quiet a long moment, and when she spoke, something in her tone carried a weight he recognized as significant even before she explained why.

"I do not know precisely," she admitted. "But I suspect the answer connects to something you have not yet fully investigated. The dormant signal from the outer solar system — the one your Colony Link first detected months ago and set aside as a lower priority. If that installation is what I believe it might be, it may hold exactly the kind of stellar observation infrastructure this curriculum requires."

He felt something settle into place, a connection he hadn't consciously made until she said it aloud — a dangling thread from earlier in the year, quietly waiting, suddenly relevant in a way it hadn't been before.

"Then that's where I need to look next," he said, already feeling the familiar, steady pull of curiosity beginning to reorganize his attention around a new question. "Not the Archive alone. The installation itself."

"Yes," Meen-4 said. "I believe that is exactly where this particular thread leads."

He closed the Stellar Scale section carefully, marking his place, and sat for a moment with the shape of what had just opened in front of him — not a threshold crossed, not yet, but the clear outline of the next door he would need to find a way through.
