# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Eight — Expanding the Network

The first practical question answered itself faster than he'd expected, once he sat down with Liang Jingzhi and explained, carefully, exactly what the survey had found.

She didn't flinch at the scale of it. He'd half-expected her to, the way most people might have reasonably flinched at hearing that the planet's geological stability depended on infrastructure nobody had known existed until last week. Instead, she pulled out her laptop within minutes, already cross-referencing his survey data against her own geological models.

"I can give you precise coordinates," she said, working through the overlay with the focused intensity he'd come to expect from her. "If the structure follows standard Ring of Fire geography, there should be major access points at the subduction zone boundaries — here, here, and here, along the deeper trenches. Eight locations along the ocean floor itself, by my estimate, plus several more where the structure surfaces closer to accessible geology in the island arcs."

"How confident are you in those estimates?"

"Reasonably. The pattern's regular enough that once I had a few confirmed points from your survey data, the rest follows predictable geological logic. I'd want to verify each one before you commit significant Weaving effort to it, but I don't expect major surprises."

He sat with the practical shape of what she was describing — twelve locations, scattered across thousands of kilometers of ocean floor and island geology, each one requiring the kind of precise, sustained Dimensional Weaving he'd only recently grown capable of producing reliably.

"I'll need Chitin Circuit nodes at each access point," he said, thinking it through out loud. "Continuous monitoring, the same principle as the Under-Web network, just considerably larger in scale and considerably more difficult to physically reach."

"You're not planning to swim to the Pacific floor."

"No. I'm planning to Weave directly to each coordinate, the way I've learned to Weave to any location I have precise enough data for. Your coordinates are exactly what makes that possible — without them, I'd be searching blind across an area larger than most countries."

She considered this, something like genuine excitement breaking through her usual careful professionalism.

"This is the largest application of your Weaving I've heard about," she said. "Planetary-scale Circuit deployment, accessing geological structures eight kilometers under the ocean surface."

"It is."

"From a pocket dimension in Chengdu."

"That's roughly accurate, yes."

She laughed, the particular delighted sound of a scientist confronting something genuinely unprecedented and finding the unprecedented part exhilarating rather than frightening.

"I'm coming with you for the planning, at minimum," she said. "Not physically — I understand the access requires capability I don't have. But I want to be involved in every coordinate confirmation, every readout, every piece of data this generates. This is, without exaggeration, the most significant geological dataset that's ever existed."

"I'd want you involved regardless," he said. "You're the only person who can read this data with the precision the work actually requires."

---

He began the deployment three days later, working through the twelve coordinates methodically, one at a time, the careful, patient discipline of someone who understood exactly how much could go wrong if he rushed work this consequential.

Each Weave took hours — not the casual, practiced ease of his Strata reconstruction work, but careful, deliberate construction at a depth and pressure his perception had never operated under before, the ocean's vast weight a constant, unfamiliar presence at the edge of his awareness throughout each deployment.

By the end of the second week, all twelve nodes held stable, the planetary-scale Circuit network finally giving him the continuous, detailed visibility into the Pacific Ring's actual condition that the original discovery survey had only glimpsed.

He sat with the completed network's first full readout, Liang Jingzhi beside him, both of them working through data that no human being, and likely no living Formican, had ever had this clear a view of before.

"How does it look?" she asked.

He extended his perception through the full network, the structures' active hum settling into his awareness with the particular clarity of something finally, fully understood.

"Functional," he said. "Degraded in places, more than I'd like. But functional, and now, for the first time, something I can actually monitor properly instead of simply hoping is fine."

It was, he thought, exactly the kind of progress that mattered most in this strange, accumulating responsibility — not a solution, not yet, but visibility, the first essential step toward understanding a problem well enough to eventually solve it.
