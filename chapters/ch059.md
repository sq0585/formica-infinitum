# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifty-Nine — The Node — Critical

He noticed it on Monday morning during his daily Qi survey.

The Under-Web survey had become habitual over the past month — a 6 AM check, walking a rough circuit of his neighborhood, assessing the filament network's condition across the standard landmarks he'd established for comparison. The node beneath the 7-Eleven was one of the landmarks. He'd been watching its depletion since the early weeks. He'd documented it as gradual, predictable, roughly three to five percent per week.

Today it was at eight percent.

He stopped on the pavement outside the store — which had opened for the day, Xiao Dong's morning shift visible through the glass — and measured the filament network under the floor against his established calibration points.

Eight percent. Maybe nine. Not the twenty-something he'd estimated for this point in the schedule.

The depletion had accelerated.

---

He stood outside the store for several minutes doing the arithmetic.

From roughly fifty percent — where he'd estimated it in early November — to eight percent in four weeks. That was a drop of forty-two percent in four weeks, versus the three-to-five percent per week rate he'd observed earlier. The rate had increased by a factor of three.

He had not changed his practices significantly in the past month. He had quit the night shift, which meant his phone was no longer sitting above the node every night drawing ambient energy. If anything, his physical proximity to the node had decreased.

Which meant the accelerated depletion had another cause.

He thought about the investigator, Elder Kuo, who had been in the city for several weeks. He thought about what it meant when an experienced cultivator began operating in proximity to a depleted node — whether the investigative methods themselves drew on nearby Qi sources, whether the process of actively scanning for anomalies consumed the ambient energy being scanned.

He wrote that evening:

> *Node: 8–9% capacity. Accelerated depletion — rate tripled in past 4 weeks. My reduced proximity cannot explain this.*
>
> *Hypothesis: the investigator's active scanning methods are drawing from the node. Either deliberately (testing the node's responsiveness) or inadvertently (scanning consumes ambient Qi from nearest available source).*
>
> *Either way: the node is nearly dead. Chen Yufei's monitoring instruments will show a complete collapse within 2–3 weeks.*
>
> *What happens when the node collapses: the investigation escalates. A node going fully dark is a significant event in the cultivation world's monitoring network. It will not be attributed to natural causes, because nodes don't naturally die.*
>
> *I am looking at a flash of guilt I don't entirely understand.*

He had written that last line without planning to and looked at it.

He had been drawing from this node for three months. He had been drawing from it without understanding what it was, without asking permission, without knowing the consequence. The node was dying because he'd used it as a power supply for a dead man's inheritance.

He had not done this with malicious intent. He had not even done it with knowing intent, not at first.

But the node was at eight percent and declining, and it had been healthy before he arrived, and the investigator now circling the area was circling because of what Lin Mushi had caused.

He wrote: *I am responsible for this. That is simply true.*

He closed the notebook.

He made tea. He sat with the specific discomfort of having caused a consequence he hadn't intended.

Then he put the discomfort where it belonged — noted, registered, kept — and turned to the Compression geometry.

The threshold crossing was Wednesday. The node would survive two more days.

After that, he would be where he needed to be to understand what he'd caused, and what he owed, and whether there was anything to be done about it.
