# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-Three — The Pocket Dimension Laboratory

He built it as an experiment and ended up living in it for three days without quite deciding to.

The idea had started simply enough: a personal workspace, large enough to spread out the full breadth of his current projects — the maintenance rotation schedules, the Heaven Map translation work with Fang Ruxue, the governance notes for the Collective, the ongoing Archive sections he hadn't finished cataloguing — without needing to pack everything away each time he left the outer hall for his shift or for sleep. A studio-sized fold, anchored to a quiet corner of the Strata, stocked with a desk, shelving, and enough chairs that he could imagine, eventually, working alongside someone else in it.

He finished the construction on a Friday evening and stepped inside to test it.

The geometry held perfectly — stable, comfortable, the kind of space that felt, once he was inside it, indistinguishable from any ordinary room except for the faint, specific quality of light that all his folds seemed to share, something adjacent to the Strata's amber glow but not quite identical to it. He sat down at the desk, opened his notebook, and began working through the translation backlog.

He looked up, at some point, and found it was dark outside the fold's single window-shaped aperture, which let in nothing but the Strata's ambient light regardless of the actual hour.

He checked his phone. It was past midnight. He'd been working for six hours without noticing.

He decided, reasonably, to simply continue.

---

By the time Wei Canglan found him, he'd lost track of the exact day count, though his phone insisted it had been three days since he'd left the fold.

"You disappeared," Wei Canglan said, standing at the fold's entrance with the particular expression of someone who'd spent a non-trivial amount of effort tracking down where exactly *disappeared* had led.

"I was working."

"For three days. No messages. Chen Yufei thought Stone Hall might have finally escalated past surveillance." Wei Canglan's jaw was tight, the controlled irritation Lin Mushi had learned to read as genuine worry dressed in a less vulnerable shape. "Where, exactly, is *working*?"

"In where?" Wei Canglan repeated, slower this time, looking around the fold's interior — the desk, the shelving, the window that showed nothing but uniform amber light regardless of which direction he faced. "This isn't a room I recognize from anywhere in the Strata."

"It's a room I made."

A long pause, Wei Canglan's expression cycling through several states before settling on something flat and carefully unimpressed.

"That's deeply unsettling," he said finally.

"I know."

"You built yourself a pocket universe to work in and then forgot to tell anyone it existed, and then forgot, inside the pocket universe, that time was still passing outside it."

"That's an accurate summary."

Wei Canglan exhaled, the tension easing slightly now that the immediate worry had resolved into something closer to exasperation. "You need a rule. Some kind of external check. If you're going to keep building rooms that make you lose track of three days at a time, someone outside needs to know where you are and roughly when to expect you back."

Lin Mushi considered this — the genuine practicality of it, underneath the irritation — and found he had no real argument against it.

"That's fair," he said. "I'll set something up. A check-in, before I enter anything I haven't built a known duration limit into."

"Today. Not eventually." Wei Canglan looked around the fold once more, something almost grudgingly curious crossing his face despite himself. "It's a good workspace, though. I'll give you that. Once you stop using it to vanish on people."

"I'll work on both."

He closed the notebook, finally, and followed Wei Canglan back out through the fold's entrance into the Strata's ordinary light, the three lost days settling into his memory not as a mistake exactly, but as a lesson about a capability large enough that it required a different kind of discipline than the ones he'd already learned to manage.
