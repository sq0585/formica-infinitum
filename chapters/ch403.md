# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 403 — Lady Shen's Assessment — Correct

She came three days after the result, once the initial wave of relief across the Strata had settled into something steadier, and Lin Mushi found her waiting again in the outer garden terrace, sitting on the same low bench where they'd last spoken honestly, watching light filter through leaves that existed nowhere except inside a fold of dimensional space he had shaped himself.

"I wanted to let the news settle before I said this," she said, before he'd fully sat down beside her. "You deserved a few days of simply having it, without me complicating it with anything else."

"I appreciate that."

She was quiet a moment, gathering something he could tell had taken real effort to arrive at fully formed. "The Orthodoxy's concern was not wrong," she said finally. "I want to say that plainly, because I think it matters that I still believe it, even now. You are approaching a level of capability that represents genuine risk if your judgment ever fails at the wrong moment. That was true before the review. It remains true after it. The classification changed. The underlying reality it was measuring did not."

He absorbed that, not with the defensiveness he might once have brought to it, but with the same careful attention he'd learned to give every hard truth Lady Shen had ever offered him.

"I engaged them preparing for that failure," she continued. "Not because I wanted it to happen. Because I judged the probability high enough to warrant insurance, and because I did not trust anyone else — myself included, if I'm honest — to act quickly enough if it ever did happen." She turned to look at him directly, something unguarded in her expression that he'd rarely seen from her before. "It hasn't happened. Not once, across everything I have watched you face in the months since I made that choice. Not the betrayal's discovery, not the Colony Link strain, not the pressure of an entire civilization's fate resting on a single presentation. You have made mistakes. You have never once, in any of it, lost the thing that made me trust you were worth watching in the first place."

"So what does that mean," he asked, though he suspected he already understood the shape of her answer.

"It means I am no longer preparing for that failure," she said simply. "I engaged the Orthodoxy as insurance against a version of you I feared might eventually exist. I have watched that version continue not to appear, session after session, choice after choice, for months now. I am not going to keep insuring against a possibility the evidence no longer supports."

He sat with that, feeling the particular weight of it — not an apology, not exactly, Lady Shen had never once, in three years, offered him anything so straightforward as a simple *I'm sorry.* But this was something adjacent to it, something closer to the truth of who she actually was: a woman who did not soften her judgments to make them easier to hear, and who therefore, when her judgment finally shifted, offered that shift with the same unflinching honesty she'd offered every warning before it.

"Is that as close as you get to an apology," he said, not unkindly.

She considered the question with the particular seriousness she brought to everything, and something almost like a small, rueful smile touched the corner of her mouth. "Probably," she admitted. "I have never found the words for straightforward regret to come easily. I find it easier to simply revise my position and tell you honestly that I've revised it, and trust that the revision itself carries the weight an apology would carry for someone less complicated than I apparently am."

"I'll take it," he said, and meant it, understanding that from her, this was, in fact, close to everything she had.

They sat together a while longer in the filtered light, neither of them saying much else, and he found, watching her in the quiet, that whatever she remained to him — not fully trusted, not fully forgiven, something more complicated than either of those simple words could hold — he no longer felt the need to resolve it into something cleaner than it actually was.

"What happens now," he asked eventually. "For you. Now that the insurance is gone."

"I remain an observer," she said. "Neutral, as I told you I would. I will keep watching, honestly, the way I always have. If the day ever comes that the version of you I once feared actually appears, I will tell you so directly, the same way I have told you everything else." She stood, smoothing a fold from her sleeve that didn't need smoothing. "I hope, sincerely, that day never comes. I have found, somewhat to my own surprise, that I would prefer to be wrong about you for the rest of both our lives."

She left the way she always left, without ceremony, and he sat alone a while longer in the garden's filtered light, holding the strange, hard-won shape of whatever understanding they had finally, carefully, arrived at together.
