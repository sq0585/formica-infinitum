# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifteen — Mapping the 7-Eleven

He spent three days on it.

Not consecutive days — he worked nights, slept mornings, had waking hours in the afternoon that he could dedicate to the project without interruption. But he also mapped during his shifts, in the dead hours between two and five when the store sat empty and the filaments were visible and he could walk the space with his notebook and a growing confidence that he was learning to read something real.

The methodology was simple: grid the space, sample each cell of the grid, note the intensity and direction of the filament at each point, build a composite picture.

The execution was complicated by the fact that he was the only instrument, had no calibration standard, and had to simultaneously stock shelves and run the register.

He managed.

---

The first thing the map showed him was that the store sat at the edge of something, not the center.

He had been assuming, from the depth sensation's dominant direction, that whatever was below ran mostly north and that the store was in the path of it. The spatial survey corrected this: the filaments that ran through the store's floor were tributaries. The main current — denser, more directional, the heaviness of the Codex's principal nodes — ran further north and significantly deeper, at an angle suggesting it passed through the earth beneath the residential blocks between here and the river, or below the river itself.

The store was a minor stop along a route. Not the terminus.

He noted this without surprise. He had suspected the store wasn't special in itself; it was simply where he worked, which was where he'd been standing when a man died.

He drew the tributary pattern with careful dotted lines — his convention for filaments below a certain density threshold — and marked the probable heading of the main flow with a dashed arrow pointing north-northwest.

---

The second thing the map showed him was the drain.

He had documented the battery anomaly across multiple nights without being able to locate its source spatially. Now, with the overlay active, he could see it: a faint draw, a direction of flow that was oriented not toward the store's floor but upward and slightly inward — toward him specifically, toward the phone in his pocket, toward the Codex and whatever process the Codex was sustaining.

The ambient filaments in the immediate vicinity of the counter where he usually stood were consistently lighter than the same filaments measured twenty meters away. The depletion was real and it was local.

He was the depletion. The Codex was drawing through him.

He sat with this for a long time, standing at the counter with his notebook open.

The drain was small. At the current rate, it would take — he estimated from the lightening he'd observed over seventeen days — a very long time before the tributary lost meaningful function. But *a very long time* and *infinite* were not the same thing, and someone, somewhere, might notice the change in the filament's behavior before it became critically depleted.

He wrote:

> *Mapping note: depletion pattern centered on my position (counter area). Consistent with battery anomaly documentation. The Codex draws from the local filament network to maintain its processes.*
>
> *Rate of depletion: low. Not immediately concerning. But visible.*
>
> *Implication: visible means potentially visible to someone else. I am leaving a trace.*
>
> *Action: monitor rate. Do not increase draw.*

He underlined the last line.

---

The third thing the map showed him was the most interesting.

On the third night, near the end of his shift, walking the northwest corner of the store where the cold drinks case met the wall, he noticed something in the overlay that he had missed in the first two sweeps because he had been too close to it. At a distance of about three meters, with the angle of view adjusted, one of the heavier nodes in the filament network was not sitting in the floor.

It was sitting in the wall.

More precisely: it was sitting in the boundary between the store and whatever was beyond the wall — not the street, which was to the east, but the building's interior wall that backed onto the kitchen storage area. A boundary, not a solid material. And the node sat precisely on that boundary as if the boundary itself was significant, as if the node was marking the threshold rather than simply occupying space that happened to be there.

He stared at it for four minutes.

He had, in his notation analysis, begun to decode what the three-symbol cluster that marked heavy nodes actually said. He had two of the three symbols confidently parsed. The third still escaped him. But the two he had were consistent with something like *interface* or *transition point* — not a concentration of energy so much as a place where one state became another.

The node was not a pool. It was a door.

He put his hand on the wall.

The familiar membrane quality: not physical, not empty, something with tension at the surface and a different texture on the near side. He had felt this at the crack in his apartment ceiling. He was feeling it now in the boundary of the drinks case wall, except stronger — denser — more definitively there.

He pressed.

His palm met resistance and then, for a fraction of a second, the resistance changed quality and he felt something on the other side of the wall that was not more store and not more building.

He pulled his hand back.

He stood with his heart doing something loud and counted his breaths until it quieted.

Then he opened his notebook and wrote with a hand that was steadier than he expected:

> *Day 20. Spatial survey complete (preliminary). Three findings:*
>
> *1. The store sits at the edge of a larger network; main flow runs north-northwest, deeper. Not the terminus.*
>
> *2. Local depletion confirmed as caused by my presence and the Codex's draw. Rate is low but visible. Risk: I am leaving a trace.*
>
> *3. Heavy node (NW corner, drinks case wall boundary) is a threshold marker, not a concentration point. The node is marking a transition between this space and something adjacent.*
>
> *The transition is accessible.*
>
> *I am not going through it yet. But it is accessible.*
>
> *Rule: do not enter anything you cannot map first. Map further before approaching again.*

He closed the notebook. Xiao Dong would arrive in twenty minutes.

He spent those twenty minutes standing at his usual position behind the counter, looking at the overlay, learning the shape of the store as it actually was — the threads of light running north-northwest through the floor, the depletion his presence caused, the threshold in the northwest corner that marked the edge of something he didn't yet have a name for.

He was mapping.

This was the first step.

He had always known the first step.
