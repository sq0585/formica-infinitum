# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-One — What 18 Months Means

He spent the following day running the numbers, the same careful, methodical accounting he'd brought to every threshold before this one, trying to understand exactly what eighteen months actually meant for his own trajectory.

He found Liang Jingzhi in her office, the two of them working through it together, her instinct for scale and pacing as valuable here as it had been during his geological training months earlier.

"I need to understand where I'll actually be, in eighteen months, if my cultivation continues at its current pace," he said, spreading his own careful notes across her desk. "Not hopeful projection. Realistic estimate."

She considered the question seriously, drawing on everything she'd learned about the shape of his progression across their long collaboration. "Planetary Architect maturity, most likely, if the current trajectory holds. You're still relatively new to the threshold — full comprehension typically deepens considerably over the year or two following initial emergence, the way your understanding of geological time kept sharpening throughout our fieldwork, even after the first breakthrough."

"What about beyond that? The next tier."

She was quiet for a moment, working through the estimate with visible care. "Stellar Scale, based on what little the Archive has documented about progression rates at this level, typically requires considerably longer than eighteen months from where you currently stand. I wouldn't want to give you false precision — the sample size for comparison is essentially nonexistent, given how rarely any cultivator reaches this tier at all. But if I had to estimate honestly, I'd say you're looking at years, not months, before Stellar Scale becomes realistic."

He sat with that, the shape of the timeline settling into an uncomfortable clarity. "So I'll have matured into Planetary Architect by the time the review concludes. But I won't have reached whatever comes next."

"That appears to be the honest estimate, yes."

---

He brought the same question to Meen-4 later that day, wanting her perspective on what the gap actually meant, given her considerably deeper understanding of how the Collective measured worlds under review.

*I do not believe raw cultivation power is likely to be the primary standard the Collective applies,* she signaled, once he'd laid out Liang Jingzhi's estimate. *Veth-Karun reached considerably further than Planetary Architect, and reached it in a fraction of the time you have taken. That achievement did not save the Amber Lattice School. If anything, it condemned it.*

He felt the weight of that land with uncomfortable clarity. "You're saying more power wouldn't help me here, even if I had it."

*I am saying more power alone has never been the correct answer to a review of this kind, as far as the historical record indicates,* Meen-4 signaled. *If the Collective's monitoring bodies are cautious about worlds capable of producing uncontrolled Formless Scale events, then a world racing toward greater raw power, faster, is not demonstrating the kind of judgment likely to reassure them. You will not win this review by force. You must win it politically — by demonstrating, clearly and convincingly, that this world's development is careful, structured, and worth trusting with continued autonomy, rather than by demonstrating that it is simply becoming more powerful.*

"Politically," he repeated. "I don't know the first thing about that kind of politics. I don't even know who I'm arguing my case to."

*Then that is precisely where we begin,* Meen-4 signaled. *You have eighteen months. Cultivation power will accumulate on its own trajectory, regardless of how closely you attend to it. What will not accumulate on its own is genuine understanding of how the Collective evaluates worlds like this one, and what evidence actually persuades a body of that kind. That understanding requires deliberate teaching, and deliberate teaching requires time we do not have in unlimited supply.*

---

He sat with the full reframing that evening, understanding, with a clarity that felt both sobering and strangely steadying, exactly what the next eighteen months actually needed to become.

Not a race toward greater power. A different kind of work entirely — the patient, careful accumulation of evidence, understanding, and trust, built through everything he'd already spent two years learning to value: honest relationships, careful governance, communities built around genuine care rather than raw capability.

He wrote about it that night, the entry carrying the specific weight of a decision finally settling into clarity.

> *Eighteen months. I won't reach Stellar Scale in that time, no matter how hard I push, and pushing harder wouldn't help even if I could. Veth-Karun proved that, forty million years ago, in 0.7 seconds. What I need instead is something I've actually been building all along, without fully understanding its future purpose — the Strata City, the Collective, the careful, patient relationships that make this world worth trusting. I need to understand how to make that case, clearly, to someone I've never met and don't yet fully understand. That's the actual work ahead. Not becoming more. Proving what I already am is enough.*
