# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Nine — The Planetary Architect Prerequisites

Meen-4 raised the subject during what had otherwise been an ordinary review of the Strata City's expanded footprint, her signal arriving with the particular weight she reserved for things that had been waiting, patiently, for the right moment to be said.

*You will reach a threshold soon,* she signaled. *I have been watching your cultivation's trajectory for some months. I believe it is time to name what lies beyond it, so that you approach it with understanding rather than simply arriving there unprepared.*

He set down the survey data he'd been reviewing. "Beyond city-scale."

*Yes. The tier the Archive's older records designate Planetary Architect — the capacity to hold, coherently, the emotional and structural texture of a region, and eventually a world, rather than a single city.* She paused, the pause itself carrying information. *It is not merely a larger version of what you currently hold. Colony Link expansion at this scale requires a different kind of comprehension than emotional sensing alone provides.*

"What kind?"

*Geological-timescale understanding of Qi.* She let the phrase sit for a moment before continuing. *Everything you have learned to sense so far moves on human timescales — a resident's mood shifting over hours, a Circuit node's status changing over days. A planet's Qi structure moves differently. Mountains accumulate density over millions of years. Fault lines hold and release tension across intervals no human institution has ever existed long enough to fully observe. To comprehend a planet's field, rather than merely sense fragments of it, you must learn to think in timescales your own life is far too short to naturally hold.*

He turned the requirement over, understanding, slowly, the shape of what she was describing — not simply more power, but an entirely different relationship to time itself.

"You said comprehend. Not sense."

*Correctly noted.* Something in her signal carried the particular satisfaction she showed when he caught a distinction she considered important. *Sensing without comprehension is what nearly destroyed Veth-Karun. I do not intend for you to repeat that pattern. The Planetary Architect threshold cannot be reached through raw capability alone. It requires genuine understanding of the scale you are attempting to hold, or it will not stabilize.*

---

"Who could teach that?" he asked, though he suspected, even as he asked it, that he already knew the answer.

*Liang Jingzhi,* Meen-4 signaled. *Her expertise is geological, not cultivation-based, but the underlying comprehension your Colony Link requires and the comprehension her professional discipline has spent decades building are, I believe, considerably closer to identical than either of you currently realizes. She thinks in the timescales you need to learn.*

He found Liang Jingzhi later that same day, in the small office she'd claimed within the geological survey section, surrounded by core samples and seismic data with the particular contented focus she brought to work she genuinely loved.

"Meen-4 thinks you're the key to my next cultivation threshold," he said, without preamble, because he'd learned, across their working relationship, that she preferred directness over careful lead-up.

She looked up, genuinely surprised. "I'm not a cultivator."

"You don't need to be. She says the threshold requires geological-timescale comprehension of Qi. Not technique. Understanding. The kind of understanding you've built studying rock formations for — how long, now?"

"Nineteen years." She set down the core sample she'd been examining, her expression shifting from surprise into something more considering. "What would that actually look like? Me teaching you geology so you can reach some cultivation milestone?"

"I don't fully know yet. I think it means spending real time with you, learning to actually see what you see when you look at a mountain range — not the surface, but the millions of years underneath it. I don't think it's a skill I can absorb quickly. Meen-4 implied it would take real collaboration, not a few lessons."

She considered this seriously, the specific weighing attention she brought to any proposal that asked something significant of her time. "How long, do you think?"

"I don't know. Weeks, at minimum. Possibly longer."

"I have fieldwork scheduled for the next several weeks anyway — the deep survey work along the fault systems feeding the Pacific Ring. If you wanted to actually understand geological time rather than just have it explained to you, joining that fieldwork would probably teach you more than any amount of sitting in this office listening to me talk about it."

"Then let's do that."

Something eased in her expression — not quite a smile, but adjacent to one, the particular satisfaction of a proposal landing somewhere genuinely interesting rather than merely obligatory. "I'll admit, I'm curious what six weeks of fieldwork does to someone whose usual timescale is measured in days and Colony Link updates. It might be good for you, independent of whether it actually unlocks anything."

"I think that's probably true regardless of the outcome."

She nodded, already reaching for the survey schedule to work out logistics, and he found, watching her plan with the same careful precision she brought to everything, that he was genuinely looking forward to it — not merely as a means toward the next threshold, but as its own kind of discipline, the deliberate, patient work of learning to see time the way a mountain range saw it, one careful season at a time.
