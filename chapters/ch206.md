# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Six — Lady Shen's New Approach

The message came through a channel he hadn't given to anyone affiliated with a Court — a number that reached his phone only because he'd once used it, briefly, for a transaction with a Northern Peak intermediary back when Lady Shen's people had still been pretending to be ordinary cultivators.

He recognized the careful precision before he reached the signature.

*I am asking you what you intend to build, because what you build will determine whether I treat you as a resource or a threat. I am not asking on behalf of Northern Peak. I am asking for myself. — Shen Baoling*

He read it twice, the way he'd read the thirty-first message two weeks earlier, looking for the shape underneath the words.

It wasn't a threat dressed as a question. It wasn't an apology, either — he didn't expect one, and he suspected she wasn't capable of offering one in any form he'd recognize as sincere. It was something rarer in his experience of her: a genuine request for information, sent by someone who had apparently run out of better ways to get it.

He thought about the two coalitions she'd lost in the space of a week. About Elder Kuo's ultimatum landing with her name attached to it, whether she'd wanted that or not. About a woman who had spent thirty years building institutional authority watching a third of her foundation walk away over a question of trust she could no longer pretend didn't matter.

He typed a response, deleted it, typed another.

What he finally sent was short.

*Come to the Strata. Alone.*

He didn't add conditions. He didn't specify a time. If she wanted an honest answer to an honest question, the only honest answer he had was the place itself — not a negotiated description of it, not a curated demonstration, but the thing as it actually was.

Her reply came back within four minutes, which told him she'd been waiting for exactly this kind of invitation and hadn't expected to receive it.

*Tomorrow. Noon.*

---

Chen Yufei's reaction, when he told her, was less surprised than he'd expected.

"You're letting her walk in," she said. Not a question.

"She asked an honest question. I'm giving her an honest place to find the answer."

"She nearly captured you three weeks ago."

"She did," he agreed. "And then she withdrew, and recalculated, and lost two-thirds of her coalition, and is now asking me what I intend to build instead of trying to take it from me. People change their approach when their old one stops working. I'd rather know what her new approach actually is than guess."

Chen Yufei studied him for a moment, the look she gave him when she was deciding whether to argue or simply make sure he'd thought it through.

"I want to be there," she said finally.

"I was hoping you would be."

"Not in the room. Nearby. Where she doesn't know I am, in case her honest question turns out to have a dishonest second half."

He hadn't considered that arrangement, and found, turning it over, that he had no objection to it.

"Reasonable," he said.

Outside, the Chengdu afternoon carried on its ordinary business, the kind of indifferent daylight under which the city had absorbed every strange thing he'd done in it so far without once noticing. Tomorrow at noon, the most dangerous person who had ever tried to take something from him would walk into the most important place he had ever found, unaccompanied, asking him to tell her the truth.

He found, somewhat to his own surprise, that he was looking forward to it.
