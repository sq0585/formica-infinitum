# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-Two — Fang Ruxue Is Hurt

The follow-up attack came four days later, smaller than the conference room assault but more precisely targeted — three of Gou Ping's cultivators, intercepting a Collective working group meeting at the modest office space Fang Ruxue had begun renting for exactly this kind of routine, unglamorous administrative work.

Lin Mushi wasn't there. He learned what happened from Chen Yufei's call, the particular tightness in her voice telling him before she'd said anything specific that the news wasn't going to be easy to hear.

"Fang Ruxue intervened," she said. "Three attackers, targeting the working group directly. She put herself between them and two of the younger members before backup arrived."

"Is she alright?"

"She's stable. But she sustained a cultivation injury — a disrupted Qi channel in her left arm. The clinic's assessment is it'll take months to heal properly, and there's a real chance it never returns to full function."

He sat with this, the cold weight of it settling slowly and completely.

"I should have had the technique ready," he said. "I spent four days building something better than what failed at the conference room, and it still wasn't fast enough to protect her before they reached her."

"You can't be everywhere at once, Lin Mushi. You weren't even at the meeting."

"I know. That doesn't change the fact that she's hurt because of a conflict that started with documents I released, institutions I disrupted, a chain of consequences that traces back to choices I made." He closed his eyes for a moment, the guilt sitting heavy and undeniable, exactly the kind of feeling he'd learned, slowly, not to perform but also not to suppress. "I need to see her."

---

He found her at the clinic the next morning, her left arm immobilized in a careful brace, her expression composed despite the visible strain underneath it.

He didn't say anything for the first ten minutes. He simply sat with her, the silence stretching between them, comfortable in the way silence could be comfortable between two people who'd built genuine trust without needing constant words to maintain it.

She broke it first.

"You're not going to apologize," she said. Not a question.

"No."

"Good. I'd find it tiresome if you did. I made my own choice to put myself between those attackers and the people I was responsible for in that room. That choice was mine, made with full knowledge of what this conflict has already cost other people, and I don't need you taking responsibility for a decision I made with my eyes open."

He considered this carefully, the particular discipline of accepting what she was offering instead of insisting on a guilt that wasn't strictly his to claim.

"I'm not apologizing," he said. "I'm sitting with you because you're hurt and I care, and because I don't have anything useful to say that would actually help beyond simply being here."

Something in her composed expression eased, fractionally, the genuine relief of someone who had braced for a particular kind of conversation and received a different, easier one instead.

"That's the correct response," she said. "Most people would have spent the last ten minutes performing guilt at me. I appreciate that you didn't."

"I learned, a while ago, that performing guilt mostly serves the person doing the performing. It doesn't actually help the person who got hurt."

"Wise lesson. Expensively earned, I'd guess."

"Most of mine are."

She looked down at her immobilized arm, the careful brace holding it still, and something in her expression shifted toward the quiet, considering look he'd come to associate with her working through a problem rather than simply reacting to one.

"This changes my calculation about the working group's safety," she said. "We need better protection than what we currently have, before someone gets hurt worse than I did."

"I'm building something," he said. "It's not finished. I'll have it ready as fast as I genuinely can, not faster than that, because rushing it risks it failing exactly when it matters most."

"I trust your timeline," she said. "I'd simply like it to be a short one."

"It will be," he said, and meant it, the cold resolve from the night in the Archive settling deeper now, sharpened by the particular weight of watching someone he respected sit injured in a clinic bed because his answer hadn't been ready in time.
