# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Six — Dormancy

The Chitin Seed's dormancy arrived without warning, somewhere past the fourteenth hour, the way a held breath finally gives out regardless of how badly the body wants to keep holding it.

One moment he was maintaining the reinforcement, his vision narrowed, his hands shaking but his interface still — barely, but still — functioning. The next moment, the interface simply wasn't there.

Not weakened. Not strained past comfort the way it had been for hours already. Gone, entirely, the way a phone screen goes dark when the battery finally empties past the point where even a low-power warning can display.

He couldn't perceive Qi.

He sat on the cold ground at the anchor's edge, his perception suddenly, completely ordinary — no ambient field, no Compression, no Weaving, nothing but his own eyes and the dust-filled air where a parking structure's foundation had, somewhere above him, finally finished coming apart.

He understood, with the particular clarity of someone whose body had just made a decision his conscious mind hadn't been consulted on, exactly what had happened. He'd read about Chitin Seed dormancy in the Codex's biological sections months ago, filed it away as theoretical — a protective mechanism, the Seed shutting down entirely rather than allowing sustained overuse to cause permanent damage to the underlying neurology it had grown into.

He'd pushed past whatever threshold triggered it. His own safety mechanism had simply taken the decision out of his hands.

Chen Yufei found him within minutes, her own reinforcement work shifting seamlessly to cover the gap his sudden absence had opened, her expression carrying a fear he hadn't seen from her since the early days of knowing each other.

"Lin Mushi." She crouched beside him, checking his pulse with hands that weren't entirely steady themselves. "Can you hear me?"

"I can hear you," he said, and found his own voice sounded strange to him — ordinary, unremarkable, the voice of someone who had simply talked too long rather than someone whose entire relationship to reality had just been temporarily switched off. "I think the Seed's gone dormant."

"For how long?"

"I don't know. The Codex mentioned hours, not days. I don't know more precisely than that."

She was quiet for a moment, her hand still at his wrist, and he watched her make the same rapid calculation he would have made in her position.

"I'll hold the anchor," she said. "You stay here. Don't try to help — you can't, right now, and trying will just put you somewhere dangerous while you're vulnerable."

"I know," he said. "I'm not going anywhere. I don't think I could if I wanted to."

She left to return to the reinforcement work, and he sat alone at the anchor's edge, ordinary in a way he hadn't been ordinary in nearly two years, watching dust settle through grey early-morning light with nothing but his own unaugmented eyes.

It was, he found, a strange kind of quiet.

Not peaceful exactly — the anchor's fate still hung in genuine uncertainty above him, Chen Yufei and the others still working through exhaustion to hold what he could no longer help hold. But quiet in a way his perception hadn't been quiet since the night a dying man's fingers had first moved in a pattern his eyes could register and his mind couldn't parse.

He sat with it. There wasn't anything else to do.

He thought, somewhere in the long, ordinary hours that followed, about how much of what he'd built over the past two years he'd come to think of as simply who he was — inseparable from himself, the way breathing was inseparable from being alive. Sitting here now, stripped of all of it without warning, without choice, he understood something he suspected he'd needed to understand for a long time.

He was still himself, even like this. Slower. Smaller. Considerably more frightened for the people still working above him than he had any ability to help with.

But still himself.

He held onto that, quietly, for as long as the dormancy lasted.
