# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Forty-Four — Chen Yufei's Recall

The formal expulsion notice was served on a Friday.

Not a letter — the Iron Meridian Court did not use letters for expulsions, which was either a dignity consideration or an acknowledgment that letters could be ignored. A senior practitioner arrived at Chen Yufei's apartment at 10 AM, knocked, and when she opened the door, said the formal words and handed her a sealed document.

She called Lin Mushi from the stairwell, still holding the document.

"It's formal," she said.

"I know."

"Thirty days from today. Failure to report for the review committee results in expulsion and immediate Qi-seal." She paused. "The senior practitioner who served it was Elder Zhou. She was kind about it. She said she thought the Council was making a mistake."

"Elder Zhou is in the forty percent," he said.

"Yes." A pause. "There are people in the Iron Meridian who don't want this. They're not the majority right now."

He was quiet for a moment.

"What do you want to do?" he said.

The question again. The same question he'd asked in October when she'd first chosen not to report. It was the right question to keep asking, because the answer could have changed, because she was the one bearing the cost, because the decision belonged to her.

She was quiet for longer than usual.

"I want to keep working," she said. "I've been thinking about whether thirty days changes anything. About whether there's something that could happen in thirty days that would change what I want." She paused. "The document is out. The correction is distributed. The archive is — the archive is what it is. Thirty days doesn't change any of it."

"No."

"I don't want to stop," she said. "That's still the most important thing."

"Then you have thirty days to do what you want to do before the formal status changes."

"Yes." Another pause. "What do *you* want me to do?"

He had been waiting for this question. He had thought about it.

"I want you to do whatever is most accurate to what you value," he said. "Not what serves me, not what serves the archive, not what I think is strategically optimal. What's true to you." He paused. "Because this has to be worth it to you on its own terms, not because you made a commitment to me you feel obligated to."

A very long silence.

"It's worth it on its own terms," she said.

"Then thirty days."

She laughed once — the brief, real one. "Thirty days."

She went back inside. He heard her door close.

He sat at his desk.

He thought about Chen Yufei, nine years of Iron Meridian, independent operational status, three months of their alliance, the fire meridian correction, the archive visits, the document's forty-seven recipients. He thought about what she was going to lose in thirty days — institutional community, research access, the social structure that had organized her professional life for nearly a decade.

He thought about what she was keeping.

He wrote:

> *Chen Yufei's expulsion: formal notice served. Thirty days.*
>
> *She asked what I wanted. I told her to do what's true to herself. This is correct. It's also the easy answer — it places the decision with her, where it belongs, and relieves me of a responsibility I don't have.*
>
> *What I actually feel: I am glad she's staying. I'm aware that's a self-interested feeling. I'm trying not to let it obscure the honest assessment of whether this is good for her.*
>
> *Honest assessment: it is. The archive work, the corrected framework, the understanding of what she's been practicing — these are real gains that don't disappear when the institution removes its recognition. She is more capable now than she was in October, and will be more capable in thirty days than she is today.*
>
> *What the institution takes back: the social structure. The community. The identity.*
>
> *What it can't take back: the channels. The practice. The understanding.*
>
> *She will build a different community. She already has one, in the archive.*
>
> *The obligation: make sure that community is real and stable before the institutional one is gone.*

He put the pen down.

He looked at his phone.

Gao had sent a message: *checking in. How is the situation?*

He replied: *complicated. But stable.*

Gao: *You always say that.*

He: *It's usually true.*

Gao: *Also true. Get some sleep.*

He did.
