# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-Three — Chen Yufei's Network

Chen Yufei had built her own sources over nine years, and in the weeks following her expulsion she used all of them.

Not to seek reinstatement. She had been clear with him, and clearer with herself, that the Iron Meridian Court's recognition was not what she wanted back. What she wanted was information — a picture of what was actually happening across the five Courts' informal practitioner networks, the layer of communication that moved faster and more honestly than official Council statements.

She came to his apartment on a Tuesday evening with a face that told him, before she said anything, that the picture had clarified.

"They're building a coalition," she said, sitting down without taking off her jacket. "All five Courts. Not formal — nothing as clean as a treaty. But the senior-practitioner networks are talking to each other in a way I haven't seen in nine years of watching them."

"About what specifically?"

"About you." She accepted the tea he handed her. "They're not sure what you are. That's the through-line in every conversation my contacts have reported. Not 'what is he planning' — 'what is he.' An unaffiliated twenty-two-year-old with complete access to a functioning Formican archive, who corrected Iron Meridian and Northern Peak technique within the same six months, who has an entity at his archive capable of making a third-generation Elder retreat in four minutes." She looked at him. "They've decided you're worth organizing over. Not opposing, necessarily. Organizing over. Understanding. Positioning around."

He thought about this.

"That's different from the acquisition framing," he said.

"Significantly different. Acquisition treats you as a target. This treats you as a factor — something that exists in the landscape that all five Courts now need to account for in their own planning, regardless of what you do next." She drank her tea. "The Celestial Compass School is the furthest along. They've stopped debating whether to engage and have moved to debating how. The Verdant Stream Collective is characteristically slower but moving the same direction. The Void Mirror Sect—" she paused — "no one knows what the Void Mirror Sect is doing. They don't talk to anyone, including each other apparently."

"And Iron Meridian?"

"Split, predictably. Elder Zhao's faction is engaging constructively. The faction that pushed for my expulsion is quieter than I expected — I think Senior Practitioner Liu's testimony reached them too, even though it was a Northern Peak session."

He sat with this.

"What does 'worth organizing over' mean practically?" he asked. "What are they actually going to do?"

Chen Yufei thought about this carefully — the specific care she brought to questions where she didn't want to overstate her certainty.

"I think," she said, "they're going to try to understand the shape of what you've built before deciding how to respond to it. Information-gathering, formalized. Possibly attempts to establish independent contact with the archive, separate from going through you — which won't work, but they don't know that yet, or they suspect it and want to verify."

"And the more aggressive elements? The forty-five percent in the Northern Peak vote?"

"They're the ones I'm most uncertain about." She set her cup down. "My sources don't have visibility into what they're planning specifically. I know the character of some of the people — patient, institutionally minded, used to long timelines. I don't know their next move."

He nodded. This matched what Kuo had told him.

"What do you think we should do?" he asked.

She looked at him for a long moment — the question landing the way it always landed, as a genuine request for her judgment rather than a rhetorical opening.

"Keep doing what we're doing," she said. "Honestly, carefully, with the archive's interests held above any single Court's. The thing that's working is that you haven't given anyone leverage. No Court can claim special access. No Court can claim you owe them anything. That neutrality is the strongest position we have."

"And if the formal delegations start arriving — multiple Courts, simultaneously?"

"Then we need a framework that scales," she said. "Right now you're handling Elder Zhao, Lady Shen, and Kuo as individual relationships. That worked when it was three people. It won't work at five Courts' worth of formal delegations."

He thought about this. It was accurate, and it was a problem he hadn't fully confronted yet.

"What would a scalable framework look like?" he asked.

"I don't know yet," she said. "But I think it starts with understanding what the archive itself wants in terms of structure. Not what you want to offer the Courts. What the Formicans want their relationship with the surface to look like, going forward, now that it's clearly going to be a relationship rather than an isolated archive."

He looked at her.

"That's the right question," he said.

"I know," she said, without false modesty. "I've been thinking about institutional structure for nine years. Even from the wrong side of it, apparently."

He smiled slightly — the rare, brief expression that had become more frequent over the past months.

"I need to ask Axis-7," he said. "And probably Meen-4. What the Layer 3 community actually wants from a formalized surface relationship."

"That conversation should happen soon," Chen Yufei said. "Before the formal delegations arrive and you're improvising a framework under pressure."

"Tomorrow," he said.

She nodded, satisfied. She picked her tea back up.

"In the meantime," she said, in a different tone — lighter, the tone of someone setting institutional weight aside for a moment — "I found an old farming record in the agricultural containers. Formican crop rotation theory, fourteen thousand years old. It's extraordinary. Want to see it?"

He did.

They spent the rest of the evening with the farming records, which had nothing to do with Courts or coalitions or formal delegations, and which were, in their own way, exactly what both of them needed.
