# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 473 — The Surveyor's Response

The Surveyor's formal transmission arrived three days later, routed through the Witness Node in the same dense diplomatic notation that had once carried an implicit ultimatum and now, apparently, carried something considerably different.

Meen-4 brought the full text to him, and something in her expression — careful, watchful, faintly satisfied in a way she rarely allowed herself to show openly — told him, before he'd read a single line, that this document mattered beyond its length.

*To the full Collective body, regarding access granted to the Pelagic Archive and the account therein,* it began, the formality of the opening giving no hint of what followed. *I attended the reading of Veth-Karun's account as designated observer. I record my findings for the Collective's consideration.*

He read through it carefully, the notation resolving with the fluency five years had finally earned him, and found the Surveyor's careful, institutional voice building toward something he hadn't fully anticipated.

*The Containment Orthodoxy's standing position regarding Pelagic Archive access has been, since the account's original sealing, that its contents would provide dangerous technical uplift to any practitioner attempting Formless Scale — that exposure to Veth-Karun's final understanding would accelerate development toward a technique the Orthodoxy considers, correctly, among the most hazardous in Collective history.*

*I attended the reading with this concern actively in mind. I observe the opposite conclusion.*

He sat up straighter, reading the next passage twice to be certain he'd translated it accurately.

*The account contains no technical uplift toward achieving Formless Scale more quickly, more powerfully, or with less preparation. It contains, instead, the single specific mechanism that was absent from the only prior documented attempt — a mechanism the petitioner Lin Mushi had already independently constructed, years before this reading, in the form of what he terms the Anchor Protocol. I observe, having reviewed both Veth-Karun's account and five years of the petitioner's documented practice, that this protocol directly addresses the exact failure mode described in the account's final lines.*

*The Containment Orthodoxy's concern was that this knowledge would accelerate dangerous development. I observe instead that this knowledge provides the safety mechanism that was missing from the previous Formless Scale attempt. Where the Orthodoxy anticipated uplift toward hazard, I record uplift toward survival. I recommend the Collective body weigh this distinction carefully before accepting the Orthodoxy's continued characterization of Pelagic Archive access as inherently dangerous.*

He set the document down, feeling something between vindication and genuine surprise settle into his chest.

"They inverted the Orthodoxy's own argument," he said. "Using their own logic against them — not defying it, just following it more carefully than they did."

"That is precisely what makes the Surveyor dangerous to underestimate," Meen-4 said, something like admiration entering her tone. "It does not argue from sentiment. It argues from the Collective's own stated principles, applied more rigorously than the party invoking them originally applied them. The Orthodoxy built its case on risk assessment. The Surveyor simply assessed the actual risk, rather than the assumed one, and found it ran the opposite direction."

"What happens now?"

"The transmission goes to the full Collective body for consideration," Meen-4 said. "I do not expect the Orthodoxy to withdraw its concerns entirely — institutions rarely abandon a position simply because a single observation complicates it. But the Surveyor's formal record now exists, permanently, in Collective archives, stating clearly that the Pelagic Archive's contents make Formless Scale attempts safer to consider, not more dangerous. That is not nothing. That may, in fact, matter considerably more than either of us can currently measure."

He thought of the installation's own crew, decades of careful preparation, an entire expedition lost to exactly the failure Veth-Karun's account had finally, fully explained. He thought of Veth-Karun themselves, alone in the last 0.7 seconds of an existence spent gathering knowledge without ever once learning to hold it alongside someone else's hand.

"I want to send something back," he said slowly. "Not through the Witness Node's formal channels. Something more direct."

"To whom?"

"The Surveyor," he said. "I think it's earned more than a formal acknowledgment. I think it's earned an actual thank you."

Meen-4 studied him for a long moment, something warm settling behind her usual careful precision. "I believe," she said, "that it would appreciate that considerably more than either of us might expect."
