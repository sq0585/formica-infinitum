# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 524 — The Void Lattice Repair — Status

The figure Dr. Shen Yue had been holding since before Axis-7's arrival finally reached the room in full, and he found, hearing it stated plainly rather than glimpsed sideways on an instrument display, that it took him a genuine moment to let it settle.

"Two hundred years," she said. "Give or take a margin I'd rather not pretend is smaller than it actually is. That's the new completion estimate for the dissolution zone repair, incorporating everything that happened this morning — the combined structure, the residual's resolution, the Lattice's own accelerated self-repair now that it no longer has an active wound actively working against it."

He sat with the number the way he sat with every number that mattered, testing its weight against what he already understood.

Twelve million years, the original estimate had said, months ago. Then, gradually, patiently, across the tending of three hundred and twenty-three volunteers and one permanent anchor, a number that had crept downward without ever losing the essential character of *longer than any single life, or any single civilization, could reasonably expect to witness completed.*

Two hundred years was not that. Two hundred years was a number a person could hold the shape of — not within his own lifetime, not exactly, but within reach of people who might still remember, secondhand, a story their grandparents had told them about the morning the wound finally started closing for good.

*Observation 524,* he wrote. *The dissolution zone repair now completes in approximately two centuries. I do not think I have fully absorbed what that number actually means yet. I suspect I will spend considerable time in the coming months returning to it, the way a person returns, repeatedly, disbelievingly, to a piece of good news too large to process in a single sitting.*

"The Collective's monitoring systems will have registered the shift already," Meen-4 said, her signal carrying the particular formal precision she reserved for observations she considered procedurally significant rather than merely interesting. "A change of this magnitude, in a metric the Surveyor has tracked continuously for forty million years, will not go unnoticed. I would expect a formal response within hours, possibly sooner."

"What kind of response?" Wei Canglan asked, already reaching for the folder, the specific reflexive caution of a man whose entire professional life had been built around anticipating institutional reactions before they arrived.

"I don't know," Meen-4 said, honestly. "There is no precedent for a repair timeline improving this dramatically, this quickly, in the Collective's entire recorded history. I can tell you what I expect based on pattern, but I want to be clear that pattern is all I have to offer. This situation has none."

Lin Mushi felt something settle in him at that — not anxiety exactly, though he recognized the shape anxiety would have taken if the morning had gone differently. Something closer to readiness. He had spent five years learning, one careful threshold at a time, how to meet situations without precedent, and he understood, sitting with the number now fully absorbed into the room's shared awareness, that whatever the Collective's formal response turned out to be, it would arrive at a version of him considerably more prepared to receive it than the person who had once nervously composed eleven lines of notation before a first, uncertain contact.

"Then we'll find out together," he said, "the same way we've found out everything else."

Chen Yufei poured the tea a fourth time, unhurried, and no one in the room seemed inclined to hurry her.

He sat with the two hundred years a while longer after the room's attention had settled elsewhere, letting himself feel the specific, quiet weight of the number properly rather than filing it away alongside everything else the morning had already delivered. Twelve million years had always carried a particular kind of grief inside its scale — not despair exactly, no one had ever framed the work as hopeless, but the specific ache of tending something patiently, faithfully, with the understanding that the tending itself, not the completion, was the entire point available to any single lifetime.

Two hundred years changed that arithmetic in a way he suspected would take him considerably longer than a single morning to fully absorb. People alive today, people he might yet meet, might live to see the wound close. Children not yet born might grow old hearing about it as history rather than as an ongoing, patient labor with no visible end.

*Observation 524, continued,* he wrote. *Three hundred and twenty-three volunteers gave a piece of themselves to a repair they believed, honestly, they would likely never see completed. I think some accounting is owed to them, eventually — not gratitude alone, though gratitude is certainly owed, but the specific, careful truth that what they gave has changed, in a single morning, from an act of faith into something considerably closer to a promise that will actually be kept within reach of a human lifetime. I do not yet know how to tell them that properly. I intend to find out.*

He set the pen down, and let the number settle the rest of the way into him, the particular slow gratitude of a person understanding, gradually, that something enormous had just become considerably more bearable to carry.
