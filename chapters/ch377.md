# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 377 — The Case for Earth

The second session, scheduled a week later, was framed differently — not an accounting this time, Meen-4 explained, but a presentation. A chance for the zone under review to make its own affirmative case, rather than simply answer for what had already happened.

"They're asking you to argue for something," she said, the night before. "Not defend yourself. Argue. There's a difference, and I want you to feel it before you walk in tomorrow. Defense is reactive. This is the first moment you get to actually choose what they see."

He spent that night, and most of the following morning, deciding what belonged in the argument and what didn't. It would have been easy, he thought, to lead with fear — with everything Earth stood to lose, with the twelve hundred lives in the Strata, with his own mother's ordinary mornings that a sterilization order would erase without ceremony. But fear, he suspected, was not evidence a body like this would weigh. It needed something else. Something built, not felt.

He opened with the Veritonal Energy framework — not as a threat, but as a genuine scientific contribution, the kind of thing that expanded understanding rather than merely exploited it. He walked the Surveyor through Liang Jingzhi's research, through Dr. Shen Yue's papers, through three years of a civilization learning to study its own hidden physics with the same rigor it applied to anything else.

He described the Strata City not as a fortress or a weapon but as a cooperative-habitation model — humans and Formicans and the small number of independent cultivators who had chosen to join them, building something none of the old traditions had managed: a shared life, negotiated rather than conquered, imperfect but genuinely collaborative. He did not claim it was utopian. He told the Surveyor, plainly, about the tensions that still existed, the residents who had voted with their feet to leave rather than stay, the arguments over resource allocation that sometimes ran late into the night. *It works,* he said, *not because it is free of conflict, but because the conflict resolves without violence, most of the time, and we keep trying to make that ratio better.*

He described the Pacific Ring's maintained stability, the six years of accumulated tectonic monitoring, the way a structure originally built by a dead civilization had been kept functioning, carefully, by a living one that had learned to respect what it didn't fully understand before trying to improve it.

He described the Under-Web's improved management — quieter now than it had been in his early years, fewer of the emergency stabilizations that had once eaten months of his life, evidence, he argued, that competence could grow without arrogance replacing caution.

And then, last, because it was his strongest card and he wanted it to land with the full weight of everything that had come before it, he described the Veth-Karun analysis.

"I am not going to claim I understand what happened to your predecessor civilization in this zone," he said, choosing every word with the care he'd have given a single stubborn symbol three years earlier. "I don't. Not fully. But I have spent months studying the clinical record of its dissolution — not from curiosity alone, but because the man who gave me the Codex I carry died carrying the weight of that same catastrophe, and I inherited, alongside his knowledge, an obligation to understand what killed him. That analysis is ongoing. It is incomplete. But if there is any possibility that understanding Veth-Karun's dissolution could contribute to actual Void Lattice coherence restoration — even partially, even eventually — then Earth is not merely a risk this review must manage. It may be a resource this review has not yet learned to value."

He stopped there, deliberately, and let the silence hold rather than filling it with more.

The Surveyor's response, when it came, was longer than the previous session's flat acknowledgment, though still without anything Meen-4's translation could render as warmth.

*The Veth-Karun analysis is noted with particular attention. No zone under review in the recorded history of this review body has offered a contribution of this category. This claim will require independent verification before being weighed. The remainder of the submission is received and will be considered alongside the Containment Orthodoxy's counter-position, forthcoming.*

Meen-4 exhaled — an oddly human gesture from her, one he'd rarely seen — after the channel closed.

"That is the first time," she said, "I have heard the Surveyor use the word *particular* about anything."

"Is that good?"

"I don't know yet," she admitted. "But it is not nothing. You have made them curious, Lin Mushi. That is not the same as making them merciful. But in a review process built on precedent, curiosity is the only thing that has ever produced an outcome without one."

He sat with that, the exhaustion of the presentation still heavy in his shoulders, and allowed himself, for the length of a single slow breath, something almost like hope.
