# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Thirty-One — Week Six

Six weeks after the man who would turn out to be named Ren Wuji died at a counter in a convenience store in Wuhou District, Lin Mushi could feel wind direction through walls.

Not metaphorically. Literally: he could stand in his apartment with all the windows closed and detect the direction and rough speed of airflow outside from the pressure differential its Qi signature left in the filament network adjacent to the building's exterior. It was not precise enough to be useful for weather forecasting. It was precise enough to be impossible by conventional sensory standards.

He had also developed, in the past week, the ability to sense the Qi state of small objects by touching them — not deeply, not with anything like the resolution he needed to read the structural properties of a wall, but enough to tell the difference between a rock that had been sitting in one place for decades and one that had been moved recently. Objects accumulated Qi impressions of their habitation. This was the same phenomenon he'd noticed in his mother's kitchen, operating at a smaller scale.

He noted all of this in the observation log. He noted it factually, without excitement, because excitement was the cognitive state most likely to make him sloppy and sloppiness was what he could least afford right now with nine weeks remaining on the timeline.

---

His notebook entries had become dense. Not because he wrote more per entry — he still worked toward concision, valued it, found overlong notes usually indicated unclear thinking. Dense in the sense of cross-referential: every observation now connected to multiple prior observations through shared terminology and developing frameworks, the way a conversation gets dense when everyone involved already knows the shared vocabulary and doesn't need to explain it.

He had started keeping an index.

The index was in the back of notebook nine, which was the current general observation log. It referenced notebooks one through eight and the four dedicated notebooks (notation analysis, spatial survey, energy dynamics, biological substrate). This was, he acknowledged to himself while building it, a somewhat obsessive organizational structure. It was also the only way he could find anything.

The index organized by concept rather than by date. Under *meridian channels*: eight notebook references. Under *Under-Web / Veritonal Energy*: seventeen. Under *Codex notation*: twenty-two. Under *Phase 3 (bilateral exchange)*: three, all from the past week, all noting new developments.

He had opened a second channel.

---

He had done it on Thursday evening, following the Standard Protocol's sequencing exactly, using the breath regulation and the attention exercises and the meridian map he'd spent a week refining. The second channel — associated in the TCM texts with the hand *shaoyin* pathway, running from the heart region down the inner arm to the little finger — had opened after forty minutes of sustained practice.

The experience was specific: a line of warmth tracing from a point below his left ribs, through his chest, down his arm, arriving at his little finger with a clarity that felt almost auditory. Then settled. Then present.

He could now sense electromagnetic fields.

Not strongly, not with precision, not in a way that would allow him to read data off a hard drive. But the change in his phone's signal between one room and another was legible. The cable running through the wall toward the fuse box was detectable. A turned-on laptop had a different ambient quality than a turned-off one.

His documentation of this filled three pages.

He closed the notebook.

His left hand felt different from his right. His right hand felt different from how it had felt at the start of this.

He was becoming, incrementally, something other than what he had been when he started, and the strangeness of this was present but not alarming — less alarming, he noted, than it probably should be.

He opened the next notebook.

He kept going.
