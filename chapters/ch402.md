# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 402 — Wei Canglan's Response

Wei Canglan found him in the pocket-dimension laboratory that afternoon, and Lin Mushi understood, from the particular unhurried way he approached — no rush, no visible relief, just the same careful, deliberate presence he brought to everything — that this conversation had been building in him since the news had spread through the Strata that morning.

"Heard the result," Wei Canglan said, settling onto the bench across from the relay component he'd been slowly rebuilding for weeks, not quite looking at Lin Mushi directly, the way he often approached things that mattered most to him.

"You heard right."

Wei Canglan was quiet for a moment, turning a tool over in his hands without using it, and Lin Mushi waited, recognizing the particular silence that meant a real question was still assembling itself.

"Does this mean you're safe," Wei Canglan said finally.

"For now," Lin Mushi said honestly. "The Collective can revise classification if circumstances change significantly enough. It's not an ironclad guarantee forever. But the sterilization threat specifically — that's gone. Formally, permanently removed, as long as we don't do something drastic enough to trigger a fresh review."

Wei Canglan nodded slowly, absorbing that, and Lin Mushi could see him working through the next question before he asked it, the particular careful construction of someone who'd learned, over years of hard-won honesty between them, to ask the real thing rather than the comfortable version of it.

"Does that mean you're going to get more powerful," Wei Canglan asked. "To protect yourself. Now that the immediate threat is handled — does that just become the next project?"

"Yes," Lin Mushi said, without hesitation, because there was no honest version of the answer that avoided it. "The Orthodoxy doesn't disappear because the vote went in my favor. They reposition, probably. Watch more closely. And there are other things out there I don't understand yet — the off-world installation, whatever's waiting in the Pelagic Archive, the Stellar Scale curriculum I haven't even fully accessed. Getting stronger isn't optional anymore. It's just the shape the rest of my life takes now."

Wei Canglan set the tool down, finally looking at him directly, and something in his expression carried a weight Lin Mushi recognized — not accusation, exactly, but a concern that had been building, quietly, across years of watching Lin Mushi's scale expand faster than anyone around him could fully track.

"Are you going to lose yourself doing it," Wei Canglan asked.

The question landed harder than Lin Mushi expected, and he found himself sitting with it longer than he'd sat with anything Wei Canglan had asked him in months — not because he didn't have an answer, but because the honest answer required more care than a reassuring one would have.

"I don't know," he admitted finally. "I want to tell you no, definitively, and mean it completely. I can't, not honestly. I already lost the thread of myself once, a few weeks ago, when the Colony Link's weight and the Lady Shen situation hit at the same time. Chen Yufei had to hold four hours straight to bring me back. That's not nothing. That's a real cost, and I don't think it's going to be the last time it happens as the scale keeps expanding."

Wei Canglan absorbed that without flinching, which told Lin Mushi, more than anything, that he'd been expecting exactly this kind of honesty and would have distrusted anything easier.

"So what's the plan," Wei Canglan said. "Not for the power. For not losing yourself while you're getting it."

"I'm working on that part," Lin Mushi said quietly, and found, saying it aloud, that it was the truest and least comfortable answer he had — no polished strategy, no confident reassurance, just the ongoing, unfinished work of staying himself while everything around him kept demanding he become something larger.

Wei Canglan was quiet for a long moment, and then, unexpectedly, something close to a small, tired smile crossed his face.

"That's the first time in weeks you've given me an answer that actually sounded honest instead of managed," he said. "I'll take it. Keep working on it. And when you feel yourself slipping again — tell someone before Chen Yufei has to spend four hours dragging you back. Don't wait until it's already happening."

"I'll try," Lin Mushi said.

"Try harder than that," Wei Canglan said, standing, picking the relay component back up with the same unhurried care he brought to everything. "You survived the thing that could have ended all of us today. Don't let the next thing be the version of yourself that survives it."

He left Lin Mushi alone with the weight of that, turning it over long after Wei Canglan had gone, understanding, with a clarity that settled uncomfortably but honestly into place, that the review's ending had not resolved the harder question at all. It had simply, finally, given him room to start actually answering it.
