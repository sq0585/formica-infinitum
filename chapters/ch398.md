# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 398 — The Veth-Karun Presentation

He prepared for the presentation the way he had once prepared for the eleven-minute stare at a single stubborn notation symbol — methodically, without rushing, trusting that thoroughness would matter more than speed.

It took two weeks to finalize, working alongside Meen-4 and, in the final stretch, Dr. Shen Yue, whose scientific rigor proved essential to structuring six hours of dense Formican notation into something that would hold together across its full length without losing the audience it needed to persuade. Liang Jingzhi contributed the geological framing — how the Veth-Karun dissolution's residual effects had shaped Earth's own subsurface structures over forty million years, a physical record as legible, in its own way, as the clinical documentation itself.

He did not translate the symbols. That boundary held throughout every stage of preparation, unquestioned, though he found himself, more than once, staring at the three untranslated marks at the center of the analysis and wondering, briefly, what they might mean, before setting the wondering aside and returning to the work he could actually complete honestly.

The presentation itself was delivered live, through the Witness Node, to the Collective's full membership — all two hundred and some member civilizations, simultaneously, in a broadcast format Meen-4 assured him was itself almost unprecedented at this stage of a review.

Six hours, in Formican notation, structured the way Meen-4 had taught him structure mattered most: observation first, then comparison, then hypothesis, then the honest limits of what remained unproven. He walked the assembled Collective through the clinical facts of Veth-Karun's collapse — the specific failure signatures, the Void Lattice coherence disruption, the pattern that connected the ancient catastrophe to Earth's own geological anomalies across forty million years of slow bleed. He presented Ren Wuji's original research, careful to credit the man whose life's work this analysis continued, and felt, delivering that section, something that was not quite grief anymore, worn gentle by three years into something closer to companionship across time.

He built, piece by piece, toward the conclusion — not a triumphant discovery, but a careful, evidence-supported claim he had spent months earning the right to state plainly.

*The dissolution was not inevitable to Formless Scale,* he said, in notation Meen-4 had helped him refine until every symbol carried exactly the weight he intended. *It was the consequence of reaching that threshold without full understanding of what it required. This is not conjecture. It is the pattern the clinical record consistently supports, once its data is organized without the assumption that catastrophe was simply the price of power. I do not yet have the complete mechanism for coherence restoration. I am not claiming to. But I am claiming that the failure was not fate. It was a specific, identifiable gap between capability and understanding — and that gap is one this analysis suggests can, eventually, be closed.*

He held the silence that followed longer than any silence he'd faced in the entire review, aware that somewhere across an unimaginable distance, two hundred separate civilizations were weighing six hours of careful evidence against forty million years of accumulated caution.

The first response arrived from a member civilization he had no prior contact with, its designation unfamiliar, its notation carrying a formal register distinct from the Surveyor's own. Meen-4 translated it slowly, her voice carrying something careful and hopeful in equal measure.

*Request for full data access, pending review body approval. This analysis, if verified, represents the first credible advance toward Lattice-restoration theory this civilization has encountered in recorded memory.*

A second followed. Then a third. Not universal agreement — several member civilizations registered formal skepticism, questions about methodology, requests for clarification he answered as honestly as he could manage in the hours that followed. But the overall shape of the response, gathering across the following day as more civilizations weighed in, was unmistakable.

"They're taking it seriously," he said to Meen-4, once the initial wave of responses had settled into something he could actually parse.

"They are taking it very seriously," she said. "More seriously, I believe, than any single piece of evidence has ever been weighed in a review of this category. You did not merely defend Earth today, Lin Mushi. You offered something this body did not know it still needed, forty million years after it stopped expecting to find it."

He sat with the weight of that, exhausted in the particular way that came from having given everything he had to a single sustained effort, and felt, underneath the exhaustion, something he recognized, cautiously, as the beginning of something he had not let himself fully believe in until this exact moment.

Hope, not as a feeling he was managing carefully. Hope as a fact the day's work had, finally, earned.
