### ARC IV — THE ARCHITECT'S WORK

## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty-Six — Dimensional Weaver — Mature Stage

Lin Mushi noticed the change the way he noticed most changes now — not in the moment it happened, but three days later, doing something unrelated, when the absence of effort became conspicuous by comparison to how much effort used to be required.

He was carrying a crate of reclaimed stone through the Strata's east corridor, thinking about lunch, when he passed through the boundary of a pocket dimension he'd opened that morning to store surplus building material. He didn't slow down. He didn't feel the familiar drag of attention the crossing used to demand — the small mental hand reaching out to hold the fold steady while he walked through it. The fold held itself. It had been holding itself, he realized, for at least a week, maybe longer, while he'd been too occupied with everything else to notice that one of his hands had quietly stopped needing to be used.

He set the crate down and stood still for a moment, running a private accounting the way he'd trained himself to since the beginning: count what's active, check what it's costing.

Eight.

He counted them again to be sure. Eight simultaneous pocket dimensions, open and stable across the Strata's expanding footprint — the material storage fold, two active construction folds where new corridors were still knitting themselves out of reclaimed stone, the small archive annex Meen-4 had asked him to maintain, three residential test spaces he'd built and then left standing to see whether they held, and the training hall Wei Canglan used most evenings. All of them open. None of them costing him anything he could feel as cost.

Six months ago, three had been his ceiling before the strain became a headache he'd learn to recognize by early afternoon.

"You've stopped," Chen Yufei said, coming around the corridor bend with a data slate under one arm, the Iron Meridian ease she carried into everything now making the observation sound less like a question than a simple report of fact.

"Counting," he said.

"Counting what?"

"How many pockets I'm holding open right now." He gestured at the corridor generally, at the walls that weren't quite walls, the stone that remembered being reclaimed rubble a season ago and had since forgotten. "Eight."

She raised an eyebrow, unimpressed in the specific way she'd become unimpressible by things that would have staggered her eighteen months earlier. "Is that a lot?"

"It's more than I was doing consciously two months ago. I'm not tracking these anymore. They're just — there. Background." He turned his attention inward, toward the place where the Chitin Seed's growth registered as something closer to felt architecture than active thought, and found the eight folds sitting in his awareness the way his own hands sat in it — present, available, requiring nothing until called on.

"That's the maturity Meen-4 kept talking about," Chen Yufei said. "The thing where the technique stops being a skill you perform and starts being a structure you inhabit."

"I think so." He picked the crate back up, testing the weight of the observation against the ordinary weight of the stone. "It means I can build more than I used to be able to hold."

"How much more?"

He thought about it honestly, running the numbers the careful way he'd learned to, rather than reaching for the satisfying answer. "I don't actually know yet. That's the part I want to find out."

---

He spent the following two days finding out, and the answer, when it arrived, came less as a dramatic breakthrough than as a long, quiet afternoon of testing that ended with him standing in a corridor he hadn't fully believed he could build until he'd built it.

The old Strata — the version he'd inherited, the version Ren Wuji had left behind — had been built for one purpose and one occupant: an archive, folded carefully around itself, its rooms shaped to hold texts and Meen-4's careful centuries of watching. Every expansion since had been repair work, patching collapsed sections, restoring what erosion and time and one catastrophic battle had taken from it.

What he built now wasn't repair. It was addition — new corridors branching outward from the Strata's stabilized core into space that had never existed before, shaped not around storage but around the specific, unglamorous requirements of people who needed somewhere to sleep.

He worked from a simple brief he'd sketched the night before, sitting at his mother's kitchen table with a cup of tea going cold beside him: rooms with doors that closed properly. Windows, even if the light behind them was manufactured rather than real, because something in him understood that a room without a window read as a cell no matter how comfortable everything else about it was. Floors that held warmth. Walls thick enough that sound didn't carry between them.

The Chitin Seed's growth made the actual construction almost meditative — stone folding into shape at the edge of his attention while the more conscious part of his mind handled the details that mattered, the human details, the ones no amount of cultivation mastery would have told him to prioritize on its own.

By the second evening, he had four finished rooms, small and plain and entirely livable, arranged along a corridor with actual daylight-equivalent light filtering through windows that opened onto nothing but held, convincingly, the impression of somewhere.

He stood at the corridor's threshold, looking at what he'd made, and felt something settle in his chest that he recognized, after a moment, as pride — not in the technique, which had simply matured the way techniques mature, given enough time and enough use, but in the specific, deliberate choice he'd made about what to build with it.

Two years ago, this same maturity would have gone toward defense. Reinforcement. Structures built to survive an attack.

Tonight it had gone toward windows.

He found that difference worth sitting with for a while before he went to find Meen-4 and ask her whether the residential wing was something the Strata's older sections would tolerate having beside them.
