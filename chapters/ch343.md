# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Three — Meen-4 as Teacher

The first session began the following morning, Meen-4 and Lin Mushi alone in the Deep Archive's quiet chamber, a stack of governance records surfaced and waiting the way the Veth-Karun files had once waited, months earlier.

"Where do we start?" he asked.

*With structure,* she signaled. *You cannot understand what the Collective values without first understanding how it is organized, because the two are inseparable. A governing body's structure reveals its priorities as clearly as its stated laws do, and sometimes more honestly.*

She walked him through it methodically over the following hours — the Collective's federated structure, member civilizations retaining significant autonomy over internal affairs while ceding specific, narrow authority over matters the Collective judged to require coordinated oversight. Quarantine classification. Interstellar resource conflicts. The prevention, wherever possible, of exactly the kind of uncontrolled Formless Scale event that had cost Earth its unrestricted status forty million years ago.

"It sounds almost reasonable," he said, several hours in, surprised by his own reaction. "I think I expected something more alien. More difficult to relate to."

*Why did you expect that?*

"I don't know. Maybe because everything I've learned about the Collective so far has come wrapped in warnings — Veth-Karun, the Dissolution Path, the review clock. It's easy to imagine something purely threatening, when all you've heard is the danger it represents."

*That is a reasonable assumption to have carried, and I am glad the actual structure is correcting it,* Meen-4 signaled. *The Collective is not, as far as my understanding extends, malicious. It is cautious, and its caution is proportional to genuine risk — worlds capable of producing Veth-Karun-scale events are, by any reasonable governing standard, worth watching closely. I do not believe caution and hostility are the same thing, though I understand why living under one might feel, from the inside, difficult to distinguish from the other.*

---

He found the teaching considerably more demanding than he'd anticipated, the six sessions across the following two weeks each requiring him to hold concepts that resisted easy translation into anything his own world's political frameworks had prepared him for — governance structures that operated across timescales and distances no human institution had ever needed to reckon with, precedent drawn from case histories spanning millions of years and dozens of civilizations, each one a data point in a body of practice considerably older than his own species' entire recorded history.

"You're an extraordinary teacher," he told her, midway through the fourth session, meaning it plainly rather than as simple flattery.

*I have had considerable practice,* she signaled, something in the cadence carrying a warmth he'd learned, across their long acquaintance, to recognize even through her careful, measured signals. *Four hundred years of teaching cultivation technique to a series of human carriers. This is different material, but the underlying discipline — breaking a vast, unfamiliar structure into pieces a single mind can absorb one careful step at a time — is not so different from what I have always done.*

"Did you expect to be teaching this, when you first took on the role of Keeper?"

*No,* she admitted. *I expected, when I first accepted this posting, to be a caretaker for records and technique, not a bridge between an entire world and the body governing its fate. I have found, across four hundred years, that expectation and outcome rarely align as cleanly as either of us might prefer.*

---

By the sixth session, he found himself holding the framework with a facility that surprised him — not full mastery, the material's genuine complexity resisted anything so complete after only two weeks, but a working comprehension solid enough that he could, for the first time, imagine constructing an actual argument within its terms rather than simply absorbing its shape.

"I think I understand enough now to start building the actual case," he said, reviewing his accumulated notes with the same careful satisfaction he'd once felt completing the residential wing's first finished rooms.

*I believe you do,* Meen-4 confirmed. *I would caution you against overconfidence — six sessions has given you a functional foundation, not deep expertise. But a functional foundation, applied carefully and honestly, is considerably more valuable than deep expertise applied without genuine understanding of what actually matters to the audience you are addressing.*

He nodded, gathering his notes, and found, walking back toward the residential wing that evening, that the weight of the eighteen-month clock felt, for the first time since Meen-4 had first named it, less like an impending catastrophe and more like a genuinely achievable task — vast, demanding, but no longer entirely shapeless, thanks to two weeks of careful, patient teaching from someone who had, once again, proven exactly why he trusted her more completely than almost anyone else in this impossible world.
