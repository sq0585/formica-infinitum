# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty-Four — What This Changes

He didn't see Axis-7 again for the rest of that day, and found himself, that evening, uncertain whether he'd said too much or exactly enough, the specific discomfort of not knowing sitting alongside the larger, still unresolved weight of everything he'd learned that week.

He raised it with Meen-4 the following morning, wanting her perspective on whether he'd handled the conversation as carefully as it deserved.

"I told her what she did couldn't be undone," he said. "But that what she's doing now could be part of what she is. I don't know if that was the right thing to say, or if it was too easy, given what she actually carried."

*I do not believe there is a fully correct thing to say to someone carrying what she carries,* Meen-4 signaled. *I believe you said something honest, which is generally the closest approximation to correct that exists for situations this heavy.*

"She didn't respond. I don't know if it landed."

*Axis-7 rarely responds immediately to things that land deeply,* Meen-4 signaled. *I would not read her silence as rejection. I would read it as exactly what it likely was — the beginning of a process that will take her considerably longer than a single conversation to complete.*

---

He found out how right Meen-4 was the following day, when Fang Ruxue mentioned, in passing, during the history project's third session, that a new participant had joined without formal announcement.

He looked up from the shared table and found Axis-7 seated among the group, unremarkable, unannounced, working through a fragment of translated record alongside two human researchers with the same careful, patient attention she brought to everything she chose to engage with.

He didn't say anything. He understood, watching her, that drawing attention to her presence would undercut whatever quiet, deliberate choice had brought her here, and that the moment deserved, instead, to simply be allowed to happen without ceremony.

She stayed for the entire session, contributing carefully, precisely, her insight into the Isolationist Council's decision-making process offering the group a perspective on the historical record that none of them could have accessed any other way — not defensive, not minimizing what had happened, simply accurate, the specific honesty of someone who had decided, finally, to stop protecting herself from her own history by refusing to examine it.

---

He caught her alone at the session's end, the shared table slowly emptying around them.

"You joined the project," he said.

*Yes.*

"I didn't expect that so quickly."

*I did not expect it either,* she signaled. *I considered your words carefully, over the past two days. I concluded that continuing to avoid this history, the way I have avoided it for three thousand years, was no longer a choice I could justify making, given everything else I have watched you choose to face directly instead of avoiding.*

"That's not a small thing to have decided."

*No,* she agreed. *It is not. I do not expect it to be comfortable, continuing this project's work. I expect, in fact, that it will become considerably less comfortable before it becomes more so, as the group's research inevitably arrives at the specific details of what I helped execute.*

"You don't have to do this alone."

*I know,* she signaled, something in the cadence carrying a warmth he'd rarely heard from her directly. *That is, I believe, precisely the point. I have done this alone for three thousand years. I find I no longer wish to.*

---

He walked back toward the residential wing that evening with the specific, quiet satisfaction of having watched something genuinely shift — not resolved, not healed in any complete sense, but moving, deliberately, in a direction it hadn't been moving in for three thousand years.

He thought, too, of Ren Wuji, of four hundred years of careful, patient teaching built on grief he'd never once mentioned directly, and understood, with a clarity that felt both sad and steadying, that this world was full of people carrying old, heavy things quietly, and that the smallest, most significant work he could do, sometimes, was simply to notice when someone was finally ready to stop carrying something entirely alone.

He wrote a brief entry that night, the words simple.

> *Axis-7 joined the history project. Unannounced, unceremonious, exactly the way the most significant things in this world tend to happen. What you did cannot be undone. What you're doing now can be part of what you are. I said it to her two days ago, not fully certain it would land. I think, watching her today, it finally did.*
