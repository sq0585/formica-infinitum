# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eighty-Three — The Agreement's Terms

He had negotiated the broad terms at the tea house. The details took longer.

They worked them out across two additional meetings, which was appropriate — broad agreements made in the immediate wake of a crisis tended toward the convenient rather than the sustainable, and both of them knew enough to want sustainable. The details that mattered:

*Access to documentation.* He would share interpretations and translated extracts, not raw Codex files. He had three reasons for this, which he stated plainly: he didn't yet know what was sensitive in the later documents; some content was calibrated to Chitin Seed integration and could be actively harmful to a reader without the appropriate preparation; and control of the original files was the only leverage he had if the institutional situation deteriorated.

She accepted this, with one caveat: if he determined something was safe to share in its original form, he would share it, not simply the interpretation.

"Yes," he said.

*Prior notice before public release.* If he planned to release translated Codex material beyond the two of them — to other practitioners, to academic channels, to anyone — he would tell her first. Not for veto authority. For preparation time.

"How much notice?" she asked.

"Enough for you to decide what you want to do with the information before it becomes public. That's different amounts of time depending on what the information is."

She accepted this.

*The honest accounting, in both directions.* This was the term that took the longest to establish the shape of, because honesty between people with different institutional affiliations was not a simple thing. She had a reporting obligation to the Iron Meridian Court. He had no reporting obligation to anyone. These were different positions.

They arrived at: she would tell him what she knew about the Courts' methods, resources, and plans. She would not misrepresent the Courts to him. She would tell him when her institutional obligation required her to share information about him, before she shared it, so he had time to respond.

"You're asking me to tell you when I'm going to report on you," she said.

"I'm asking you not to report on me without my knowledge. Which is different."

"The Court will eventually ask directly."

"I know. When they do, I want to know the question before they get the answer."

She looked at him with the expression she was developing for moments when he said something that revealed a different understanding of the situation than she'd expected. Not surprise exactly. Recognition.

"You negotiated this before I arrived," she said.

"I prepared for it." He did not pretend otherwise.

"You're twenty-two."

"Yes."

"You're twenty-two and you're negotiating institutional information protocols with a trained cultivator who has been operating in a Court structure for nine years."

"I have notebooks," he said.

It was the second time he'd said this to her, and this time she did laugh — properly, briefly, the sound of genuine amusement breaking through a professional posture.

"All right," she said. "Advance notice before reporting. Agreed."

---

He walked home afterward and wrote the agreement summary in a dedicated notebook — a new one, labeled *Alliances and Protocols*, which made him feel slightly like someone playing an elaborate game of governance. But the record was real and the need for it was real and the feeling of slight absurdity was just what it felt like to be building something from scratch without a template.

He wrote:

> *Alliance agreement, Chen Yufei (Iron Meridian Court, independent operational status). Effective: this week.*
>
> *Terms summary: access to Codex interpretations (not originals); advance notice before public release; mutual honesty about relevant knowledge and plans; advance notice before she reports to her Court specifically about me.*
>
> *What she brings: institutional knowledge of five Courts, operational history in Chengdu, cultivation experience (9 years, Iron Meridian framework now being corrected), social network within cultivation world I do not have.*
>
> *What I bring: Codex access, Strata access, Formican documentation, a cultivation framework that is complete rather than partial.*
>
> *Balance of leverage: she has more institutional resources. I have more information. This is unstable in the long term. Stability will require one of two things: the institutional resources becoming less relevant to her, or the information gap closing enough that the alliance is built on something other than asymmetric access.*
>
> *Working toward the second option.*

He noted: *she negotiates like someone who knows her leverage.*

He added: *so do I, apparently.*

He was not sure when this had happened. He thought it might have been the months of geological consulting — eleven contracts now, each one a negotiation between what he could perceive and what the client needed to hear. He had learned, through those contracts, that the structure of an information exchange mattered as much as the information.

He put the notebook away.

He had an alliance, a timeline of about a week, and a library in the Strata that he had only begun to map.

He opened the Compression training notebook.

There was work to do before the backup arrived.
