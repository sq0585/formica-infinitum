# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixteen — Chen Yufei, Fully Healed

The last lingering stiffness in Chen Yufei's left shoulder disappeared sometime during the third week of the maintenance rotation, though she didn't mention it until she'd already confirmed, on her own, that it wasn't coming back.

"I want to test something," she said, finding him in the training space adjacent to the outer hall, a section he'd cleared months ago for exactly this kind of work. "Watch the readout."

She settled into a stance he recognized, the careful entry posture for a controlled Compression attempt, and he watched the Circuit network's portable reader rather than her directly, the way she preferred for these tests — fewer variables, fewer unconscious adjustments made in response to his expression.

Her Qi signature deepened steadily, the familiar climb through the ratios he'd watched her work toward for months. Ratio 1. Ratio 2, stable. Ratio 3, holding longer than her previous best.

Then it kept climbing.

He watched the number on the reader pass her established ceiling and continue, slower now but unmistakably still rising, until it settled at a value he had to check twice against her historical baseline to be certain he wasn't misreading the display.

Compression Ratio 5.

She opened her eyes at the same moment he looked up from the reader, and the expression on her face told him she'd felt the number arrive before she'd had time to process what it meant.

"That's not possible," she said. "I was at four, barely stable, three weeks ago."

"You're not at four anymore." He turned the reader toward her so she could see the number herself. "You're at five. Stable, by the look of it — that wasn't a brief spike, that was you holding it."

She stared at the display for a long moment, and he watched her run through the same calculation he'd already started running himself.

"The injury," she said slowly. "The channel disruption. When it healed —"

"It didn't heal back to where it was before. It healed better." He thought through the mechanics as he said it, the Archive's notation on regenerative cultivation surfacing in fragments he was only now connecting properly. "Disrupted channels that repair under active cultivation sometimes rebuild along more efficient pathways than the original structure had. You weren't just recovering. You were rebuilding from a more refined blueprint, channel by channel, every time you trained through the recovery instead of waiting it out."

"I trained through it because sitting still made me feel useless," she said. "Not because I was trying to engineer a breakthrough."

"The mechanism doesn't care about the motive. It only cares about the repeated stress and repair cycle." He looked at her, the startled, careful expression she wore now, so different from the controlled composure she usually carried. "You're ahead of where you'd have been without the injury. Months ahead, possibly more."

She sat down slowly on the training space's low bench, the reader still glowing softly in his hand, and was quiet for long enough that he didn't interrupt it.

"I spent weeks angry about that injury," she said finally. "Angry at Lady Shen, angry at the politics that put me in the way of it, angry at myself for not seeing the attack coming in time. And it turns out it gave me something I might not have reached for years otherwise."

"That doesn't make the anger wrong," he said. "Both things can be true. It was a real injury, and it cost you something real, and it also produced a result you wouldn't have chosen that path to reach deliberately."

"That's an uncomfortable kind of true."

"Most of the useful ones are."

She looked up at him, something settling in her expression that he recognized as the particular mixture of unease and quiet pride people carried when a cost they hadn't asked for turned out to have purchased something they wouldn't give back even if they could.

"Ratio 5," she said again, testing the number out loud as though saying it might make it more real. "I should test the new ceiling properly. Carefully, this time, not by accident in the middle of demonstrating something to you."

"I'll watch the reader," he said. "Same as always."

She nodded, settled back into her stance, and reached, with deliberate care this time, for whatever new room had opened up inside her since the last time she'd checked.
