# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 470 — The Letter to Ren Wuji

He returned to the translation after a long moment, understanding, before he'd read another word, that the account's remaining lines were going to ask something of him he wasn't certain he was fully prepared to give.

*I do not know if you are still alive to receive this,* the account continued, the register shifting once more, quieter now, the specific, careful vulnerability of someone who had already said the hardest part and had only the smaller, more personal things left. *You were, when I last held any confirmed knowledge of you, considerably further along in your own years than I was in mine. I have no way of knowing, across the distance and the time this transmission will require to arrive anywhere at all, whether you are still there to read it.*

*I am sending it anyway. I have learned, in the last 0.7 seconds of my existence, that some things deserve to be sent regardless of confirmed arrival. You taught me that, actually, though you likely never said it in those exact words. You taught it through everything you did — every letter you sent to students you weren't certain would ever write back, every lesson you offered without demanding proof it had landed. I understand it now, far too late to have learned it from you directly. I am learning it instead from the specific shape of my own ending.*

Lin Mushi sat with the passage a long while, the chamber's cold stillness pressing in around him, and found, reading it, that he could not separate the words on the page from a different letter entirely — the one folded into a dying man's hand, five years and an entire impossible life ago, in a convenience store neither Veth-Karun nor whoever this ancient teacher had been could ever have known existed.

*I hope you are curious,* Ren Wuji's own message had said. *Everything depends on it.*

He had never gotten to ask Ren Wuji whether he'd known, writing those words, whether his own student would live long enough to read them. He had never gotten to hold Ren Wuji's hand and tell him what he'd become, the way Fang Ruxue or Chen Yufei or his own mother had all, eventually, gotten to hear it from him directly. Ren Wuji had died before Lin Mushi had understood enough to say anything back at all — a letter, in its own way, sent without any confirmation it would ever be answered.

He did not know, reading Veth-Karun's account, whether the teacher it addressed had been named Ren Wuji, or whether the resonance he felt was simply his own grief finding its shape in someone else's forty-million-year-old ending. The account itself offered no name — only the same intimate, informal address it had opened with, teacher and nothing more, the specific privacy of a relationship that had never needed a fuller title between the two people who'd actually lived inside it.

But the resonance did not require an answer to matter. He understood that, sitting with it, the same way he'd understood everything else this whole extraordinary life had eventually taught him: some connections were true without being literal, some echoes mattered without needing to be traced back to an unbroken, provable line.

*I should have held someone,* the account said again, closing the thought the way it had opened it, the sentence's full weight only now, finally, entirely landing.

He read the final lines slowly, deliberately, understanding they were the last words Veth-Karun had ever composed.

*Whoever you are, reading this — teacher, if some impossible chance has kept you alive to receive it, or a stranger considerably later, if it has not — I ask only this. Do not do what I did. Do not prepare alone. Do not mistake accumulated knowledge for adequate preparation. Hold someone, before you attempt what I attempted. I did not, and I am dissolving now, and the last complete thought I will ever have is the specific, unbearable knowledge of exactly what I should have done differently.*

*I should have held someone.*

The account ended there, mid-thought, the transmission's final 0.7 seconds apparently exhausted before whatever remained of Veth-Karun could write anything further.

Lin Mushi sat in the ancient chamber's cold, patient silence, the translation complete, and did not move for a long while.
