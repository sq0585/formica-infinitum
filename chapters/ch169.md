# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixty-Nine — Wei Canglan, Introduction

The message arrived through Chen Yufei's network on a Wednesday, forwarded to Lin Mushi with a brief note: *This one is different from the others. Read carefully.*

*My name is Wei Canglan. I am a third-rank disciple of the Northern Peak Court, twenty-two years old. My family has been in the sect for three generations — my grandfather, my father, and now myself, all trained under the Court's atmospheric cultivation framework.*

*I have read the document twice. I am not writing to argue with it or to praise it. I am writing because I have access to a scroll in my family's private collection — passed down through three generations, predating the Court's formal founding records — that contains what we have always understood to be an early, possibly corrupted version of our foundational technique. I have never been permitted to study it seriously; my grandfather always said it was "incomplete and potentially dangerous," kept only as a historical curiosity.*

*Your document's description of the Northern Peak's technique derivation includes details that, if accurate, should be verifiable against this specific scroll — details about a particular notation sequence that the Court's official teaching has simplified away. I would like to compare them. I do not have the expertise to do this myself with confidence. I am asking whether you would be willing to look at images of the relevant passages, if I can photograph them without drawing attention.*

*I want to be direct about why I'm asking. If you're wrong, I want to know that clearly, so I can stop being troubled by questions I've had for years and return to trusting what I was taught. If you're right, I want to know that too, even though I suspect it will cost me more than I currently understand.*

He read this twice.

He thought about what distinguished this message from the dozens of others he'd received — not its tone exactly, which was calm and precise, but its method. Wei Canglan wasn't asking to be persuaded or reassured. He was proposing a specific, falsifiable test: a verification against material the document's author couldn't have known existed, that would either confirm or disconfirm the claims independent of trust in either party.

It was, he recognized, exactly the kind of approach he would have taken himself, in Wei Canglan's position.

He wrote back: *I would be glad to look at the images, with the understanding that I'll tell you honestly what I find, whichever direction that points. Please be careful about how you photograph and transmit them — I don't know your Court's internal security posture and I'd rather you not take unnecessary risks for a verification exercise.*

Wei Canglan's reply came within the hour: *Understood. I'll be careful. Thank you for taking this seriously rather than simply assuming I'd agree with you because I reached out.*

That last line stayed with Lin Mushi for the rest of the day.

He showed the exchange to Chen Yufei that evening.

"He's testing you as much as he's testing the document," she said, reading it.

"I know. I think that's correct of him."

"Most people who've reached out have already decided what they believe, one way or another, and are looking for confirmation. He hasn't decided yet. He's actually trying to find out."

"That's rarer than it should be," Lin Mushi said.

"It's rarer than it should be everywhere," Chen Yufei said. "Not just in this."

The photographs arrived two days later — carefully framed, well-lit, the notation visible in enough detail to work with. Lin Mushi spent three hours with them, cross-referencing against the Codex's primary source material, checking the specific notation sequence Wei Canglan had flagged against what he knew of the Northern Peak's official, simplified teaching.

He found what he expected to find.

He wrote his answer carefully, with full documentation, the same precision he'd brought to everything since the first document four months ago.

He sent it.

He did not know yet what it would cost Wei Canglan to receive it.

He would learn soon enough.
