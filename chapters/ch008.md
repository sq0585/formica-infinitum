# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eight — The Drain

The phone was not supposed to work the way it was working.

Lin Mushi noticed this on the eleventh night, at 1:30 AM, when he checked his battery out of habit and found it at 63%.

He had started the shift at ten with 61%.

He had been using the phone. He had opened the Codex twice, checked his messages, read half an article about collective decision-making in social insects, and used the flashlight function for thirty seconds when a bulb blew out in the back corridor. By normal metrics, he should have been somewhere around 35%.

He stared at the number.

The phone was not charging. The charger was at home. He was looking at a battery percentage that had, over the course of three and a half hours of moderate use, increased by two percent.

He set the phone face-up on the counter and watched it for a while.

The Codex icon, faint and hexagonal, did not do anything visible. The percentage ticked down to 62 while he watched, in the normal way, from normal use. Then it held at 62 for nine minutes before ticking down to 61.

He wrote:

> _11th night. Battery anomaly. Started shift at 61%, now at 63% (1:30 AM), despite regular use. No charging. Battery now at 61% and behaving normally — rate of decrease standard._
>
> _Duration of anomaly: approximately 2.5 hours (10 PM to ~12:30 AM estimate). During this period, net battery increase: 2%. Should have decreased ~12%._
>
> _Hypothesis: the Codex is drawing power from an external source. When it has sufficient external supply, it draws from that and supplements the phone battery. When the external supply diminishes, it reverts to normal drain._
>
> _Question: what is the external source? And what determines when it's available?_

He put the pen down and looked at the floor.

North and slightly down.

The depth sensation was there as always, a consistent presence now, something he'd stopped being surprised by and started treating as information. Tonight it felt slightly — thinner. Not absent. The heading was the same. But the quality had changed the way a radio signal changes when something comes between you and the tower.

He noted this. He noted the time. He sat with it for an hour, checking the battery at ten-minute intervals, watching the decrease stabilize into something ordinary.

At 3 AM, a thought arrived fully formed, the way thoughts sometimes did when he'd been turning something over long enough that the conclusion finally surfaced on its own:

_The phone isn't just connected to whatever is beneath the store. It's been drawing from it._

And if it was drawing from it — using it, consuming it, in whatever way energy could be consumed — then the thing beneath the store was not infinite. It was a resource. It could deplete.

He sat with this for another hour.

He did not know whether that was a problem yet. He did not know the rate, or the total quantity, or what depletion would mean. He had nothing but a two-percent battery anomaly and a gut sense that direction and magnitude were not the same thing.

He wrote one more note before his shift ended:

> _Rule: do not treat 'external source' as guaranteed. Monitor._

He walked home in the early light, hands in his pockets, the city assembling around him in its usual indifferent way, and thought about what it meant to be borrowing from something without knowing you were borrowing from it.

The depth sensation had been there since the ninth day.

He had assumed it was merely perception.

He was revising that.
