# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Eleven — The Comprehension Threshold

The six weeks ended not with a dramatic final lesson, but with an ordinary afternoon at the fault system's northernmost survey point, Liang Jingzhi packing away her instruments while he sat with his eyes closed against the rock face, running one final, careful extension of his sensing across the accumulated understanding the weeks had built.

He felt it differently than he had at the beginning. The rock no longer read as inert, faintly humming static. It read, now, as something closer to breathing — slow beyond any human measure, but unmistakably, patiently alive with process, layer folding into layer across timescales he'd finally learned to hold without his attention sliding away from the scale.

"I think I actually understand it now," he said, opening his eyes. "Not just sense it. Understand what I'm sensing."

"That's the difference Meen-4 was describing, I think," Liang Jingzhi said, closing her field case with the satisfied efficiency of someone finishing a project she'd genuinely enjoyed. "Sensing tells you something's there. Understanding tells you why it moves the way it moves, and what it will likely do next. You've built both, over these six weeks."

They returned to the Strata that evening, and he went, almost immediately, to find Meen-4, the accumulated weight of six weeks' patient work sitting in his chest with a specific readiness he hadn't felt before an attempt in some time.

*You have changed,* she signaled, before he'd said anything at all, her attention settling on him with unusual intensity. *I can perceive it even without direct testing. The quality of your presence has shifted.*

"I think the final prerequisite is met."

*I believe you may be correct.* She was quiet for a moment, the particular stillness he'd learned to read as careful consideration rather than hesitation. *I would like to confirm this properly, rather than proceeding on impression alone.*

---

The confirmation took the form of a single, careful test — Meen-4 guiding him through a structured extension of his Colony Link toward a section of bedrock beneath the Strata itself, asking him not simply to sense it, but to describe, in detail, what he perceived about its history and its likely future behavior.

He worked through it slowly, drawing on six weeks of Liang Jingzhi's patient teaching, describing pressure accumulations he could feel building across intervals of tens of thousands of years, fault relationships that would, he suspected, eventually shift in ways the region's human engineers had likely never fully accounted for.

*Accurate,* Meen-4 signaled, when he finished, cross-referencing his description against records considerably older than anything Liang Jingzhi's instruments could access. *Precisely accurate, in fact, against Archive records spanning considerably longer than your description could have been guessing at. The comprehension is genuine.*

He felt something settle in his chest at the confirmation — not triumph, exactly, but the specific, grounded satisfaction of six weeks of patient, unglamorous work finally proving itself real.

*The final prerequisite is met,* Meen-4 confirmed. *I do not know precisely what happens next, or how quickly. The Chitin Seed's own response to comprehension thresholds has, in the past, varied considerably.*

---

The response arrived that same night, while he slept.

He didn't wake gradually. He simply became aware, sometime past what his internal clock suggested was midnight, that something inside him had shifted into a state he didn't immediately recognize — not sleep, not waking, but something adjacent to both, a stillness settling over the Chitin Seed's presence in his awareness that felt, unmistakably, deliberate.

He tried to reach for it the way he might reach for any other cultivation process, and found nothing to reach toward. No resistance. No activity. Simply an absence, patient and total, the Seed's usual quiet hum of ongoing growth gone entirely silent.

He understood, lying in the dark, what was happening even before he had language for it — the longest dormancy he'd experienced since this entire impossible journey had begun, the Chitin Seed withdrawing fully into whatever internal process the comprehension threshold had triggered, asking nothing of him now except the one thing he'd learned, across two years, was often hardest to give freely.

Rest.

He let his eyes close, the dormancy's stillness settling over him like a weight he didn't need to fight, and slept, for the first time in longer than he could easily remember, without any part of his awareness reaching outward toward anything at all.
