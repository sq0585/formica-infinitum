# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 529 — Three Months, Lived

He had expected, in the first days after Formless Scale settled into something like permanence, that the sheer scale of it would eventually announce itself again — some morning, some unguarded moment, when the enormity of everywhere would reassert its claim on him the way novelty always eventually reasserted itself on anything a person hadn't yet fully lived inside.

It never quite did. Three months in, what remained instead was something considerably quieter, and considerably harder to describe in a way that would sound, to anyone who hadn't lived it, like anything remarkable at all.

He woke most mornings the way he always had, the ordinary grey light of a Chengdu dawn finding him before he'd bothered to check a single connection. He made tea. He ate breakfast — properly, the way his mother had once instructed him, an instruction he had never once stopped honoring even at scales she had no framework to imagine. He ran the daily Anchor check, a ritual so worn into the shape of his mornings now that it had become, in every meaningful sense, indistinguishable from brushing his teeth: five confirmed, three hundred and twenty-three confirmed, the Chitin Circuit network humming its ordinary hum, the Strata's residents going about an ordinary Tuesday with no particular awareness that anything about the morning was extraordinary.

*Observation 529,* he wrote, the entry unhurried, the specific quiet satisfaction of a person recording something not because it demanded urgent attention but because the recording itself had become a comfort. *Coherence has held stable at 0.99 for six consecutive weeks. Dr. Shen Yue tells me this is, by every measure her framework can offer, as close to a settled baseline as the instrument is capable of describing. I believe her. I no longer feel the discipline as effort. It has become, the way every prior threshold eventually became, simply the shape of being awake.*

Sunday calls continued, unchanged in every particular that mattered. His mother asked about his eating — fine — his sleeping — adequate — his work — ongoing, always ongoing, a word she had stopped requiring further explanation for years ago. She told him about her garden's early spring growth, the specific soil amendment Meen-4 had suggested that she was still testing with the patient, methodical curiosity of a retired schoolteacher who had discovered, late in life, an entirely new subject worth being thorough about.

Strata council meetings ran their ordinary length, occasionally too long, occasionally productive in ways that mattered more than any single cosmic threshold he'd crossed that same week. He sat through zoning disputes and infrastructure budgets and the specific, grinding tedium of consensus-building among several thousand residents who had, each of them, entirely legitimate opinions about how their shared city should grow. He found he did not want to skip any of it, however tempting the capability to simply resolve every disagreement unilaterally might have been. The tedium, he had come to understand, was not an obstacle to the life he'd built. It was the life he'd built, working correctly.

The extraordinary had become, thoroughly, completely, domesticated.

He thought, some evenings, of the person who had once worked a night shift at a convenience store because the pay was slightly better and the silence suited him, and found that the distance between that person and the one currently maintaining perception across a solar system had stopped feeling, most days, like a distance at all. It felt instead like a single continuous life, unbroken, each ordinary Tuesday connected to every other ordinary Tuesday by the same small, stubborn habits: tea, breakfast, a notebook, a phone call, a city he had never once stopped loving.

He closed the current entry the way he closed most entries now — briefly, without embellishment, the specific plain accuracy he had learned, across six years, mattered more than any grander language ever could.

*Three months lived at Formless Scale. Nothing has broken. Nothing, more importantly, has changed about what actually matters. I remain, entirely, myself. I intend to keep it that way for as long as the work requires.*

He set the notebook down, finished his tea, and went to find out what the day's council agenda actually held.
