# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 384 — The Anchor Protocol — Under Stress

It happened three days after the garden terrace conversation, without warning, in the middle of an otherwise unremarkable afternoon.

He was reviewing Circuit diagnostics in the administrative chamber when the Colony Link, which had carried the ordinary hum of twelve hundred lives for months without incident, suddenly did not feel ordinary at all. The Strata's collective emotional field — anxious, distracted, threaded through with the low persistent worry of a community that knew, in broad strokes if not in detail, that its fate was being weighed by something vast and unreadable — surged against him with a force he had not braced for, arriving at the exact moment the Lady Shen revelation was still settling, unresolved, somewhere in the part of his mind that had not fully finished processing it.

For a moment — brief, but long enough to frighten him more than anything the Surveyor had said in weeks — he could not tell which feelings were his own.

Was the tightness in his chest his grief over Lady Shen's betrayal, or was it borrowed, pulled in raw from some resident three levels down who had just received bad news from a doctor who had nothing to do with any of this? Was the low hum of dread the Strata's collective anxiety about the review, or was it his own fear finally surfacing after weeks of careful containment? He sat frozen at the desk, diagnostics forgotten, trying to draw a line between *his* and *theirs* and finding, for the first time since the Anchor Protocol had become daily practice, that the line simply would not hold.

"Lin Mushi."

Chen Yufei's voice arrived through the bond before she arrived in the room physically, sharp with a concern that cut through the noise enough for him to grab onto it like a handhold.

"Something's wrong," he managed. "I can't — I don't know which parts are mine."

She was beside him within minutes, kneeling at the desk, her hand finding his without hesitation. "Then don't sort it right now. Just hold still. I'm here. Find me first, and use me as the edge you measure everything else against."

He did as she said, focusing everything he had on the specific, familiar texture of her presence in the bond — steady in a way the Strata's diffuse anxious field was not, singular where the borrowed grief and fear were a chaotic overlapping mass. Slowly, painstakingly, he began sorting outward from her, the way a person lost in fog might work outward from a single fixed point of light. This feeling — steady, warm, hers. That one — sharper, more scattered, the Strata's collective worry, not his own. And underneath both, finally locatable once he had somewhere solid to measure from, his own grief, quieter than he'd expected, and entirely his.

It took the better part of an hour before the line held reliably again.

"That's never happened before," he said afterward, still slightly unsteady, his hand still in hers.

"I know." Chen Yufei didn't let go. "I think it's because you were already carrying more than you'd admitted to yourself. The Lady Shen situation, unresolved. The review, unresolved. Twelve hundred people's fear, all of it arriving at once through a channel that's never had to hold this much before. The Protocol was built for connection, not for capacity. Maybe it's reaching a limit it hasn't had to test until now."

He thought about that — about three years of steadily expanding what the Colony Link could carry, node by node, without ever once stopping to ask what happened when the carrying itself became the strain rather than the distance.

"I don't like not knowing where I end," he said quietly.

"No," she agreed. "I imagine you don't. But you found the edge again. That's the part that matters. You didn't need someone to draw the line for you forever — you needed someone steady enough to measure against, once. Next time, it might take less."

He wasn't sure he believed that yet, but he let himself lean on the certainty in her voice anyway, the way he'd learned, over years, to trust her judgment even in moments when his own felt unreliable.

"Thank you," he said. "For being the edge."

"Always," she said, simply, and did not let go of his hand for a long while after, both of them sitting quietly in a chamber that hummed, distantly, with a Strata that had no idea how close its Architect had just come to losing the shape of himself inside it.
