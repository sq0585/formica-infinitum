# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 375 — Lady Shen and the Containment Orthodoxy

Dr. Shen Yue accepted the position on Friday, quietly, by a short message that said only *yes, and thank you for making me sit with it,* and Lin Mushi was still turning that small, hard-won resolution over in his mind when Lady Shen returned to the Strata with information that pushed it, temporarily, out of the front of his attention entirely.

She came at dusk this time, which felt, in retrospect, like it should have told him something about the register of what she'd brought.

"The Orthodoxy is not operating alone," she said, before he'd finished pouring tea he suspected neither of them would fully drink. "They have recruited a human ally."

He set the pot down carefully. "A human. Working with the Orthodoxy directly?"

"Someone with access, and someone with belief. Not a mercenary. Not someone bought. Someone who has looked at what you are becoming and has genuinely concluded, on their own reasoning, that your continued development constitutes a risk to Earth itself — and who has decided that risk is significant enough to justify assisting an institution most humans have never heard of and would never willingly cooperate with, if the alternative is trusting you to manage the danger yourself."

Something in the specificity of it unsettled him more than the fact itself. "You make it sound like you know who."

"I have a description. Not a name — not yet, and I want to be careful about that distinction, because a wrong name spoken aloud can do damage a right one never would." She held his gaze, steady, unreadable in the particular way she became unreadable when she was choosing her words with unusual care. "Whoever this is, they understand the cultivation world intimately. Not academically. Not the way an outside observer understands it. The kind of understanding that comes from having lived inside it, fought inside it, paid a price inside it that ordinary access could never explain."

"That's a short list of people," he said slowly.

"It should be. And yet the description fits no one currently active in the Collective that I have been able to identify." She let that sit a moment before continuing, and he noticed, without being entirely sure why it registered, the faint pause before she spoke again — the smallest possible hesitation, gone almost before it existed. "Which suggests either someone outside the Collective's current membership, operating independently. Or someone inside it, whose position gives them enough cover that their true activity has simply not yet surfaced where I can see it."

He thought about that for a long moment, running through the small circle of people who fit the description in even the loosest sense — people who understood the old cultivation world from the inside, who had paid genuine costs within it, who might plausibly fear what he was becoming enough to act against him through a channel this drastic. The list, when he actually built it in his head, was uncomfortably short, and every name on it was someone he trusted, which made the exercise feel less like investigation and more like betrayal rehearsed in advance.

"You'll keep looking," he said. It wasn't quite a question.

"I will keep looking," she confirmed. "And when I have a name I am certain of, you will have it before anyone else does. I owe you that much, at minimum."

There was something in the way she said *at minimum* that he filed away without examining too closely — a debt implied rather than explained, the kind of phrase Lady Shen used when she was carrying more weight than she intended to set down in front of him tonight.

"Thank you," he said. "For bringing this instead of sitting on it."

"Don't thank me yet." She stood, the tea between them still mostly full, and for a moment her expression carried something he couldn't quite name — not guilt, not exactly, but adjacent to it, the shape guilt might take in someone who had spent a lifetime training herself not to show it. "Whoever this is, Lin Mushi, they are not your enemy in the way the Orthodoxy is your enemy. I would ask you to remember that, whenever the name finally surfaces. Fear wearing the shape of betrayal is still, underneath it, fear. It deserves to be understood before it's condemned."

She left before he could ask what made her so certain of that distinction, and he sat alone with two full cups of cooling tea, turning the strange, specific tenderness of her warning over in his mind, unable yet to see the shape of the thing it was quietly, carefully preparing him for.
