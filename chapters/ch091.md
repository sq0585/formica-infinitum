# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ninety-One — What the Circuit Does

The second-generation circuit took three days.

He used the first circuit's failure patterns as a teaching device — three ways not to inscribe an intersection geometry was worth more than one correct example, because the correct example only told him what worked and the failures told him why. He mapped each failure against the Codex's inscription specifications, identified the divergence between what the notation described and what his hand had produced, and corrected systematically.

Second generation. Better proportions, corrected intersection geometry, a housing machined from an aluminum project box he'd ordered specifically for this because cardboard was not adequate and he had accepted this.

The function: ambient Qi detection and amplification, cycling, passive. He added one element the first circuit hadn't had: a modulation layer, a secondary inscription that tuned the detection range toward a specific frequency of Veritonal signal. He set it to the frequency of the Under-Web's primary flow, which he knew precisely from six months of observation.

The second-generation circuit's detection radius: five meters at baseline, twelve in areas of elevated Qi density.

He integrated it into the aluminum housing along with a powerbank and a Qi-to-electrical transducer inscription that he'd found in Document 6's technical appendix and which he'd spent a day figuring out how to execute at his current Compression level.

The assembly produced a device that looked, on the outside, like a modified powerbank with several irregular notches along one edge that made it look either damaged or artisanal, depending on your perspective. On the inside it was a Qi-sensing system that ran continuously, required no practitioner attention, and produced an electrical output signal that he could read on his phone.

He tested it in his apartment first. Then on the street. Then in a park. Then inside the Iron Meridian Court building, at a safe distance, walking past on the opposite side of the street.

---

The signal readings from the Court building were interesting.

The array inside — the node-tapping architecture he'd assessed on the walk-through with Chen Yufei — showed up on the circuit's output as a regular periodic fluctuation in the ambient field. The frequency matched what he'd expect from a passive Qi draw at the volume he'd estimated. What the circuit showed him that he hadn't been able to assess before: the flow direction.

The array was drawing from the tributary that ran past the building toward the north. But it was also — slightly, probably unintentionally — pushing Qi back toward the south. Not as a deliberate return flow. As leakage. The array was inefficient in both directions: it wasted energy on intake and it leaked what it collected on the edges of the draw.

He wrote:

> *Circuit field test. Iron Meridian Court building. Signal: periodic fluctuation, intake dominant. Leakage toward south: approximately 8% of total draw. Efficiency: approximately 35% of theoretical maximum given the tributary density in this location.*
>
> *What this tells me: the Court's cultivation infrastructure is significantly less efficient than it could be. Not because the practitioners are unskilled — Chen Yufei is very skilled — but because the infrastructure was designed empirically over centuries rather than from the Codex's specifications.*
>
> *Comparison: the Strata's equivalent installations are operating at approximately 90% efficiency. The gap is not incompetence. It is access to the correct theoretical framework.*
>
> *The circuit detected this without my Qi perception being active. It detected it from a distance and produced an electrical signal my phone can read. I can now monitor the Qi field of a location without being present in it.*

He looked at the phone output.

He had built an instrument.

Not a Qi perception device — he couldn't share his Qi perception. But a secondary system that could detect, measure, and report the ambient Qi field of any location where he placed a circuit. He could place circuits without being there. The circuits would send him data.

This was different from everything else he'd built. The Compression was personal. The channels were biological. The directed release was a technique. This was a *tool* — something that operated independently of him and produced information he could share with others.

He sent Chen Yufei a message: *come see what the circuit does.*

She arrived in forty minutes and spent an hour looking at the readings, asking questions about the signal interpretation, and then sitting in silence for approximately three minutes.

"You made an instrument," she said.

"Yes."

"That anyone can use."

"Anyone who can read the output signal. The signal format is standard electrical — you could wire it to a display."

"You could give this to the Courts."

He had been thinking about this. "Not yet. The circuit requires Chitin-level inscription, which requires Compression and Codex notation fluency to manufacture. I can make them. Currently I'm the only person who can make them." He paused. "When I understand more about who should have this capability, I'll be more comfortable sharing the manufacturing process."

"But the readings," she said. "The output. That's just data."

"Yes."

She looked at the phone with the signal readings. "The Iron Meridian Court has been running inefficient infrastructure for forty years. If they knew what their array was actually doing, they'd change it."

"Probably," he said. "Depending on what the change required."

"If I brought them these readings—"

"You can." He had made one of the circuits for her. He handed her the housing. "Take it. Show them the data. Don't tell them how the device works until I've thought more about the manufacturing question."

She held the circuit housing. It was glowing faintly through the aluminum's seam lines, the amber-gold of the active inscription visible as a hairline light.

"It's warm," she said.

"Active Veritonal channel. Same as the Strata walls." He paused. "The Iron Meridian will want to study it."

"I'll manage that," she said.

He looked at the circuit in her hand — the ugly, functional, alien-amber thing that had taken four attempts to exist — and thought about what it meant to give someone a tool you could build and they couldn't.

*The debt is specific, owed to specific people.*

This was one specific repayment.
