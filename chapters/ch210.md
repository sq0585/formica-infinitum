# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Ten — The Weaving Breakthrough

He'd been holding the eleven-second fold for a week before he decided to try something larger.

The shoebox-sized pocket had become reliable in the way a new skill becomes reliable just before it stops feeling new — he could build it in under a minute now, hold it for nearly thirty seconds, collapse it cleanly without the displaced-air shudder that had marked his early attempts. Reliable wasn't the same as useful. A fold the size of a shoebox couldn't hold anything he actually wanted to put inside it.

He picked a target the size of a wardrobe.

The geometry scaled up in ways the Codex's notation had warned him about but that he hadn't fully understood until he tried to build it himself — not a simple multiplication of the smaller structure, but a different architecture entirely, one where the load-bearing relationships between the fold's edges mattered more as the volume grew. A shoebox could hold its shape through brute symmetry. A wardrobe needed the kind of structural thinking he associated with actual buildings.

He spent two days on the notation before touching the substrate.

The first three physical attempts collapsed before they fully formed. The fourth held for six seconds, longer than he'd expected, and then failed in a way that told him something useful — not a structural collapse but a kind of internal contradiction, as though two parts of the fold had been built according to slightly different rules.

He found the error on the fifth read-through of his own notation. A unit conversion, embarrassingly basic, carried over from the shoebox-scale calculations without adjustment for the larger volume's different relationship to ambient Qi density.

He corrected it. He tried again.

---

The wardrobe-sized fold held.

He felt it settle into place with more weight than the shoebox version had — a fuller pressure at the edge of his attention, the sense of holding something with genuine mass to it rather than a small contained patch of wrongness in the air. He stood very still, watching the fold's edges, the faint distortion where ordinary space gave way to the pocket he'd built.

Forty minutes.

He didn't move for most of it, monitoring the structure with the same careful attention he'd give a flame he wasn't sure wouldn't catch the curtains. Around the twenty-minute mark, he reached into his pocket — carefully, slowly — and pulled out his phone, and then a second phone he'd brought specifically for this, both synchronized to the same time server that morning.

He set the second phone inside the fold.

He watched the seconds on his own phone tick forward. He waited what felt like a careful, measured interval, and then reached in and retrieved the second phone, comparing the two displays side by side.

A 90% rate. The fold's internal time was running at ninety percent of the external rate — not dramatically different, not the kind of distortion that would be obvious to someone living inside it without instruments, but real, measurable, undeniable.

He sat down where he was standing, the fold still holding above him, and understood something he hadn't fully grasped until the two phone screens sat side by side in his hands.

Dimensional Weaving wasn't only spatial.

He'd assumed, without quite articulating the assumption, that what he was building was a kind of pocket — a contained volume, folded smaller, existing alongside ordinary space the way the Strata itself existed alongside the surface world. He'd understood, abstractly, that the Strata had its own time rate. He hadn't connected that fact to what he was now doing with his own hands, at a scale small enough to hold in a room.

Every fold he built carried its own relationship to time, inseparable from its relationship to space, because the two had never actually been separate to begin with.

He wrote in his notebook, his hand steadier now than it had been after the shoebox's first success, though the implications sitting underneath the steadiness were considerably larger.

> *Forty minutes held. Wardrobe-scale. Internal time rate: 90% of external, measured with synchronized devices. This is not a side effect — it's the same phenomenon as the Strata's own time dilation, just smaller and under deliberate control.*
>
> *Which means: every fold I build from now on needs a time-rate calculation alongside the spatial one. Which means: I could, theoretically, build spaces where time moves differently on purpose. Faster training. Slower storage. I don't fully understand the implications yet.*
>
> *Next: hold this size longer. Then: try to control the rate deliberately instead of just measuring whatever rate the geometry happens to produce.*

He closed the notebook and let the wardrobe-sized fold collapse, gently, the way the shoebox had learned to collapse a week ago.

Forty minutes. Ninety percent. A door he hadn't known existed, standing open now, with no clear sense yet of how far the room behind it went.
