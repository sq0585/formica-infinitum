# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Seven — The Relationship Strain, First Sign

He didn't notice it happening. That was the part that troubled him most, once he finally understood what Chen Yufei had been quietly tracking for weeks.

It started, as far as he could later reconstruct, on an ordinary evening — the two of them sitting together after dinner, the easy quiet of their usual routine settling over the room, when he'd asked her a simple question about her day and taken, by his own later estimate, close to five seconds to actually respond.

"You went somewhere," she said, not accusatory, just observing.

"Sorry. Amber-0 flagged something in the Sector 3 flow and I was checking whether it needed my attention."

"That's fine. It happens." She'd let it go, that first time, and he'd thought nothing more of it.

It happened again two days later. Then three days after that. Small pauses, four or five seconds each, his attention pulled somewhere else mid-conversation by the Colony Link's constant background processing — a resident's discomfort registering somewhere at the edge of his awareness, a Circuit node's status shifting, Amber-0 surfacing a recommendation that needed a decision. Each pause was small. Each one, on its own, was nothing.

He didn't notice the pattern forming. She did.

---

He found out how closely she'd been tracking it on a Tuesday evening, when she set her tea down with unusual deliberateness and said, without preamble, "That was the sixth one today."

He blinked. "The sixth what?"

"Pause. Since dinner started." She said it plainly, no accusation in her voice, just the flat report of someone who had been quietly counting. "I started keeping track about three weeks ago. I wanted to know if it was actually increasing or if I was just noticing something that had always been there."

"And?"

"It's increasing. Slowly. But it's real." She met his eyes directly, and he saw, beneath the careful neutrality of her tone, something more complicated sitting underneath. "I'm not upset about it. I want to say that clearly before you start apologizing, because I can already see you gearing up to."

He closed his mouth on the apology that had, in fact, already been forming.

"I just think you should know," she continued. "Because I don't think you've noticed it happening to yourself. And I think that's worth noticing, given everything else you're carrying right now."

---

He sat with it for a long moment, running his own accounting the way he'd learned to across two years of self-examination, and found, to his quiet discomfort, that she was entirely correct. He hadn't noticed. The pauses had felt, from the inside, like nothing at all — a fraction of attention diverted, resolved, returned, so brief that each individual instance had seemed too small to register as anything worth tracking.

"How long has this actually been going on?" he asked.

"I don't know precisely. I only started counting three weeks ago, once I noticed the pattern. But thinking back — " she paused, working through her own memory carefully, "I think it's been building for at least a month before that. Since around the time your Colony Link reached city-scale, if I had to guess."

The timeline landed with uncomfortable precision. City-scale. Sixteen million people's accumulated emotional weather, arriving each evening, managed through a discipline he'd built specifically because he'd anticipated needing it — and had apparently, somewhere in the process, missed an entirely different cost accumulating quietly alongside it.

"I'm sorry," he said, the apology arriving despite her earlier warning.

"I know you are. I'm not asking for an apology." She reached across the table, her hand settling over his with the same steady warmth that had, across two years, become one of the few constants he could always trust to be exactly what it appeared to be. "I'm asking you to notice it. That's different. I don't need you to fix something instantly. I need you to know it's happening, the same way I know it's happening, so we're not the only two people managing this asymmetrically — you unaware, me quietly counting."

He turned his hand over beneath hers, holding on properly rather than simply accepting the gesture.

"I'll notice it," he said. "I don't know yet what I'll do about it. But I'll notice it, starting now, instead of finding out three weeks late from your careful counting."

"That's enough for tonight," she said, something in her voice easing, though not entirely. "I don't need a solution yet. I need to know you're actually here, paying attention to this the same way you pay attention to everything else that matters."

He nodded, and made himself, deliberately, entirely present for the rest of the evening — no pauses, no distant processing, just the two of them, sitting together in the quiet, while somewhere at the edges of his awareness twenty million people's weather waited, patiently, for him to let it back in later, on his own terms, once this mattered more than it did right now.
