# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty-Eight — Meen-4's Concession

The record update came without announcement, the way most of Meen-4's significant decisions did, and he only found it because he'd gone looking for something else entirely.

He'd been in the archive's administrative section, cross-referencing maintenance schedules against the Strata's structural logs, when a line in the general status record caught his attention — not because it was highlighted or flagged, but because the phrasing was unfamiliar enough to snag against everything he'd come to expect from Meen-4's usual documentation style.

*Human community, east residential wing: provisional co-habitation arrangement, pending review.*

He read it twice, then a third time, the specific administrative dryness of the phrase doing nothing to disguise how much weight it was quietly carrying.

He found Meen-4 in the circular chamber she favored, working through a stack of translated records with the same unhurried focus she brought to four-hundred-year projects and single afternoons alike.

"I found the update," he said, holding up the tablet with the record displayed.

*Yes,* she signaled, not looking up. *I updated the status classification three days ago. You were not informed because the update did not require your input. It was a matter of Archive record-keeping, not Strata governance.*

"You called it a co-habitation arrangement."

*That is what it is.*

"You called it provisional."

*It is that also.* She finally looked up, her attention settling on him with the particular stillness he'd learned to read as her deciding how much explanation the moment warranted. *You are surprised.*

"I'm — " He considered the honest answer. "I'm surprised you updated it at all. You could have left it unclassified indefinitely. You've done that with other things."

*I have.* A pause. *The prior classification for this section of the Strata was four hundred and twelve years old. It described the space as archive overflow storage, unoccupied, low priority. That classification stopped being accurate the moment the elder Mrs. Tan moved into it. Continuing to record something false because updating the record was administratively inconvenient is not a standard I hold myself to.*

He sat with that, understanding, slowly, that he'd walked in expecting to have to argue for something Meen-4 had already decided on her own terms.

"Thank you," he said. "For updating it honestly."

*Do not thank me for accuracy,* she signaled. *Accuracy is not a gift. It is simply correct record-keeping.* Something in the signal's cadence shifted, fractionally, into a register closer to admission than instruction. *The word provisional is doing real work in that sentence. I want you to understand that clearly, rather than mistaking this for something it is not.*

"What work is it doing?"

*It means I have not yet decided what a permanent human population inside a Formican Strata implies, structurally, for the Archive's function. Four hundred years of precedent exist for temporary human presence — carriers, scholars, occasional visitors under supervision. No precedent exists for residency. I do not know yet whether this arrangement will remain stable, whether it will require protocols the Archive does not currently have, whether it changes what the Strata fundamentally is.* A pause, longer than usual. *Provisional means I am watching. Not that I am opposed.*

---

He carried the exchange to Axis-7 later that afternoon, finding her in the outer garden section she'd claimed as her own, tending to Qi-cultivated plants with the same patient attention she gave to everything she chose to care about.

"Meen-4 updated her records," he said, and told her the substance of the classification, watching Axis-7's response carefully, curious whether it would land the way it had landed with him.

Axis-7 was quiet for a moment, her attention on a cluster of pale, unfamiliar leaves she was pruning with careful precision.

*She has not updated her records in two hundred years,* she signaled finally. *Not in any substantive way. The Archive's classification schema has been static since before the Amber Lattice School's destruction. She maintains it. She does not revise it.* She set down the small tool she was using. *You should note this. It is a larger event than the words themselves suggest.*

"I noticed the wording was careful. I didn't realize it was that rare."

*Meen-4 does not make declarations. She makes assessments, and she revises them only when the evidence supporting the old assessment has genuinely failed.* Axis-7 returned to the leaves, her attention splitting between the plant and the conversation with the ease of someone who had spent millennia learning to do both at once. *Two hundred years of unrevised records means two hundred years in which nothing occurred that she judged worth reclassifying. You have now occurred.*

He turned that over, the specific phrasing — *you have now occurred* — settling into him with more weight than a gentler version might have carried.

"Is that a good thing?"

*I do not know yet either,* Axis-7 signaled, with something that might, in a human voice, have carried the shape of dry amusement. *That is, I believe, the entire point of the word provisional.*

He left the garden with the exchange sitting differently than it had when he'd walked in — not a small administrative footnote, but a two-hundred-year silence quietly, deliberately broken, one careful phrase at a time, by someone who had apparently been waiting, this entire time, for something worth the trouble of being wrong about.
