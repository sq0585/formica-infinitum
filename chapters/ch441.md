# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 441 — The Decision to Continue

He spent the better part of a day doing nothing that looked, from the outside, like progress.

He sat in the command chamber, mostly, moving occasionally to the archive's outer section to reread passages he'd already catalogued, not because he expected new information from them but because the physical motion of walking gave his thinking somewhere to go that wasn't directly at the question sitting, unresolved, at the center of everything.

*What do I actually need,* he wrote, and made himself answer honestly rather than in whatever direction his curiosity kept trying to pull the pen.

He had come here for one clear reason. Stellar Scale comprehension — the specific, disciplined gap between what he could sense of the solar system's field and what he could actually understand of it, a gap the archive's standard classifications had already narrowed considerably, more than he'd expected in the time he'd had. He had a working grasp of resonance, dampening, amplification, the whole architecture of how stellar fields interacted across interstellar distance. He suspected, cautiously, that it was close to enough.

7.4-N was not part of that goal. It had never been part of that goal. It had arrived alongside it, adjacent, compelling in exactly the way every unanswered question in his life had always been compelling, and he recognized, sitting with it honestly, that the pull he felt toward it right now had almost nothing to do with Stellar Scale and almost everything to do with simply wanting to know.

*Rule,* he wrote, and pressed the pen into the page the way he did when a rule was costing him something to actually hold. *Wanting to know is not, by itself, sufficient reason to proceed. It has never been sufficient reason. It has occasionally been dangerous reason, and I have the scars — figurative and otherwise — from at least two occasions where I let it be enough anyway.*

He thought of Liang Jingzhi, years ago now, patient and exact, teaching him the specific discipline of setting a fact down rather than chasing it before its moment arrived. He thought of the deep-space signal itself, sitting quiet and unexamined for three full years after its discovery in Arc IV, because he'd understood, even then, that some doors deserved to open on their own schedule rather than his impatient one.

7.4-N, he understood now, was exactly that kind of door. An entire expedition — eight people, he suspected, though he had no confirmed count yet, no manifest, nothing beyond the pattern of a record that had simply stopped — had approached it with decades of preparation behind them, remote sensors specifically calibrated for the investigation, and had still, evidently, not survived whatever direct contact with it actually required.

He had none of that preparation. He had a comprehension gap he'd come specifically to close, a relay chain stretched already to its practical limit, and, waiting on the other end of that chain, five people who had each, independently, made sure he understood he wasn't allowed to disappear into this quietly.

*I do not have what they had, and they still did not come back,* he wrote, and underlined it twice, because some rules needed the emphasis to actually hold against the specific pull of curiosity that had, five years running, been the single most reliable engine of everything he'd ever managed to become.

*Decision,* he wrote, finally, the word arriving with more weight than he expected from four letters. *I complete the stellar comprehension work the message actually specified — the standard classifications, thoroughly understood, exactly as instructed. I do not investigate 7.4-N directly. I catalogue what the record already contains about it, I flag it clearly for Meen-4 and the wider Collective's own attention, and I set it down. Not because I have stopped being curious. Because I have learned, over five years, the specific difference between a question worth answering now and one worth waiting for.*

He read the decision back once, twice, and found, to his own mild surprise, that it settled rather than chafed — the particular, hard-won relief of choosing discipline deliberately, rather than discipline being the only option curiosity had left him.

He checked the relay chain and sent a longer message than his usual check-ins, laying out what he'd found and what he'd decided, and Meen-4's reply, when it arrived hours later, carried a warmth he hadn't quite expected from her usual formal precision.

*This is, I believe, the most significant demonstration of judgment you have offered me in five years,* it read. *Not the comprehension. The restraint. Come home when the classification work is finished. Whatever 7.4-N is, it has waited forty million years already. It can wait considerably longer for someone properly equipped to actually survive asking it questions.*

He smiled at that, alone in the quiet cold of a command chamber built by hands that had, evidently, learned the same lesson too late to save themselves, and turned back, finally, to finishing the work he had actually come here to do.
