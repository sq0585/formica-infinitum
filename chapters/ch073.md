# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Seventy-Three — The Chen Yufei Problem

Chen Yufei visited the 7-Eleven on a Friday morning.

She arrived at 10:15 AM, which Xiao Dong's replacement was covering, and she spoke to the morning clerk — not Xiao Dong, who was at his noodle shop by that hour — and asked about unusual events in the area. Standard questions. She had done this before.

What was different: she asked specifically about a former employee named Lin Mushi.

The morning clerk said he'd never met him. She left.

Lin Mushi tracked her Qi signature east from the store, through two blocks, and watched it stop at an intersection for several minutes — the signature of someone consulting their phone or making a call — before moving south.

She had his residential address from the same source the investigator had used. She was going to come to him.

He had twenty minutes.

---

He spent ten of them standing in his apartment with his jacket on, deciding exactly how to handle the next twenty-four hours.

He could wait for her to knock on his door. This would put her on the initiative — she would arrive with a plan, he would respond. The question would be hers.

He could not be in the apartment when she knocked, which bought time but didn't resolve anything.

He could intercept her before she reached the building. This was the third option, and the one he had been preparing for.

He locked the apartment. He took the stairs. He exited through the back of the building onto the alley, which put him on a line that allowed him to round the block and come out onto the street ahead of Chen Yufei's current position.

He walked steadily.

He came out onto the street.

She was thirty meters away, walking with the focused attention of a person who was looking for an address.

He turned and fell into step beside her.

She stopped.

He stopped.

He said: "I'm the one who drained the node. I didn't know what it was at the time. I'd like to understand what I should have done instead."

---

The silence lasted approximately six seconds.

Then Chen Yufei laughed — once, sharp, the involuntary sound of someone whose prepared scenario has been replaced by something they didn't predict.

"You followed me," she said.

"I intercepted you. You were going to my apartment."

She looked at him with the assessing gaze of a trained practitioner who was rapidly revising her model of the situation. She was 28, by his assessment — though he was bad at ages — with the posture of someone who had been disciplined about physical training and the specific Qi signature of a person with eight years of deliberate cultivation practice. Her channels were different from his: her training framework had produced a different geometry, something the Iron Meridian Court had developed from fragments of the original system. More rigid, less efficient, but genuinely built.

She was significantly more practiced than him, in the sense of having been doing this for eight years to his five months.

She was not, by his assessment, more capable. His framework was complete in a way hers was not. He wasn't sure she knew this.

He waited for her to speak.

"You drained a monitored node," she said. "Without authorization. For months."

"Yes. I didn't know it was monitored, or what authorization would mean in this context, or that the node's capacity was finite. I know all of these things now. I have documentation of the full timeline."

"Documentation."

"I keep notebooks."

Another pause. Then: "You'd better show me."

He said: "Not here. Is there somewhere you'd prefer?"

She thought for a moment. "The tea shop on Jinhua Street. Twenty minutes."

"Yes," he said.

She walked one way. He walked another. They arrived at the tea shop, as it happened, at almost exactly the same time, which he found quietly amusing and which she appeared to find neither amusing nor the opposite of amusing.

She sat down. She looked at him across the table.

She said: "Start from the beginning."
