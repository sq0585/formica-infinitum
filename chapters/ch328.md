# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Twenty-Eight — The Inclusive Archive Project

Meen-4 raised the proposal on an otherwise unremarkable afternoon, the two of them working through routine Archive maintenance when she paused, mid-task, with the specific stillness he'd learned to recognize as her preparing to say something she'd been considering for a while.

*I have been thinking about a project,* she signaled. *I would like your assessment of whether it is wise, before I commit resources to it.*

"Tell me."

*A joint history project. Formican and human participants, working together to translate and annotate the outer Archive's historical records — not the Deep Archive material you have already accessed alone, but the broader, less carefully curated body of Formican history that has never been systematically organized for outside interpretation.*

He considered the proposal, understanding, even before she elaborated, that it represented something significant coming from her specifically. "You want human researchers examining Formican history."

*Yes.* She held his gaze with unusual steadiness. *I have spent four hundred years as this Strata's Keeper. I have, across that time, come to certain settled interpretations of the events I have catalogued — the Amber Lattice School's destruction, the broader consequences of the Isolationist consolidation, the general shape of what our civilization became after Veth-Karun's dissolution. I have begun to wonder, recently, whether those settled interpretations are entirely accurate, or whether four hundred years of solitary interpretation has simply hardened certain assumptions past the point where I can examine them freshly.*

"You think other perspectives might see something you've missed."

*I believe it is possible,* she signaled, the admission carrying, even in its careful phrasing, a weight he understood was considerable coming from her. *The Formicans have been alone with this history for forty million years. An entire civilization, interpreting its own past without meaningful outside perspective for a span of time I find difficult to fully hold even with my current comprehension. I do not believe that isolation has served accuracy well. I believe it may have simply allowed certain conclusions to calcify, unchallenged, because no one outside the tradition was ever positioned to challenge them.*

---

He sat with the proposal's full weight, recognizing, beneath its careful academic framing, something that felt considerably more personal than Meen-4 was directly acknowledging.

"This is about more than historical accuracy," he said gently.

She was quiet for a long moment, her attention shifting, briefly, toward something he couldn't quite read. *Perhaps,* she signaled finally. *I have spent considerable time recently reconsidering certain assumptions I have held for a very long time. I do not wish to elaborate further at present. I would prefer the project itself to proceed on its own honest terms, rather than framed, from the outset, around whatever personal reconsideration is prompting it.*

He didn't press further, recognizing, in her careful reticence, the shape of something she wasn't yet ready to name directly — though he found himself, walking away from the conversation, increasingly certain it connected somehow to the Amber Lattice School's destruction, and to whatever role she herself might have played, or not played, in whatever the Isolationists' "disciplinary action" had actually involved.

"I think it's a good project," he told her, once he'd sat with it properly. "I'll help you recruit participants. I think Fang Ruxue would be an excellent choice to help structure it, and I suspect several Collective members would be genuinely eager for the opportunity."

*Thank you,* she signaled. *I would like to begin within the week, if participants can be assembled that quickly.*

---

He brought the proposal to Fang Ruxue that same afternoon, and found her response immediate and enthusiastic in a way that surprised him slightly, given her usual measured caution about new commitments.

"This is exactly the kind of work the Collective should be doing," she said, already sketching a rough structure for how the project might be organized. "Real collaborative scholarship, not just cultivation technique. I'd like to participate directly myself, if Meen-4 doesn't object."

"I don't think she'll object. I think she specifically wants perspectives she hasn't already fully absorbed into her own thinking."

Fang Ruxue nodded, already drafting a call for participants, and he left her to the work with a quiet satisfaction that sat alongside a deeper, more careful curiosity about what Meen-4's private reconsideration might eventually reveal.

He wrote about it that evening, the entry brief but attentive.

> *Meen-4 wants human eyes on Formican history — forty million years of solitary interpretation, finally opened to outside perspective. She wouldn't say directly why now, but something in how she said it suggests this isn't purely academic. I think she's been sitting with something. I think this project might be, for her, less about correcting the historical record and more about finally being ready to let someone else help her carry it.*
