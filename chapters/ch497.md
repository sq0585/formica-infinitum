# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 497 — The Last Sunday Call

He called her at the usual time, the same steady rhythm he'd kept faithfully since the disclosure had finally set both of them free of every careful omission, and found himself, dialing, more grateful than usual for the sheer ordinariness of the ritual.

"You're early," she said, answering on the second ring, the particular warmth in her voice that had, across the past months, become one of the most reliable constants in his entire extraordinary life. "I was still in the garden. The eastern bed is finally coming along properly — Meen-4's fungal theory turned out to be exactly right, in case you were wondering."

"I wasn't doubting it," he said, smiling despite everything sitting underneath the conversation. "I'm glad it's working."

"How are you eating?" she asked, the same question she always led with, the specific maternal thoroughness that hadn't changed one bit despite everything else that had.

"Fine," he said. "Chen Yufei's been making sure of it."

"Sleeping?"

"Adequate," he said, which was honest enough not to feel like a lie, even if *adequate* was doing some careful work for a man who'd spent the past several nights turning an enormous threshold over in his mind more than actually resting beneath it.

"And the work? The large thing you've been building all these months?"

"Nearly done," he said, and felt the conversation's easy rhythm shift, gently, toward the thing he'd actually called to say. "Mā, I want to tell you something, and I want to tell it to you the honest way, the way we've been doing everything since I finally told you the truth."

"I'm listening," she said, and he could hear, even through the phone, the particular stillness she brought to moments she understood mattered.

"I'm going to be doing something very large soon," he said. "Larger than the extension. Larger, probably, than anything I've done before it. I don't know exactly how long it will take. I don't know, if I'm being completely honest, exactly what it will cost."

She was quiet for a moment, and he waited, letting her take whatever time she needed, the same patience he'd learned, slowly, to extend toward every difficult truth across the past year.

"Will you call me when it's finished?" she asked finally.

"Yes," he said, without hesitation, meaning it entirely.

"Then finish it properly," she said.

He sat with that a moment, the simplicity of it landing with more weight than any longer, more elaborate response could have carried. Not *be careful.* Not *come back safely,* though he understood, clearly, that both of those things lived somewhere underneath her four plain words. Just the same practical, steady wisdom she'd offered him across his entire life, distilled now into the truest advice anyone had given him in the whole of this extraordinary year.

"I will," he said.

"Good." Her voice carried a brightness that he suspected was, in part, deliberately offered — the same gift she'd given him once before, making him laugh about a garden bed at the exact moment he'd needed laughter most. "Now. Tell me something ordinary before you go. I don't want the last thing we talk about before your very large thing to be the very large thing itself."

He laughed, despite everything, at the specific, familiar practicality of it. "The noodle shop's parking feud is still going," he offered. "I checked in on it yesterday. No resolution in sight."

"Some things," she said, with evident satisfaction, "should simply never resolve. It gives everyone something reliable to complain about."

They talked a while longer, easy and unhurried, the ordinary rhythm of a Sunday call carrying them both gently away from the conversation's earlier weight and back into the familiar, comfortable territory of gardens and parking disputes and nothing at all resembling stellar mechanics.

"I love you," he said, before he finally let her go, the words arriving as easily now as they'd once felt impossible to say honestly.

"I love you too," she said. "Go finish your very large thing. Properly. I'll be here, same as always, waiting to hear how it went."

He held the phone a moment after she'd hung up, the particular, grounding warmth of her voice still settling in his chest, and understood, sitting there in the quiet aftermath, that he had just received, from the person who had raised him from nothing, exactly the advice he needed to carry into whatever came next.

*Finish it properly.*

He wrote it in the notebook that evening, plainly, without elaboration, understanding that some instructions didn't need any embellishment beyond the truth of them.
