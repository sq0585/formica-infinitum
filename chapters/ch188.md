# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty-Eight — Strata Navigation

He led her through sections of the Strata he had mapped to a degree of detail that no fifteen-minute pursuit could match, regardless of the pursuer's raw capability.

Through the east corridor's narrowing passages, where the column spacing dropped to a configuration he knew by feel even at speed, his shoulder brushing stone he'd memorized the exact angle of months ago without ever consciously deciding to. Past the Secondary Archive's sealed entrance, where he knew the floor's ambient Qi density spiked unpredictably in a way that would disrupt anyone unfamiliar with the pattern — he felt the spike arrive as he crossed it, a brief lurch in his own footing that he'd long since learned to absorb without breaking stride, and heard, a half-second behind him, Lady Shen's pace catch on exactly the irregularity he'd hoped it would. Toward a section of the territory near the circular room — Axis-7's space — where the Qi field's character shifted toward something denser and more structurally complex than the outer hall's relatively open architecture.

His goal, formed in the seconds after Lady Shen's first technique forced his improvised Ratio 4 evasion, was specific: reach a zone where the ambient density was high enough that any large-scale Qi manipulation — the kind of spatial-capture technique she'd already demonstrated, or worse — risked destabilizing the surrounding dimensional structure in ways that even a practitioner of her caliber would be unwilling to risk.

He was betting on two things simultaneously. First, that Lady Shen's combat instincts, honed for thirty years in conventional engagements, did not include detailed knowledge of how the Strata's specific architecture would respond to high-output techniques deployed inside it. Second, and more quietly, that whatever the forced Ratio 3 was costing her would continue compounding the longer she held it — that every minute of pursuit was a minute her body spent paying a debt she had decided, three weeks ago in isolated training, was worth incurring.

He was right about both.

She caught up to him twice in the chase — close enough that he felt the edge of a second containment attempt brush past his trailing shoulder, a near miss that cost him a stumble he barely recovered from — but each time, the terrain itself slowed her by fractions of a second he didn't have to spare and somehow did, and each time, the gap between her attempts widened by a margin too consistent to be coincidence. The first attempt had come within four seconds of the chase beginning. The second came nine seconds after that. He didn't have the attention to spare for careful arithmetic in the middle of a dead run, but some quieter part of him was tracking it anyway, filing it for later, the way fifteen months of obsessive documentation habit didn't simply switch off because the circumstances had become urgent.

Behind him, faintly, through the Circuit network's passive monitoring that he'd left running on his phone at the surface entrance, he was vaguely aware that the system would be logging this entire pursuit in granular detail — not that it could help him in real time, but that the record would exist afterward, for whatever account of events eventually needed to be given, and for whatever Chen Yufei would need to see to understand what had actually happened down here once it was over.

He reached the high-density zone near the circular room's outer approach with Lady Shen perhaps fifteen meters behind him, closing, but closing more slowly now than she had been three minutes earlier.

He turned to face her, breathing hard, and felt — for the first time since the confrontation began — something other than pure reactive momentum.

He felt, faintly, Chen Yufei's Qi signature, moving fast, somewhere in the hall behind both of them.

She had felt the disturbance through the Circuit network too.

She was coming.
