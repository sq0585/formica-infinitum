# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fourteen — The Earth's Field

He spent six hours simply perceiving before he attempted anything else, understanding, with the same discipline that had carried him through every previous threshold, that a scale this large deserved to be witnessed carefully before it was used for anything at all.

He sat in the small pocket dimension he'd built for exactly this kind of work, Chen Yufei nearby but giving him the space the moment required, and let the whole of Earth's field settle into something he could actually examine rather than simply survive.

The Under-Web came first, clearer now than he'd ever perceived it — the vast, layered network of Formican infrastructure threading beneath the planet's surface, considerably more extensive than he'd previously understood, stretching in patterns that suggested decisions made across geological time by minds thinking, deliberately, in the timescales Liang Jingzhi had spent six weeks teaching him to hold.

He found the Strata locations next, and felt something catch in his chest as their full scope resolved — scattered across the planet in numbers he hadn't previously grasped, dozens upon dozens of sites, some active and populated the way his own Chengdu Strata had become, others quiet, dormant, waiting the way his own had once waited for two years before Ren Wuji's death had brought him stumbling into it.

*Do not attempt to count them precisely tonight,* Meen-4 signaled, sensing, correctly, the direction his attention was heading. *The number is large, and precision is less important right now than simply understanding the general scope. There is time to inventory this properly, in the coming weeks.*

He let the impulse go, and continued the slower, more patient work of simply witnessing.

---

The Pacific Ring resolved with a clarity that made his breath catch — the twelve structures he'd previously understood only through Liang Jingzhi's survey data and his own partial sensing now visible in full, ancient and vast and unmistakably alive in the specific, slow-breathing way geological structures were alive, their combined field forming something that felt, at this scale, less like separate installations and more like a single circulatory system wrapped patiently around the ocean floor.

He held that awareness for a long while, understanding, more fully than he ever had before, exactly what Liang Jingzhi had meant when she'd first described the Ring's role in Earth's continued habitability — not metaphor, not abstraction, but literal, structural fact, a system he could now feel functioning in real time, its slow work of maintaining planetary stability unfolding beneath his awareness like a held note sustained across millions of years.

He found the Tibetan Witness Node third, its presence in the field distinct from everything around it in a way he couldn't immediately articulate — not simply another piece of Formican infrastructure, but something that felt, subtly, different in character, the way a single instrument in an orchestra sometimes carries a texture the others don't quite share.

He didn't understand the difference yet. He noted it carefully, marking it for closer attention, and moved on.

---

He found the deep-space signal's origin point last, near the end of the six hours, his attention drawn toward it less by deliberate search than by a kind of magnetic pull he couldn't fully explain — the same faint, dormant signature he'd first detected weeks earlier through the Chitin Circuit network's routine diagnostic sweep, now visible directly through the planetary field itself, its point of origin sitting somewhere far beyond Earth's own boundary, distant and quiet and unmistakably still transmitting, patiently, into a silence that had apparently gone unanswered for longer than any record he'd yet encountered could account for.

He sat with all of it — the Under-Web's vast layered architecture, the scattered Strata locations waiting across the planet, the Pacific Ring's slow circulatory function, the Tibetan Witness Node's subtly different texture, and the deep-space signal's patient, distant call — and felt, beneath the sheer overwhelming volume of it, something he hadn't expected to feel this early in the experience.

Not fear. Not even, primarily, the exhaustion the scale should reasonably have produced.

Wonder.

He wrote a single line in his notebook once the six hours finally ended, too tired for anything longer, the entry brief in a way that felt, nonetheless, entirely adequate to the moment.

> *The whole world, held at once. Terrifying. Beautiful. I don't have better words yet. I'm not sure better words exist.*

He closed the notebook, and let his attention drift, one final time before sleep finally claimed him, toward the Tibetan Witness Node's strange, distinct texture, some quiet instinct at the edge of his exhausted awareness already, faintly, suspecting that whatever made it different from everything else in the field was going to matter considerably more than he currently understood.
