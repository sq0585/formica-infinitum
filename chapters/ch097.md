# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ninety-Seven — The Descent — Day One

He had entered the Strata fifteen times through the threshold in the northwest corner of the 7-Eleven.

This was the sixteenth entry, and it was different: this time he was taking Chen Yufei to the outer edge of the mapped territory, past the circular room and deeper into Layer 2's eastern reach, toward a section of the vaulted hall network he'd been able to see on the Codex's schematic but hadn't physically explored.

The Codex called this section the *Passage Archive* — not the library, which he'd already found, but a second structure, further in, that the schematic showed as a series of smaller rooms branching off the main hall's eastern corridor.

The thin section's compression had continued. Entry now required sustained Ratio 1.4. He managed it in seven minutes. Chen Yufei, whose Compression capability had advanced significantly with the posture-corrected technique, managed it in nine.

They both emerged into the vaulted hall with their hands slightly warm from the effort.

---

"The floor's lag is shorter," Chen Yufei said, looking back at where they'd entered.

"Yes. The recognition protocol is calibrating faster to me." He was already walking east. "It's also beginning to calibrate to you — your lag is down to eight seconds."

"The Strata is learning my pattern."

"The Keeper station's monitoring system is. Same thing, practically." He stopped at the junction where the main hall's eastern arm branched off. "Further in from here. I haven't mapped beyond this point."

She looked down the eastern arm. The vaulting was lower here — twenty meters rather than thirty-two — and the columns were spaced more tightly. The floor illumination ran in a different pattern, narrower bands of amber that ran parallel to the walls rather than radiating from the center.

"Different infrastructure," she said. She had been reading the Codex sections he'd shared with her for the past three weeks and was developing the technical vocabulary. "Purpose-built for a specific use?"

"Archive access corridor," he said. "The lighting pattern indicates a directional function — it's guiding rather than recording." He started walking. "Follow the light."

They followed the narrow bands east, which became slightly north-east after a hundred meters. The corridor narrowed until the columns were ten meters apart rather than twenty. The ceiling dropped again — fifteen meters. Still enormous by ordinary standards, but after the main hall's scale it had the quality of moving from a cathedral to a large chapel.

The sound changed. Not louder, not quieter. A different quality of acoustic — more absorbed, less reverberant, as if the material around them had changed in density.

He stopped.

"This wall," he said, pressing his palm to the right side of the corridor.

She pressed hers.

They both felt it: the material here was not the same as the main hall's walls. Denser, more information-rich in the way that books were information-rich compared to blank paper. The hexagonal geometry ran deeper, the inscriptions layered over each other in a way that suggested the material had been modified repeatedly rather than built once.

"Chitin Circuits," she said. "Multiple generations. Built on top of each other."

"Yes. This section has been actively maintained. Not just maintained — *added to.* Someone has been inscribing new circuit layers onto this wall at intervals over a long time."

"How long?"

He pressed harder, let the Ratio 1.4 Compression do the detailed reading. The material's Qi signature had stratifications, the way geological formations had stratifications — each era of inscription sitting above the previous one in a way his perception could approximately date by the signature's character.

"The oldest layer: very old. Formican-age original, probably millions of years by the material quality. The most recent: about two hundred years ago."

Two hundred years ago.

The last Carrier before him.

He took his hand off the wall.

He looked at Chen Yufei.

"Someone was here two hundred years ago," she said.

"And they were adding to the archive." He turned back to the corridor. "Let's find what they added."

The corridor ended in a door.

Not the same kind of door as the library entrance — this one was smaller, built into the wall rather than standing free, with an inscription around the frame that he could now read in full at his current comprehension level.

He read it aloud, translating as he went: "*Secondary Archive — Documentation from the Third Maintenance Period. Access requires — requires — active Chitin Seed integration and—*" He paused. "And fluency in the communication register."

He had sixty percent of the communication register.

He attempted the door.

It yielded.

Not smoothly — he felt the resistance of the two incompletely parsed requirements, the lock's system acknowledging his Seed integration fully and his register fluency partially. Like a door that opened two-thirds of the way before hitting something.

He pushed.

It opened fully.

Inside: shelves. Hundreds of them, floor to ceiling, lined with containers — smaller than the library's, made of a darker material, closed with seals that had the Chitin Seed integration marker on their outer surface.

He stood in the doorway and looked at the accumulated documentation of someone who had stood where he stood two hundred years ago and tried to understand what he was trying to understand.

He stepped inside.

"This," he said, "is what someone did with two hundred years of being a Carrier before me."

He looked at the first shelf.

He started reading the container labels.

His notebook was already open.
