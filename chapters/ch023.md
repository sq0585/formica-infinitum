# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Twenty-Three — What Dark Energy Is

The problem with dark energy was that nobody knew what it was.

This was not a controversial statement. This was the consensus position of every credible paper he had read on the subject, stated with varying degrees of discomfort depending on the author's threshold for admitting uncertainty. Dark energy was the name given to whatever was causing the universe's expansion to accelerate. It constituted approximately sixty-eight percent of the total energy content of the observable universe. It had been inferred from observation — specifically from measurements of Type Ia supernovae by two teams in 1998, work that had won a Nobel Prize — but had never been directly detected, measured, or explained by any existing physical model.

Something made up two-thirds of everything. Nobody knew what it was.

Lin Mushi found this, as he found most problems of this type, intensely satisfying.

---

He spent three days on it. Not exclusively — he was still working nights, still doing notation analysis, still running his daily Qi observation log — but dark energy occupied the background of his attention the way an unresolved problem always did, surfacing when his hands were busy and his mind was free.

He read the original 1998 papers. He read the follow-up observations from the Hubble Space Telescope and the Planck mission. He read the theoretical proposals: cosmological constant, quintessence, phantom energy, modified gravity. He read the criticisms of each. He read a long and frustrating survey of why dark energy was particularly difficult to reconcile with quantum field theory, which predicted a vacuum energy density approximately 120 orders of magnitude larger than what was actually observed, which was one of the largest discrepancies between theory and observation in the history of physics.

120 orders of magnitude. The theoretical value and the observed value disagreed by a factor of ten to the power of 120.

He sat with that number for a while.

Then he looked at the Codex diagram.

---

The second message had used the term *Veritonal Energy* in passing, in the context of the Under-Web. He had noted it and moved on, because the message was dense and he was still processing the warning about the timeline. But the term had stayed.

*Veritonal Energy.* A named thing. Something that had been named by people who spent four hundred years understanding it.

He opened a fresh notebook page and wrote:

> *Hypothesis: dark energy and Veritonal Energy are the same phenomenon, observed from different methodological positions.*
>
> *Evidence for: (1) dark energy is distributed through space in a way that produces specific gravitational effects without electromagnetic interaction — exactly what you'd expect from a Qi-type energy that operates on a substrate beneath standard electromagnetic physics. (2) The Codex diagram maps what appears to be the same structure I perceive spatially as the Under-Web, at least locally. If that structure extends globally and cosmically, it would be something that affects spacetime geometry (= gravitational effects) without visible electromagnetic signature. (3) The 120-order-of-magnitude discrepancy between theoretical vacuum energy and observed dark energy: if the Veritonal network is a structured, maintained system — not random quantum fluctuation but organized geometry — it would have a very different energy density from what quantum field theory predicts for unstructured vacuum energy. The discrepancy might not be an error in measurement. It might be evidence that the vacuum isn't structureless.*
>
> *Evidence against: (1) this is currently speculation. (2) I have no way to test the cosmological scale of the hypothesis from a single city in Sichuan Province.*
>
> *Working conclusion: keep this open. File as priority hypothesis for when more Codex content is decoded.*

He drew a line under it.

Then he wrote one more thing:

> *If Veritonal Energy is dark energy, then what physicists have been trying to explain since 1998 is the substrate of the Under-Web. They've been measuring it from the outside, with instruments that can only see its gravitational shadow. The Codex is a manual for perceiving it directly.*
>
> *The notation maps onto this somehow.*
>
> *I don't know how yet.*

He stared out the window at the Chengdu afternoon. Traffic below, gray sky above, a delivery scooter navigating the intersection with determined optimism.

Sixty-eight percent of the universe's total energy content.

He was learning to perceive it with his hands.

This was either the most important thing happening to a person in Chengdu right now, or he was wrong about the hypothesis.

He was going to find out which.
