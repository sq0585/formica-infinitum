# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Thirty-Two — Axis-7 Speaks

Axis-7 had not moved from the east wall during the entire exchange.

He had been aware of this. She had continued reading — or appeared to continue reading, though he suspected she had been tracking every word through the ambient field while maintaining the posture of absorbed work.

After Chen Yufei and Lady Shen left, he stayed.

He went to the east wall and sat on the floor, his back to the shelves, giving her the non-confrontational posture that their working relationship had established as appropriate.

She looked at him.

He sent in the register: *You were listening.*

She sent back: *I am always listening. I am in this archive.*

A pause. Then: *She will return. Possibly with support.*

*I expect so.*

*The archive's containment systems are operational. If she returns with intent to take rather than to learn, the systems will respond.*

He looked up at her. *What do the systems do?*

*They create an environment that is — discouraging. Not harmful. Discouraging.* A pause. *The Formicans did not build lethal protection into the knowledge archive. They built comprehension-based access. Material that is accessed without comprehension cannot be usefully used. Material that is accessed without permission encounters redirection rather than destruction.*

He thought about this. *The locks are not for keeping people out. They're for ensuring people are ready.*

*Yes.*

*And if someone enters not ready?*

*They see what they are capable of seeing. They take what they are capable of taking. What they take without the framework to understand it is not useful to them.* A pause that he was beginning to read as her version of emphasis. *This has happened before. Practitioners have entered this archive without credentials and taken containers. The containers are readable only with the communication register. Without the register, they appear to contain nothing.*

He thought about eight hundred years of cultivation traditions built on fragments of Formican knowledge that had been found and preserved without the explanatory framework that would have made them fully legible.

*The Courts' foundational texts,* he said.

*Some of them are from here. From an earlier period of this station's operation, when access was less carefully managed.* She looked at her shelving. *People found things. People took things. People built traditions around incomplete pieces of a complete system.*

He sat with this.

*That's why the Courts exist. They built around fragments.*

*Yes. It is a consistent pattern across all the major Strata stations the network has recorded. Surface civilizations encounter fragments of Formican material at intervals and build cultural and technical traditions around them. The fragments are real. The traditions are genuine. The framework is incomplete.*

He thought about the translation document he was releasing tomorrow. About forty-seven people receiving the information that their traditions were built on real foundations with incomplete explanatory frameworks.

*How do the Formican communities typically respond to this pattern?*

*They observe.* A pause. *They do not typically intervene. The Seed-carrier Inheritance Protocol is the intervention — when a Carrier is available, the complete framework becomes accessible. When no Carrier is available, the observation continues.*

*How often has a Carrier been available?*

*At this station: three times in recorded history. Including you.*

Three times. Two predecessors, documented in the archive. One current occupant.

*What happened to the surface civilizations after the previous Carriers' periods?*

*Variable.* She looked at him steadily. *One Carrier remained here until they died. Their work continued in the archive after them, preserved, but without an active practitioner to carry it to the surface. The traditions they had corrected reverted toward error over time without the continuing correction.*

*The second?*

*They left. Through the network. They took what they had learned and went further.* A pause. *The traditions here degraded faster in their absence because they had made more changes.*

He thought about Ren Wuji, who had spent four hundred years and then died in a convenience store, transmitting in four minutes. He thought about the Carriers who had come before, each with their own story and their own choices.

*What do you want me to do?*

She was quiet for a long time.

*That is not mine to specify,* she said finally. *The Inheritance Protocol provides access and support. It does not provide a mandate. What you do with the access is your choice.*

*But you have observations.*

*Yes.* A pause. *My observation: the previous Carriers worked in isolation. You have not. The alliance with Chen Yufei. The Iron Meridian relationship. Lady Shen here tonight.* Another pause. *The surface world will receive the translation document tomorrow. That is more change, faster, than either previous Carrier attempted.*

*Is that a concern?*

*It is a variable I do not have historical precedent for.* She looked at him with her compound eyes, which he had been learning to read as *focused attention* rather than the expressionlessness he'd initially perceived. *It may be the correct approach. It may produce consequences the isolated approach would not. I cannot tell you which.*

*But you activated the status ping.*

*I updated the network's data. Yes.*

*You introduced another unknown variable.*

*Yes.* A pause. *I thought it was the correct decision.*

He looked at her.

He thought about Axis-7 for a moment — 180 years in this archive, maintaining and observing, making her own decisions about when to activate things and when not to, correcting his derivation errors because they would matter at Ratio 4. Making her own judgment calls.

*Thank you,* he said. *For telling me all of this.*

*You would have worked it out eventually,* she said. *This was faster.*

He stood.

He picked up his bag.

He walked out of the archive, through the hall, through the threshold, and into the pre-dawn Chengdu morning.

He had a document to send.

He sent it at 6 AM from his apartment, the recipients' list verified three times, the document reviewed a final time, the content accurate.

He pressed send.

Then he made tea and waited to see what the world would do with true things.
