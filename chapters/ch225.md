# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-Five — What This Changes

Chen Yufei noticed the shift before he'd fully explained it to her.

"You're different around her," she said, the morning after Lady Shen's visit, watching him work through the maintenance rotation schedule with an attention she usually reserved for things that genuinely puzzled her. "Less guarded. I don't think I've ever seen you less guarded around Lady Shen Baoling."

He told her about the scroll — not the full content, which felt like something that belonged to Lady Shen's decision rather than his to spread further, but the shape of what had happened. A six-hundred-year lie, handed to him voluntarily, by someone who'd had every reason to keep it buried.

Chen Yufei was quiet for a while after he finished.

"That's not a small thing," she said finally. "I spent two years inside Iron Meridian's hierarchy. I know what it costs someone like her to admit her tradition's foundation is rotten, let alone to admit it to you specifically."

"I think she's been carrying it alone since she found it. Three days, by herself, before she decided what to do."

"And what she decided was to trust you with it instead of burying it deeper."

"Yes."

Chen Yufei considered this, turning it over with the careful precision she brought to most things that mattered to her.

"I don't think I'd have predicted this," she said. "Six months ago, if you'd told me Lady Shen Baoling would hand you evidence that could destroy her Court's legitimacy, voluntarily, with no leverage attached — I'd have assumed you were describing a trap."

"I considered that. Briefly."

"What changed your mind?"

He thought about how to answer this honestly, turning the question over the way he turned most things he didn't have a quick answer for.

"The shape of it didn't fit a trap," he said eventually. "Traps want something specific from you in exchange. She didn't ask for anything. She just handed it over and waited to see what I'd do with it, and seemed almost relieved when I didn't immediately turn it into a weapon." He paused. "People don't usually construct that level of vulnerability as a strategy. It's too expensive, and it doesn't actually buy anything if the other side doesn't take the bait the way you expect."

"So you trust her now."

"I trust this specific thing she did. That's different from trusting her completely. I'm not foolish enough to think thirty years of institutional habit dissolves because of one honest afternoon." He looked up from the rotation schedule. "But it changes what I think is possible between us. It's the first time she's shown me something true that wasn't also, somehow, advancing her own position."

---

He brought a careful, sanitized version of the development to the next Collective planning session, framed without naming the specific scroll, because the decision about how to use it still belonged to Lady Shen and not to him.

"Northern Peak's internal history is more complicated than the official mythology," he said, watching Fang Ruxue's expression as she absorbed the implication. "I think, eventually, that complication becomes relevant to the Collective's work. Not yet. When the person holding it decides she's ready."

Fang Ruxue studied him for a moment, the particular careful attention she brought to anything that touched the project's long-term structure.

"You're protecting her timeline," she said. "Even though the information would presumably strengthen the Collective's case for replacing the old hierarchies."

"It would. It would also cost her something I don't think is mine to spend on her behalf."

Something in Fang Ruxue's expression shifted toward approval, the same quiet, careful recognition she'd shown him in the outer library weeks ago over the Heaven Map.

"That's a more patient instinct than most people in your position would manage," she said. "I'll trust your judgment on it. For now."

He nodded, and turned the conversation back toward the rotation schedules and the governance draft still sitting unfinished between them, carrying the quiet weight of a trust he hadn't expected to be building with Lady Shen Baoling, and finding, somewhat to his own surprise, that he was glad to be carrying it.
