# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Twenty-Four — Liang Jingzhi and the Ring

Liang Jingzhi called him at an hour that immediately told him the matter was serious, her voice carrying the specific tight focus she reserved for genuine anomalies rather than routine findings.

"I need you to look at something with me," she said. "I don't fully trust my own read on it yet, and I want your Colony Link sensing alongside my instruments before I say anything more definite."

He met her at the geological survey office within the hour, finding her surrounded by data displays, the Pacific Ring's twelve nodes represented as a wide, layered visualization he'd grown familiar with over the months of their collaboration.

"Show me," he said.

She highlighted two of the twelve nodes — one along the deep trench off the coast, another further south, considerably distant from the first. "These two. Watch the cycle timing."

He watched the display, and after a moment, understood what had alarmed her. The two nodes' activity cycles, previously independent, now pulsed in near-perfect synchronization — a pattern that shouldn't have been possible given their distance and the Ring's original design, each structure meant to function autonomously rather than in coordinated rhythm with its distant counterparts.

"How long has this been happening?" he asked.

"Based on the historical logs, approximately six weeks. It started gradually — small drift toward alignment, easy to dismiss as measurement noise. It's not noise anymore. The synchronization is now precise enough that I'm confident it's a real physical phenomenon, not an artifact of how we're measuring it."

He extended his own sensing toward the two nodes, drawing on the geological-timescale comprehension Liang Jingzhi herself had spent six weeks teaching him, and found, beneath the visualization's clean data, something considerably harder to quantify — a faint, steady pull, present at both locations, drawing their independent cycles toward alignment in a way that felt, unmistakably, external rather than internal to the Ring's own structure.

"Something's pulling them together," he said slowly. "It's not the nodes themselves malfunctioning. Something outside the Ring is influencing both of them simultaneously."

---

"That's what I suspected, but couldn't confirm without your sensing," Liang Jingzhi said. "My instruments can measure the synchronization. They can't tell me what's causing it."

"Two nodes, out of twelve. Why only these two?"

"I don't know yet. They're not adjacent. They're not structurally similar in any way I've identified. If I had to guess — and I want to be clear this is a guess, not a finding — I'd say these two nodes might simply be the most sensitive to whatever's causing the pull. The others may be experiencing the same influence at a level too subtle to register yet."

He sat with that, understanding, with growing unease, that the implication reached considerably further than two synchronized nodes.

"You think this might spread. To the rest of the Ring."

"I think it's a real possibility I can't rule out, and I'd rather flag it to you now, while it's still contained to two nodes and manageable to study carefully, than wait until it's affecting the whole system and we're reacting under pressure instead of investigating deliberately."

---

He stayed with her through the following hours, the two of them working methodically through every possible explanation the data could support — geological causes first, the most familiar territory for her expertise, ruled out one by one as the synchronization's precision proved too exact for anything the fault systems beneath the Ring could plausibly produce on their own.

"It's not geological," she said finally, setting down her tablet with the specific frustration of a careful scientist confronting a phenomenon her own discipline couldn't explain. "Whatever this is, it's coming from somewhere your expertise understands better than mine does."

He thought, unbidden, of the Tibetan Witness Node's inbound signal, still arriving patiently after three years, still unexplained beyond Meen-4's careful, uncertain framework of contact, interference, and enforcement.

"I don't want to jump to conclusions," he said slowly, "but I think we need to look at this alongside the Witness Node's signal. I don't know that they're connected. But the timing, and the fact that neither one has an internal explanation, makes me want to rule out the connection carefully rather than dismiss it."

Liang Jingzhi's expression sharpened, the specific alertness of someone recognizing a hypothesis worth taking seriously. "You think something external to Earth might be affecting the Ring directly, not just watching from a distance."

"I don't know yet," he admitted. "I think it's the first explanation that actually accounts for both the precision of the synchronization and the fact that it started roughly around the same period the Witness Node's activity changed. I'd rather investigate that properly than assume it and act wrong, but I don't think we should ignore the possibility either."

She nodded slowly, already pulling up the timeline data to cross-reference the two events properly. "Then let's investigate it properly. Together. I don't think either of us should be carrying this alone, given what it might mean if you're right."

He agreed, and the two of them settled into the careful, patient work of cross-referencing two mysteries that had, until tonight, seemed entirely separate — the Ring's quiet synchronization and the Witness Node's patient, unexplained signal, both of them now, uncomfortably, beginning to look like they might be pieces of the same larger, still unclear picture.
