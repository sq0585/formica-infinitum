# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Forty-Eight — Meen-4's Offer

Keeper Meen-4 was in the library on a Wednesday afternoon when he arrived, which was unusual — she typically occupied the circular room deeper in the east corridor and came to the outer library only when she had reason to.

She was at the table. Not his table — the reading table on the north side, which was taller and larger than the one he'd been using. She had three containers open in front of her and was working through them in sequence.

He came in, saw her, and went to his usual corner without disturbing her. He unpacked his materials. He started working.

Twenty minutes later she sent a register signal.

He caught it at near-full fluency: *I have been reviewing the annotated translations you provided in the exchange arrangement.*

*Yes.*

*They are useful. Your annotations include observations about why specific techniques produce specific effects in human biology that are not in the Formican literature because the Formicans do not have human biology.* A pause. *This perspective is specifically what the exchange was structured to obtain.*

*I know.* He had been careful to include the human-biology observations specifically because Meen-4's exchange interest had been in the human practitioner's perspective on Formican records. *Has the usefulness changed the arrangement?*

*It has generated an extension offer.* She looked at him directly. *I have access to the Intermediate Archive's cultivation curriculum sections. Not the full curriculum — those are in the restricted section with Dimensional Weaver prerequisites. The foundational sections through the third tier.*

He waited.

*I am prepared to extend your access to these sections in exchange for a continuation of the annotation work. The volume is higher. Four-to-one: four containers of Intermediate material for each annotated translation you provide.*

He thought about this. Four-to-one was an aggressive ratio in his favor — the annotated translations were work, but they were work he was doing anyway as part of his documentation practice. The Intermediate Archive's foundational sections through the third tier contained everything in the restricted section's preliminary curriculum: the theoretical foundation for Dimensional Weaving, the complete Colony Stage development map, the historical record of Formican cultivation practice across twelve thousand years.

"What is the Intermediate Archive's third tier?" he asked aloud, then shifted to the register: *What is the third tier?*

*Historical records of the Amber Lattice School and its predecessor institutions. Records of human students who studied here over the centuries. Practice logs from cultivation sessions spanning six hundred years.*

He was quiet for a moment.

*Ren Wuji's record,* he sent.

A pause. *The scholar who preceded the most recent Carrier gap. His attendance record is in the third tier.*

He looked at his hands.

He thought about the audio files. The carefully prepared transmissions, the warm amusement in the voice, the four hundred years of someone working in complete isolation because the cultivation world he existed in didn't have the context to understand what he was doing. He thought about the predecessor's archive — two hundred years of documentation, also in isolation. He thought about the Carriers before that.

*How complete is Ren Wuji's record?*

*Partial. The Amber Lattice School was — disrupted. Some records were lost. What remains covers his first thirty years of study here and his departure.*

Disrupted. He noted this word without unpacking it yet.

*I accept the exchange arrangement,* he sent. *Same annotation format, four-to-one ratio.*

Meen-4 looked at him for a moment — the sustained compound-eye focus that he had learned to read as her version of acknowledgment rather than scrutiny.

*The first four containers will be ready for you tomorrow,* she sent.

She went back to her own work.

He sat at his table and wrote in his notebook:

> *Meen-4's offer: formal exchange extended. Intermediate Archive access through third tier, four-to-one ratio. Accepted.*
>
> *Third tier includes: Amber Lattice School historical records, human student logs, Ren Wuji's partial attendance record.*
>
> *Meen-4 said the Amber Lattice School was 'disrupted.' She didn't say how or when. This is a careful word choice for something significant.*
>
> *Tomorrow: four containers to annotate. Four containers of Intermediate material in exchange.*
>
> *The work builds what's needed.*
>
> *I will read Ren Wuji's record. Not tonight. When I have the context from the foundational sections first.*
>
> *In order. Always in order.*

He went back to the Dimensional Lock practice.

He had a feeling — not premonition, just pattern recognition — that the Intermediate Archive's third tier was going to answer questions he hadn't known to ask yet.

He was right.
