# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eighty — The Weight of It

Arc I closes. But it does not close with a dramatic door.

It closes like the end of a shift: ordinary in its logistics, significant in what has accumulated.

---

He called Gao at 7 AM on a Monday, after a night that had lasted longer than nights usually lasted in the conventional sense — eight hours in the Strata with Chen Yufei, both of them reading, the Keeper observing from its position near the north wall with what Lin Mushi had begun to identify as a species of patient amusement.

"It's early," Gao said.

"I know. I need backup thinking."

A pause. He could hear Gao sitting up. "Give me a minute."

He gave Gao a minute. He made tea. He sat at his desk with his notebooks stacked in a way that no longer made the stacking feel precarious — he had outgrown precarious. The stacks were a record now, not a worry.

Gao came back. "Go."

"Chen Yufei was in the library with me last night. Eight hours. She read what she could read, which was approximately twenty percent of what I can read now, and spent the rest of the time asking me to translate. The Keeper — the Formican — observed the entire time. At the end, it gave Chen Yufei what I can only describe as an evaluation nod. Whatever that means in terms of Strata access rights, I don't know yet."

"Okay," Gao said. He was writing. Lin Mushi could hear the pen. "And Chen Yufei?"

"She's not going to report immediately. She's gathering information." He paused. "She said — and I'm quoting — 'What you learn, you owe to those who could learn it from you.' I hadn't told her Ren Wuji said that."

Silence.

"She got there herself," Gao said.

"Yes."

"That's either a good sign or she's been reading the same texts."

"Both, I think." He turned his tea cup around in his hands. "The Northern Peak investigator has backup arriving in two weeks. That's the hard timeline. Whatever the arrangement with Chen Yufei is, it has to be established before that."

"What do you want from the arrangement?"

Lin Mushi had been thinking about this since 3 AM. "Information about how the Courts work from the inside. A contact who knows both worlds and can translate. Time — the investigator's acquisition protocol will be harder to execute if there's already an established relationship with an Iron Meridian member who can speak to the situation."

"And what does she want?"

"Access to the library. Understanding of why her framework works, at the level she can work with it." A pause. "She asked me something, leaving the Strata this morning. She said: 'What do you want this to become?' And I said I didn't know yet. She said: 'Neither do I. But I want to find out.' And I said: 'Then that's enough for now.'"

Gao was quiet for a moment.

"That's the most you've said to someone outside your notebooks," he said.

"I know."

"Good. Keep saying things." A pause. "What do you need from me right now?"

"Nothing specific. I needed to say it out loud to someone who already knows the context."

"Okay," Gao said. "I'm here. Do you want me to come over?"

"Not yet. I'm going to sleep, then write the month-five assessment. Then tonight I'll go back in."

"You're going back in tonight?"

"The Keeper communicated something I don't fully understand yet. Through the Colony Link field — it changed quality at a specific moment in a way I read as meaningful. I need to go back with more Codex decoding to understand what it was asking."

"You need to decode more of an alien language to understand what the alien in the basement was implying."

"Yes."

"This is your life now."

"Yes." Lin Mushi looked at his notebooks. "It's a good life."

---

He walked home from the tea shop where he'd made the call at 8 AM, hands in his pockets, through the Chengdu December morning.

He passed the 7-Eleven on the route — not deliberately, just the way a route could still pass a place that had changed everything about the route. The morning shift worker was unlocking the shutters. He did not know her. He had stopped working there months ago, and the store had moved on without him the way stores did, filling in the gaps with different people and different schedules.

Auntie Zhang would still come at 4 AM. He would not be there.

He had thought about this once, briefly, and not known how to think about it properly, so he had filed it under things that were true and didn't need to be resolved. She would buy her warm soy milk from whoever was on shift. She would tell them about her father's shop, or her sister's neighbor, or the chrysanthemums that came back in a corner where they shouldn't have been able to come back. Someone else would listen. Not with the same attention — he had, by now, an unusual quality of attention — but well enough.

She hadn't known what she was giving him. He hadn't known what he was being given. That was how that kind of thing worked.

He kept walking.

---

He had five months of documentation. He had twelve channels and seven auxiliary refinements on top of those twelve. He had stable Compression and library access and a provisional ally and an investigator with two weeks and a dead man's inheritance that he was beginning to understand how to carry.

He turned onto his street.

He opened his notebook to a blank page. He wrote:

> *Month 5. What I know: the world is layered. What I don't know: most of what's in the layers. What I have: six months ago this would have been terrifying. Now it seems like enough to start.*

He put the pen down.

He thought: Ren Wuji chose me in four minutes at 3 AM. He had criteria I didn't know I met. He spent four hundred years alone, building something he could only transmit in the last four minutes, and he found a stranger in a convenience store who was curious enough.

He thought: I am that stranger, now five months further in, with a library and a Keeper and a tentative ally and a dead node and an investigator on a two-week clock.

He thought: this is the right shape for a life to take. Not easy. Not finished. Just — right.

He went upstairs. He slept until noon.

When he woke, he opened the Codex to Document 6.

He had glimpsed its title once, when the previous document's final section had been scrolling — two words in notation he'd translated weeks ago. He had not opened it then because he had not yet earned the access; the comprehension lock on Document 5 had still been partially closed. Now Document 5 was fully open. Document 6 waited behind it, its title visible in the Codex's list: *The Colony Link — Field Architecture.*

He opened it.

The first readable passage said:

*What you have learned to perceive in isolation, the network has always perceived as a whole. You are not an observer of the Colony Link. You are a node within it. This document describes what that means.*

He read the sentence three times.

Then he turned to a fresh notebook page and uncapped his pen.

There was more to understand.

There was always more to understand.

That was what made it worth doing.
