# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-Eight — Dimensional Defense

He spent the morning after the assault writing up what he'd actually done, less for anyone else's benefit than for his own — the careful documentation habit that had served him since the very first weeks of decoding a dying scholar's notation, applied now to a technique that had, until yesterday, existed only in theory.

> *Dimensional Defense, working notes:*
>
> *The principle isn't new — the Codex describes it in Document 7's later sections as a structural consequence of Weaving rather than a deliberately designed weapon. A fold doesn't have to present the same geometry to every observer. If the architecture beneath the fold routes differently depending on entry timing, entry angle, even the specific Qi signature of whoever crosses, then a group entering together can be separated without any of them perceiving the separation as deliberate.*
>
> *What I built yesterday wasn't combat. It was navigation made impossible. Fourteen cultivators expecting a fight found a maze instead — not malicious, not designed to harm, simply designed to prevent coordination. The Scale Philosophy applies here as directly as it's applied to anything: don't meet force with force at the scale force expects. Change the scale of the problem until force becomes irrelevant.*

He set the pen down and considered the cost of what he'd done, the way he tried to consider the cost of everything that worked too cleanly to trust without examination.

No one had been hurt. That mattered, and he didn't want to undervalue it simply because the alternative outcomes he'd imagined in the eight minutes of warning had been considerably worse. But fourteen people had spent ninety minutes lost, disoriented, increasingly frightened in a space that had stopped obeying any rule they understood — and frightened people, he'd learned, didn't always process being frightened as gratitude for the restraint shown them. Sometimes they processed it as humiliation, and humiliation had its own consequences down the line.

Wei Canglan found him still writing, mid-morning, and read the notes over his shoulder without asking permission, a habit Lin Mushi had stopped minding months ago.

"You're worried this was too clean," Wei Canglan said. Not a question.

"I'm worried about what fourteen humiliated cultivators do next, once the immediate fear fades and what's left is the memory of being made to feel powerless without ever landing a single blow."

"Some of them will respect it. Strength that doesn't need to draw blood to win is still strength, and plenty of cultivators understand that even when they don't like being on the receiving end of it." Wei Canglan considered for a moment. "Some of them won't. Some of them will want a rematch where the rules are different — where it's their geometry instead of yours, or where the fight happens somewhere this technique doesn't apply."

"That's roughly my own assessment."

"Then you already know what you need to do next." Wei Canglan met his eyes. "Find out where this technique doesn't apply. Before someone else does."

It was, Lin Mushi thought, exactly the kind of unglamorous, necessary work that never made it into anyone's account of a victory — the careful audit of your own success, looking specifically for the conditions under which it would have failed.

He spent the rest of the day on it. The technique required, he confirmed through careful testing, a meaningful gap between his own Weaving capability and his opponents' — against a Dimensional Weaver of comparable or greater skill, the maze could in principle be navigated, or worse, actively unwound from the inside. It required advance warning sufficient to prepare the geometry, which yesterday's eight minutes had barely provided and a more careful opponent might deny entirely. It required, above all, opponents willing to be lost rather than opponents willing to simply force their way through brute structural collapse regardless of the cost to themselves or the space around them.

He wrote each limitation down plainly, without softening them, the same discipline that had kept him alive through every crisis the past two years had handed him.

> *This technique bought time and prevented harm. It is not a permanent solution to anyone determined enough, skilled enough, or simply willing to absorb damage in exchange for forcing their way through. I should treat yesterday as a successful delay, not a resolved threat.*

He closed the notebook, the morning's relief tempered now by the more sober understanding that the hardest part of yesterday's confrontation — finding out what Gou Ping and Lady Shen would each decide to do in response — hadn't actually started yet.
