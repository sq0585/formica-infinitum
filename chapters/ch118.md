# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighteen — Strata Protocols

They established the protocols on a Thursday evening, in his apartment, with the Chitin Circuit network's real-time readings on his phone and Chen Yufei's map of the district's cultivation-practitioner movement patterns spread on the desk.

It took two hours to do this properly.

Not because the protocols were complicated — they weren't, the principles were straightforward — but because establishing protocols that both people would follow under pressure required both people to understand not just what the protocols were but why each element was what it was. Protocols followed from rote memory failed at exactly the moment when circumstances changed. Protocols understood from principle adapted.

He wrote them as they agreed each item:

*Rule one: no entry when actively monitored at distance of less than 150 meters.*

The circuit network's detection radius extended to approximately eighty meters on a good day. A practitioner at Lady Shen's level could probably detect the circuits if she was actively scanning rather than passively monitoring. The 150-meter threshold gave a buffer that accounted for her capability being higher than his current circuit network's sensitivity.

"What defines 'actively monitored'?" Chen Yufei asked.

"The circuits show a sustained elevated signal with directional consistency toward the entry point. Passive ambient readings fluctuate. Active monitoring produces a steady directional signature." He showed her the readings from two nights ago when Lady Shen had been doing her circuit. "This signature."

She looked at the graph. "I wouldn't have recognized that as distinct from ambient."

"You will after a few weeks of watching it."

*Rule two: randomize approach routes. Never use the same route twice in a seven-day period.*

He had mapped six viable approach routes to the parking structure above the thin section. Routes through the hospital complex to the east, through the residential blocks to the west, through the commercial district to the south. Different time-of-day profiles for different routes — the hospital route was better at night when foot traffic was lower; the commercial route was better during the day when there were enough people that one more person meant nothing.

*Rule three: establish two alternate entry points.*

He had identified two other thin sections within the Strata's perimeter — neither as thin as the primary (the primary was forty meters; the others were sixty-five and seventy-two), but both crossable at his current Ratio 1.5 capability with sustained effort. He walked Chen Yufei through both locations on a map and then in person, testing her Compression at each.

She could cross the sixty-five-meter section. The seventy-two was at her current limit and unreliable.

"Practice at the sixty-five," he said. "I'll focus on the seventy-two — if my Compression develops, it becomes another reliable option."

"You're building redundancy."

"The primary entry point is above a parking structure that is accessible to Lady Shen. If she begins using it regularly, we need alternatives."

*Rule four: no sensitive documentation in the apartment.*

He had been keeping most of his translation work and archive notes at home. This was convenient and, he was now acknowledging, insufficiently cautious. The notebooks were moving to the archive itself, stored in the Secondary Archive's sealed containers. A few working copies would stay at his desk but nothing that couldn't be replaced.

"What about the Chitin Circuit manufacturing materials?" she asked.

He had a collection of circuit boards, engraving tools, and Qi-transducer components. Not sensitive in themselves — none of it was meaningfully readable without the inscription knowledge — but it would answer the question of what he was doing if anyone searched his apartment.

"I'll move the inscribed circuits to the Strata. The materials themselves can stay — they look like electronics components." He paused. "If someone with cultivation knowledge examines the components without the circuits present, they won't know what they are."

*Rule five: communication check-ins every six hours.*

He was sending Gao a location check every morning. This was not sufficient for the current situation. Six-hour check-ins: if either of them missed one, the other stopped moving toward the Strata and assessed the situation from a known-safe position.

"If I miss a check-in," he said, "go to Elder Zhao. Not the Iron Meridian Court directly. Elder Zhao specifically. Tell her what you know."

"You've thought about this," Chen Yufei said.

"I've thought about this for three months. I just hadn't written it down." He looked at the protocol list. "The situation has changed enough that writing it down matters now."

She read the protocols. She asked two clarifying questions. She agreed to all five.

They sat with the apartment's quiet for a moment — the circuit network pulsing its data on his phone, the translation document in its sealed container on the desk corner, the Strata below the city doing whatever the Strata did at ten PM on a Thursday.

"There's one more thing," she said.

"Yes."

"If Lady Shen enters the Strata before we do tomorrow, and if she has achieved the threshold Compression level—" She paused. "What do we do?"

He thought about this. He had thought about it.

"We go in," he said.

"Even with her inside."

"Especially with her inside. The Strata is not mine to defend. Axis-7 and the Keepers will respond to her presence in their own way. What I need to be is present when that happens, so that the response has context."

She looked at him. "You're going to introduce Lady Shen to the Formicans."

"I'm going to be present when they meet. What Lady Shen makes of the meeting is her business." He paused. "The Formicans know what they want to show and not show. I trust that."

"You trust them."

"I trust that they have been managing human contact with the archive for several hundred years and are likely to be more sophisticated about it than I am." He paused. "They told me my derivation had three errors. They gave me the Carrier notes. They've been careful."

Chen Yufei looked at the protocol list.

"All right," she said.

She picked up her bag. She stopped at the door.

"The intimacy of this," she said, "planning entry routes and check-in schedules and what to do if the other one doesn't respond—" She paused. "It's strange. I've known you for four months."

He looked at her. "Is it strange?"

She thought about it. "No," she said. "That's what's strange."

She left.

He looked at the protocol list.

He added, in his own hand, at the bottom:

*Rule six: if it goes wrong in a way neither of us planned for, the correct response is to do the thing that preserves the archive and the community below before doing the thing that preserves ourselves.*

He read this back.

He did not revise it.

He went to bed.
