# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Seventeen — What the Courts Know

Chen Yufei received the intelligence on a Wednesday morning.

A message from her contact in the Celestial Compass School — someone she'd worked alongside during an inter-Court collaboration four years ago, a woman who thought carefully and reported accurately and had maintained the connection out of the same pragmatic sense that Chen Yufei herself had for building networks that operated outside official channels.

The message said: *Northern Peak has briefed the other Courts. Not official. Unofficial senior-practitioner communication. The substance: the Chengdu investigation has identified something of significant interest. An unaffiliated source with Formican connections. They're not sharing details but the framing was: mobilization may be appropriate.*

Chen Yufei read this twice.

Then she called Lin Mushi.

"The Courts know," she said when he picked up. "Not the details. The frame: something significant, Formican connections, mobilization language."

He was quiet for a moment. "Which Courts received the briefing?"

"All five, by the sounds of it. Northern Peak used their senior-practitioner communication network. That's informal but fast — it reaches the right people within hours."

"How many of the forty-seven on the distribution list are in that senior network?"

She had to think. "Most of them. It's a small world." She paused. "They're going to receive the translation document in the context of already knowing there's something in Chengdu worth mobilizing over."

"Yes."

"That's not ideal framing for the document."

"No." He sounded like he was writing something. "But it's also not preventable. The Northern Peak has had three months to build a narrative about this situation. The forty-seven people who receive the translation document will have that narrative in their heads when they read it." A pause. "What the document does is give them accurate information to compare against the narrative. Whether the comparison produces questioning or defensive reaction depends on each person."

"You're saying the Northern Peak's narrative might actually help — it creates a primed audience."

"I'm saying people who have been told 'something significant exists here' are more prepared to engage with a document showing what the significant thing actually is than people who receive it cold." A pause. "The Northern Peak has been doing my preliminary work for me. Not intentionally."

She thought about this. It was the kind of reframe that she'd been learning to expect from him — not optimistic, not pessimistic, just a rotation of perspective that showed something she hadn't seen from the previous angle.

"Lady Shen's unofficial approach," she said. "Do you think she told them about the Strata?"

"She told them enough to justify mobilization language. What she told them about what specifically is in the Strata—" he paused — "I don't know. If she told them about the archive, the mobilization would be more urgent. The framing you've described sounds like 'significant but unclear.'"

"She's protecting the information."

"She's protecting her access to the information," he said. "Different thing. If the Courts know exactly what's in the Strata, they'll send someone more senior than Lady Shen. She doesn't want that."

Chen Yufei absorbed this.

Lady Shen Baoling, who had filed an assessment that revised the Northern Peak Court's mandate, and was now in Chengdu on her own time, working toward the entry threshold independently, while briefing her colleagues in terms vague enough to prevent them from sending reinforcement. She was building her own position while the institutional wheels turned around her.

"She's running her own play," Chen Yufei said.

"Yes. And her play intersects with ours in the Strata, which is where it gets complicated." A pause. "She's going to enter in the next few days. Whatever happens in there will determine the shape of what comes after."

"For the Courts."

"For everyone. The translation document goes out the day after she enters. Whatever the Strata shows her becomes part of the context for how she reads it."

Chen Yufei sat with this.

"You've sequenced this," she said.

"I've tried to sequence it. The Strata is not mine to manage — what happens in there is Formican-jurisdiction territory. But the document release I can time."

She looked at her message thread with her Celestial Compass contact.

"Twelve of the forty-seven are going to be at the same cultivation gathering in Leshan next weekend," she said. "It's been scheduled for months. If the document arrives before that gathering, they'll have it fresh when they're in the same room."

He was quiet for a moment. "Leshan is where my mother lives."

"I know."

"That's a coincidence."

"Yes." A pause. "A useful one, in the sense that I know the gathering's schedule and the hotel they're using. The release can be timed to reach them twenty-four hours before they convene."

"Do it," he said. "Schedule the release for—"

"When Lady Shen enters the Strata," she finished.

"Yes."

She made the note. She sent another message to her Celestial Compass contact, asking about the gathering's start time.

She was doing the same thing Lin Mushi did, she realized — treating the release as an engineering problem with timing requirements and specific audiences, thinking about the document as a thing that would land in a context she could partially shape.

She had not been thinking this way three months ago.

She thought about the fire meridian correction, the forty-percent Qi output she'd been leaving unused for nine years, the way understanding the correct mechanism had made everything downstream easier.

The same principle, applied to information strategy.

She put the phone down and started preparing the distribution.
