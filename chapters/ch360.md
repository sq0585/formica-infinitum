# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Sixty — The Paper Goes Public

The numbers arrived faster than any of them had fully anticipated, Dr. Shen Yue tracking the paper's circulation with the same rigorous attention she brought to her original research, updating Lin Mushi daily as the figures climbed.

"Forty thousand downloads," she reported, four days after publication, meeting him in the laboratory with a tablet full of analytics she'd clearly been monitoring closely. "Two hundred formal responses, across preprint servers and academic forums, within seventy-two hours. That's — I want to be precise about this — that's an extraordinary reception, for a paper this technically dense."

"Is that good or alarming?"

"Both, I think, though I'm still working through exactly how to weigh the balance." She pulled up a breakdown of the responses, sorted by category. "About sixty percent skeptical, exactly as I'd expect for genuinely novel findings. But forty percent engaged seriously, several proposing independent replication studies. I've already heard from two separate physics departments beginning their own instrumentation setups to attempt to verify the readings."

He sat with the specific weight of that, understanding, with growing clarity, exactly how quickly the careful, patient timeline she'd once described — years, she'd said, before independent verification meaningfully began — had apparently compressed into a matter of days.

"You expected this to take longer."

"Considerably longer," she admitted. "I think the timing coincided with something I hadn't fully accounted for — the broader scientific community's growing appetite for exactly this kind of anomaly, following several years of increasingly strange dark energy measurements from unrelated research groups. My paper didn't arrive in a vacuum. It arrived into a field already primed to take unexplained readings seriously."

---

The most unexpected development came four days later, when Dr. Shen Yue forwarded him an email with a brief, urgent note attached.

*This is not what I expected to happen this quickly.*

The email itself came from the Celestial Compass tradition's astronomy division, a formal, carefully worded inquiry requesting a direct conversation about the paper's methodology and, more specifically, about the geographic correlation data she'd deliberately left unexplained in the published version.

"They found the pattern," she said, when he arrived to discuss it directly. "Or at least, they're asking pointed enough questions that I suspect they've found something close to it. I don't know yet whether this is dangerous or simply an opportunity I hadn't anticipated this early."

"What do you want to do?"

"I want to respond honestly, within limits," she said, after careful consideration. "I'm not going to explain the full truth in an email. But I think ignoring a direct, serious inquiry from a group with genuine astronomical expertise would be more suspicious than engaging carefully."

---

He brought the full development to the review preparation group that evening, watching the room absorb the accelerated timeline with varying degrees of concern.

"This is happening faster than we planned for," Wei Canglan observed, his careful precision immediately assessing the practical implications. "We built our review case assuming years of gradual public awareness. This suggests months, possibly less."

"I think that's accurate," Lin Mushi said. "I don't think it changes the fundamental case we're building. But it does mean the disclosure question — how we handle a world that's beginning to genuinely notice — needs to move up our own timeline considerably."

*I would agree,* Meen-4 signaled. *I would also note that this acceleration is not entirely coincidental. The Collective's review, and Dr. Shen Yue's paper's reception, may be responding to some of the same underlying reality — this world's rate of change and visibility has genuinely increased, and multiple observers, both terrestrial and otherwise, appear to be noticing simultaneously.*

He sat with that, understanding, with a specific, sobering clarity, that everything he'd carefully built across two years — the Strata City, the Collective, Amber-0's quiet efficiency, the Ring's careful repair — had reached, apparently all at once, a threshold of visibility that neither he nor anyone around him could fully control any longer.

He wrote about it that night, the entry carrying real weight.

> *Forty thousand downloads. Physics departments beginning replication. Celestial Compass astronomers asking pointed questions. This is moving faster than any of us prepared for. I think the review clock and this paper's reception are, somehow, the same story — a world becoming visible, from more directions than I'd fully accounted for, all at once.*
