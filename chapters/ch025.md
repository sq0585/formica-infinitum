# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Twenty-Five — The Depth Beneath

At 4:30 AM, with the store empty and the fluorescent light doing its patient hum, Lin Mushi stood at the counter and looked through the floor.

Not metaphorically. Not in the way he had been looking through the floor for three weeks, perceiving the filament network as luminous threads running through a space he understood as sub-floor. This was different. This was depth.

The Codex had changed something overnight. He didn't know what exactly — he hadn't touched it since his shift began, had left it open in his pocket and let it do whatever it did when he wasn't watching. But some threshold had passed in the notation analysis, some accumulation of comprehension reaching a point of structural change, and what he was now seeing through the floor of the 7-Eleven was not the Under-Web.

It was what the Under-Web was built on.

---

The depth was vertical and it was enormous.

Beneath the filament network — beneath the sub-floor concrete and the building's foundations and what should have been several meters of Chengdu's alluvial geology — the space opened.

Not a cave. Not a cavity in the geological sense. Something that had been built to have interior volume the way a building was built to have interior volume, but at a scale that made buildings seem preliminary. The ceiling — if that was the word for the underside of what he was standing on — was vaulted, and the vaulting was deliberate, with the kind of considered geometry that only appeared in structures someone had thought about for a long time.

Walls, at the periphery of what he could perceive. Columns at intervals he couldn't quite map because the space was too large. Something on the floor of the space far below him that his perception read as surface texture rather than open ground — a floor that had been finished, not left rough.

It was a room. A very large room.

It extended north-northwest as far as he could perceive, which currently wasn't far enough.

---

He drew it over four pages of the spatial survey notebook, working fast, not stopping to review each sketch before moving to the next because the perception was effortful to sustain and he didn't know how long it would hold.

The vaulting. The columns — or what might be columns, the spacing was regular, which meant they were load-bearing rather than decorative, which meant the structure above them (the floor he was standing on) was being supported. The surface texture of the floor below: a geometric pattern, fine-grained, hexagonal at close range.

He drew the hexagonal floor pattern and stopped.

Looked at it.

Looked at the Codex diagram.

Same geometry. Same scale of repetition.

The floor of the structure below him was using the same geometric system as the Codex's notation and the Under-Web's filament layout. The geometry was not decorative. It was functional — the same function at every scale, from the molecular (the biochemist's paper, the protein superconductivity, the lattice of nerve tissue) to the spatial (the filament network, the threshold markers) to the architectural (the floor of the structure below).

One system. Every scale.

He wrote in the notebook, under the sketches:

> *4:30–5:12 AM. First perception of below-floor architectural structure.*
>
> *Scale: much larger than expected. Vaulted interior. Deliberately constructed. Columns at regular intervals. Finished floor — hexagonal geometry, fine-grained, same system as Codex notation.*
>
> *This is not natural formation. This is not geological anomaly. This is a built space.*
>
> *Who built it? For what purpose? When?*
>
> *Dimension: I cannot perceive the full extent. What I can see extends north-northwest beyond my current range.*
>
> *The Codex notation uses the word I've been translating as 'below' in contexts that now make literal sense. Not below as metaphor. Below as direction. Below as location.*
>
> *The first message: 'Beneath it is everything that has ever mattered.'*
>
> *He meant this literally.*
>
> *There is a city beneath Chengdu.*

He stared at that sentence after he wrote it.

The perception faded at 5:12, the effort of sustaining it collapsing naturally as his concentration slipped. The floor became floor again. The Chengdu morning was beginning outside — the first sounds of the city reassembling itself, a delivery truck, a distant bus.

He had four minutes before Xiao Dong arrived.

He sketched the column spacing one more time from memory, trying to estimate the implied interior height from the angle of the vault.

Twenty meters minimum. Possibly forty.

A city beneath Chengdu.

Built by someone who used hexagonal geometry at every scale.

He was going to have to go down there.

Not yet. But the map was being drawn.
