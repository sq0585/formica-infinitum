# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Nine — Chen Yufei's Question

She asked on a Tuesday, in the archive, unprompted, while he was working on the translation and she was reading a container of Formican agricultural records from approximately 800 CE.

"Are you planning to share any of this with the Courts?"

He set down his pen.

It was not a hostile question. It was the question of someone who had been holding it for three weeks, since the fire meridian correction, since her cultivation speed had begun visibly accelerating and the Iron Meridian Court's inquiries had intensified. It was the question of someone who existed between two worlds and needed to understand which direction the information was moving.

He considered for a long time.

Not to stall — she deserved a real answer, which required real thought, which was what the silence was for.

"Which Courts deserve it?" he said finally.

She looked up from the agricultural records.

"That's not an answer," she said.

"It's the only honest answer I have right now." He picked up the pen, turned it in his hands, put it down again. "The Codex comparison translation — what I've been building, the document that maps Formican notation against each Court's foundational texts — I'm planning to release that. The Courts have been operating on wrong foundations for centuries. Correcting the foundation isn't optional."

"When?"

"When it's complete enough to be legible. Two months, maybe three." He paused. "The Scale Walking manual — what I learned from it, the technique theory — I'm not ready to share that. Not because it's dangerous in itself, but because without the prerequisite training and comprehension framework, the techniques are either useless or harmful, and the prerequisite framework can't be separated from the Codex notation. Which leads back to the translation document."

"So: the translation document, eventually. Everything else: not yet."

"Not yet is not never. But yes."

She looked at the agricultural records in her hands. "The Iron Meridian has been asking about my progress. They've noticed the acceleration. Elder Zhao has been diplomatic about it but three of her colleagues have been less so." She set the records down. "If the translation document comes out through proper channels — if the Courts receive it as information rather than as a challenge — some of them will engage with it genuinely."

"And others won't."

"Some will treat it as an attack on their authority structure." She met his gaze. "Which it is. Regardless of intent."

"Accurate information that conflicts with an institution's foundational narrative is not an attack," he said. "It's information. What the institution does with it is the institution's choice."

She was quiet for a moment. "You're twenty-two."

"I know."

"You're about to destabilize a power structure that has been maintaining itself for eight hundred years."

"I'm about to share accurate information about how a phenomenon that eight hundred years of institutions have been built around actually works. What happens to those institutions as a result of receiving accurate information is their own work to do." He paused. "I'm not responsible for what people choose to do when they learn true things."

She looked at him for a long time.

"This," she said, "is the first time you've said something I disagree with."

He held her gaze. "Tell me why."

"Because you *are* responsible for what happens when you release information of this magnitude to populations who are not prepared to receive it. Not morally responsible — *practically* responsible. The consequences will happen in your vicinity, with your name attached, affecting people you know." She spread her hands. "Not an argument against releasing it. An argument for thinking carefully about the *how* rather than just the *what.*"

He thought about this.

It was not wrong.

"The how," he said.

"How many people. Which people first. What framing. Whether you release to individuals who can absorb it before it becomes institutional." She paused. "Chen Yufei — who has had eight months of context and three weeks of the fire meridian correction and has been rebuilding her framework incrementally — is not the same as a senior Iron Meridian practitioner reading this cold."

He was quiet.

She was right.

He had been thinking about *what* to release. He had not been thinking about the *what* as a function of the *to whom* and the *when* and the *in what context.*

He picked up his pen. He opened his notebook to a fresh page.

"Help me think about the how," he said.

She pulled her chair to the same side of the table as his.

They worked until the archive's ambient Qi dimmed toward the cycle that served as the Strata's equivalent of late afternoon.

This was the conversation that, two months later, would produce the specific distribution list of forty-seven recipients — people Chen Yufei knew to be honest and troubled by contradictions in their own traditions, people prepared by their own intellectual integrity to receive what the translation said rather than by who was sending it.

He had been going to release it to everyone.

She helped him understand why that was wrong, and what to do instead.

This was what the alliance was for.
