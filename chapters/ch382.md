# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 382 — Confronting Lady Shen

She was waiting for him, the next afternoon, in the same low chitin-ribbed chamber where she'd once told him about the Orthodoxy's existence. He wondered, briefly, whether she'd chosen the location deliberately — the same room where the first warning had begun, now hosting the conversation that would either close that thread or tear it open completely.

"I'm not going to deny it," she said, before he'd fully crossed the threshold. "I want that stated plainly, first, before anything else. I engaged the Containment Orthodoxy. I have maintained parallel contact with them since Arc IV — since before this review even began. I did not tell you, and I let you believe, when I described their human ally, that I was describing someone other than myself."

"Why," he said again, the same question from the night of the reveal, still unanswered.

She was quiet a moment, and when she spoke, her voice carried none of the practiced composure he was used to from her. "I believe you will succeed in everything you are attempting," she said. "I want to be clear about that first, because I think you may assume the opposite, and it isn't true. I believe you will reach Formless Scale, eventually. I believe you may reach it faster than anyone in the Collective's records has ever reached an equivalent threshold."

"That sounds like a compliment."

"It's a diagnosis." She held his gaze, unflinching. "I also believe that if you achieve Formless Scale without full understanding of Veth-Karun — without knowing, completely, what destroyed the civilization that came before you in this exact zone — Earth is at as much risk from you as it is from the Collective's sterilization threat. Perhaps more, because a mistake made by someone with your capability and your intentions would still be catastrophic, and intentions do not slow catastrophe down once it starts."

He absorbed that, feeling the familiar cold clarity of an argument that refused to be easily dismissed.

"So you went to the people who want to end me," he said, "as insurance against the version of me you're afraid might exist someday."

"I was buying insurance for Earth," she said. "Not selling it. There is a difference, and I need you to actually hear it, not merely note that I said it. If you fail — if you reach a scale beyond your judgment and something goes wrong the way it went wrong for the civilization Ren Wuji mourned his whole life — I wanted there to be something already in motion that could act quickly, rather than nothing at all. The Orthodoxy is monstrous in its remedy. But they are also, currently, the only body in existence with both the will and the capacity to intervene at the scale a genuine failure would require. I did not trust that capacity to anyone else. I still don't, fully. But I have watched you long enough, closely enough, to have started trusting something else instead."

"What."

"You," she said simply. "Not blind trust. Earned, incremental, and reluctant trust, built session by session, decision by decision, over three years of watching you choose the harder honest thing when the easier dishonest one was sitting right in front of you. I did not go to them because I wanted your civilization destroyed. I went to them because I was afraid of a version of you that has not yet appeared, and I have spent this entire review watching that version continue not to appear."

He sat with it, turning both halves of it over — the reasoning, which he could not, in complete honesty, call wrong, and the method, which he could not forgive quite yet.

"Your reasoning is sound," he said finally. "Your method was wrong. Both of those are true at the same time, and I don't think either one cancels the other."

Something in her posture shifted at that — not relief exactly, but something adjacent to it, the particular loosening of someone who had expected condemnation and received, instead, precise and unflinching judgment that left room for both halves of the truth to stand.

"I know," she said. "I have known it since I stepped into that chamber as their observer and watched your face when you understood who I was. I am not asking you to excuse the method. I am only asking you to let the reasoning matter, when you decide what happens next."

"I'm still deciding," he admitted. "I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But I heard you. All of it. That's more than I could have said an hour ago."

He left her there, in the same room the warnings had begun in, and walked back out into the Strata's ordinary afternoon light, carrying a truth that was, he suspected, going to take considerably longer than one conversation to fully settle.
