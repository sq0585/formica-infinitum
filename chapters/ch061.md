# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Sixty-One — Geological Consulting

The engineering firm was called Chuanyu Foundation Assessment, and they worked out of a third-floor office above a hot pot restaurant on the east side of the river.

The lead engineer was a woman named Ms. Fang, who had the energy of someone who had been dealing with unreliable data for long enough to have developed a specific wariness about any result that came in faster than she expected. She had been wary of Lin Mushi's first report. She had been less wary after the second, when one of his predicted subsurface anomalies was confirmed by a ground-penetrating radar survey she'd ordered as a check.

She was now what he'd classify as cautiously confident — the position of someone who didn't understand why he was right but had enough data to accept that he was usually right.

"The Longhua site," she said, on Wednesday morning, handing him a folder. "Residential development, eighty units. Foundation assessment needed before we submit for permit. The soil reports are clean but the senior engineer doesn't like the feel of the east corner."

Lin Mushi took the folder. He noted the address. He noted the senior engineer's instinct about the east corner, which he'd learned to treat with respect — people who worked with soil long enough sometimes developed a rough sensitivity to anomalies, not at anything close to his resolution, but a genuine prior signal worth incorporating.

"When do you need it?"

"End of the week. Can you do a site walk today?"

He could do a site walk today. He was going to be crossing a threshold in a Strata entry point in four hours, but the site walk would take ninety minutes and he could eat something and cross the threshold after.

He said yes.

---

The Longhua site was a cleared lot, the kind that had once been several older buildings and had been levelled and was now waiting for the concrete-and-rebar future that the city was assembling from pieces like it. He walked the perimeter first, then the interior in a grid pattern, notebook in hand, perception extended to survey mode.

The east corner, as the senior engineer had suspected, was wrong.

Not dramatically wrong. The soil reports were technically accurate — the surface geology was clean. But at approximately six meters depth, running diagonally from the northeast corner toward the center, there was a water table incursion that the standard reports hadn't caught because it was intermittent: present after rain, absent in dry periods, which meant the standard survey had been conducted in a dry period and had found nothing.

He paced the exact boundary of the incursion. He noted the depth and trajectory. He noted the seasonal variance his perception could detect in the moisture signature — the soil at that boundary had specific Qi characteristics that matched what he'd learned to associate with periodic saturation.

He wrote the report standing at the site, filling in his standard template with measurements that were internally consistent and externally verifiable and that he would not be able to explain to Ms. Fang's satisfaction if she asked how he'd obtained them.

He listed surface indicators: minor differential settling in the old building's remnant foundation along the anomaly's line, which he genuinely could see and which was consistent with his Qi-derived assessment. He listed the methodology as *detailed surface observation and multi-point probe assessment*, which was not false and was not the whole truth.

He submitted the report by email from the site.

Ms. Fang replied within an hour: *Can you be more specific on what 'multi-point probe assessment' means?*

He replied: *I use specialized tactile assessment methods developed through fieldwork. I'm working on a formal methodology document. The results are verifiable through standard GPR survey if you want a check.*

She ordered the check. The check confirmed.

She did not ask further questions about methodology.

---

He bought lunch from a street stall on the way home — a jianbing, quick and good — and ate it walking, thinking about the relationship between the geological consulting and the Codex.

The Formicans had built the Under-Web. They had built the Strata below cities. They had worked, for millions of years, with the geological substrate of the planet — and in doing so had produced a network of Qi flows that human beings had been living above, and building above, and sometimes building well or badly in relation to, without knowing it.

The old cities and the good buildings were where they were because someone, somewhere, had felt something without knowing what they felt.

He was learning to feel it with precision.

> *Geological consulting as cultivation application: I am finding what is true below the surface and reporting it in terms the surface world can use. The gap between what I perceive and what I can explain is wide, and I am bridging it with inference and verifiable output.*
>
> *This is the practical discipline of working at the correct scale: I perceive at Micro Scale, I operate in the world at ordinary scale, I report in a language ordinary-scale people can work with.*
>
> *Scale philosophy, applied to professional work.*

He finished the jianbing. He went home.

He had three hours until the threshold crossing.

He needed to eat properly and sleep an hour and prepare.

He had work to do both above and below the ground, and neither could wait any longer.
