# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Ten — Geological Time and Qi

The fieldwork began at a fault system two hours outside the metropolitan boundary, a stretch of exposed rock face that meant nothing to him the first morning beyond its obvious, ordinary geology — grey stone, striated in places, unremarkable to eyes that hadn't yet learned what to look for.

"Tell me what you see," Liang Jingzhi said, the two of them standing at the base of the exposure, her tablet already recording measurements he didn't yet understand the purpose of.

"Rock. Layers. I assume the layers mean something."

"They do. But that's the surface answer. I want the Colony Link answer. What does it feel like, when you extend your sensing into it?"

He closed his eyes and reached, the way he'd learned to reach toward anything within range — and found, to his mild frustration, almost nothing. A faint, static hum, present but inert, nothing like the vivid emotional texture he could read from a resident three corridors away.

"Nothing. Or almost nothing. It's just — quiet."

"That's because you're listening for the wrong tempo," she said, not unkindly. "You're used to reading change. A mood shifting, a conversation's texture moving moment to moment. This rock is changing too. It's just changing on a scale your sensing isn't tuned to catch."

---

The first week taught him more about patience than about geology directly. Liang Jingzhi walked him through the fault system's history the way a historian might walk through a civilization's — not as a single fixed fact, but as an accumulation, layer building on layer, each one representing pressures and releases that had unfolded across intervals he found genuinely difficult to hold in his mind without his attention sliding away from the scale entirely.

"This layer," she said, on the fourth day, pointing to a dark band running through the exposed rock, "took approximately eighty thousand years to form. Sediment, accumulating grain by grain, compressed slowly into stone. Try to feel it. Not as a static object. As a process, still technically ongoing, just moving too slowly for you to have ever noticed motion in it before."

He tried, closing his eyes, extending his sensing into the rock the way he'd learned to extend it into people — and found, this time, something faint but real. Not the sharp emotional texture of a living mind, but a slow, patient pressure, present and accumulating, moving at a pace so far beneath his usual attention that he'd simply never thought to listen for it before.

"I feel it," he said, surprised by his own voice. "It's — slow. Almost still. But not actually still."

"That's the beginning of it," she said, something satisfied entering her voice. "Geological time doesn't feel like stillness once you actually learn to read it. It feels like extremely patient motion. The trick is recalibrating what patient means."

---

By the third week, the work had deepened into something closer to genuine collaboration than instruction — Liang Jingzhi's professional expertise meeting his growing capacity to sense what she'd spent nineteen years learning to calculate, the two of them cross-referencing her seismic data against what his Colony Link could now, increasingly, perceive directly.

"This is remarkable," she admitted one evening, reviewing a day's readings against her own instruments. "Your sensing is starting to catch pressure differentials I usually need weeks of instrument data to confirm. Not more accurate than the instruments. But faster, and increasingly close in accuracy."

"I couldn't do any of it without your framework, though. I wouldn't know what I was feeling if you hadn't spent three weeks teaching me what patient actually means at this scale."

"That's collaboration," she said simply. "Neither of us built this alone."

He thought, that night, about how considerably his relationship to time itself had shifted across the past three weeks — the accumulated grains of eighty thousand years no longer an abstraction, but something he could, however faintly, feel directly, sitting alongside his awareness the same way a resident's mood or a Circuit node's status had once been the outer limit of what he could sense.

He wrote about it in his notebook, the entry longer than most of his recent ones.

> *Six weeks isn't finished yet, but something has already changed that I don't think reverses. I used to sense a city in an evening. I'm learning, slowly, painfully, to sense a fault line across eighty thousand years. It's not more power. It's a different relationship to what counts as motion at all. Liang Jingzhi didn't teach me a technique. She taught me to recalibrate what patience means, and I think that recalibration is going to matter for the rest of whatever I eventually become.*

He closed the notebook, the fault system's slow, patient pressure still humming faintly at the edge of his awareness even now, days removed from the site itself, and understood, for the first time with real clarity, exactly what threshold Meen-4 had been quietly preparing him to approach.
