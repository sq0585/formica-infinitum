# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty — The Witness Node, Decoded

Meen-4 requested his presence with an urgency that reached him even through the deliberately formal phrasing of her message, and he understood, walking toward the administrative chamber, that whatever she'd found had finally resolved into something concrete.

*The signal is fully decoded,* she signaled, the moment he arrived, no preamble, the weight of the announcement carrying through even her characteristically measured cadence. *I would like to walk through it with you carefully. The content is significant.*

He sat down across from her, the decoded transmission displayed between them in a format he'd grown fluent enough with, over the months, to read directly rather than requiring her full translation.

*Earth's formal classification,* Meen-4 began, *is confirmed as Quarantine Zone QZ-7. One of nine such zones currently maintained under active Collective observation across this region of the galaxy.*

He sat with the designation, the specific, bureaucratic coldness of it landing harder than he'd expected — not a world, not a civilization with two-year-old cities and grieving mothers and careful, patient teachers, but a zone, numbered, one of nine.

"Nine," he said. "There are eight other worlds under the same restriction."

*As far as this transmission indicates, yes.* She continued through the decoded content methodically. *The signal includes a three-year activity log — records of everything the Witness Node has transmitted outward since its reactivation, cross-referenced against what appears to be a standard monitoring protocol applied uniformly across all nine zones.*

"And the log itself. What does it actually contain?"

*Everything you might expect, given what has changed here over the past three years,* she signaled. *Your own cultivation progression, in general terms. The Strata City's construction and growth. Dr. Shen Yue's research, flagged specifically as an emerging disclosure risk. The Ring's synchronization, recorded as a physical anomaly requiring further observation.* A pause. *And, appended to the activity log: a formal Zone Review Process, now initiated, with an associated timeline of approximately eighteen months to conclusion.*

---

He felt the weight of the phrase settle into his chest with a specificity the earlier, more abstract warnings hadn't quite carried.

"Eighteen months. Conclusion of what, exactly?"

*The transmission does not specify the review's possible outcomes in detail,* Meen-4 admitted. *It describes only that the review exists, that it is now active, and that its conclusion will determine QZ-7's continued classification going forward.* She paused, the careful stillness he'd learned to recognize as her preparing to deliver something particularly difficult. *I would not want to overstate what I do not know. But I would also not want to understate what a review, by its nature, implies. A classification under active review is a classification the Collective is actively reconsidering. It would not undertake that reconsideration without genuine possible outcomes on the table.*

"Including outcomes that change everything."

*Including outcomes that change everything,* she confirmed.

---

He sat with the full weight of it for a long while, the eighteen-month timeline settling into his chest with a gravity that felt, finally, entirely concrete rather than the diffuse, mounting unease the Ring's synchronization and the Witness Node's intensifying signal had carried until now.

"Eighteen months," he said again, testing the number's weight. "That's not abstract anymore. That's a clock."

*It is a clock,* Meen-4 agreed. *I would encourage you to treat it as exactly that — not a doom, not a certainty of catastrophe, but a genuine, bounded interval in which the outcome remains, as far as I can determine, genuinely undecided. What happens within that interval will likely matter considerably more than anything that has happened across the preceding forty million years of passive observation.*

He thought of everything he'd built across two years — the Strata City, the Collective, the careful, deliberate relationships that had accumulated one honest choice at a time — and understood, sitting with the weight of an eighteen-month deadline now formally, concretely real, that all of it was about to be judged by a body he'd never met, according to standards he didn't yet fully understand.

"I need to understand what the Collective actually values," he said slowly. "If this is a review, there's presumably a standard it's being reviewed against."

*I agree,* Meen-4 signaled. *I would like to begin teaching you what I know of Collective governance, formally and in depth, starting as soon as you are ready. I do not believe eighteen months is an abundant amount of time, given the scale of what needs to be understood.*

He nodded, the clock's weight settling into him fully now, and understood, standing to leave the chamber, that Movement Four of this impossible journey had just, quietly and irrevocably, begun in earnest.
