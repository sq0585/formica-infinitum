# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 494 — Axis-7's Last Word

She came the night before, without warning, the way she always came, and settled into the pocket dimension laboratory's far corner with a stillness that told him, immediately, this was not an ordinary visit.

He didn't speak first. He had learned, across five years of increasingly frequent encounters, that Axis-7's silences carried their own particular weight, and that filling them prematurely usually cost more than it gained. So he simply continued his own quiet preparation — checking the relay one final time, reviewing Dr. Shen Yue's monitoring readiness, the ordinary, careful business of a man readying himself for something enormous — and let her stand there, watching, exactly as she had watched him from the outer library's shadows four years ago.

Forty minutes passed. He counted them, not out of impatience, but because the specific discipline of noticing time had become, across five years, one of the few constants he trusted to hold steady even now.

When she finally spoke, her voice carried a weight he had never once heard from her before — not the careful, precise formality of every previous exchange, but something closer to a confession, offered plainly, without preamble.

"Ren Wuji was my student's teacher," she said.

He went very still, the sentence landing with a force he hadn't braced for, though he understood, even in the first shock of it, that she had chosen her words with the same careful precision she brought to everything.

"You are their legacy," she continued, before he could ask the dozen questions already forming.

He opened his mouth, and closed it again, understanding, somewhere beneath the urgent pull of curiosity, that this was not a moment built for questions.

"I destroyed what came before," she said, and something in her voice, for the first time in four years, carried a texture that might genuinely have been grief. "I would like to see what comes after."

She said nothing further. She simply turned, the same quiet, unhurried motion he'd watched her make a hundred times before, and departed the way she always departed — without ceremony, without farewell, the laboratory's warm light settling back into its ordinary stillness as if she had never been there at all.

He sat with it a long while, the specific vertigo of an entire hidden lineage suddenly, briefly, glimpsed and then withdrawn before he could ask a single clarifying question. Ren Wuji's own teacher. Axis-7's student, whoever they had been, connected somehow to the man who had died in a convenience store and left a boy considerably more curious than prepared. Something Axis-7 herself had destroyed, long enough ago that the grief in her voice had sounded almost geological, worn smooth by however many centuries had passed since.

He reached for the notebook, and found his hand hovering a moment before he actually began writing, the old, familiar pull of curiosity insisting he chase the thread immediately, follow it back through whatever records the Archive might hold, demand from Meen-4 every fragment of history that might explain what Axis-7 had just handed him.

He did not chase it.

*Observation 515,* he wrote instead, plainly, the same discipline that had carried him through 7.4-N's unresolved mystery, through every question he had learned, across five years, to set down rather than pursue before its proper time. *Axis-7 visited tonight and left me a fragment of history I did not ask for and cannot currently place. Ren Wuji, her student, some destruction she has never named. I want to chase this. I am not going to. Tomorrow is too large to prepare for while also unraveling forty minutes of silence and three enormous sentences from someone who has never once, in four years, said anything without meaning exactly what she said and nothing more than that.*

*Whatever this is, it will keep. I have learned, by now, that the things worth understanding almost always do.*

He closed the notebook, and sat a while longer in the quiet, feeling the particular, specific weight of a question he was choosing, deliberately, not to answer — not out of incuriosity, but out of the same hard-won discipline that had carried him this far, intact, toward whatever waited at the actual threshold ahead.

Tomorrow was too large for anything else. He let Axis-7's words settle, unresolved, into the same patient category as everything else he had learned, across this long and extraordinary life, to hold without needing to solve.
