# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifteen — Month Nine — Lady Shen Arrives

She arrived in Chengdu for the second time on a Thursday, nine months after Ren Wuji had died.

Lin Mushi knew because his Chitin Circuit network — seven units now, distributed across the district's key Qi-flow intersections — registered a new structured presence at 6 PM moving from the direction of the airport. He saw it on his phone as a spike in the ambient field readings, then a sustained elevated signal that moved through the network's coverage area on a path consistent with a vehicle heading toward the city center.

He monitored it until the signal stopped moving at a hotel two kilometers north of the Strata entry point.

Then he sent a message to Chen Yufei: *She's back.*

Her reply came in two minutes: *I know. Elder Kuo contacted me this morning. Different mandate this time.*

He wrote back: *What mandate?*

*Personal investigation. Not Court business. She's here on her own time.*

He sat with this.

Lady Shen Baoling, senior Northern Peak Court operative, who had reviewed an acquisition report and volunteered, who had done three weeks of reconnaissance and apparently filed an assessment that caused the Northern Peak Court to revise its entire protocol. She was now in Chengdu on her own time.

He thought about what her personal interest in the situation was, separate from the institutional interest.

He thought about what she had seen during her reconnaissance that she had not put in the official report.

He wrote in his notebook:

> *Lady Shen Baoling: second arrival. Personal investigation, not Court mandate. This is a different calculation.*
>
> *Institutional motivation: she was following an acquisition mandate. Now she isn't. She's here because she wants something for herself.*
>
> *What does she want? Based on her background: she was the practitioner who located the previous unaffiliated acquisition. She is the Court's most accomplished operative. If she's acting independently, she has assessed that what's here is worth pursuing outside institutional channels.*
>
> *Specifically: she saw the Strata's threshold on her reconnaissance. She didn't enter because she couldn't — entry requires Ratio 1.4 minimum and she was at 1.2-1.3 at the time.*
>
> *She is now here to do what she couldn't do last time. She has been working on her Compression.*

He looked at this last line.

He checked the circuit network's historical readings for the past three weeks.

There was a pattern he hadn't noted specifically until now: a regular low-level Qi flux in the area of the parking structure above the thin section, starting about ten days after Lady Shen's first departure. Small, not alarming by itself, but consistent and regular in the way of someone practicing a technique in the same location repeatedly.

She had been practicing at the thin section.

She had been here for ten days before today.

He had not caught her on the circuit network until she'd gone to the hotel, because she'd been coming in from a different direction and the hotel was in a coverage gap.

She had been practicing at the Strata's entry point for ten days.

He wrote: *She has been here longer than I thought. She knows the entry point. She has been working toward the compression threshold. She is significantly closer to 1.4 than she was three weeks ago.*

He thought about the older Keeper's maintenance function. About Axis-7's three weeks of watching before she spoke. About the archive's access tiers and what each tier's lock tested.

He thought about what would happen if Lady Shen Baoling entered the Strata.

He had no way to prevent this. The threshold was not his to close. The Strata was not his to defend. He was a Carrier — a practitioner with acknowledged relationship with the station — but the Strata's authority structure was the Formicans', not his.

What he could do: make sure that when she entered, she entered a situation she hadn't prepared for.

He called Gao.

"I need you to come to the Strata tomorrow," he said.

A pause. "I thought you said non-practitioners can't enter."

"They can, at standard Compression. You've been watching me practice — you have observation data. I need a witness who can describe what happens from the outside."

"What's going to happen from the outside?"

"Something that will establish the situation clearly," Lin Mushi said. "Bring a notebook."

"I always bring a notebook when you call me like this," Gao said.

This was true. This was one of the things he valued about Gao.

"Tomorrow night," he said. "I'll explain on the way."

He put the phone down and went to tell the Keeper station that a guest would be visiting.

He had learned, in the past three weeks, how to use the station's communication system to send simple messages to the Keepers on duty. The message he sent was: *one additional visitor tomorrow, non-Seed practitioner, friendly.*

The Keeper station's acknowledgment came back in the floor's recognition pattern — a specific illumination sequence he'd been learning to read.

He interpreted it as: *noted.*

He went back to the translation document.

Sixty-five pages. He needed one more.

He wrote the final page that night.

The document was ready.

Everything was converging at once.

He had always known this would happen. He had been building toward it. The arc was arriving at the place it had been heading since the first week of the alliance with Chen Yufei.

He was ready.

He thought he was ready.

He went to bed and discovered, lying in the dark, that being ready and feeling ready were different things, and that the difference was where the work of the next week lived.
