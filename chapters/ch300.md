# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred — Month 26, Inventory

He took the inventory on a Sunday, the way he'd taken most of his inventories since the habit had first formed sometime in the disoriented early weeks after Ren Wuji's death — sitting alone at the small table in his room with the notebook open in front of him, a cup of tea going cold beside it, the quiet discipline of counting exactly what this impossible world had made of him so far.

Two years and two months. He wrote the number first, the way he always did, because everything else in the entry only made sense measured against it — against the specific, ordinary fact that all of this, the Strata and the Colony Link and the careful accumulated weight of everyone he'd come to be responsible for, had grown out of a convenience store shift that had ended in a stranger's dying hand closing around his own.

He worked through the categories methodically, the same structure he'd used for every inventory before this one, resisting the urge to let any single entry run longer than the facts required.

*Dimensional Weaver: mature.* Eight simultaneous pocket dimensions, held without conscious effort, background architecture rather than active technique. The residential wing built from that maturity — four rooms, then more, an elderly woman no longer cold in a fourth-floor walk-up she'd finally left behind.

*Colony Link: city-scale.* The whole of Chengdu, twenty million people's accumulated emotional weather, arrived without warning during ordinary meditation and now managed through a discipline he'd built for himself before he'd needed it rather than after — five minutes of breath, five minutes of simply being one located person, before letting the vast field back in each evening.

*Anchors: three.* Chen Yufei, whose trust had accumulated slowly across two years of shared danger until it had become something steadier than either of them had initially dared name. Liang Jingzhi, whose reasoning had been clear and practical and kind, in exactly that order, arrived at through careful analysis rather than instinct and no less genuine for the difference. Dr. Shen Yue, whose answer had come in under a minute and had never once wavered since.

*Strata population: 340.* The residential wing filling slowly, deliberately, each new resident arriving not for cultivation advancement but for something plainer and more human — a room with no stairs, a window that felt real even knowing it wasn't, warmth calibrated by an old woman's single honest complaint rather than by anyone's assumption of what she needed.

*Collective membership: 112.* Fang Ruxue's careful governance framework now drafted, soon to be presented, built to survive her own eventual absence rather than to depend on it. A laboratory expanded from one cramped workstation into eight, Dr. Shen Yue's manuscript already moving through whatever slow institutional process would eventually decide whether the rest of the world was ready to start asking questions about where its anomalous readings actually came from.

He paused at the next line, the pen hovering, because this was the entry that mattered most and he wanted to get its wording right.

*Missing: Planetary Architect.*

He sat with the phrase for a long moment before writing it, understanding, even as he set the words down, that he was naming something he could feel approaching without yet being able to describe its shape — a threshold Meen-4 had mentioned only in passing, months ago, the next tier past the Colony Link's current city-scale reach, requiring, she'd said, something he didn't yet have: a geological-timescale comprehension of Qi that only someone like Liang Jingzhi could teach him, and a regional Colony Link expansion he hadn't yet attempted.

He thought about Veth-Karun as he wrote it — power reached for without comprehension, dissolved in less time than a single breath, three untranslated symbols the only trace left behind. He thought about the Pelagic Archive, sealed and waiting, forty million years of careful Isolationist caution sitting patiently at the bottom of an ocean he had no immediate plans to visit. He thought about the deep-space signal, dormant and distant, a piece of his own Formican inheritance sitting quietly in the outer dark, transmitting to no one in particular that it was still, somehow, there.

None of it was urgent yet. Liang Jingzhi had taught him that discipline only weeks ago, and he found himself grateful for it now, sitting with three separate mysteries that none of them demanded immediate action, only honest acknowledgment that they existed and were waiting.

He closed the entry the way he'd closed every inventory before it, with the small, deliberate honesty that had become, somewhere along the way, as much a part of his cultivation as anything Meen-4 had ever taught him.

> *Two years, two months. Eight pocket dimensions I no longer have to think about holding. A city's worth of feeling arriving each evening, managed now instead of merely survived. Three people who've chosen, deliberately, to anchor whatever I'm becoming to something steady. Three hundred and forty people calling this place home for reasons that have nothing to do with power. What's missing isn't a gap I'm anxious about. It's a threshold I can feel approaching the way you feel weather changing before the sky actually shows it — not yet here, but close enough that I've started, quietly, getting ready.*

He closed the notebook, finished the last of his cold tea, and sat for a while longer in the small, quiet room that had become, against every reasonable expectation two years ago, something he was allowed to call home — the Strata's vast, expanding architecture humming faintly at the edge of his awareness, patient, waiting, exactly as unfinished as everything worth building always is.
