# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifty-Four — What Compression Means

He spent a week on the theoretical geometry before attempting anything physical.

This was not timidity. It was the Protocol's explicit instruction: *map the geometry of the compression fully in notation before attempting practice. The geometry exists first in understanding; the body follows understanding, not the other way.* He had enough experience now with how the comprehension lock worked to know this was not advisory — it was architectural. He couldn't compress an interface geometry he hadn't understood.

He understood it on paper first.

---

The geometry was this:

At normal scale, his biological interface to the Veritonal network occupied a volume roughly corresponding to his body — his channels ran through him, his perception extended outward from that volume, his engagement with the substrate was approximately body-scale. Call this Ratio 1: one-to-one correspondence between physical body and interface volume.

Compression reduced the interface scale while maintaining the channel architecture within a smaller volume. Ratio 2 meant the interface operated within a volume half his normal dimensions in each axis — roughly one-eighth the total volume. Ratio 3: one-quarter dimensions, one-sixty-fourth volume. And so on. The physical body didn't change at first; the interface geometry compressed, and the body was pulled into correspondence with it progressively as the compression deepened.

The Codex specified several stable Compression ratios. Ratio 1 was normal operation. Ratio 1.5 was a preliminary state, useful for training. Ratio 2 and beyond were the working states.

What changed at each ratio: the scale of phenomena that became perceptible and manipulable. At Ratio 1, the practitioner could work with building-scale Qi flows. At Ratio 2, molecular-scale phenomena became accessible. At Ratio 3 and beyond, the practitioner began to interact directly with the substrate network at the scale of its own organizational structure — which was why Compression was the key to actually doing anything in the Strata, rather than simply perceiving it.

He wrote the summary:

> *Compression mechanics (preliminary):*
>
> *The interface geometry compresses first. The body follows. Not the other way.*
>
> *Each ratio shift: new scale of perceptible and manipulable phenomena.*
>
> *Ratio 1: building/ambient scale. Ratio 2: molecular. Ratio 3+: substrate organizational scale.*
>
> *To cross the threshold, I need Ratio 1.5 minimum — enough compression to pass through the interface membrane at the boundary. The membrane is calibrated to the Strata's entry requirements. Normal-scale passage would be like trying to read fine print at arm's length.*
>
> *Practical target: achieve stable Ratio 1.5 in the next week. Attempt threshold crossing after.*
>
> *The body will follow the geometry. Hold the geometry. Trust the body.*

He had written that last line from the Codex's notation. He had also underlined it because it was the kind of instruction that was both practical and the kind of thing he'd need to remember in a moment when his body was doing something unusual and his instinct was to fight it.

*Hold the geometry. Trust the body.*

He spent the rest of the week holding the geometry in his mind, turning it in his attention the way you turned a physical object to understand its shape, until the shape was familiar enough that he could hold it without effort.

Then he would try.
