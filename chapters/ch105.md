# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Five — Month Eight — The Colony Feeling

It started during a geological survey in the Longhua district, three weeks after his first Strata visit with Chen Yufei.

He was walking a construction site for Ms. Fang's firm — a foundation assessment on a residential development, the kind of work he could now do in ninety minutes what would have taken a conventional survey team three days — when he became aware of something below the building site that was different from the standard Under-Web signal.

He had been perceiving the Under-Web for eight months. He knew its texture the way you knew the temperature of a room you'd spent a lot of time in: not precisely, not as a number, but as a quality that varied from the baseline in ways that were immediately legible.

This was not the Under-Web.

The signal was deeper — Layer 3, by his estimate, which was below his usual perception range. It was not the filament network's ambient Qi but something more organized, more interior. It had the quality of a field rather than a flow.

A Colony Link field.

Not individual Formicans — he couldn't perceive them separately at this distance and through this much geology. But the collective field of their presence. The same phenomenon he'd observed in the park with the ant colony, scaled up by the size and sophistication of the community generating it: a distributed intelligence, its signatures too numerous and too integrated to resolve into components, perceivable only as a whole.

He stood in the middle of the construction site with the morning crews moving around him and felt, for the first time, that the building was not empty below.

He noted the time. He noted the location. He noted the quality of the signal.

Then he finished his foundation survey, delivered the report, and walked home thinking about what it meant to perceive a community you'd never met.

---

He mapped the signal over the next week.

It was not uniform across the city. It concentrated in specific areas — the Strata's known Layer 3 regions, which corresponded to the depth readings he'd been able to get through the archive. The signal was strongest below the city's older districts, weaker in the newer development zones to the east where the geological formation was shallower. It was absent entirely in areas where the Strata's structure was disrupted.

He could tell, from the surface, where the Formican community lived.

Not individual locations. Not specific chambers or halls. The general geography of habitation, a warmth distributed through the ground beneath the city like the warmth he'd felt in his mother's kitchen.

The Colony Link had always worked this way for ant colonies. A colony's field didn't reveal the location of specific ants. It revealed the shape of the collective.

He wrote:

> *Month 8. Colony Link expansion: Formican community perception at surface level. Signal character: collective field, not individual signatures. Perceivable from surface through approximately 150–200 meters of geology.*
>
> *What this tells me: the Formican community beneath Chengdu is substantial. The field's strength and distribution suggests several hundred individuals at minimum, possibly more — my calibration for Formican field strength is still developing.*
>
> *Geographical distribution: concentrated below older districts. Absent below 30-year-old development zones. Stronger below areas with well-maintained Under-Web structure.*
>
> *Implication: the Formican community and the Under-Web health are correlated. They are not using the network — the network is their environment. Degraded nodes create degraded living conditions for the community below.*
>
> *My node depletion: I owe them a repair. Not just the Under-Web abstract system. Specific beings whose environment I degraded.*

He looked at this entry.

He added:

> *This is more personal than 'I damaged the network.' The network is inhabited.*

He began planning the tributary repair work in earnest.

Not as an abstract remediation exercise. As something specific.

He had neighbors he hadn't met.
