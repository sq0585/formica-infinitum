# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifty-Nine — Dr. Shen Yue's Paper, Accepted

The text arrived at 3:04 in the morning, and Lin Mushi woke to it with the specific, disoriented clarity of a phone screen's light cutting through sleep, the message brief enough that its full weight took a moment to register.

*Accepted. Just heard. I couldn't wait until morning to tell you.*

He sat up fully, reading it a second time to be certain, and felt something rise in his chest that took him a moment to properly name — not surprise, exactly, since he'd trusted her confidence in the work from the beginning, but a specific, bright relief that arrived with more force than he'd expected, given how long they'd all been preparing for this exact moment.

He called her rather than texting back, understanding, somehow, that this deserved more than a written reply.

"Tell me everything," he said, once she'd picked up, her voice carrying an energy he'd rarely heard from her — the careful, measured composure she brought to almost everything replaced, for once, by something unguarded and genuinely delighted.

"The revised submission, the one with the additional controls, went through full review this time," she said, the words arriving faster than her usual deliberate pace. "Three reviewers, all recommending acceptance with minor revisions. Minor. After everything, after the first rejection, after months of anticipating years more of this — accepted, with minor revisions."

"How do you feel?"

"Awake," she said, something like a laugh entering her voice. "Entirely, completely awake, at three in the morning, and I don't think I'm going back to sleep tonight."

---

He found himself smiling, genuinely, the specific late-hour intimacy of the call carrying a warmth that felt, after months of careful, methodical review preparation, like something purely, uncomplicatedly good.

"You should be proud," he said. "This wasn't luck. This was years of careful, rigorous work, finally landing exactly where it deserved to land."

"I am proud," she admitted, something more vulnerable entering her voice than her usual scientific composure typically allowed. "I don't say that often, about my own work. I default to analysis over feeling, you know this about me. But tonight, I think I'm allowed to simply feel proud, without immediately qualifying it with everything that comes next."

"Everything that comes next," he repeated, understanding, even in the moment's genuine warmth, that the acceptance represented not an ending but a beginning — the first real, public thread in a process she'd warned him, months ago, would eventually reach considerably further than either of them could fully anticipate.

"I know," she said, hearing the shift in his tone. "I promise, we'll think about it properly, starting tomorrow. Tonight, I just wanted you to know first. Before the formal notification, before anyone else. You've been part of this since the draft, and I wanted you to hear it from me directly, at whatever hour it happened to arrive."

"I'm glad you called," he said, meaning it fully. "Thank you for trusting me with this moment, exactly as it is, before it becomes anything more complicated."

---

He sat awake for a while after the call ended, the residential wing quiet around him in the deep hours of the night, and found himself thinking, not about the eighteen-month review or the disclosure question this acceptance would inevitably accelerate, but simply about the specific, uncomplicated joy in Dr. Shen Yue's voice — years of careful, rigorous work, finally, publicly validated.

He wrote about it before finally returning to sleep, the entry short and genuinely warm.

> *Dr. Shen Yue's paper was accepted, 3 AM, and she called immediately rather than waiting for morning. I've rarely heard her sound that unguarded. Whatever this means for the disclosure question, whatever comes next in the considerably more complicated conversation this acceptance will eventually demand — tonight, I just want to hold onto how genuinely happy she sounded. Some victories deserve to be simply celebrated, at least for one night, before the world asks anything more of them.*
