# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 463 — What This Requires

The shape of the answer, once he'd let himself sit with it for a few days, turned out to be far more demanding than the elegant simplicity of the insight itself had suggested.

He worked it through methodically, the way he worked through everything, breaking the theoretical breakthrough into its actual practical requirements and testing each one against what he currently understood of his own capability.

*Requirement one,* he wrote. *The Colony Link must extend to stellar scale. Currently it operates at planetary scale — comprehensive, mature, but bounded to Earth and, in a limited sense, the immediate solar neighborhood I can now perceive since the Stellar Scale crossing. The dissolution zone sits thousands of light-years distant. The gap between "I can perceive it" and "I can extend a coherent connective structure to it" is not small.*

He thought of the eleven weeks he'd spent closing a considerably smaller gap — the difference between sensing the solar field and comprehending it — and understood, soberly, that this requirement alone represented a project of a scale he didn't yet have a clear timeline for.

*Requirement two,* he continued. *The extended pattern must anchor directly to the dissolution zone itself, not merely reach toward it from a distance. A reference pattern that only approaches the wound without actually touching it cannot give the Lattice anything to reinstantiate against. This means the Colony Link's structure would need to extend into the zone's own territory — into forty million years of accumulated entropy, close enough to actually matter.*

He paused at that requirement longer than the others, feeling the specific unease of a plan whose second step already involved placing something he'd spent five years carefully building directly inside the largest wound he knew of in the entire galactic structure.

*Requirement three,* he wrote, finally, and found his hand slower on this one, the words arriving with more weight than the previous two combined. *Throughout the entire extension and anchoring process, individual coherence must be maintained. Not merely the pattern's coherence — mine. The Colony Link has always worked because it connects distinct consciousnesses without erasing any single one of them, myself included. If extending it to this scale, into this specific proximity to a dissolution event, costs me that distinctness — if the vastness of what I'd be holding simply dissolves the specific, located person doing the holding — then the pattern itself might still succeed while I do not.*

He sat with the sentence a long while, the pocket dimension laboratory's quiet settling around him with a weight it hadn't carried in months.

*This,* he wrote, finally, *is precisely the problem Formless Scale exists to solve. Not power for its own sake. Not the vague, dramatic transcendence the term probably sounds like to anyone hearing it from the outside. Formless Scale is, as far as I currently understand it, the specific technique that makes holding something this vast survivable — the discipline of maintaining a located, specific self while simultaneously extending far enough to become, in some real sense, far larger than any single self should reasonably be able to remain.*

*Veth-Karun did not survive attempting exactly this,* he added, and underlined it, because the sentence deserved the weight of underlining. *Whatever went wrong forty million years ago, it went wrong at precisely this juncture — extending far enough to matter, and failing to remain coherent while doing it.*

He closed the notebook and sat for a while in the quiet, feeling the specific vertigo of a plan whose every individual requirement he understood clearly, and whose combined weight, taken together, amounted to something he did not yet know how to survive attempting.

He thought of Chen Yufei, of Liang Jingzhi, of the four other Anchors whose steady presence had carried him through every previous threshold. He thought, too, of the specific limit even five careful, devoted people might eventually represent, if what waited ahead demanded a scale of anchoring well beyond five.

*I don't have the answer yet,* he wrote, finally, closing the entry. *I have the shape of the problem, which is more than I had a week ago, and considerably less than I'll need before I can responsibly attempt any of this. That will have to be enough, for now, to keep working from.*

He set the notebook aside, and reached, instead, for something he'd been deliberately not thinking about for weeks — the Pelagic Archive's long-pending review, still waiting, somewhere in the wider Collective's careful, patient deliberation, for an answer that might, if it finally arrived, tell him much more about exactly what he was preparing to attempt.
