# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty-Two — Telling Meen-4

He told her plainly, without softening it, the same directness Meen-4 had always shown him deserving of in return — the obscured notation, the Planetary Architect comprehension finally resolving it, the name that had settled into legibility and rearranged everything he thought he understood about the man who'd given him this life.

*Veth-Karun,* he said. "Ren Wuji's student. He taught the person who caused the Formless Scale event."

Meen-4 was silent for a long moment, a stillness he'd never quite seen from her before — not the careful, deliberate stillness of considered response, but something closer to genuine shock, absorbed slowly, the way a structure absorbs an impact it wasn't built to anticipate.

*We knew,* she signaled finally.

He went very still. "You knew."

*Not which human,* she clarified quickly, something almost urgent in the correction. *The record of Veth-Karun's teacher was never fully specific — the Archive's account, even at its most detailed, described only that a teacher existed within the School, without naming which of the School's several instructors had held that role. I did not know, until this moment, that the teacher was Ren Wuji specifically.* She paused. *But we knew Veth-Karun had a teacher in the Strata. We have known that for forty million years.*

"You knew the Amber Lattice School had trained the person who caused the dissolution."

*Yes.*

"And the disciplinary action Dr. Peng found in the record last week — "

*The Isolationists destroyed the Amber Lattice School as punishment,* Meen-4 signaled, the admission arriving now with a directness that felt, given everything she'd carried so carefully until this moment, almost like relief at finally saying it plainly. *They believed the School — and specifically, the teacher within it — was responsible for failing to prevent Veth-Karun's dissolution. The reasoning, as I understand the historical record, held that a student capable of reaching Formless Scale should have been recognized, guided, and prevented from attempting an unsupported ascension. The failure to do so was judged a catastrophic institutional failure, and the punishment was total.*

---

He sat with the full weight of it, understanding, now, the shape of the thing he'd been circling for two days.

"Ren Wuji survived," he said slowly. "He's the only member of the School anyone's ever mentioned surviving. That's not a coincidence, is it."

*I do not believe it is,* Meen-4 signaled. *I do not have a complete record of how the destruction unfolded, or why any single individual might have escaped it. But given what you have now told me — that Ren Wuji was specifically the teacher whose failure the Isolationists were punishing — his survival, and his subsequent departure to the surface world, take on a considerably different character than I had previously understood them to hold.*

"He didn't just lose his school. He was the reason it was destroyed."

*That is one possible reading,* Meen-4 signaled, careful even now not to state more certainty than the evidence supported. *I do not know whether the Isolationists' judgment was accurate — whether Ren Wuji genuinely failed to prevent something preventable, or whether Veth-Karun's ascension was simply beyond what any teacher could have stopped once it began. I do not believe the record, as it currently exists, is sufficient to answer that question definitively.*

He thought about four hundred years of careful, patient solitary work, a man building a transmission system for someone he'd never meet, and understood, with a clarity that ached, exactly what that work had actually been.

"He spent four hundred years trying to make sure it never happened again," he said quietly. "The Codex. The careful, methodical caution in everything he taught me. Every warning about power without comprehension. He wasn't just teaching me cultivation. He was trying to prevent — "

He couldn't finish the sentence.

*He was trying to prevent another Veth-Karun,* Meen-4 finished for him, her signal carrying a gentleness he'd rarely heard from her. *I believe that is likely accurate. And I believe, given everything I have observed of you across these two years, that he succeeded in teaching exactly the caution he needed to teach, whether or not he ever forgave himself for what came before it.*

He sat with that for a long time, the Archive's quiet stone corridor holding the weight of the conversation between them, and understood, finally, with a clarity that felt both devastating and steadying at once, precisely what kind of inheritance he had actually been given — not simply knowledge, but the hard-won caution of a man who had watched, once, exactly what happened when that caution wasn't there.
