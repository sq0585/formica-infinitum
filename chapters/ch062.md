# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Sixty-Two — Under the City

He went at 2 AM on a Wednesday.

The store was open — it was always open — and Xiao Dong's replacement on the night shift was a young man named Peng who had never made eye contact with Lin Mushi in any of their brief encounters and did not make eye contact now. Lin Mushi bought a bottle of water he didn't particularly need to establish his presence as a customer, which bought him two minutes of standing near the northwest corner without being remarkable.

He had prepared for this for a week. He had the Compression geometry held in his attention the way you hold a piece of music you're about to play — present but not yet performed. He had his channels in full harmonic. He had the Protocol's entry specification memorized.

He pressed his palm to the northwest corner wall.

The membrane was there, as it had always been: the surface tension of the threshold, the resistance that was not physical material. He held the Compression geometry in his attention and let the geometry reduce — not his body, but the interface, the architecture of how he engaged the substrate.

Ratio 1.5. He felt it the way you feel a key turning in a lock: specific, unmistakable, the geometry aligning with the threshold's requirements.

The membrane yielded.

His hand went through.

He stepped through.

---

The vaulted hall was real.

He had perceived it from above. He had drawn it in four pages of sketches. He had built a schematic from the Codex's visualization. None of it had prepared him adequately for standing inside it.

The space was thirty-two meters tall at the crown of the vault, which he knew from his measurements and which was still, experienced from the floor, arresting. The vault was not stone — it was something he didn't have a geological category for, a material that read in his Qi perception as solid but with the internal structure of something between mineral and engineered composite. The hexagonal geometry of its surface was not decoration; it was the load-bearing structure of the material, the same pattern from molecular scale up.

He stood at the threshold, still partially in the membrane, and looked.

The hall extended north-northwest as far as he could perceive, which was now sixty meters. Columns at regular intervals, as his sketches had suggested. The floor: hexagonal tile in a fine-grained Qi-resonant material that lit faintly under his feet with the contact of his presence, the amber-gold of the Codex diagram made physical.

The floor was lit. The hall was not dark.

He had not expected it to be lit.

He had been standing in the doorway for ninety seconds when he became aware that the hall was not empty.

---

He could not see anything. The hall extended north-northwest into a darkness beyond his perception range. But the Qi field ahead of him had something in it — not the dormant, architectural Qi he'd been expecting from a centuries-empty ruin. Something that moved. Something that had the signature of living biology, the slow warmth of a metabolism, a presence with an interior.

At some distance he could not yet estimate, something was in the hall.

He held perfectly still.

The presence did not approach. It did not retreat. It was simply there, at the edge of his perception, in the way of something that had been there for a long time and had not required a response from the world above it.

He exhaled slowly.

He looked at the lit floor. He looked at the geometry of the vault. He looked at the hall that extended into the dark.

He was not alone in the Strata.

He had known, intellectually, that the Strata might not be empty. The Codex had said *functional, not merely ruins.* He had noted this and filed it.

He was filing it differently now.

He stepped fully through the threshold. He let the membrane close behind him. The hall's ambient Qi was different from the surface — richer, denser, more organized, the way carefully maintained equipment is different from equipment that's been running unattended.

Someone maintained this place.

He stood in the lit hall, sixty meters from the threshold, with a living presence at the edge of his perception somewhere to the north, and he opened his notebook.

He began writing.

He had entered the Strata.

He was not yet sure who else was in it.
