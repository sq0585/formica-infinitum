# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 475 — The New Anchors

Fang Ruxue read through the draft request twice before setting it down, her expression carrying the particular thoughtful weight she brought to anything she considered genuinely consequential for the Cultivation Research Collective she'd spent years carefully building.

"You understand this is unprecedented," she said. "Not the extension protocol — we have precedent for that now, thanks to your mother, thanks to me before her. This is different. You're asking people to extend their own Colony Link anchoring to stellar scale, into direct contact with a dissolution zone, for a repair effort with no guaranteed outcome."

"I know," he said. "I've tried to write the request as honestly as I can manage. No softening the actual cost."

She read through it a third time, more slowly, and he watched her attention catch, visibly, on the passage he'd worried most about — the section describing what a distributed anchor point would actually experience, drawn carefully from everything Meen-4 had helped him understand about the mechanism.

"This line," Fang Ruxue said. "'You will feel yourself present here, and simultaneously present as forty million years of absence in the Lattice's memory, becoming present again.' That's going to frighten some people away before they finish reading."

"Good," he said, and meant it entirely. "I don't want anyone volunteering for this without understanding exactly how large and how strange it's actually going to feel. I'd rather lose volunteers to honest fear than gain them through a description soft enough to mislead."

She nodded slowly, satisfied, and made a small correction to the wording — tightening a sentence here, clarifying a technical term there, the particular careful editing of someone who had spent years learning exactly how the Cultivation Research Collective's members preferred difficult information delivered.

"I'd like to be among the first to volunteer, for what it's worth," she said, once she'd finished the edit.

He looked up, surprised. "Fang Ruxue —"

"I extended my own life specifically because I understood this Collective would need me for what's coming," she said, meeting his eyes steadily. "I did not extend it to watch safely from a comfortable distance while you and whoever else volunteers carry the actual weight of what comes next. I would like to be part of this properly, if you'll have me."

He felt something in his chest tighten at the specific, quiet generosity of it — five years of steady, careful partnership, culminating in exactly the kind of commitment he hadn't quite let himself hope for when he'd started drafting the request that morning.

"I'll have you," he said. "Gladly."

They worked through the rest of the draft together, refining the language until it said, as precisely as either of them could manage, exactly what was being asked and exactly what it would cost — no romanticizing the scale of it, no minimizing the risk, simply the plain, complete truth of a project considerably larger than anything the Cultivation Research Collective had attempted before.

"How many do you actually need?" Fang Ruxue asked, once they'd finished.

"I don't have an exact number yet," he admitted. "The zone's scale suggests well more than five. Whether that means dozens, or considerably more, I won't know until I understand how much coherence each individual anchor point can actually sustain. I'll learn that as people come forward, I suspect, rather than knowing it in advance."

"Then we'd better find out how many are willing," she said, gathering the finalized document. "I'll route this through every channel our own Collective maintains — research network, Strata residents, the wider community we've built connections with since the disclosure. If there's a Formican among the volunteers too, so much the better. This should not be exclusively human, given whose failure we're finally trying to help heal."

He nodded, understanding the wisdom of that immediately — Veth-Karun's own dissolution had been a Formican failure, and the repair, if it succeeded, deserved contributors from both civilizations that had, together, finally understood how to prevent it happening again.

"Send it," he said. "Whatever comes back, we'll build the plan around exactly who actually answers."

Fang Ruxue nodded once, decisively, and turned to transmit the request through every channel the Cultivation Research Collective's careful, patient years of building had made available — the first formal call for what would, in the weeks ahead, become something considerably larger than either of them currently understood it might grow into.
