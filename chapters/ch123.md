# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Twenty-Three — The Gao Problem

Gao called on a Tuesday.

Not a message — a call, which from Gao was an indicator of urgency or confusion or both.

"Someone walked past my building last night," Gao said.

Lin Mushi had been working on the translation document. He set his pen down. "Describe the walk."

"Deliberate. Slow. The kind of slow that's chosen, not random." A pause. "I'm a physicist. I don't have pattern recognition for people who are surveilling other people. But I have pattern recognition for deliberate systems behavior."

"And this looked like deliberate systems behavior."

"The building past four AM on a Tuesday night. No cigarette, no phone. Walking." A pause. "I felt — looked at."

Lin Mushi thought about Lady Shen's route patterns. He pulled up the circuit network logs on his phone. The building where Gao lived was two blocks outside his current circuit coverage area — he had not installed units there because Gao was surface-level, not near the Strata entry point.

He should have installed a unit there.

"Are you at home now?" he said.

"Yes."

"Stay there for the rest of today. I'm going to install something in your building."

A pause. "What something?"

"A sensor. It'll look like a powerbank. Don't try to take it apart."

"Obviously I'm going to try to take it apart."

"Don't." He was already putting on his jacket. "I'll explain when I get there. Text me your building entrance code."

The code arrived thirty seconds later. He was out the door in under a minute.

---

He installed a circuit in Gao's building's second-floor landing while Gao watched from a respectable distance and did not in fact try to take it apart, though Lin Mushi could see the effort this was costing him.

The circuit's output registered: ambient Qi in the building's vicinity, baseline reading established, no active practitioner signature at current radius.

"The person who walked past," Lin Mushi said. "What time specifically?"

"Twelve-forty, approximately."

He checked the primary circuit network's archived readings for 12:40 AM Tuesday. Lady Shen's known signature: not in the logged coverage area at that time. Someone else, or an extension of Lady Shen's team, or the coffee-buying Iron Meridian practitioner who had resumed his own visits since the node's death.

Or a coincidence.

He did not assume coincidence.

"Gao," he said. "Is there anything at your apartment that connects you to me?"

A pause. "My notebook from the observation sessions. The photos from when I watched your Compression. The emails."

"The emails are fine — they're not searchable without a device. The notebook and the photos: put them somewhere that isn't your home. A locker at the university, an envelope with a friend." He paused. "Not because you're in danger. As a precaution."

"Are you in danger?"

He thought carefully before answering. "The situation is more active than it's been. I don't know if the interest in you was related to me or coincidental. If related, you're a peripheral contact, not a target. Being a peripheral contact in this situation means — I want to know your location is clear."

Gao was quiet for a moment.

"You're calm about this," he said.

"I'm useful about this. There's a difference."

"I've heard that line before."

"It was accurate then too." He looked at the circuit unit on the landing. "The sensor will log if anyone with a structured Qi signature comes within its range. It updates to my phone. I'll know if the person comes back."

"And if they do?"

"I'll deal with it." He looked at Gao. "The situation is — I'm more visible than I was. The people who are aware of me are more sophisticated than before. It's still manageable."

Gao looked at him.

"You're preparing to release the document," Gao said.

He had told Gao about the translation document in general terms — the Codex comparison, the distribution plan, Chen Yufei's role in the recipient selection. "Yes."

"The document will accelerate everything."

"Yes."

"And you've decided to release it anyway."

"It's the right thing to do," he said.

"I know," Gao said. "I just wanted to hear you say it plainly." He looked at the sensor on the landing. "The universities have lockers at the main library. I'll put the notebook there today."

"Good."

He went home. He added Gao's building to the list of locations he checked every six hours.

He went back to the translation document.

He had forty-seven recipients and one person who wasn't on the list who was nevertheless part of the situation.

He thought about Gao at the tea shop saying *I believe you* and writing in his own notebook.

He thought about what it cost people who were on the periphery of this to stay on the periphery.

He wrote in the margin of the translation document, in small characters that weren't part of the text: *the cost to people adjacent to this is something I haven't fully accounted for.*

He would need to account for it.
