# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Seventy-Four — He Follows the Trace

He had been counter-investigating her since October.

He didn't tell Chen Yufei this immediately. He told her the beginning first — Ren Wuji, the 3:17 AM death, the hand movement, the app on the phone. He described it in order, factually, the way he'd described it to Gao. He showed her the Codex interface on his phone, which she looked at for a long time without speaking.

He described the weeks of observation, the notation decoding, the channel openings. He described the node depletion with precise honesty: what he'd understood when, what he hadn't understood when, what he should have been doing differently if he'd had the information he hadn't had.

He was two hours into this account when she interrupted.

"You said you didn't know the node was monitored."

"Not until the second audio file from Ren Wuji's recordings, which I received approximately six weeks in. The first explicit mention of monitoring organizations was in that message."

"And after you knew?"

"I continued drawing from the node because I didn't yet understand how to stop or compensate, and the rate was slow enough that I believed I had more time than I did." He paused. "I was wrong about the timeline because I didn't account for the investigator's own scanning contributing to the depletion rate."

She was quiet.

"I'm not asking for absolution," he said. "I'm telling you what happened."

"I know," she said. "That's why I'm still listening."

---

After the account, he told her what he knew about her.

Not everything — but enough to establish that the counter-investigation had been real.

"You're with the Iron Meridian Court," he said. "You've been monitoring the Wuhou node for approximately two years. Your monitoring equipment is fixed-position — I identified the building it's in from your Qi signature trail. Your skill level is approximately eight years' practice, genuine and solid, using a framework that works for the wrong reasons."

She stopped her tea cup halfway to her mouth. "What do you mean 'wrong reasons.'"

"The Iron Meridian technique is derived from Formican cultivation theory, like all the Court systems. But the derivation lost the explanatory framework — it preserved the methodology without understanding why the methodology works. What you call 'harmonizing the meridians' is protein-realignment optimization in the biological superconductor network. It works. The explanation you've been given for why it works is wrong."

The tea cup went down.

"That's a significant claim," she said, very carefully.

"I have documentation." He took out the notebook he'd designated for this conversation. "Approximately a hundred pages of it, cross-referenced against a 2019 biochemistry paper and the original Formican source material."

She looked at the notebook. She looked at him. She looked at the notebook again.

"Who are you?" she said. Not hostilely — genuinely.

"Lin Mushi. Former night-shift convenience store worker. I dropped out of university three years ago because the curriculum moved too slowly." He met her eyes. "I've been studying the Codex for five months."

She was quiet for a long time.

Then: "You said the explanation is wrong. Does that mean the technique is wrong?"

"No. The technique works. The explanation doesn't explain why. I can show you the actual mechanism." A pause. "If you want to know."

She looked at him across the table.

"Yes," she said. "I want to know."

Lin Mushi had been counting on this. He had not been certain of it, but he had been counting on it — the specific quality of a practitioner who had spent eight years with a framework that worked but didn't satisfy, and who now had someone offering to show them why it actually worked.

She wanted to know. That was enough to start.

He opened the notebook to the meridian architecture section.

"Start here," he said. "We can take it in order."
