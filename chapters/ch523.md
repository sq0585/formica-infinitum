# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 523 — The Anchors Respond

He had not expected the choice to register with the others so distinctly. He understood, in principle, that the Anchor Protocol meant every significant shift in him arrived, in some form, at all five of them simultaneously — he had felt the mechanism work in exactly that direction a hundred times over five years, their steadiness reaching him, his gratitude reaching them. He had not fully considered what it would feel like from their side, to feel something as specific as a decision arrive through the connection rather than merely a state.

"I felt you decide to stay," Chen Yufei said, quietly, setting the teapot down at last.

He looked at her, and understood, from the particular softness in her expression, that she meant it exactly as plainly as she'd said it — not a metaphor, not an approximation, the literal, felt experience of a choice arriving through a bond that had, across five years and one ended romance and a friendship that had somehow only deepened on the other side of it, never once stopped being entirely real.

"I felt you decide to be here instead of everywhere," Wei Canglan said, and there was something in his voice that carried more weight than his usual careful precision allowed itself to show. "I want to say, for the record, that I did not expect that specific sensation to affect me the way it did."

"How did it affect you?"

Wei Canglan considered the question with the same seriousness he gave every question he considered worth answering honestly. "I have spent five years building infrastructure for a city I was, on some level I did not examine closely, afraid you would eventually outgrow the need for. Feeling you choose it anyway, at a scale where choosing it required something, rather than simply being convenient — that adjusted the fear considerably. I am not currently prepared to discuss by how much."

"The Anchor Protocol held completely," Meen-4 signaled, from her corner, her characteristic formality carrying, underneath it, something he had learned over the years to recognize as her version of warmth. "I have watched practitioners reach comparable scale before. I have never watched one choose, freely, having tested every alternative available, to remain primarily located among specific individuals rather than dispersed across the totality of what they could perceive. This is not merely unprecedented. It is, I believe, the correct outcome — the one the technique was always quietly hoping for, though the Archive's language was never permitted to say so directly."

"Coherence 0.98," Dr. Shen Yue said, simply, and then, after a beat, added something the framework itself had never required of her. "I want it on record that I am pleased by this number in a way that exceeds its clinical significance."

He felt Liang Jingzhi's presence last, arriving the way it always arrived now, warm and vast and specific, undivided between the office and the center.

"The zone is stable," she said. "I felt you choose to come back, too. Even from here — especially from here, I think. It reached the node the same way everything reaches the node now. Steady. Unmistakable."

He held all five responses at once, distinct, unblended, each one carrying the particular texture of the person who'd offered it — Chen Yufei's quiet warmth, Wei Canglan's careful vulnerability, Meen-4's ancient formal tenderness, Dr. Shen Yue's honest clinical pleasure, Liang Jingzhi's steady, permanent constancy.

*Observation 523,* he wrote. *I chose to come back. Five people, in five entirely distinct ways, each felt the choice arrive, and each responded from exactly who they already were, undiluted by the enormity of everything surrounding the moment. I think this is the clearest evidence I have yet received that the discipline is working correctly. Nothing about the scale I now hold has made any of them less themselves. If anything, it has made the shape of each of them considerably easier to see.*

He closed the notebook, and looked around the room once more, at five people who had, in their own distinct languages, all just told him the same simple thing.

He thought, sitting with it, of how differently this moment might have played out five years ago, when he had still believed, in the quiet unexamined way a much younger person believes most things, that growing more capable meant eventually needing less from the people around him. He understood now how wrong that assumption had been, and understood it not as an abstract lesson but as a felt, specific fact: the more he had grown, the more precisely each of these five people's individual presence had come to matter, not less. Capability had never replaced connection. It had only ever clarified how much connection had been doing all along.

"You're thinking something," Chen Yufei said, watching him.

"I'm thinking that I used to believe I'd eventually outgrow needing this," he said. "All of this. Any of you."

"And now?"

"Now I think needing this was never something to outgrow. I think it was the entire architecture the rest of it was built to protect." He looked around the room once more, at each of them in turn. "I'm glad I was wrong about that, five years ago. I'd rather have been wrong about it than right."

Wei Canglan closed his folder, finally, entirely, and said nothing further, but Lin Mushi felt, through the Anchor Protocol, that nothing further needed saying.
