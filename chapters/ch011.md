# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eleven — Day Ten

Ten days after Ren Wuji died in the convenience store, Lin Mushi sat down at his desk at 9 AM and wrote the following sentence at the top of a fresh page:

> *I am going to stop treating this as a possible symptom.*

He put the pen down. He looked at the sentence. He picked the pen back up and added:

> *Reason: symptoms don't have consistent directionality. Symptoms don't correlate with external events. Symptoms don't respond to changes in the interface of an app that appeared on my phone the night a man died.*
>
> *These are perceptions. I am going to treat them as data.*

---

The decision took longer to make than a sentence implies. He had been circling it for nine days, documenting the perceptual anomalies with one hand and writing *possible sleep deprivation* in the margins with the other, maintaining two explanations simultaneously the way you hold a door open while stepping through it. The door-holding was over. He was through.

What he had, treated as data:

The depth sensation. Consistent for ten days. Directional — north and slightly down from wherever he stood inside the store, fixed heading regardless of his own orientation. Not present everywhere: strongest under the store's back wall, weakest near the entrance. Present in his apartment with a different heading. Present at the construction site he'd walked past on day four, overwhelming and oblique. The sensation had a geography.

The translucency at corners. He had tested this seventeen times across six different locations. Wall junctions — the specific three-way meeting of two vertical surfaces and one horizontal — appeared perceptually thinner than solid material. Not see-through. Not visually different in any measurable way. But when he put his hand near such a corner, the sensation arriving from his palm was wrong. Not cold, not warm. Wrong in texture, as if the air there disagreed with his skin about how much resistance it was supposed to provide.

The battery anomalies. Documented across seven nights. The app consumed something other than — or in addition to — phone charge. The external source was finite and directional. He was drawing from something, and had been drawing from it without knowing what it was or whether that was acceptable.

The warmth. Present in his palm now as a baseline, low-grade, easy to ignore but always there when he looked for it.

And the Codex. Open on his phone beside his notes, doing its slow patient rotation, amber-gold lines moving in ways that had already taught him three things: it had a taxonomy, it had grammar, and it had been made by someone who spent four hundred years on it and still apparently had not finished.

---

He made a second cup of tea. He aligned his notebooks by date — three now, plus the store notebook, plus the dedicated notation analysis notebook he'd started on day twelve.

He wrote at the top of a new section:

> *Working definition of my current condition:*
>
> *I am in the early stages of perceiving a structure that has always been present and that I previously lacked the apparatus to detect.*
>
> *The apparatus was provided by contact with the Codex, which was provided by Ren Wuji in 0.4 seconds before he died.*
>
> *The structure is real. My perception of it is real. The apparatus is biological — it is happening in me, not in the phone.*
>
> *The phone is a tool. I am the instrument.*

He paused over that last line.

*I am the instrument.*

He didn't know exactly what that meant yet. But it felt more accurate than any other framing he'd tried, and accuracy was where he always started.

He put the pen down. He picked up his tea. Outside, the October sky was the flat white of overcast without rain — a color that made Chengdu look like a photograph of itself, slightly drained.

The warmth in his palm was steady. North and slightly down, the depth ran through the floor beneath his chair.

He opened the notation notebook and kept working.

This was the first true activation of what the Codex called — in characters he could now almost read — something he was translating as *the comprehension lock*. A mechanism. A requirement. Not a test of courage or strength or dedication. A test of whether he was paying attention closely enough to treat what he perceived as real.

He had passed it, apparently, by writing a sentence in a notebook at 9 AM.

He found this reasonable.

---

> *Observation 18 (Day 10):*
>
> *Official decision: perceptions are data. Treating them accordingly from this point forward.*
>
> *Implication: if they are data, they have sources. Sources can be located. Located sources can be studied.*
>
> *Next step: map the depth sensation across more locations. Build a spatial picture. Find the geometry.*

He wrote *geometry* and underlined it once, then kept going.
