# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Two — What the Box Means

He brought it to Fang Ruxue first, the small inscribed box wrapped carefully in cloth, and set it on the table between them without preamble.

"Ten centimeters," he said, removing the cloth. "Stable pocket dimension. No Qi input beyond ambient density. No practitioner attention required after activation."

She studied the box for a long moment without touching it, the careful, unhurried attention she brought to anything that might reshape her understanding of a problem.

"May I?" she asked.

"Carefully. The inscription is delicate near the access seam."

She lifted it, turned it over, examined the fine notation worked into its surface with the focused precision of someone who had spent thirty-one years learning to read structures most people would dismiss as decoration.

"This means cultivation can be partially externalized," she said slowly, setting it back down. "Anyone with sufficient understanding could build and operate this. Not perform the Weaving themselves — operate a device that performs it for them."

"Correct."

"The Courts maintained power partly by restricting access," she said, the implication settling visibly across her expression as she worked through it out loud. "Years of training, gated knowledge, hierarchy built on who had earned the right to a technique and who hadn't. This eliminates that restriction. Anyone with the device gets the result, regardless of what they've earned."

"Yes."

She was quiet for a long moment, her eyes returning to the small box, and he watched her arrive at the same conclusion he'd already spent three days sitting with alone.

"You built it anyway," she said. Not a question.

"Yes."

"Why?"

He considered the question carefully, the way he tried to consider most things she asked him.

"Because the restriction was never actually about wisdom or readiness," he said. "It was about control — who got to decide whose understanding counted enough to deserve access. I don't think that's a principle worth preserving just because preserving it would be more comfortable for the institutions built on top of it."

"That's a significant statement to make with a piece of hardware instead of a manifesto."

"I'm not good at manifestos. I'm reasonably good at building things and then dealing carefully with what they turn out to mean."

She almost smiled at that, the particular dry amusement he'd come to associate with her more than almost anyone else he knew.

"This will destabilize things further," she said. "Permanently, this time — not a political fracture that might eventually heal into a new equilibrium, but a structural change to what cultivation even means once devices like this exist. The Courts can reorganize around new alliances. They can't reorganize around a restriction that no longer functions as a restriction."

"I know."

"Are you certain you want to release this knowledge? Not the device itself — I assume you're not planning to mass-produce ten-centimeter boxes — but the principle underneath it. Once people understand externalization is possible, the work of building toward it begins regardless of whether you're the one doing it."

He thought about this carefully, the question he'd been sitting with since Sunday afternoon.

"I think the principle was always going to surface eventually," he said. "The Codex describes it plainly enough that anyone with sufficient Archive access would have found it given time. I'd rather it surface now, through the Collective, with careful thought about consequences attached to it, than surface later through someone with less reason to be careful."

Fang Ruxue considered this, and something in her expression settled into the particular quiet resolve he'd learned to recognize whenever she'd finished weighing a difficult decision and arrived somewhere solid.

"Then we should bring it to the working group," she said. "Carefully. With exactly the framing you just gave me — not a weapon, not a shortcut, but a fact about how this technology actually works that the world deserves to understand before it discovers the fact some less careful way."

He nodded, and began, with her help, drafting the language they'd use to introduce the small box and everything it implied to the rest of the Collective.
