# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ninety — Chitin Circuit — First Attempt

He had been thinking about the circuit for six weeks.

The idea had started in the Codex's technical diagrams — Document 6 contained a section on what the Formicans called *resonance inscription*, the practice of encoding Veritonal channel geometries into physical materials so that the material could sustain a Qi function without requiring a practitioner's continuous attention. A Chitin Circuit was a passive system: once established, it ran on the ambient Qi available to it and did its designated function without anyone having to be present.

The Strata's walls were full of them. The floor tiles that illuminated under his feet were them. The material of the circular room where the older Keeper lived was dense with them.

He had been building toward attempting it for six weeks, accumulating the prerequisite understanding: the geometric encoding required, the material substrate properties needed, the minimum Compression ratio for inscription (Ratio 1.7, more than he'd been achieving until two weeks ago), and the specific function he wanted to build.

The function he wanted: a Qi-sensing and amplification circuit that would extend his environmental perception range without requiring his active concentration. A passive sensor.

He bought a circuit board from an electronics components shop. He bought an engraving tool. He ate a good breakfast.

---

The notation sequences required for the circuit inscription were not the same as written notation — they were the same system, but in three dimensions, carved rather than written, the depth and width of the inscription carrying information the way ink weight carried information in calligraphy. He had read the inscription specifications four times and understood them technically before he started.

Understanding something technically and executing it with an engraving tool were different skills.

Circuit one: the geometric proportions were fractionally off. He could feel it when he ran Qi through it — the channel ran, but ran irregularly, the equivalent of a water pipe with a kink. He set it aside.

Circuit two: he adjusted the proportions. The channel ran more smoothly but the termination point was wrong — the function discharged into the ambient field rather than cycling back through the circuit. Passive sensors needed to cycle. He set it aside.

Circuit three: he corrected the termination geometry. He ran Qi through it. The channel established, the function cycled, the detection radius extended to approximately three meters.

Then the channel collapsed.

He looked at the circuit. The collapse had happened at a specific junction point where he'd inscribed an intersection of two channel geometries — a necessary complexity for a cycling function, but one he'd apparently not gotten right.

He set it aside and got a fourth circuit board.

---

On the fourth board he worked more slowly. He inscribed the primary channel first, tested it, confirmed it, then inscribed the secondary channel, tested it alone, then inscribed the intersection geometry.

At the intersection: he held Ratio 2, focused his awareness on the specific point where two Qi channel geometries had to merge without losing coherence, and inscribed the intersection symbol from the Codex with the precision of someone who understood what the symbol was mathematically rather than just what it looked like.

He ran Qi through it.

The channel established. The function cycled. The detection radius extended — six meters this time. He moved the circuit to the other side of the room. Still six meters.

And then the fourth circuit did something that circuits one, two, and three had not done: it glowed.

Not brightly. Not visibly except in the dark, except at Ratio 1.5 or above. But the inscription itself was emitting a faint amber-gold luminosity that was not coming from the LED components of the circuit board.

It was the same color as the Codex diagram.

He sat on the floor with the circuit board on his desk and thought about what this meant.

The Formican inscription had embedded a Veritonal channel into a physical medium. The channel was active. The glow was the active channel's ambient emission — the same phenomenon as the Strata's bioluminescent walls, the same phenomenon as the floor tiles that lit when he walked on them.

He had made something that the Strata was made of.

It was crude. It was small. It was his third attempt at something Formican engineers had been doing for millions of years and had probably found simple by the time they were doing it at building scale.

It glowed.

He wrote in the notebook with a hand that was, he noted, slightly unsteady:

> *Ch.90. First successful Chitin Circuit. Fourth attempt. Function: ambient Qi detection and amplification, cycling. Range: 6 meters. Duration: testing.*
>
> *Glow: yes. Veritonal channel active emission, same frequency as Codex diagram.*
>
> *What this means: the inscription encodes a functional channel in physical matter. The matter sustains the channel passively. I have made a thing that runs on ambient Qi without practitioner input.*
>
> *It is ugly. It works.*
>
> *The three failed circuits are in the bin. The fourth is on my desk glowing.*
>
> *Next: determine duration. Then: test with modified powerbank housing. Then: second generation with improved channel architecture.*

He tested duration: thirty-one hours of continuous function before the ambient Qi available to the circuit dropped below the function threshold. In an area with better Qi density — the Strata, for instance — he estimated significantly longer.

He put the circuit in a cardboard box with cotton wool around it because he didn't have a better housing yet.

He went to sleep.

The box glowed faintly through the cardboard.

He lay in the dark and looked at the faint amber light leaking through the box flaps and thought: *the fourth successful circuit is always the one that glows with a light that doesn't come from the LED.*

He would remember this.
