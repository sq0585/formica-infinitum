# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty-Nine — Chen Yufei Intervenes

She arrived at the edge of the high-density zone six seconds after Lin Mushi reached it, her Compression signature reading at the full Ratio 3.5 she'd touched weeks ago, now stabilized into something she could deploy deliberately rather than simply experience.

She had been at home, on the surface, when the Circuit network's anomaly alert reached her phone — a structured combat-signature reading from deep in the mapped territory, paired with Lin Mushi's own signature moving at a speed and pattern the network had never logged from him before.

She had not hesitated. She had crossed the threshold faster than she had ever managed, the urgency overriding whatever careful, controlled technique she usually applied to the compression.

She took in the scene in an instant: Lin Mushi, breathing hard, positioned at the edge of a zone she recognized from his own mapping notes as unusually dense; Lady Shen Baoling, fifteen meters back, controlled and unhurried in the specific way of someone who knew she had the advantage and was simply choosing her moment.

Chen Yufei did not hesitate.

She deployed a technique she had been developing privately for weeks, born from the corrected Iron Meridian framework applied to a tactical purpose her original tradition had never quite articulated: a Qi interference field, designed not to attack but to disrupt — to introduce enough ambient noise into the immediate Qi environment that any precise technique attempting to operate within it would lose coherence.

It was not a combat technique in any traditional sense. It was closer to jamming a signal.

It worked.

Lady Shen, in the middle of reorienting for her next attempt at containment, felt her technique's structured geometry begin to fray at the edges — not collapse outright, but lose enough precision that completing the capture within the timeframe she needed became suddenly, critically uncertain.

She broke off the attempt.

"Interesting," she said, looking at Chen Yufei with something that, despite the circumstances, carried genuine professional appraisal. "Iron Meridian framework, applied to interference rather than direct technique. I haven't seen that approach before."

"I've had unconventional teachers," Chen Yufei said, positioning herself between Lady Shen and Lin Mushi without quite making it look deliberate.

Lin Mushi used the brief reprieve to move further into the high-density zone — close enough now to the circular room's outer approach that he could feel the dimensional structure's increased complexity directly, the specific quality of space that made large-scale Qi manipulation a genuinely dangerous proposition.

He reached the zone's effective center and turned to face both women.

"Lady Shen," he said, his breathing finally steadying. "You cannot deploy a containment-scale technique here without risking dimensional instability. I don't think that's a risk the Northern Peak Court has authorized you to take, regardless of what the Council's override said about jurisdiction."

She looked at him for a long moment, then looked around at the zone's architecture, her own perception — considerably more developed than his — confirming what he'd said.

"You're correct," she said, after a moment. "This was not in my preparation."

"I didn't expect it to be," he said. "I've spent fifteen months mapping every meter of this territory. You've been here for less than an hour."

She was quiet, recalculating visibly, the same quality Lin Mushi had read in her field months ago during her first cooperative visit — assessment, recalibration, the specific intelligence of someone whose institutional position and personal judgment were once again, clearly, in conflict.

"You cannot hold this position permanently," she said finally. "Eventually you will need to leave the zone, or eat, or sleep. I can wait."

"I don't need to hold it permanently," he said. "I just need to hold it long enough."

"Long enough for what?"

He didn't answer that directly.

He was already, through the Circuit network's emergency channel, sending a message to Wei Canglan on the surface — a single instruction, prepared in advance for exactly this kind of contingency, though he had hoped never to need it.

*Release the third document. Now.*
