# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 437 — Qi Field Interaction

The next several thousand entries, once he settled into their actual structure, resolved into something closer to a curriculum than a record — the installation's long-dead crew had not simply measured the interaction between stellar fields, they had built, patiently, across what must have been decades of their own working lives, a systematic account of how it functioned.

He worked through it the way he'd worked through every difficult curriculum before this one, breaking it into pieces small enough to actually hold, testing each principle against the last before trusting himself to move forward.

The first principle was resonance. Two stellar fields, close enough in the interstellar network and similar enough in their underlying Veritonal character, would begin, over sufficiently long timescales, to synchronize — not merging, never merging, but falling into a shared rhythm the way two instruments tuned to compatible frequencies would eventually, played together long enough, begin to reinforce rather than compete with each other's sound.

*Observation 371: resonance appears to require both proximity and compatibility. The record documents several dozen stellar pairs exhibiting measurable resonance across distances up to forty light-years. Pairs lacking Veritonal compatibility show no resonance regardless of proximity — distance alone is insufficient.*

The second principle, harder to grasp, was dampening. Where resonance amplified, incompatible fields in close proximity produced the opposite — a kind of interference that reduced both fields' effective coherence, the stellar equivalent, he thought, of two conversations happening too close together, each one slightly harder to follow because of the other.

He tested this against what he already understood of the solar system's own architecture, cross-referencing the asteroid belt's diffusion function against the record's dampening principle, and found, to his quiet satisfaction, that the two ideas fit together cleanly — the belt didn't simply diffuse the Sun's field passively, it dampened specific frequencies selectively, the way the installation's crew had described dampening working between full stellar fields, just performed by smaller, more numerous bodies instead of a single competing star.

*Rule: dampening and diffusion may be the same underlying mechanism operating at different scales. If true, this means the principles I'm learning here are not specific to stellar-scale interaction — they're general, and I've simply never had the range to observe them operating at this size before.*

He sat with that rule longer than most, because it suggested something he hadn't quite let himself consider directly until now: that the gap he was trying to close wasn't a wall between two entirely different kinds of understanding, but a single continuous structure he'd already been building the foundation for, patiently, without knowing it, since the very first weeks of learning to sense the Colony Link's own resonance between individual people.

The third principle — amplification — took him longer than either of the first two, and he suspected, reading the crew's own notation, that it had taken them considerably longer as well; their entries on this section were denser, more hesitant, corrected and re-corrected in ways the earlier sections hadn't been. Certain stellar configurations, under specific conditions, didn't merely resonate or dampen but genuinely amplified one another — a field passing through a compatible neighbor and emerging measurably stronger than it had entered, energy that appeared, on first inspection, to violate every conservation principle he understood, until he traced it carefully enough to realize the amplification was borrowed, temporarily, from the broader network itself, redistributed rather than created.

*Observation 379: nothing here creates energy from nothing. Everything borrows, redistributes, and eventually returns to equilibrium across a large enough scale. The network is conservative. It simply operates on timescales long enough that conservation isn't obvious from any single, briefly observed moment.*

He set the notebook down, finally, and noticed, almost as an afterthought, that something had changed in the way he was holding all of it.

He reached, carefully, toward the edge of his own perception — the same reach that had, eleven weeks ago in Chengdu, produced nothing but an overwhelming, incomprehensible density he couldn't parse. This time, the Sun's field resolved, faintly, into something with shape. Not full comprehension. Not yet. But structure, where before there had only been noise — resonance here, dampening there, the faint outline of a network he was, for the first time, actually beginning to read rather than merely sense.

*It's not dramatic,* he wrote, and found the observation mattered enough to underline. *It's not a breakthrough. It's the same thing that's worked every single time before this — slow, patient, methodical accumulation, until one day the thing that made no sense simply starts making some.*

He checked the relay chain — stable, six weeks in now by Amber-0's count, Chen Yufei's messages steady and unworried — and allowed himself, briefly, something that wasn't quite triumph but sat comfortably in the same neighborhood.

Then he turned to the archive's next section, because the crew's own careful organization suggested there was considerably more curriculum left before he'd earned the right to understand why they'd stopped writing it.
