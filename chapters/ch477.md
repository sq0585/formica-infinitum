# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 477 — Who Answers

The full shape of the response became clear over the following two weeks, and it surprised him considerably more than the raw numbers had.

He'd expected, if he was honest with himself, mostly practitioners — people already fluent in Colony Link anchoring, already carrying some meaningful cultivation background that made the request's technical demands feel less abstract. That population answered, certainly, in respectable numbers. But they were, he discovered, working through Meen-4's careful daily summaries, only one part of a considerably stranger and more varied response.

Scientists answered — not cultivators, not people with any prior Qi-sensing capability at all, but researchers who had spent the past year studying Dr. Shen Yue's papers and Liang Jingzhi's geological data, and had concluded, independently, that a project this significant deserved their contribution regardless of whether they'd ever personally touched a Colony Link before. Meen-4's screening process would need to determine, individually, whether each of them could actually be trained into the necessary capability in time, but their willingness alone struck him as its own kind of remarkable.

Independent cultivators answered — practitioners who had never joined the Strata formally, who'd maintained their own private traditions across years of quiet, solitary practice, and who read the request as an invitation into something larger than the isolated paths they'd each been walking.

Several Formicans answered as well, and this, more than anything else, settled something in his chest he hadn't fully realized needed settling. Fang Ruxue had been right to insist the call reach beyond the human community — Veth-Karun's failure belonged to Formican history as much as to anyone's, and the volunteers who understood that most viscerally were, unsurprisingly, other Formicans, several of them considerably older than the Strata itself, offering to extend anchoring capabilities they'd maintained for centuries toward healing a wound their entire civilization had carried, quietly, for forty million years.

And then there was the response that moved him most, the one he hadn't anticipated at all: a scattering of ordinary Strata residents, people with no scientific credentials, no deep cultivation background, no obvious qualification beyond having lived, for months or years now, inside something extraordinary and having decided, individually, that they wanted to matter to it directly rather than simply benefiting from its existence.

He read one such volunteer's message personally, forwarded to him by Meen-4 with a note suggesting he might want to see it directly rather than only in aggregate.

*I run the small vegetable stall near the Strata's eastern market,* it read. *I am not a cultivator. I do not fully understand the physics of what you're building. But I have lived here for two years, in a city that should not exist, built by a man who used to work in a convenience store, and I have watched this place become the most genuinely kind community I have ever been part of. I would like to help hold whatever comes next, even if all I can offer is willingness rather than expertise. Please consider me, if there's a way someone like me can actually contribute.*

He sat with that message a long while, feeling the specific, unexpected weight of it — not the scientists' careful expertise, not the Formicans' ancient, patient capability, but simply a person who had decided, without any obligation compelling her, that she wanted to be part of something larger than her own life.

"Can she actually contribute?" he asked Meen-4 later. "Realistically — does the screening process have room for volunteers without existing capability?"

"Some," Meen-4 said. "Not all. Distributed anchoring requires baseline coherence capacity, which correlates loosely with practice but is not identical to it. Some people simply possess it naturally, the way some people are naturally gifted musicians without formal training. We will assess her properly, the same as everyone else — no favor for sentiment, no exclusion for lack of credentials."

He nodded, satisfied with the fairness of it, and turned back to the growing list — scientists and Formicans and quiet market vendors, considerably wider and stranger than he'd expected, all of them, in their own varied ways, choosing to answer.

The number, by the end of the second week, stood at just past two hundred.

It was, he understood, only the beginning of the actual count.
