# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 389 — The Strata Under Pressure

The transmission bypassed him entirely, which was, Lin Mushi realized once he understood what had happened, exactly the point.

He learned about it from Fang Ruxue, who came to him with a face carefully arranged into calm that didn't quite hold. "Six residents have come to me in the last two days," she said, "each separately, each asking a version of the same question. I didn't understand why until the third one showed me what she'd received."

The message, when Lin Mushi finally saw it, was formatted in a register he recognized immediately — Formican notation, though simplified, deliberately, into something a non-fluent reader could parse with effort, sent not through the Witness Node's formal channel but through a side transmission he hadn't known was possible until this exact moment.

*The Containment Orthodoxy offers relocation to a Collective-protected micro-civilization, outside Zone QZ-7's jurisdiction, to any current Strata resident willing to provide testimony regarding Lin Mushi's judgment, stability, and fitness to continue development at current scale. Safety guaranteed regardless of Zone QZ-7's review outcome.*

He read it twice, feeling something cold and precise settle into his chest — not the personal betrayal of Lady Shen's revelation, but something colder, more calculated. This was not fear finding an unfortunate outlet. This was an institution, losing ground in a formal review, reaching directly past every safeguard he'd built and speaking to the twelve hundred people who had trusted him with their lives.

"Who received it," he asked.

"I don't fully know. The six who came to me are the ones willing to admit it. There may be more who haven't." Fang Ruxue's calm cracked slightly at the edges. "None of them are accepting the offer, for what it's worth. But they're frightened, Lin Mushi, in a way they weren't frightened yesterday. It's one thing to hear, secondhand, that some invisible cosmic institution is deciding Earth's fate. It's another to receive a personal offer of escape and realize the danger was specific enough to include *you*, not just the abstract civilization you live inside."

He sat with that, the particular vertigo of watching a threat he'd been treating as a distant, procedural thing suddenly reach directly into the lives of people who had never asked to be caught inside it.

"What are they asking," he said. "The six who came to you."

"Not to leave. Not one of them wants to leave. They're asking whether the offer means the Orthodoxy actually has the standing to guarantee something like that — whether it's a real option being dangled or an empty threat dressed as one. And underneath that, they're asking whether staying is still the right choice, now that leaving has been made explicitly available to them in a way it never was before."

He thought of the six residents by name, once Fang Ruxue supplied them — none particularly prominent within the Strata's structure, none with any special standing that would have made them obvious targets, which told him, chillingly, that the Orthodoxy's outreach had likely been broader than six people, and that six was simply the number willing to admit they'd been approached.

"I need to address this directly," he said. "Not through Fang Ruxue relaying reassurance secondhand. Directly, to everyone, before rumor does more damage than the actual message did."

"I agree," Fang Ruxue said. "But be careful how you frame it. If you come to them with confidence you don't actually have, and the review goes badly anyway, you will have spent trust you can't easily rebuild. If you come to them with the full truth — including the parts that are genuinely frightening — you give them something harder to hear, but something they can actually trust, regardless of how the review ends."

He nodded slowly, already turning the shape of what needed to happen next over in his mind — not a private conversation with six anxious residents, not a quiet reassurance passed hand to hand through Fang Ruxue's careful diplomacy, but something larger and more exposed than anything he'd attempted with the Strata before.

"Call a full meeting," he said. "Everyone. Not a representative sample. Everyone who wants to be there."

"That's never been done. Not at this scale."

"No," he agreed. "It hasn't. But the Orthodoxy just went around every structure I've built to protect people from having to carry this weight alone, and spoke to them directly, one at a time, in the dark. I'm not going to answer that by doing the same thing in reverse. I'm going to answer it in the open, where everyone hears the same truth at the same time, and no one has to wonder what was said to someone else in private."

Fang Ruxue studied him for a long moment, something like approval settling into her expression despite the gravity of everything underneath it.

"Then I'll start arranging it today," she said. "You should know, before you walk into that room — some of them are going to be angry. Not at the Orthodoxy. At you, for the risk they didn't fully understand they'd agreed to when they moved in."

"I know," he said quietly. "I'd rather they be angry with the full truth than grateful for a comfortable lie."
