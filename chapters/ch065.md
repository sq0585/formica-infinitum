# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Sixty-Five — Xiao Dong's Noodle Shop

The soft opening was on a Thursday.

Xiao Dong had sent a WeChat message with the address and the time and two food emoji, which was the most enthusiasm Lin Mushi had ever seen him express digitally. He had responded with a thumbs up and a confirmation, and on Thursday evening he walked to the address — a former tea shop on a side street fifteen minutes from his apartment — and found a room that smelled of good broth and was filled with approximately thirty people who all seemed to be Xiao Dong's friends, relatives, and colleagues from every job he'd ever held.

Xiao Dong was behind the counter with the energy of someone who had been anxious for months about whether a thing would work and was now watching it work and didn't quite know what to do with the relief.

Lin Mushi found a stool at the counter. Xiao Dong put a bowl in front of him without being asked. "The signature broth," he said, which was a phrase that was more formal than anything else Lin Mushi had heard him say.

"Congratulations," Lin Mushi said.

"You can tell me if the broth is good."

He tasted it. It was very good. "It's very good."

Xiao Dong nodded with the carefully-contained satisfaction of someone who had been told this by thirty people tonight and each time had allowed himself to believe it a little more.

---

Lin Mushi sat at the counter and ate his noodles and watched the room with the full attention he now brought everywhere, which included the Qi dimension.

The room was warm. Not the Leshan-kitchen warmth of long habitation — this was new, barely hours old as a dedicated space. But it had the immediate warmth of a lot of people feeling good in one place, the collective emotional field that he could now read at room-scale: contentment, pride (Xiao Dong's specifically, identifiable), the relaxed pleasure of people eating something that was actually good.

Xiao Dong's Qi signature itself was clean in a way that Lin Mushi had been learning to associate with people who were doing the thing they were meant to be doing. Not perfect — he was nervous, and nervous had a texture — but underneath the surface nervousness was a settled quality. This was right. He was in the right place.

Lin Mushi noticed this, and noted it, and found that he was glad.

---

When the room had thinned out, Xiao Dong came around the counter and refilled his tea.

"You still doing the research?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Going well?"

"I think so. Hard to know from the inside."

Xiao Dong nodded with the understanding of someone who was managing his own inside-view problem about whether the noodle shop was actually good or he was just too close to it to tell. "What kind of research?"

Lin Mushi considered his standard answers — *physics*, *geology*, *theoretical biology* — and found them all more deflective than usual, in this specific room, with this specific person.

"Something under the city," he said.

Xiao Dong blinked. "Like, sewers?"

"More like — the history of the place. What it's built on."

Xiao Dong thought about this. "The foundation," he said.

"Yes. Exactly."

"Like you said. The floor has good bones."

"Yes."

"And you're finding out what the bones are."

"Yes."

Xiao Dong looked at him for a moment with the uninterpretable expression of someone who was deciding whether to ask the next question. He decided not to. "Okay," he said. "That sounds important."

"I think it is."

"Good." Xiao Dong went back behind the counter. "I'll put you on the regular customer list. You can come eat and tell me if I've messed up the broth."

"That seems like a reasonable arrangement," Lin Mushi said.

He paid. He walked home through the December streets, which were cold in the persistent damp way of Chengdu's December, and thought about what it meant that Xiao Dong had, in four sentences, described exactly what he was doing.

*Finding out what the bones are.*

Yes. That was it exactly.

He was going back into the Strata tomorrow. He had a presence to the north and a Colony Link field in the walls and a floor that remembered his footsteps.

He was finding out what the bones were.
