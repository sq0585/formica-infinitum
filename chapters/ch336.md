# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty-Six — The Cognitive Distance, Named

The pause came four days after her return, small and unremarkable, the kind he'd have once dismissed entirely if three weeks apart hadn't sharpened his own attention to exactly this pattern.

They were sitting together in the residential wing's common room, an ordinary evening, when Amber-0 surfaced a routine flag and his attention drifted, briefly, toward resolving it before returning.

Chen Yufei didn't say anything immediately. She simply watched him, her expression carrying something more thoughtful than the mild irritation such moments used to carry, and he found himself, watching her watch him, understanding that something had shifted in how she was holding this.

"The pauses have gotten longer," she said finally, once he'd fully returned his attention to her. "Not just more frequent. Longer, individually. That one was almost six seconds."

"I noticed too," he admitted. "The planetary field is larger than the city field was. I think the pauses scale with the scope of what's pulling at my attention."

"I've thought about that, actually. During my three weeks away." She set down her tea, giving the conversation her full, deliberate focus. "I don't think the answer is asking you to somehow make the pauses shorter, or pretending they'll disappear as you get better at managing the scale. I don't think that's realistic anymore, given how large the field has become."

"Then what's the answer?"

"I don't think there's a clean one. I think — " she paused, choosing her words with visible care, "I think what I actually want isn't for the pauses to stop. I want to keep going, even with them. And I want to know that you see it happening, every time, rather than either of us pretending it isn't there."

He felt the weight of what she was offering land fully — not a solution, not a demand that he somehow become someone smaller than what this world required him to be, but something harder and more mature than either.

"You're not asking me to fix this," he said slowly, understanding arriving as he spoke it aloud.

"No. I don't think it's fixable, not in the sense of making it disappear. I think it's something we're going to carry together, indefinitely, the same way we carry everything else this world keeps handing us." She met his eyes directly. "I want to keep going. I want that clearly understood, first. And I want to know that you see it — that the pause isn't invisible to you, that you're not simply drifting somewhere I can't follow without either of us acknowledging it's happening."

---

He sat with the offer's full weight, understanding, more clearly than he had in any previous conversation about this, exactly what kind of maturity she was bringing to something that could, easily, have become a source of quiet, accumulating resentment instead.

"I see it," he said. "Every time. I want you to know that, even in the seconds I'm not fully here, some part of me is aware that I've left, and some part of me is always working to come back as quickly as I can."

"I believe you," she said, something steady and certain in her voice. "I think that's actually enough, for me. Not a fix. Just — being seen, in the gap, rather than the gap pretending not to exist."

"I don't know if I can promise the gap gets smaller."

"I'm not asking you to promise that. I watched what happened when I asked for smaller, unconsciously, without saying it directly — you tried, and it helped a little, and then the Planetary Architect emergence happened and the gap grew anyway, because the world you're holding grew. I don't think smaller is a promise you can actually keep, given what you're becoming. I'd rather ask for something you can actually keep."

He held her hand across the small space between them, the weight of the conversation settling into something that felt, despite its difficulty, considerably more solid than anything easier might have felt.

"Seen, in the gap," he said. "I can do that. I'll keep doing that, for as long as the gap exists."

"That's what I'm asking for," she said, something in her voice carrying the specific relief of having finally found language for something she'd been circling for months. "I think that's the most honest thing either of us can offer, given everything this world keeps asking of you. I don't need you smaller. I need you seen, even when you're vast."

He thought, sitting there with her hand steady in his, that this was quite possibly the most mature conversation about love he'd ever had in his life — no ultimatum, no quiet resentment, just two people choosing, deliberately and with open eyes, to keep building something real inside the specific, ongoing difficulty of what he'd become.

"Thank you," he said. "For asking for the right thing, instead of the easy thing."

"Thank you for actually hearing it," she said, and the evening settled around them, quiet and ordinary and entirely, deliberately theirs.
