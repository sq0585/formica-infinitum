# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 489 — What Remains Unresolved

He took a quiet evening, once the sixth week's self-sustaining milestone had settled properly into the project's new, steadier rhythm, to do something he hadn't done in longer than he should have let pass — a careful, honest accounting of everything the past months had left genuinely unresolved.

*Observation 501,* he wrote, the number itself, arriving so close to a milestone, striking him as its own small, quiet marker of how far the notebook had carried him since a dying man's hand first closed around his in a convenience store. *An inventory of what remains unfinished, recorded honestly rather than glossed over simply because the larger project has reached a stable footing.*

*Veth-Karun's residual has acquired direction. It has not acquired resolution. The entropy still spans a scale I cannot yet fully measure, and the network's steady presence, however sound its foundation, has addressed only a fraction of forty million years' accumulated damage. I do not know how long full repair will actually require. I am choosing not to guess at a number I cannot yet responsibly estimate.*

He sat with that entry a moment before continuing, understanding that the next item carried a different, more personal weight.

*Solar position 7.4-N remains exactly as unexplained as it was when I first catalogued it and chose, deliberately, to set it down. I have not investigated it further. I have not been tempted to, not seriously — the discipline that decision cost me months ago has held steady, reinforced considerably by everything Veth-Karun's own account taught me about the specific danger of approaching something enormous without adequate preparation. But I want to record, honestly, that the question has not gone anywhere. It sits exactly where I left it, patient, unanswered, waiting for whatever future version of myself eventually proves ready to actually face it.*

*The eight Formicans remain, in Meen-4's own formal accounting, simply unknown. No body. No signal. No explanation, forty million years running. I think of them more often than I expected to, given how deliberately I set their mystery aside. I do not know if that means I am secretly still chasing the question, or simply that some griefs, once genuinely felt, don't fully leave regardless of how disciplined the decision to set them down actually was.*

He closed that entry and sat for a while, the pocket dimension laboratory's warm light steady around him, feeling the particular, specific weight of holding multiple genuine uncertainties at once without letting any of them destabilize the ground he'd actually managed to build.

*Rule,* he wrote, finally, the same discipline he'd first articulated months ago, at the installation, now considerably more tested and, he found, considerably more comfortable to hold. *Unresolved is not the same as neglected. I have not abandoned any of these questions. I have simply chosen, deliberately and repeatedly, not to let curiosity outrun preparation. Veth-Karun's account confirmed, more thoroughly than I could have confirmed it myself, exactly how dangerous that particular mistake can become. I am not going to make it now, with this much more at stake than I've ever held before.*

He thought, briefly, of every mystery this extraordinary life had eventually resolved — the Codex, the Strata's true nature, Colony Link, the Surveyor's actual purpose, Veth-Karun's own translated account, each one arriving, in the end, exactly when he'd finally earned the capacity to properly receive it. He trusted, sitting with the current, considerable list of what remained genuinely unresolved, that the same pattern would eventually hold here too.

*I don't need every question answered right now,* he wrote, closing the entry. *I need the ones currently within reach handled well, and the discipline to leave the rest exactly where they are until I've actually earned the right to face them. That has never once failed me yet. I don't expect it to start failing now.*

He closed the notebook, checked the dissolution zone's steady, self-sustaining progress one final time before the evening properly ended, and allowed himself, for the first time in weeks, something close to genuine, unhurried rest.

Chen Yufei found him a while later, still sitting quietly with the closed notebook in his lap, and didn't ask what he'd been writing — she'd learned, across five years, when a silence wanted company rather than conversation.

"You look lighter," she said, settling beside him anyway.

"I think I am," he admitted. "I spent the evening writing down everything I still don't have an answer for. I expected it to feel heavier, doing that all at once. It didn't. It felt more like finally putting a stack of unopened letters somewhere I could actually see them, instead of pretending they weren't accumulating on the table."

"And now?"

"Now I know exactly what I'm still carrying," he said, "instead of carrying it without looking at it directly. I think that's going to matter, eventually, more than I currently understand."
