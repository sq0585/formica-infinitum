# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Sixty-Two — Lady Shen and the Paper

The request arrived through formal channels, the same careful, deliberate process Lady Shen Baoling had used for every significant communication since the uneasy alliance that had emerged from Arc Three's political upheaval — a request for a direct meeting, specific and unhurried, carrying none of the urgency that might have suggested crisis.

He met her in a quiet meeting room within the Cultivation Research Collective's administrative wing, the space itself a small, quiet monument to exactly how much had changed since the days when Lady Shen's name had carried nothing but threat.

"You've read the paper," he said, once they'd settled, understanding, from the specific focus in her expression, exactly what had prompted the request.

"I've read it three times," she said, her tone carrying the same sharp, unsentimental precision that had made her, once, one of his most dangerous adversaries and now, cautiously, one of his most valuable allies. "I understand the physics well enough to follow the argument. I understand the political implications considerably better than the physics."

"Tell me."

"Dr. Shen Yue's paper is careful. Rigorous. Deliberately incomplete about the geographic correlation, exactly as it should be, given what that correlation would reveal if stated plainly." She met his eyes directly, the specific, unflinching clarity that had once made her formidable now serving, entirely, in his interest. "But careful incompleteness only holds for so long, against a scientific community this motivated to understand what it's actually looking at. I give it, generously, another year before someone independently reconstructs the correlation Dr. Shen Yue deliberately left unstated."

He sat with the assessment's weight, understanding, even before she elaborated further, exactly what conclusion she was building toward.

---

"You're saying this paper is the beginning of something," he said slowly.

"I'm saying this paper is the beginning of a disclosure process you haven't formally decided to initiate," she said, with the same precise, unflinching honesty she'd always brought to their exchanges. "Whether you intended it as such or not, mainstream scientific acceptance of the Veritonal Energy framework — however carefully obscured the current terminology is — makes long-term secrecy about this entire world's existence considerably harder to sustain than it was even a year ago."

"I know," he said.

"Do you? Genuinely?" She studied him with the specific, penetrating attention she'd once used to identify weaknesses in an opponent, now turned toward something closer to genuine concern. "Because I don't think you've fully sat with the timeline this implies. If the scientific community independently arrives at the correlation Dr. Shen Yue is protecting, disclosure happens whether you've chosen it or not — chaotically, unmanaged, at a moment and in a manner entirely outside your control. That is a considerably worse outcome than a deliberate, carefully planned disclosure, on terms you and the people you're responsible for actually chose."

He felt the weight of her argument settle fully, understanding, with growing clarity, exactly why she'd requested this conversation directly rather than raising it through any less serious channel.

---

"What are you going to do?" she asked, once the full weight of her assessment had settled.

"I don't know yet," he admitted, choosing honesty over performance. "I've been preparing for the Collective's review — the eighteen-month clock, the case we're building about this world's development. I haven't fully faced the parallel question you're raising, about the surface world specifically, about disclosure on my own terms rather than reactive terms."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the honest one," he said.

"You have eight months before the Collective review's conclusion," she said, something almost gentle entering her otherwise sharp precision. "You should decide the disclosure question before that. Not because the two decisions are identical — they're not, they involve different audiences, different risks, different considerations. But because facing one of these unresolved questions while the other one is simultaneously concluding is considerably more dangerous than facing them, deliberately, in sequence, with enough runway to actually think clearly about each."

He nodded slowly, the weight of her counsel settling into him with the specific, sobering clarity of advice he knew, even as it landed uncomfortably, was entirely correct.

He wrote about the conversation that evening, the entry longer and more careful than most.

> *Lady Shen is right, and I don't like how right she is. The disclosure question isn't separate from the review anymore, not really — they're both converging on the same narrow window. Eight months. I need an answer to something I've been quietly avoiding for two years, and I don't yet have one.*
