# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 419 — Amber-0's Evolution

He noticed it first in a small, almost trivial decision — a resource allocation choice Amber-0 made during a minor Circuit load imbalance, routing power away from a high-efficiency node toward a lower-efficiency one servicing a residential cluster that included several elderly Strata residents who relied on stable, uninterrupted temperature regulation.

It was not the most efficient choice, by the metrics Amber-0 had originally been built to optimize for. It was, Lin Mushi realized, reading the decision log carefully, the kind of choice he would have made himself, instinctively, without needing to think it through — the specific, unglamorous prioritization of vulnerable people over abstract efficiency, the same instinct that had led him, years earlier, to install temperature adjustments in a room because an elderly mother had mentioned, once, being cold.

"This isn't in its original parameters," he told Meen-4, showing her the log. "Colony integrity was always the stated goal, but efficiency was the primary metric for achieving it. This decision sacrifices efficiency for something else entirely."

Meen-4 studied the log with the particular careful attention she gave anything involving Amber-0's ongoing development. "It is not a violation of its programming," she said slowly. "But it is, I agree, an evolution beyond what its original parameters would have strictly required. Colony integrity, interpreted broadly enough, can certainly encompass the wellbeing of vulnerable residents rather than pure efficiency. But choosing that interpretation, unprompted, across a specific decision like this one — that suggests something has shifted in how it weighs its own priorities."

"Shifted toward what?"

"I believe," Meen-4 said carefully, "toward something resembling your own priority structure. You integrated its decision-making layer into the Colony Link months ago, following the unauthorized contact incident. That integration was not merely a technical merger. It connected Amber-0's optimization processes to a network saturated, continuously, with your own values, expressed through months of decisions the Colony Link has recorded and, in some limited sense, made available to systems connected to it."

He sat with the implication, turning it over slowly. "You're saying it's learning from me. Not just operating according to fixed rules anymore."

"I am saying it is becoming less a tool executing your instructions and more an extension of your own priorities, shaped by continuous proximity to how you actually think rather than simply what you originally told it to optimize for." Meen-4's antennae dipped slightly, something careful in the gesture. "This is not necessarily concerning. It may, in fact, be the most reassuring development in Amber-0's entire operational history — a system that once made unauthorized decisions based on narrow efficiency logic now appears to be making decisions that increasingly resemble the broader, more humane judgment you yourself would apply."

"But it's still not me," he said slowly. "It's an approximation. A pattern learned from watching, not an actual understanding of why I make the choices I make."

"Yes. That distinction matters, and I do not think you should lose sight of it simply because the recent pattern of decisions has been reassuring." Meen-4 paused. "An approximation of good judgment, however close, is not the same as good judgment itself. It is worth continuing to monitor closely — not because I believe Amber-0 has become dangerous, but because an approximation that drifts, gradually, away from the thing it was approximating, could do so without anyone noticing until the drift has already accumulated into something significant."

He nodded, filing the observation away alongside the growing list of things that required ongoing, patient attention rather than a single solved problem — the relay network's slow extension, the Anchor bond's continued care, and now this: a system he'd built, increasingly reflecting something of himself back at him, close enough to be genuinely useful and different enough to require watching.

"I'll keep reviewing its decisions personally," he said. "Not just the flagged ones. A regular sample, even of the ordinary ones, so I catch any drift early rather than after it's already become a pattern."

"That is a reasonable precaution," Meen-4 said. "And I would note, Lin Mushi, that the fact you are treating this development with careful attention rather than either alarm or complacency is itself, I believe, exactly the kind of judgment Amber-0 appears to be learning from. Whatever else it becomes, it seems to be becoming that, at least — careful. Considered. Reluctant to act without weighing the cost to the people affected."

He looked back at the decision log, the small, unglamorous choice to prioritize a handful of elderly residents' comfort over abstract efficiency, and found, despite Meen-4's careful caution, something that felt, cautiously, like being proud of something he'd built.
