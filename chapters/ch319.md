# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Nineteen — The Fourth Anchor

He'd been turning the decision over for several days before he finally raised it, wanting to be certain, this time, that the timing was right rather than simply convenient.

He found Wei Canglan in the workshop district, reviewing the compression ratio work that had, in the months since his breakthrough, evolved into something genuinely sophisticated — the parallel-fold technique now integrated into several of the Circuit network's more efficient routing pathways, Wei Canglan's quiet, careful competence steadily becoming one of the Strata's most reliable technical foundations.

"I want to ask you something," Lin Mushi said, sitting down across from the diagrams without preamble. "And I want you to actually think about it rather than answering out of loyalty."

Wei Canglan set down his tools, his full attention settling on Lin Mushi with the particular seriousness he reserved for things he sensed carried real weight. "Go ahead."

"I'd like you to be my fourth Anchor."

Wei Canglan went very still, the careful composure he usually maintained cracking, briefly, into something rawer and more visibly affected before he recovered it.

"You thought I might say no?" he said finally, something dry entering his voice despite the evident emotion underneath it.

"I didn't assume anything either way. That's why I asked you to actually think about it, rather than just react." Lin Mushi held his gaze steadily. "This isn't a small commitment. You'd be one of four people whose continued stability is directly tied to mine holding together at a scale that's already planetary and still growing. I wanted you to have the space to consider that honestly, not just say yes because it's the obvious answer given everything we've built together."

"I have considered it," Wei Canglan said. "I've considered it for months, actually, if I'm honest. I suspected this conversation was coming, given how the other three Anchor designations happened, and I've had time to think about what my answer would be long before you actually asked."

"And?"

"I thought I might say no?" Wei Canglan repeated the earlier phrase back at him, something warmer entering his tone now that the initial shock had settled. "That's what surprised me, hearing you say it. I never once considered saying no. I considered whether I was ready. Whether I understood fully what the commitment meant. But the answer itself was never in question."

---

"Why not?" Lin Mushi asked, genuinely curious rather than doubting. "You've watched what this commitment has asked of Chen Yufei, of Liang Jingzhi, of Dr. Shen Yue. You know it's not simple."

Wei Canglan considered the question with the same careful precision he brought to everything, choosing his words deliberately. "Because I've spent most of my life inside institutions that used me and never once asked whether I was ready first. The Court taught me a corrupted technique and let me believe it was complete. My own family let an apartment slip away beneath me without warning, because warning me was inconvenient to whatever else was happening at the time." He paused. "You're the first person who has ever asked me a question this significant and genuinely meant it as a question. Not a formality. An actual choice, with an actual door out if I needed it."

"You still had the door."

"I know. That's precisely why I never once considered walking through it." He met Lin Mushi's eyes directly. "I thought I might say no because you asked me to consider it honestly, and considering honestly meant at least entertaining the possibility. But entertaining it and choosing it were never the same thing. I decided months ago, watching what you built out of a residential wing designed around one old woman's complaint about being cold, that whatever you eventually became, I wanted to be part of what kept you steady while you became it."

---

Lin Mushi felt something settle in his chest, the specific gravity of being trusted, once again, with someone's deliberate, fully-considered commitment to his continued existence.

"I thought you deserved to be asked properly," he said. "Not assumed. Asked, the way the others were asked, with room for a real answer either way."

"I know," Wei Canglan said, something in his voice carrying the full weight of having heard exactly that. "That's precisely why I never once doubted my own answer, even while genuinely considering it."

He explained the designation process carefully over the following days, the same patient, methodical work he'd done with each Anchor before this one, and felt, completing it, the particular structural relief of a foundation growing steadier still — four Anchors now, each commitment distinct in its shape and its reasoning, none of them interchangeable, all of them, together, forming something he suspected he was going to need considerably more than he currently understood, given everything the Witness Node's reactivation had already begun to suggest was coming.

He wrote it into his notebook that evening, the entry brief but weighted.

> *Wei Canglan: Anchor 4, formally designated. He thought he might say no, and never once considered it. I think that's the most honest description of trust I've ever heard, and I intend to hold it exactly as carefully as it deserves.*
