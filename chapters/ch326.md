# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Twenty-Six — Chen Yufei's Departure, Temporary

She raised it over breakfast, three days after the Ring conversation, with the same careful directness she brought to most things that mattered.

"I need to go away for a while," Chen Yufei said. "Three weeks. Yunnan — somewhere without cultivation networks, without Circuit nodes, without any of it."

He set down his chopsticks, giving the request his full attention. "Is something wrong?"

"Nothing's wrong, exactly. That's the honest answer, even though I know it sounds like the kind of thing people say right before something turns out to be very wrong." She met his eyes directly, the same steady honesty he'd come to rely on completely. "I think I need distance from the intensity, for a while. Not from you. I want to be clear about that specifically, because I know how that could land otherwise."

"What intensity?"

"All of it. The Ring's synchronization, the Witness Node, the scale of what the Colony Link means now that you're holding a planet instead of a city. I've been an Anchor for over a year. I've watched everything expand alongside you, and I think I've been so focused on being steady for you that I haven't noticed how much I'm carrying myself, until this week, when the Ring conversation made the whole thing feel suddenly very large and very close."

He nodded slowly, understanding the shape of what she was describing even as some old, protective instinct wanted to ask more questions than the moment probably needed.

"You don't need to justify this further," he said. "If you need distance, take it."

"I wanted to explain anyway. Not because I owe you an explanation for taking care of myself, but because I don't want you spending three weeks wondering if this is about something between us rather than something in me."

"I appreciate that. I still don't need it to say yes."

---

They spent the following two days preparing carefully, deliberately, neither of them rushing the departure despite its urgency — working through the Anchor arrangement's practical implications, confirming that three Anchors would hold the Colony Link's structural stability adequately for the duration, Liang Jingzhi and Dr. Shen Yue and Wei Canglan all agreeing, without hesitation, to carry slightly more weight while Chen Yufei took her distance.

"You're handling this well," Wei Canglan observed, the evening before her departure, watching the two of them work through the logistics with visible calm.

"I don't think handling it well is difficult," Lin Mushi said. "She's asking for something reasonable, for good reasons, and she's being honest about it rather than letting it build into something worse. That makes this easy to support, even if I'll miss her."

"Some people would still find it difficult. Even reasonable requests can feel threatening if you're not secure in what they're actually about."

"I've had two years of practice being secure in what things are actually about," Lin Mushi said. "That's mostly her doing, honestly. She's the one who taught me how to have this kind of conversation without flinching."

---

She left the following morning, a small bag and a train ticket the entire visible weight of the decision, and he walked her to the station rather than letting the goodbye happen anywhere less deliberate.

"Three weeks," she said, at the platform, her hand still in his.

"Three weeks. I'll be here."

"I know you will." She studied him for a moment, something warm and certain in her expression despite the departure ahead of her. "This isn't me leaving you. I want to say that plainly, one more time, before I go. This is me making sure I come back steady enough to actually be useful to you, rather than running on fumes because I never gave myself permission to need distance."

"I understand. I'm not worried about what this means. I'm just going to miss you."

"Good," she said, something almost playful entering her voice despite the moment's underlying weight. "I'd be a little insulted if you weren't."

He watched her board the train, the platform's ordinary bustle continuing around him, and found, standing there, that he genuinely wasn't afraid — not of what her absence might mean, not of whether the arrangement would hold. He trusted her completely, and he trusted, just as completely, that whatever came next in this world's slow, mounting pressure, he'd meet it steadily, with three Anchors instead of four for exactly as long as it took her to come back to him whole.

He walked home alone through streets that felt, for the first time in longer than he could remember, entirely ordinary — no Colony Link pulling at his attention, no Circuit diagnostics waiting, just the simple, uncomplicated fact of missing someone he loved, and trusting, fully, that the missing was itself a kind of care rather than anything to fear.
