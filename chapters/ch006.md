# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Six — Physics Forum

The problem with the Codex diagram was that he had no one to ask about it.

This was not a new problem. Most of Lin Mushi's problems had this shape: he had a thing he wanted to understand, the understanding required specific knowledge he didn't have, and the people who had that knowledge were not the kind of people you could approach at 2 AM with a screenshot of an impossible app and a list of questions you couldn't entirely articulate.

He considered his options.

He could keep the diagram to himself, which was what he had been doing, which was productive for documentation but not for interpretation. He could ask Gao, who was a physics graduate student and might recognize the notation — but Gao didn't know about the app yet, and that conversation required preparation he wasn't ready to do. He could attempt to decode the notation himself through brute pattern analysis, which was what he was already doing and which was slow.

Or he could post a screenshot to the internet and see what happened.

He took the photo on a Wednesday afternoon, phone angled to minimize the background. The diagram filled the frame, amber-gold lines on dark, all its nested hexagonal geometry captured in reasonable resolution. He cropped it twice, trying to remove contextual clues that might make someone ask how he'd obtained the software. He did not succeed entirely — the screenshot had the phone's timestamp in one corner — but it was close enough.

He signed into the physics forum he'd been lurking in for two years. His username was unimaginative: _lin_m_88_. He had forty-seven posts, most of them asking questions in the dark matter threads, one mildly contentious exchange about MOND theory that he still thought he'd been correct about.

He typed the caption without thinking about it too hard:

_Found this software — can anyone identify the notation system? Looks like it might be custom. Interested in the mathematical structure if anyone recognizes it._

He posted. He put the phone face-down and made tea.

---

He checked back six hours later.

Twenty-two views. No replies.

He checked again before his shift. Thirty-one views. Still no replies. This was the usual outcome when he posted things. He restocked the drinks case at midnight with his hypothesis about the forum confirmed.

The reply appeared at 4:17 AM.

He noticed it during the dead stretch, checking his phone in the long quiet between the last customer and the first light. The username was _ShenyueLab_, posted in the cultivation theory subforum rather than the software identification thread where he'd put the question — which meant someone had moved it, or cross-posted it, he couldn't tell which.

The reply was eleven words:

_Structure resembles anomalies in unpublished data I've been working with. Saving your images._

Then nothing. No follow-up question, no request for more information, no indication of who _ShenyueLab_ was or what _unpublished data_ meant or why they had found him in a thread he'd posted in by accident.

Lin Mushi read the reply three times.

He thought about it for the remainder of his shift, with the particular quality of attention he gave to things that didn't fit neatly into any existing category. Not exciting, exactly. More like the sensation of finding a footprint that was the same shape as one you'd made somewhere else, in a place you'd never been.

He did not reply.

He saved the thread URL in his second notebook, on a page near the back where he kept things he needed to return to.

> _Ch.6, post-shift note: Forum reply from 'ShenyueLab' — claims diagram resembles anomalies in their own data. Did not specify field. Did not ask questions. Saved images without explanation._
>
> _This is either a researcher who recognized something, or someone testing what I'd say next._
>
> _Wait. Watch._

He walked home through the early morning with the delivery trucks beginning their routes, and the first breakfast stalls unfurling their awnings, and the city assembling itself back into the day.

The diagram had been seen.

By someone with data that looked like it.

He filed this under _to be revisited_ and kept walking.
