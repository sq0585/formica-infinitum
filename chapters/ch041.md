# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Forty-One — Grief for a Stranger

He listened to the audio file three times.

The first time for information. The second time for everything he'd missed. The third time he realized, partway through, that something was wrong with his face, and he put the phone down and discovered he was crying.

Not dramatically. Not in the way of someone overcome. Just: his eyes were wet, and had apparently been getting that way for some time without his noticing, which meant it had been building while he was paying attention to other things.

He sat with this for a moment.

Ren Wuji had said, in the second audio message that had unlocked when channel four opened: *I had perhaps four minutes left. I used two of them finding you.* He had said it with the same dry precision as everything else — not as sentiment, as logistics. He had had four minutes and he had spent them precisely.

The voice had spent four hundred years alone with a knowledge that the world had no category for. Lin Mushi tried to put a shape on that and found it resisted shaping. Four hundred years was not a number that resolved into experience. It was longer than the Qing dynasty. Longer than the entire history of printed books in China. A person who had been twenty when they entered the Strata would have emerged, if they had emerged, into a world so altered that nothing they'd known would be legible.

Ren Wuji had not emerged. He had stayed. He had learned. He had spent four hundred years in a library below Chengdu, working on something he could not be certain he would ever be able to transmit, and then he had used his last two minutes to try.

---

Lin Mushi went to the kitchen and made tea and stood at the sink looking at the alley behind his building while the kettle heated. A cat on the wall below watched him with the specific indifference of a cat that had seen everything and found it insufficient.

He thought: I did not know this man. I watched him die at a counter. I have been studying what he left me for nearly two months. I know his voice, his precision, his dry humor, the way he structured information for a reader he could not see.

He thought: I did not know him and he is gone and the knowledge he spent four hundred years accumulating was transmitted in 0.4 seconds and now lives in me and I am the only place it lives.

He thought: that is a very large thing to be.

He poured the tea. He went back to the desk.

---

He wrote in the observation notebook, because writing was how he processed things that were too large to hold in just his head:

> *Audio file 2. Content: the Amber Lattice School, the Formican retreat, the Strata's history, the reason Ren Wuji chose the store.*
>
> *He was being pursued. The convenience store was on his escape route. The pursuit was from the Northern Peak Court — one of the human cultivation organizations that had been monitoring the Strata's surface energy signature for years. He knew they were closing in. He used the last two minutes of functional capacity to transmit.*
>
> *He did not choose me because I was exceptional. He chose me because I was the nearest awake mind with sufficient latent receptivity.*
>
> *This is not diminishing. It is specific. He had criteria. I met them. The criteria were about attention and receptivity and the willingness to accept the strange without flinching away — qualities that are less common than they should be.*
>
> *What I feel: I am aware of it and not going to name all of it. The key thing is this: I owe him something.*
>
> *Not completion of a mission — he didn't assign one. Not continuation of his work — the Codex will continue on its own terms, through the comprehension lock, at the pace it requires.*
>
> *I owe him: understanding what he spent four hundred years building well enough to use it as he would have wanted it used. Not exactly as he would have — I don't know enough for that. But in the direction of: carefully, honestly, with attention to consequence.*
>
> *This is what it means that knowledge creates responsibility.*
>
> *He is dead. The knowledge is here. The responsibility follows.*

He read this back.

He capped his pen.

Outside, the Chengdu morning was assembling itself with its usual civic indifference. He could hear the noodle shop owner unlocking the street door below, the faint clatter of the first delivery scooter of the day, the pigeon that lived on the power line doing whatever pigeons did before the city fully woke.

He had seven more channels to open before the threshold crossing. He had approximately five weeks on the timeline.

He opened the notation notebook.

He had something to do that was worth doing.

He did it.
