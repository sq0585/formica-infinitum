# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Forty-One — Grief for a Stranger

He listened to the audio file three times.

The first time for information. The second time for everything he'd missed. The third time he realized, partway through, that something was wrong with his face, and he put the phone down and discovered he was crying.

Not dramatically. Not in the way of someone overcome. Just: his eyes were wet, and had apparently been getting that way for some time without his noticing, which meant it had been building while he was paying attention to other things.

He sat with this for a moment.

---

The second audio file had unlocked at 2 AM, when the fourth channel opened — not the clean professional unlock of the first file but something more abrupt, the notification arriving mid-practice, as if the system had recognized the channel's completion and moved immediately to what came next.

He had listened to it in the stockroom, the way he'd listened to the first one, sitting on the thinking box with his earphones in and the store empty at this hour.

Ren Wuji's voice said:

"You have four channels now. The pace is reasonable. I want to use this file for something different from the first one.

"The first file was orientation — what the work is, where it comes from, what to expect. This file is about me.

"I recognize this is unusual. You did not ask. But I've had four hundred years to consider what a person receiving this transmission would need to know, and I believe — I have come to believe — that understanding what I was, and why I was alone for so long, matters more for the work ahead of you than any technical information I could provide here.

"My name was Ren Wuji. I was born in the Xuande period of the Ming dynasty, which is not a date that means anything immediate to you, so: I was born approximately four hundred and forty years ago. I found the Strata at thirty-one. I entered it at thirty-seven, after six years of the kind of preparation you are now doing, which I had to do entirely alone and incorrectly in many of the ways you have probably also done them incorrectly, though with less systematic documentation than your notebooks suggest you favor.

"I spent thirty years inside the Amber Lattice School. The School was — I will describe it properly because you will encounter its records in the Archive, and I want you to have the human account before the Formican one. The School was a place where Formican and human cultivators worked together. Not in the way of teacher and student exactly, though there was teaching. More in the way of a community that had found something important and was trying to understand it together. There were perhaps twenty human students in my time there. I was unusual in that I stayed longer than any of them.

"When I left, the School no longer existed. I will not tell you why yet. I have written more about this in the Archive, in the volumes that will unlock when your comprehension reaches the appropriate level. What I will tell you now is only this: when I left, I was the last human practitioner who retained full knowledge of the system. And I was being pursued.

"The Northern Peak Court — you will learn much more about the Courts, and I will address this in a later file — the Northern Peak Court had been monitoring the Strata's surface signature for years. They had a partial, garbled understanding of what lay below, and they wanted it. They wanted me, specifically, as the person who had been inside it. I spent the following three hundred and sixty years not being found.

"You should understand what that cost.

"I had no one to transmit to — not for lack of trying, but because the people I found were never quite the right combination of ready, willing, and safe. The Courts had made the cultivation world dangerous for unaffiliated practitioners with knowledge they wanted. I could not transmit to someone the Courts could then find and extract the knowledge from through their acquisition methods. I had to wait for someone who was genuinely outside their reach, or genuinely outside their awareness, or both.

"I waited a very long time.

"The loneliness of it is not the thing I want to dwell on. But I want you to understand it, because you will — in the months ahead, and the years ahead — feel a version of what I felt: the difficulty of carrying something important in a context that has no framework for it. The specific weight of knowing something that you cannot share because no one around you can yet receive it. I carried this for four hundred years. You are already carrying it. I will not pretend it is comfortable.

"What I want you to know is that it becomes familiar. Not easy. Familiar. You learn to live alongside it. And eventually, as you build the relationships that I built too late, it becomes less solitary. Not gone — the weight of this knowledge has a gravity that does not disappear with company. But the solitude inside it changes when there are people who can, at least partially, see what you see.

"Find those people. Start earlier than I did. I started in my three hundred and seventy-first year. I was, as I have mentioned, not particularly efficient about this.

"Two practical notes. First: the Amber Lattice School's records are in the Archive. The history of what happened to it is there. You will read it. I am not going to prepare you for what it says, because I think it should land as it lands.

"Second: I had perhaps four minutes left when I entered your store. I used two of them finding you. I used the remaining two in transmission.

"I did not choose you because you were exceptional. I chose you because you were the nearest awake mind with sufficient latent receptivity in the time I had remaining. The distinction matters. You were not chosen for something you had already become. You were chosen for something you had the capacity to become.

"What you become from here is not my work. It is yours.

"Keep going."

---

He had put the phone down after the third listen, and it was then that he'd noticed his face.

He sat with this for a moment.

Ren Wuji had said: *I had perhaps four minutes left. I used two of them finding you.* He had said it with the same dry precision as everything else — not as sentiment, as logistics. He had had four minutes and he had spent them precisely. And in those four minutes he had been running from the Northern Peak Court, four hundred years old, and he had done the one thing he'd spent three hundred and sixty years trying to do, and then he had died in a convenience store at 3:17 AM with no ceremony and no witness except a twenty-two-year-old night-shift clerk who had not known, when the man leaned against the counter, that he was watching four centuries of patience reach its end.

The voice had spent four hundred years alone with a knowledge that the world had no category for. Lin Mushi tried to put a shape on that and found it resisted shaping. Four hundred years was not a number that resolved into experience. It was longer than the Qing dynasty. Longer than the entire history of printed books in China. A person who had been thirty-one when they entered the Strata would have emerged, if they had emerged, into a world so altered that nothing they'd known would be legible.

Ren Wuji had not emerged. He had stayed. He had learned. He had waited, for three hundred and sixty years, for someone he could safely transmit to, and then he had used his last two minutes to try, and he had succeeded, and then he was gone.

The crying was not for himself. He understood this, sitting with his hands quiet on the desk and the city beginning its first sounds outside the window. He had not lost anything. He had received something — something enormous, from someone he had never met, who had died doing the giving of it.

He was crying for Ren Wuji.

For four hundred years of specific weight. For the twenty years of not knowing whether transmission was possible. For three hundred and sixty years of finding people who were not quite ready, not quite safe, not quite the right combination of the things that were needed. For an end in a convenience store, which was both exactly what it was and also, somehow, exactly right — a person who had learned to live alongside the ordinary finding their last moment inside it, finding their receiver among the unremarkable clutter of a Tuesday night shift.

---

He went to the kitchen and made tea and stood at the sink looking at the alley behind his building while the kettle heated. A cat on the wall below watched him with the specific indifference of a cat that had seen everything and found it insufficient.

He thought: I did not know this man. I watched him die at a counter. I have been studying what he left me for nearly two months. I know his voice, his precision, his dry humor, the way he structured information for a reader he could not see.

He thought: I did not know him and he is gone and the knowledge he spent four hundred years accumulating was transmitted in 0.4 seconds and now lives in me and I am the only place it lives.

He thought: that is a very large thing to be.

He poured the tea. He went back to the desk.

---

He wrote in the observation notebook, because writing was how he processed things that were too large to hold in just his head:

> *Audio file 2. Content: the Amber Lattice School, the Formican retreat, the Strata's history, the reason Ren Wuji chose the store.*
>
> *He was being pursued. The convenience store was on his escape route. The pursuit was from the Northern Peak Court — one of the human cultivation organizations that had been monitoring the Strata's surface energy signature for years. He knew they were closing in. He used the last two minutes of functional capacity to transmit.*
>
> *He did not choose me because I was exceptional. He chose me because I was the nearest awake mind with sufficient latent receptivity.*
>
> *This is not diminishing. It is specific. He had criteria. I met them. The criteria were about attention and receptivity and the willingness to accept the strange without flinching away — qualities that are less common than they should be.*
>
> *What I feel: I am aware of it and not going to name all of it. The key thing is this: I owe him something.*
>
> *Not completion of a mission — he didn't assign one. Not continuation of his work — the Codex will continue on its own terms, through the comprehension lock, at the pace it requires.*
>
> *I owe him: understanding what he spent four hundred years building well enough to use it as he would have wanted it used. Not exactly as he would have — I don't know enough for that. But in the direction of: carefully, honestly, with attention to consequence.*
>
> *This is what it means that knowledge creates responsibility.*
>
> *He is dead. The knowledge is here. The responsibility follows.*

He read this back.

He capped his pen.

Outside, the Chengdu morning was assembling itself with its usual civic indifference. He could hear the noodle shop owner unlocking the street door below, the faint clatter of the first delivery scooter of the day, the pigeon that lived on the power line doing whatever pigeons did before the city fully woke.

He had seven more channels to open before the threshold crossing. He had approximately five weeks on the timeline.

He opened the notation notebook.

He had something to do that was worth doing.

He did it.
