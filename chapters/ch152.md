# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-Two — Northern Peak's Politics

Elder Zhao spoke at the Northern Peak Council session.

Lin Mushi was not present — he had no standing to be present, and the session was an internal Northern Peak Court matter that an outside practitioner attending would have complicated rather than helped. He heard the substance from Elder Kuo two days later, over tea at the circuit-monitored tea house on Wulin Street.

"She was effective," Kuo said. "More effective than I expected."

"What did she say?"

"She described the Iron Meridian array's efficiency assessment — the readings your circuit provided, the corrected technique, the practical results. She was specific. Numbers, not impressions." Kuo turned his cup. "She said: 'I have spent thirty years maintaining a system I did not fully understand. I now understand it. The understanding came from outside our institution, freely given, with no demand attached beyond honesty in return. I would like the Council to consider what it means that this was possible, and what it would mean to refuse it.'"

Lin Mushi was quiet for a moment.

"That's a strong statement for someone from a different Court to make to your Council."

"It was unusual. The Council does not typically hear testimony from non-members." Kuo paused. "She was permitted because Senior Practitioner Liu vouched for the relevance, after his encounter at the Strata. He did not enjoy the encounter. He was, however, honest about what he experienced."

"What did he say?"

"That the archive's authority structure is not negotiable through acquisition, that the entity who intervened was operating at a capability level the Court has no current practitioner who could match, and that—" Kuo paused, choosing the words carefully — "that continuing to pursue an acquisition-based strategy risked a more serious response than a polite retreat."

Lin Mushi thought about Axis-7 at full scale. He thought about what "a more serious response" might mean from a station whose Keepers had, by their own report, executed disciplinary actions before.

He did not know about that yet. He would.

"The vote?" he asked.

"Fifty-five to forty-five. In favor of political engagement over acquisition." Kuo set his cup down. "Narrow. The sixty percent faction lost five points in three weeks. Lady Shen's report, Liu's testimony, and Elder Zhao's statement together moved the needle."

"What does political engagement mean in practice?"

"A formal delegation. Not to the archive — the Council understands now that the archive isn't theirs to negotiate with directly. A formal delegation to you, as the acknowledged Carrier, requesting structured information exchange and a framework for practitioner access on terms the archive sets."

Lin Mushi considered this.

"When?"

"They're drafting the formal request now. I expect it within the month." Kuo looked at him. "You should know: the forty-five percent who voted against engagement haven't disappeared. They've been told to stand down on direct action. Some of them will look for other approaches."

"What kind of other approaches?"

"I don't know specifically. I know the character of the people involved. They are patient and they are used to getting what they want eventually, through means that don't always look like the means they started with." Kuo paused. "I would not assume the situation is resolved because the vote went the right way."

Lin Mushi nodded.

He thought about Chen Yufei's earlier warning: *they're building a coalition. Five Courts. They're not sure what you are, but they've decided you're worth organizing over.*

The vote had gone the right way in one Court. The broader picture — five Courts, multiple factions, multiple agendas — was still in motion.

"Thank you," he said. "For telling me, and for what you said in the session."

"I told the truth," Kuo said. "That's not a favor. It's a habit I'm trying to maintain." He finished his tea. "The forty-five percent worry me more than the sixty did. The sixty wanted something obvious. I don't yet know what the forty-five want."

Lin Mushi thought about this on the walk home.

He added it to the notebook, under a section he'd started calling, with deliberate plainness, *Things to Watch.*

> *Northern Peak vote: 55–45 for engagement. Narrow. The losing faction is patient and unpredictable. Kuo doesn't know their next move. Neither do I.*
>
> *Formal delegation expected within the month. This will require a structured response — I need to think about what 'terms the archive sets' actually means in practice, since I'm the one who will be asked to articulate them.*
>
> *Note: ask Axis-7 what the archive's actual position is on formal human delegations. I've been assuming informal access is sufficient. A formal request changes the shape of what's needed.*

He kept walking.

The spring evening had a quality that made the city feel, briefly, uncomplicated — the trees along the river greening, the evening market smells, ordinary people doing ordinary Thursday things.

He held both pictures at once: the ordinary city, and the slow political machinery turning beneath and around it.

He had gotten better at holding both.

He went home and kept working.
