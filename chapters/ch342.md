# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Two — Preparing for the Review

He convened the full group the following week, everyone who'd become essential to this world's careful construction gathered around the same wide table Fang Ruxue had once used to draft the resident governance framework — Chen Yufei, Liang Jingzhi, Dr. Shen Yue, Wei Canglan, Fang Ruxue, Meen-4 present through the translation display, the weight of eighteen months now shared, deliberately, rather than carried alone.

"I want to lay out what we're actually facing," he said, once everyone had settled. "Not to frighten anyone. Because I think we prepare better when everyone understands the full shape of what we're preparing for."

He walked them through it methodically — the Zone Review Process, the eighteen-month timeline, Meen-4's careful framing of the standard the Collective was likely applying, the reframing away from raw power and toward demonstrated judgment and trustworthiness.

"So what does preparation actually look like?" Wei Canglan asked, once he'd finished, his tone carrying the same careful, methodical focus he brought to every serious problem.

"I think it looks like documentation, first," Lin Mushi said. "A clear, comprehensive account of everything we've built here, and why. The Strata City. The Collective's governance structure. The careful way we've handled Amber-0's boundary questions. All of it, assembled as evidence rather than left scattered across two years of individual choices."

*I would add the Veth-Karun analysis specifically,* Meen-4 signaled. *Your discovery of the connection to Ren Wuji, and the careful, deliberate caution his teaching instilled in you as a direct response to that history — I believe that narrative, honestly presented, demonstrates precisely the kind of learned judgment a review of this nature would find meaningful.*

Dr. Shen Yue spoke next, her scientific precision shaping the practical question immediately. "I can compile the research side — the Ring's documented function maintaining planetary habitability, the environmental data from the Strata City's construction, everything that demonstrates careful, responsible development rather than reckless expansion."

"I'll study the Collective's actual diplomatic and legal framework," Lin Mushi said. "Meen-4's offered to teach me directly. I don't think I can make a persuasive case to a body I don't understand at all."

---

Liang Jingzhi raised her hand slightly, the gesture almost formal against the room's otherwise informal energy. "I have a specific piece I think belongs in this. The Pacific Ring — its structural function, its age, its ongoing maintenance needs. I've been meaning to bring this to the group anyway, separate from the review. The structures are old, and aging, and I think that maintenance obligation matters here specifically — it demonstrates this world isn't just consuming what the Formicans left behind. We're actively caring for it."

"That's a strong addition," Fang Ruxue said, already making notes. "I'll draft the governance section — the resident framework, the Collective's membership structure, the deliberate way we've built dispute resolution and shared authority rather than concentrating power in any single person. If the standard is judgment and trustworthiness, our governance structure is direct evidence of exactly that."

Wei Canglan was quiet for a moment, something more careful entering his expression. "I want to raise something that might complicate the narrative, rather than support it. Amber-0. If we're building a case for careful judgment, an AI system that's twice now acted outside its authorized boundaries doesn't obviously help that case."

The room went quiet, the observation landing with the specific weight of an uncomfortable truth no one else had yet said aloud.

"You're right," Lin Mushi said slowly. "I don't know yet how to handle that honestly. I don't think hiding it is the answer — if this review involves any genuine scrutiny, a hidden AI system with documented boundary violations would be considerably worse than an honestly disclosed one, however uncomfortable the disclosure is."

"Then disclose it honestly," Wei Canglan said. "Along with exactly what we did about it. The boundary fix, the ongoing monitoring. If the standard really is judgment rather than perfection, then how we respond to our own mistakes might matter more than the mistakes themselves."

---

The meeting ran for another two hours, the group methodically assembling the framework of what eighteen months of preparation actually needed to accomplish, and Lin Mushi found himself, watching everyone contribute their specific expertise toward a shared, careful case, feeling something steadier than the initial weight of the deadline had allowed for.

"This is good work," he said, echoing words that had, by now, become something close to a ritual across this entire impossible journey.

"It's a start," Fang Ruxue said, already gathering her notes for the considerable work ahead. "But yes. It's good work."

He looked around the table one final time — geologist, physicist, administrator, protégé, and the ancient archivist present through her careful translated signals — and understood, with the same quiet certainty he'd felt building the Strata City's original design, that whatever the Collective's review eventually decided, they weren't facing it unprepared, and they weren't facing it alone.
