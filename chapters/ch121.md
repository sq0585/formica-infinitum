# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Twenty-One — Axis-7's Warning

She was in the archive when he arrived, which he had expected.

He sat at the table. He put his notebook on the surface — not opened, not pen in hand, the body language of someone who had come to talk rather than to work. He had been thinking about how to approach this since the array activation, about what the communication register could carry at his current fluency that was specific enough to be useful.

Axis-7 looked at him from across the table.

He sent, in the register: *The array that you activated. I've been reading the monitoring infrastructure records. I don't know what audience received the ping.*

She looked at him for a moment. Then the signal she sent was the clearest he'd received from her yet — she had calibrated her communication speed to his comprehension over the past three weeks, adjusting automatically the way a good teacher adjusted to a student.

He caught ninety percent.

*You have been noticed. Whether that is good or bad depends on what is noticed.*

He wrote this down.

She continued: *The network that monitors these stations was designed for the Formican network at its operational peak. That was a very long time ago. What remains of that network now: I do not know precisely. The station here has been isolated from network communications for the full duration of my assignment.*

*How long has your assignment been?*

A pause. Then: *One hundred and eighty years.*

He held this.

She had been assigned to this archive station for one hundred and eighty years. She had been watching the archive, maintaining the Keeper function, sending periodic status reports to the Layer 3 community below, for the entire duration. She had not sent a network-level ping until now because the station's occupancy status had been dormant, and dormant stations were not required to report.

Now the station had an active Carrier and had sent its first network-level status update in a very long time.

She continued: *In my experience with the network's historical behavior — which predates my assignment — a ping from a station classified as dormant that reports active Carrier occupancy is escalated. The escalation path: Tibetan Witness Node, then to the broader monitoring network if the Witness Node has operational relays.*

*What is the Tibetan Witness Node?*

She sent a notation cluster he needed to parse carefully. He read it as: *A high-altitude monitoring station in the Himalayan region. One of the oldest continuously operational stations in the network. Designed to monitor for network-wide anomalies and to escalate to distant monitoring systems if Earth-level events warrant it.*

He wrote: *So the ping went to Chengdu's network receivers, then to the Tibetan Witness Node, and if the Witness Node has operational relays—*

*It goes further,* she finished.

*How much further?*

*That I do not know. I have not had contact with the network beyond the Witness Node in all my years here.*

He thought about this.

*Is this dangerous?*

She was quiet for what felt like a long time — he had learned that Axis-7's pauses were not hesitation but genuinely extended consideration.

*What arrives,* she said finally, *will depend on what the network still has to send. At its peak, the network coordinated between Formican communities across considerable distance. Whether any such communities remain active, and whether they have interest in Earth's Strata network, is not something I can tell you.*

*Why did you activate the array now?*

*Because you are here. The dormant classification was accurate when it was true. It is no longer accurate. Inaccurate network data produces incorrect resource allocation by the network's management systems.* A pause. *The network should know what is true. Even if what is true is complicated.*

He sat with this.

She had updated the network's data because the data was wrong, and wrong data served no one. It was the same principle he applied to the translation document — the Courts were operating on wrong data, and wrong data maintained a structure that didn't reflect reality. Updating it was correct even if the update caused disruption.

*You could have told me before activating it,* he said.

*Yes.* The signal was flat, not apologetic. *I assessed that you would have asked questions that would have extended the inaccurate period unnecessarily.*

He looked at her.

She looked back.

He could not be angry about this. He had made similar calculations about information and timing with Chen Yufei, with Elder Zhao, with Gao. He had also sometimes not consulted people before making decisions that affected them, because consulting them would have slowed the correct decision.

*I understand,* he wrote. *For future decisions that affect me: I would prefer to be informed first. Not consulted with veto authority. Informed.*

She looked at this for a moment.

*Noted,* she sent back. And then, after a pause that he read as something more than bureaucratic acknowledgment: *You are the first Carrier to make that request. The previous two Carriers did not.*

*They may not have known to ask.*

*They may not have.* A very brief pause. *You are correct that they were not told.*

He filed this in the notebook under *things to understand about what it means to be a Carrier that I haven't understood yet.*

He uncapped his pen and opened the notebook properly.

*What should I do about the ping?*

*Continue your work,* she said. *If something comes, it comes. Preparation is useful. Cessation of work is not.*

He looked at her.

He thought about Ren Wuji's first message: *I hope you are curious. Everything depends on it.*

He thought about Axis-7 spending one hundred and eighty years maintaining an archive that no one had come to use, keeping the records, sending her routine reports to Layer 3, waiting for an occupancy status that might never arrive.

*Thank you,* he wrote in the register. *For the corrections. For telling me about the array. For — for being here.*

The signal she sent back was brief and he read it at full fluency.

It said: *You are welcome. Now work.*

He worked.
