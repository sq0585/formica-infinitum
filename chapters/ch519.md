# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 519 — Axis-7 Returns

She arrived the way she had always arrived — once, briefly, unannounced, no ceremony preceding her the way ceremony preceded almost everyone else who had ever entered the pocket dimension laboratory with something significant to say.

He felt her before he saw her, a presence at the edge of the room that carried its own particular gravity, ancient and careful, the same weight he had first felt years ago in an outer library he no longer needed to travel to physically in order to visit. Dr. Shen Yue's figure remained unshared, patient, waiting. Wei Canglan's folder remained closed. The room simply widened, slightly, to hold one more presence, the way it had learned to do for her specifically over the years.

"You felt something happen," Lin Mushi said, not quite a question.

"Everyone within a considerable range felt something happen," Axis-7 said. "The Void Lattice does not repair quietly, at this scale, no matter how carefully the person doing the repairing might wish it to. I came to see for myself rather than trust the readings secondhand."

"What did you find?"

"Confirmation," she said. "Of several things I have suspected for some time, and one thing I have known since long before you were born."

He waited. She did not rush the saying of it, and he had learned, across the years of knowing her, not to rush her either.

"Veth-Karun was, in some capacity, my own student," she said, "before ever reaching Ren Wuji. An elder's instruction, informal, predating the formal tutelage your teacher later provided at greater depth. I taught what I could. It was not sufficient. Ren Wuji taught what remained, and it was, by the account you have just given the pattern I felt resolve, still not entirely sufficient either — though considerably more than I managed, and I have had a long time to sit with that particular comparison."

"You're confirming what Ch.494 already said," he said, gently. "'My student's teacher.' I understood the shape of it then. I chose not to chase the rest."

"I know," she said. "I respected the choice. I am not certain I would have made it myself, at your age, with your particular hunger for understanding things fully before you were willing to set them down. I find I am pleased you did."

"Why."

"Because chasing it would have required you to treat a grief as a mystery to solve," she said, "and I have watched entire civilizations make that mistake, repeatedly, across considerably more time than either of us has lived. A mystery wants resolution. A grief wants only to be carried honestly, for as long as carrying it is required, and then, eventually, set down somewhere that can hold it. You appear, this morning, to have finally found somewhere to set it down."

He thought of the pattern, small and permanent now, folded into the Lattice's own healed memory, and understood that she was, in her characteristically indirect way, telling him she had felt that too.

"I destroyed what came before," she said, echoing the line she had left him on the eve of the arc, the words carrying now a weight they had only gestured toward then. "The School. My part in its ending, disciplinary, deserved by the standards I held at the time, and considerably less certain to me now than it was then. I have had forty million years to reconsider a decision I made in considerably less time than that."

"And what comes after."

"I would like to see it," she said. "I said that once already, and I meant it as an open question rather than a settled one. I am no less uncertain now than I was the night I said it. I find, watching what you did with the pattern this morning, that I am considerably more hopeful about the answer than I was prepared to admit to myself at the time."

He held her gaze — the specific, ancient stillness of her attention, watching him the way she had watched him for years, uncertain, curious, and, for the first time he could clearly recall, unmistakably moved.

"There's more," she said, before he could respond. "I did not come only to confirm what you already understood. I came to tell you what you don't."
