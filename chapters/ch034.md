# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Thirty-Four — The Question of Intent

He wrote it at 3 AM on a night with no customers, while the city held its particular silence and the filaments ran at whatever percentage they were at now, dimming by degrees he could track but not stop.

He wrote it not because the question was new but because he had been circling it for two weeks and circling was not the same as addressing.

> *What is this for?*

He put the pen down. He looked at the sentence.

Then he picked the pen back up.

> *What is the direction of this?*
>
> *I have been documenting for six weeks. I have been learning, opening channels, building frameworks. I have been operating under the assumption that understanding is the goal — that if I understand enough, the direction will become clear.*
>
> *But the direction is not automatically implied by the understanding. Knowing more does not specify what the knowing is for.*
>
> *The man had said: 'I hope you are curious. Everything depends on it.'*
>
> *But he also said: 'The world you live in is a surface. Beneath it is everything that has ever mattered.'*
>
> *Everything that has ever mattered. That is a claim about consequence. A claim about what the knowledge, once you have it, requires of you.*
>
> *What does it require of me?*

He sat with that.

---

He was not a person who struggled with purpose in the ordinary sense. He had dropped out of university because he had a purpose and the curriculum was an obstacle to it. He had taken the night shift because it left days free. He had been filling notebooks for three years with questions that he had not stopped asking because the not-answering was itself meaningful — the gap between what he understood and what existed was the thing that kept him present.

The purpose had always been: understand.

But *understand in order to do what* was a different question.

The man had left him a body of knowledge and a framework and a timeline. He had not left him a mission statement. He had said *I hope you are curious* — which was a character description, not an assignment. He had said *everything depends on it* — which was a consequence, not a specification.

What was the consequence of?

---

He thought about the node depleting. He thought about the coffee-buying practitioner gridding the area, systematic, looking for the source of the anomaly. He thought about the architectural depth below the floor — the vaulted space, the hexagonal tiles, the structure that someone had built and then apparently left.

He thought about the Codex. The four hundred years he had spent in it. The fact that he had chosen to transmit it rather than let it end.

He thought: The man could have let it end. He had four minutes and he chose to spend them transmitting to a stranger in a convenience store. That was a choice made in the full awareness of dying. That choice implied something about what the transmitter believed was worth preserving.

What had the man believed was worth preserving??

The knowledge. The framework. The access to whatever was below.

Why would that be worth preserving?

He picked up the pen.

> *Working answer:*
>
> *The knowledge is worth preserving because the thing it describes is real, and real things affect people whether or not people understand them. The Under-Web exists. The structure below Chengdu exists. The Formicans, whatever they were, existed.*
>
> *People are living on top of things they don't know are there. The knowledge of what is there — the ability to perceive it, to interact with it, to understand it — changes what is possible for them.*
>
> *The man spent 400 years alone with this. He chose me as successor not because I was powerful but because I was 'awake enough.'*
>
> *Awake enough to what? To receive it. And presumably to do something with it that required being awake.*
>
> *The direction: understand first. Then share. The sharing — what, with whom, how, when — is the next question. I don't have enough information to answer it yet.*
>
> *But it is the question.*
>
> *The man had said: 'everything depends on it.' I think 'it' is not my curiosity. 'It' is what I do with what my curiosity finds.*

He drew a line under the entry.

He closed the notebook.

The question had been addressed. Not answered — addressed. There was a difference, and he understood it, and for now that was sufficient.

He opened the notation notebook and went back to work.

He would understand first.

Then he would know what the understanding was for.
