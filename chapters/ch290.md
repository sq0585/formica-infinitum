# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Ninety — What Veth-Karun Was

He returned to the Deep Archive the following morning, deliberately, having slept on the decision and found it unchanged: whatever waited behind that name, he wanted to meet it clear-headed rather than exhausted.

Meen-4 was already in the chamber when he arrived, which surprised him until he understood, watching her posture, that she'd been expecting him.

*You found the reference last night,* she signaled. Not a question.

"I found a name. Veth-Karun. Cross-referenced across sections I didn't expect to connect."

*Yes.* She gestured, and the reading surface shifted, a new record surfacing beneath his hands, its formatting distinct from everything else he'd encountered in the Archive — sparse, clinical, structured the way an incident report is structured rather than the way history usually gets told. *This is the complete record. I did not withhold it from you. I withheld the context that makes it comprehensible, until you had encountered the general shape of Formican governance and understood what a dissolution actually costs a civilization that experiences one.*

He began reading.

Veth-Karun, the record stated without preamble, had been a Formican cultivator of the Amber Lattice School, active during a period the Archive dated only in relative terms — before the Isolationist consolidation, after the Empire's expansion phase had begun to slow. The record's early sections were unremarkable, the kind of biographical scaffolding that could have described any sufficiently accomplished practitioner: steady advancement, recognized talent, a teacher whose identity the record obscured with the same deliberate gap he'd learned, months ago, to associate with Ren Wuji's careful editing.

The unremarkable sections ended abruptly at a single line.

*Veth-Karun achieved Formless Scale through accumulated power rather than accumulated comprehension.*

He read the sentence three times before its full weight settled.

"That's possible?" he asked. "Reaching that scale without understanding it?"

*It is possible,* Meen-4 signaled. *It is not survivable. Power without comprehension does not stabilize. It does not know how to hold itself together at a scale that large. Comprehension is the structure. Without it, expansion simply continues past the point where any coherent self can remain intact within it.*

He returned to the record, the next section reading with a clinical precision that made the content underneath it land harder, not softer.

*The dissolution was immediate. In the interval between achievement and dissolution — measured at approximately 0.7 seconds by the monitoring array active at the time — Veth-Karun produced one coherent signal across the Veritonal network. The signal contained three symbols. Their meaning has never been determined.*

*The Veth-Karun event is the only recorded instance of a Formican cultivator reaching Formless Scale in the Empire's documented history. No attempt at replication has been sanctioned since. This record is preserved as a permanent caution against expansion undertaken without proportional structural support.*

The record ended there — no further detail, no explanation of what "dissolution" had actually looked like, what it had cost the people who'd witnessed it, what had happened to the Amber Lattice School in the aftermath. Just the bare fact of it, sealed and labeled and filed away as a warning, the same way his own world sometimes reduced its worst disasters to statistics because the alternative was too heavy to keep looking at directly.

"Three symbols," he said slowly. "You've had them for — how long?"

*Longer than your species has existed in any recognizable form.*

"And no one's ever translated them."

*No one has been able to.* Something in Meen-4's signal carried a weight he hadn't heard from her before, not quite grief, but adjacent to it, the specific exhaustion of an institution that had spent geological time failing to answer its own most important question. *The symbols do not correspond to any known Formican notation, before or after the event. Several generations of scholars believed they represented a language belonging to whatever Veth-Karun became in the final 0.7 seconds — a self no longer bound by the categories the rest of us think in. If that is correct, translation may not be possible in any conventional sense. There may be nothing left that thought the way we think, capable of explaining what it meant.*

He sat with the full weight of it — a cultivator, once a person, reaching for something too large to hold and dissolving into it in less time than it took to draw a breath, leaving behind three symbols that forty million years of careful scholarship had never managed to read.

"Why does the Archive keep this so deep?" he asked. "If it's a caution, wouldn't you want every carrier to see it early?"

*Because a caution given too early does not caution. It merely frightens.* Meen-4's attention settled on him with unusual steadiness. *You needed to understand, first, what it costs to build something carefully — the residential wing, the record I updated, the structure of governance that makes power survivable rather than merely large. Only having understood that could you read this record and take from it the correct lesson rather than the wrong one.*

"What's the wrong lesson?"

*That power is the danger.* A pause. *Power is not the danger. The danger is reaching for scale before you have built anything capable of holding you steady inside it. Veth-Karun had power in abundance. Veth-Karun had almost no one.*

He looked at the three symbols one final time before closing the record, their shape meaningless to him and likely to remain that way for longer than he could presently imagine, and understood, with the particular clarity that came from finally seeing the true size of a warning he'd been circling for months, exactly why Meen-4 had waited this long to hand it to him.
