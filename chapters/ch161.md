# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixty-One — What the Document Will Say

They spent three days drafting it properly.

Not the careful, narrow translation document from four months ago — forty-seven recipients, selected for honesty, framed to invite questions rather than provoke defensiveness. This was different in scope and intent: a full comparison of the Codex's source notation against the founding texts of all five Echo Courts, with the specific derivation errors and transmission losses documented for each tradition individually, plus the corrected techniques laid out in enough technical detail that any sufficiently trained practitioner could apply them without needing him personally to walk them through it.

This document was not going to forty-seven people.

It was going to every Court, in full, with no curation of the audience.

They worked at his apartment, mostly — the kitchen table covered in notation printouts, Chen Yufei's notes on each Court's specific doctrinal language, the two of them cross-checking every claim against both the Codex source and the historical record before it went into the draft.

On the third evening, she read through the complete draft in one sitting while he made dinner, and when she finished, she sat very still for a long moment.

"This is going to cause a catastrophe," she said.

He set the pot down and came to look at the pages over her shoulder — not because he doubted the content, but because he wanted to understand what she was seeing that had produced that particular stillness.

"Tell me what you mean specifically," he said.

She gathered her thoughts. "The Iron Meridian section, the Northern Peak section — those are calibrated. The recipients already have context, through me and through Lady Shen respectively. But the Celestial Compass School's section says their entire astronomical cultivation theory is built on a partial misreading of a Formican cosmological text that they've treated as scripture for six hundred years. The Verdant Stream Collective's section says their ecological framework conflated two distinct Formican concepts that should never have been merged. The Void Mirror Sect—" she paused — "we barely have material on the Void Mirror Sect. What we do have suggests their entire 'stillness cultivation' doctrine is a garbled, incomplete description of basic Compression-stage technique that they've spent centuries treating as a separate and superior discipline."

"All of that is accurate," he said. "We verified each section against the source material independently."

"I know it's accurate. That's what makes it a catastrophe rather than a correction." She looked up at him. "Accuracy doesn't soften the blow. If anything, accuracy makes the blow land harder, because there's no ambiguity to hide behind. Every one of these institutions is about to learn, simultaneously, in writing, with full documentation, that significant portions of what they've taught as foundational truth for centuries were built on misunderstanding."

He thought about this carefully.

"Or a correction," he said.

She looked at him.

"You said catastrophe," he said. "I'm saying: or a correction. Both are accurate descriptions of the same event, depending on what happens after the document lands. The information itself doesn't determine which one it becomes. What the Courts do with it does."

"That's a very calm thing to say about something you can't control."

"I can't control their response," he agreed. "I can control what I offer alongside the document. Not just the errors — the corrections. Not just what's wrong — what's actually true, in enough detail that any practitioner who wants to rebuild correctly has everything they need to do it." He paused. "The first document, four months ago, was an invitation to ask questions. This one needs to be more than that. It needs to be a complete toolkit for rebuilding, delivered at the same moment as the demolition."

She was quiet for a moment, looking at the pages again with this framing.

"You've structured it that way already," she said slowly. "Each error section is immediately followed by the corrected technique. I noticed that while reading but didn't name what it was doing."

"It's deliberate," he said. "I don't want anyone to read 'you've been wrong for six hundred years' without, in the same breath, reading 'and here is exactly what to do instead.' The grief of being wrong is real. It shouldn't be the only thing they're left holding."

She looked at him for a long moment — the particular look she gave him when something he'd said reorganized her sense of what they were actually doing together.

"Catastrophe and correction," she said. "At the same time. That's actually what you're trying to build."

"Yes."

She picked up her pen and turned back to the draft.

"Then let's make sure the correction half is as strong as the catastrophe half," she said. "Show me the Void Mirror Sect's section again. I think the corrected technique needs more practical detail — we have the theory but not enough of the application."

They worked past midnight.

By the time she left, the draft was as complete as either of them could currently make it.

It was not, they both knew, finished.

But it was close.
