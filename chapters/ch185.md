# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty-Five — Lady Shen's Direct Action

Lady Shen had been training quietly, away from Chengdu's attention, for the past three weeks — ostensibly continuing the cultivation work she'd begun in the archive months ago, but in fact pursuing something more specific and more urgent.

The override of Kuo's recommendation had been, for her, not an act of institutional hostility but of strategic clarity. She had read his report carefully. She did not disagree with its substance — she had told Lin Mushi herself, months ago, that the power vacuum question was real and that someone would fill it. What she disagreed with was the timeline Kuo's recommendation implied: patient, careful, years-long institutional rebuilding while the vacuum remained open and other actors — the more aggressive Northern Peak faction, the fragmenting Iron Meridian, possibly forces from outside the cultivation world entirely — moved to fill it on less favorable terms.

She had decided, in the three weeks since, that the most direct path to a stable outcome was to reach a level of access closer to Lin Mushi's own, as quickly as possible, and negotiate from a position of genuine parity rather than continued dependence on his goodwill.

What she achieved, working in isolation with a focus that bordered on the punishing, was not full Ratio 3 stability — she understood her own limits well enough, after thirty years of disciplined practice, to know that the gap between Ratio 1.4 and true Ratio 3 was not something three weeks could close cleanly, whatever the corrected technique had done for her efficiency. What she achieved was something narrower and, in its way, more dangerous: a forced, unstable approximation of Ratio 3, held through raw output rather than genuine integration — the cultivation equivalent of sprinting on a leg that hadn't finished healing. She could reach the threshold. She could not hold it long, and she could not hold it cleanly, and some part of her that had spent three decades developing exactly the judgment to recognize this kind of risk had decided to attempt it anyway.

She had also, in those three weeks, recovered and refined the spatial-capture technique she had used once before, months earlier, against a different unaffiliated practitioner — the acquisition that had left her last target in the Northern Peak's "secure cultivation environment." She had not needed new theory for this. She had needed only the will to use what she already knew.

She entered the Strata that night, through the thin section, alone.

She reached the outer library in seventeen minutes — not the polished navigation of someone fully at home in the territory, but the product of decades of disciplined movement and spatial awareness, applied with force to an unfamiliar environment and a capability she was, even as she walked, aware she had not fully earned.

His Circuit network alerted him within minutes of her crossing the threshold — not the clean structured signature of a stable Ratio 3 presence, but something jagged at the edges, a reading his system hadn't logged from her before and that he didn't have time to fully interpret before she reached the library.

He was not surprised that she had come. He had been expecting, since Kuo's report and its override reached him through Chen Yufei's network two days earlier, that Lady Shen's response to the institutional stalemate would be direct rather than political.

He was surprised, reading the jagged signature, by what kind of direct it was.

He went to the library to meet her.
