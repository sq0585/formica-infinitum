# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 413 — Fang Ruxue's Extension

She told him about her decision the way she told him about most significant things — plainly, over tea, in the administrative chamber that had, over the years, become as much her office as it was anyone's, with no more ceremony than she'd have given a routine budget update.

"I'm going to undergo the extension protocol," she said. "The Formican one. I've been considering it since before the review even began, and I've finally decided."

He remembered the conversation, months earlier, in the middle of Arc IV's slower stretches — her sitting with the same decision then, weighing it carefully, not yet ready to commit to something that permanent. She was just past fifty-eight now, he guessed, though he'd never actually asked her directly, and the months since that first conversation had clearly done the work of turning consideration into resolve.

"What made you decide," he asked.

"Practical reasons, mostly, though I suspect you'll want the sentimental ones too, so I'll give you both." She poured more tea for both of them, unhurried, the way she approached most conversations that mattered. "Practically: the Collective is entering a phase that will require sustained institutional memory across decades, possibly longer. I have spent years building the Cultivation Research Collective into something functional, and I do not want the next crisis — whatever it turns out to be — to have to be navigated by someone starting from nothing, simply because I ran out of ordinary human years at an inconvenient moment."

"And the sentimental reason?"

She was quiet a moment, something rare and unguarded crossing her expression. "I want to see what this all becomes," she said simply. "I've spent years helping build something extraordinary, watching you grow from a young man overwhelmed by a Codex he couldn't read into someone civilizations argue over. I would like more time to watch what comes next. That's not a particularly noble reason. It's simply an honest one."

He smiled at that, recognizing in her admission the same quiet, stubborn curiosity that had driven his own life for three years. "I don't think that's an ignoble reason at all."

The procedure itself took three months — a process Meen-4 oversaw personally, drawing on Formican biological understanding that predated human medicine by tens of thousands of years, extending Fang Ruxue's functional years by roughly five decades through mechanisms Lin Mushi understood only partially, despite Meen-4's patient attempts to explain the underlying Veritonal principles involved.

He visited her twice during the process, in a private chamber set aside for the treatment, and found her, both times, remarkably unchanged in demeanor — the same careful competence, the same dry humor, simply occupied, for now, with a body slowly undergoing something extraordinary rather than her usual administrative burdens.

"Does it feel strange," he asked, the second visit, near the process's end.

"Less strange than I expected," she admitted. "I thought it might feel like becoming something other than myself. It doesn't. It feels more like — " she paused, searching for the right comparison. "Like discovering a room in a house I already lived in, that I simply hadn't opened yet. The house is the same house. There's just more of it now."

When the process finished, she emerged unchanged in every visible way — no dramatic transformation, no obvious marker that anything significant had occurred at all. She looked, to anyone who didn't already know, exactly like the woman who had walked into the treatment chamber three months earlier. She simply, now, had considerably more time ahead of her than she'd had before.

"How does it feel," Lin Mushi asked, the day she returned fully to her administrative duties, already three reports deep into the backlog that had accumulated during her absence.

"Like I have a great deal of work still to do," she said, not looking up from the document in front of her, "and, fortunately, considerably longer to do it in." She glanced up, something warm and matter-of-fact in her expression. "Don't make a ceremony of it, Lin Mushi. I didn't do this to be celebrated. I did it because the work matters, and I intend to keep doing it for as long as I possibly can."

He nodded, and let the moment pass exactly as she'd wanted it to — handled, matter-of-factly, the extraordinary folded quietly into the ordinary rhythm of a woman who simply had more time, now, to keep building the thing she'd already spent years building.
