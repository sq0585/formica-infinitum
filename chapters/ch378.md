# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 378 — The Containment Orthodoxy's Counter

The counter-submission arrived through the Witness Node five days later, and Meen-4 translated it in full before Lin Mushi read a single line himself, which he understood, once he saw it, as a kindness — she had wanted to be the one to absorb the first shock of it, so that by the time he read it, she could tell him what mattered before the weight of the whole document had a chance to overwhelm him.

"It is well-argued," she said first, which was not the reassurance he'd been hoping for. "I want you to know that before you read it. It is not sloppy. It is not cruel for cruelty's sake. It is, in its own framework, careful."

He read it anyway, start to finish, in the low chitin-ribbed chamber that had become, over these weeks, something like his working office.

The document's central claim was simple, and stated with the same load-bearing precision that characterized everything from the Collective's formal channels. Zone QZ-7's measured dimensional activity — Colony Link range, Chitin Circuit network density, the frequency and scale of pocket-dimension construction — had, over the past eighteen months, exceeded the threshold that had triggered three prior zone enforcement actions in the review body's recorded history. All three of those prior actions, the document noted with clinical detachment, had concluded in neutralization.

He read that sentence three times, feeling its precision settle into him like cold water.

The document did not argue that Lin Mushi was malicious. It did not argue that the Strata's growth was intended as a threat. It argued, instead, that intent was irrelevant to the threshold — that the three prior civilizations neutralized under similar circumstances had also, in each case, possessed leadership the review body's own historical assessments described as *well-intentioned, competent, and ultimately insufficient to the scale of risk their own success had created.*

"That's the argument," he said slowly, once he'd finished. "Not that I'm dangerous. That competence itself is the danger, once you cross a line, regardless of what's driving it."

"Yes," Meen-4 confirmed. "It is technically correct, in the narrowest reading. Your activity has exceeded that threshold. That is a measurable fact, and they have measured it accurately."

"But it's incomplete."

"Morally, yes. It says nothing about what you are doing with that activity. It treats the Veth-Karun analysis, the Strata's cooperative structure, the transparency of your own accounting — all of it — as irrelevant context rather than evidence. To the Orthodoxy, none of that changes the threshold. A civilization that crosses the line with good intentions is, in their framework, exactly as dangerous as one that crosses it with bad ones, because the danger was never about intention in the first place. It was always about capability outrunning wisdom, and capability does not care what the hand attached to it believes."

He sat with that for a long time, turning it over the way he turned over any argument that refused to be simply dismissed. Part of him wanted to be angry — the document was, in its clinical thoroughness, a document arguing calmly for the end of everything he had spent three years building, everything twelve hundred people in the Strata had chosen to build alongside him. But the anger kept sliding off the surface of it, because underneath the coldness was a reasoning he could not, in complete honesty, call wrong. Veth-Karun had happened. A civilization very much like his own, in the ways that mattered, had crossed a threshold with intentions the review body's own records apparently described as good, and the result had scarred this entire zone for forty million years.

"They're not lying," he said finally. "They're just leaving out the part where I might actually be different, and treating that omission as neutral instead of as the whole argument."

"Yes," Meen-4 said. "That is, I believe, precisely the gap the Surveyor's continued attention is meant to test. The Orthodoxy has given the review body a threshold. You have given it a pattern. The question that remains — the only question that actually matters now — is which one the Surveyor believes should determine the outcome."

He looked back down at the document, at its careful, technically correct case for his own civilization's ending, and felt something harden in him that was not quite anger and not quite fear but sat somewhere adjacent to both — the particular resolve of someone who understood, completely, that he was now arguing not just for himself, but against an institution that had, in its own way, learned exactly the wrong lesson from a tragedy he was still trying, piece by careful piece, to understand well enough to make sure it never repeated.

"Then I need to make the pattern impossible to ignore," he said. "Not louder. Clearer."

"Yes," Meen-4 said quietly. "That is exactly what you need to do."
