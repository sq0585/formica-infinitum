# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-One — Lady Shen's Move, Final

She called the meeting herself, in a modest room above a teahouse rather than any formal Court chamber, and Lin Mushi understood the symbolism before she'd said a single word about why she'd chosen it.

Northern Peak's remaining senior practitioners filled perhaps two-thirds of the room — fewer than he'd expected, though Lady Shen's expression suggested the attendance hadn't surprised her at all.

"I am formally dissolving the Northern Peak Court as a political entity," she said, without preamble, her voice carrying the same controlled flatness he remembered from the censure announcement months ago. "Effective today. Members present who wish to continue cultivation under a structured framework may apply for individual membership in the Cultivation Research Collective, under its existing governance and review processes. I will not be requesting any special status or exemption for myself or for any former Northern Peak leadership."

The room's reaction was immediate and varied — some faces carrying relief, others a kind of quiet grief, a few showing the particular stillness of people who had expected this for weeks and were simply relieved the waiting had ended.

She did not pause for questions. She continued, methodical and precise, laying out the practical mechanics: how the Court's remaining assets would be distributed, how ongoing training relationships would transition, how members uncertain about their next step could take time without losing standing in whatever new structure they eventually chose.

Lin Mushi, watching from the room's edge at her invitation rather than as a participant, saw what the speech cost her in the small, controlled details — the slight stiffness in her shoulders, the careful, deliberate pacing of someone managing more than her voice was letting through.

It wasn't graceful. He understood that clearly, watching her work through thirty years of institutional identity in front of the people who had built careers and lives around her authority. Her voice never wavered, but something underneath it carried the weight of a genuine ending, not a strategic pivot dressed up as principle.

She finished with a single sentence that landed harder than anything that had come before it.

"I built something I believed was worth building. I was wrong about its foundation. I am not wrong, I believe, about what should come after it."

She closed the meeting without further ceremony, the room slowly emptying into careful, quiet conversation, and Lin Mushi waited until most of the attendees had gone before approaching her.

She was standing near the window, looking out at the ordinary Chengdu street below, her composure fully restored but something in her stillness suggesting the cost hadn't fully settled yet.

"That was significant," he said.

"It was necessary," she said, not turning. "Significance and necessity aren't always the same thing, but today they happened to coincide."

"Are you alright?"

She considered the question for a long moment before answering, the honesty he'd come to expect from her arriving slower than usual but no less complete.

"I built thirty years of identity around an institution that turned out to be a misunderstanding wearing the costume of tradition," she said. "I don't think 'alright' is the correct word for what I am today. But I think I made the right choice, and I intend to keep believing that even on the days when it doesn't feel particularly good to have made it."

He nodded, understanding the particular discipline of that distinction better than most people would have.

"Northern Peak ceases to exist as an independent faction," he said. "That's not a small thing to have done."

"No," she agreed. "It isn't."

She turned, finally, to face him directly, and something in her expression carried the particular exhaustion of someone who had just finished the hardest thing she'd done in years and hadn't yet found anywhere to set the weight of it down.

"I'd like to apply for membership," she said. "Under the Collective's standard process. No special consideration. I'll submit to Fang Ruxue's governance structure the same as anyone else."

"I'll let her know to expect your application."

"Thank you." She paused, something almost vulnerable crossing her careful expression before she let it pass. "I think this is the right thing. I'd appreciate it if you didn't make me say so more than once today."

"I won't," he said, and meant it, recognizing in her the particular need for the conversation to simply be allowed to end.
