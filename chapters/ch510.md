# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 510 — The Dissolution Zone

He did not travel there.

This was, he understood, the plainest possible way to describe what happened next, and also the most inadequate — travel implied distance, and distance, he was still in the early days of learning, had become one more local convention rather than a rule he was bound by. He simply turned his attention, the way he might once have turned his head, and the dissolution zone was there, entire, thousands of light-years distant and no farther from him now than the cushion beneath his hands.

He had seen it before, in fragments — through Meen-4's careful monitoring, through Liang Jingzhi's own patient, precise reports, through the six-week extension of the original three hundred and twenty-three that had, over the intervening months, become simply *the network*, a fact of the world rather than an ongoing effort. He had never seen it like this.

The wound was enormous. He had always known that, intellectually, the way a person knows a number is large without ever quite feeling its weight. Now he felt it — the sheer, ancient scale of what forty million years of unrepaired dissolution had left behind, a tear in the Void Lattice's fabric that dwarfed, without effort, every human structure he had ever helped build.

And laid across it, patient and deliberate, a gradient.

*Observation 510,* he wrote, and felt, writing it, the particular satisfaction of watching a piece of work someone else had built prove, on close inspection, to be exactly as good as its architect had promised. *The three hundred and twenty-three volunteer anchors have created a genuine coherence gradient across the zone — not a patch, not a containment, an actual directional pull toward repair. The Lattice's own self-repair function is already following it. This has been happening for months. I am only now capable of seeing it clearly.*

He felt something that was not quite pride and not quite relief, some third thing that belonged to neither category cleanly — the specific feeling of watching people he trusted do good, careful, unglamorous work in his absence and finding it exactly as sound as if he had done it himself.

The repair was proceeding. It had been proceeding the whole time, at a pace no human instrument could have easily measured against the scale of what remained undone, but proceeding nonetheless, the Void Lattice's ancient self-correcting nature slowly, patiently following the path the network had laid for it.

He could accelerate it.

He understood this the moment he perceived the gradient's actual shape — not through any specific technique the Archive had described, but through the same plain logical extension every prior threshold had eventually offered him. Where the network's coverage ran thick, the repair moved with confidence. Where it ran thin, at the gradient's furthest, most exposed edges, the repair slowed, uncertain, the way a path loses definition at the edge of where anyone has actually walked it.

He could stand in the thin places.

*I want to be careful here,* he wrote, and meant it, the caution arriving less as fear than as the accumulated habit of five years spent learning, repeatedly, that the difference between help and harm at this scale was rarely a matter of intention. *This is not my repair. It belongs to three hundred and twenty-three people who chose, months ago, to give a piece of themselves to this specific work, permanently, without any certainty it would ever matter this much. Whatever I do here, I want it to complete what they started. I do not want it to overwrite it.*

He felt Liang Jingzhi's presence beside him then, not summoned, simply there, the way she was always there now — a fixed point at the center of the gradient, warm and vast and specific all at once, exactly the paradox she had once tried, patiently, to describe to him secondhand.

*You can see it now,* she sent, and there was something in the signal that was not quite a question.

*I can see all of it,* he sent back. *I can see what you built.*

*Then you understand there's nothing to rescue,* she said. *Only something to finish.*

He held that thought a moment, turning it the careful way he turned everything that mattered, and found it held.

He reached toward the gradient's thinnest edge, and began, deliberately, to add his own weight to a structure three hundred and twenty-three people had already, patiently, months before he was capable of doing this, decided was worth building together.
