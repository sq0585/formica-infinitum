# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eighty-Six — The Strata's Edge

He had been in the vaulted hall eleven times. He still hadn't gotten used to the ceiling.

It wasn't a matter of adjustment. Every time he crossed the threshold, the scale arrived again — thirty-two meters of vault, the columns at their precise intervals, the floor's amber glow tracking his passage at a ten-second lag — and his nervous system registered it as newly significant rather than familiar. This was not a failure of adaptation. He had come to think it was a feature: the Strata refused to become background.

Chen Yufei, stepping through behind him, stopped walking entirely.

He gave her the silence.

Her threshold crossing had been smoother this time — she'd been practicing the Compression geometry he'd shared from the Standard Protocol, and the membrane had yielded in four seconds rather than eight. Her recognition lag on the floor tiles was down to twelve seconds. The Strata was reading her more clearly.

"The scale," she said, eventually.

"Yes."

"My contact from the Celestial Compass School told me she'd heard rumors of an underground formation below Chengdu. She thought it was a natural cavern system." She looked at the vault. "This is not a natural cavern system."

"No."

"The geometry—" She looked at the column spacing, then at the vault's crown, then at the floor pattern. "The same geometry as the notation."

"Yes. Every scale. Molecular through architectural."

She walked to the nearest column and pressed her palm to it the way he had, weeks ago, when he'd first understood what the material was. She held the contact for a moment. Her expression shifted.

"It's warm," she said.

"It's maintained. Active biological-analog processes running through the material."

She took her hand away and looked at it. "The Court has texts that describe Formican construction materials. We always assumed they were mythological." She paused. "We have been assuming a great deal is mythological."

He started walking north. She followed.

The Keeper was not at its usual position near the library door. As they moved deeper into the hall, his Qi perception found it further in — different location than expected, toward the eastern wall rather than the north-center. He adjusted course.

"There's a second structure," he said.

"Where?"

"Eastern wall. Forty meters, approximately. I haven't been in this direction before."

She looked at where he was looking. "You can perceive it from here?"

"My range has increased since the last time I mapped this space. I can feel something there that I couldn't before." He paused. "The Keeper is there."

They walked east.

---

The eastern wall, at forty meters from their approach, had a different quality than the rest of the hall. Not a door — something older than a door, the suggestion of an entrance in the wall's geometry, the hexagonal pattern interrupting and then resuming around an aperture that had no physical frame but was unmistakably meant to be crossed.

The Keeper stood before it.

At Ratio 1 it appeared as approximately the size of a large dog — the way Lin Mushi had described it to himself on first sight, though the comparison did the Formican a disservice. It had the composite exoskeleton he'd read about in the Codex's biological records, amber-gold in base color with darker striations running along the joint lines, compound eyes that caught the hall's ambient light in multiple facets and reflected it in a way that made its field of vision obviously wider than any human's. It stood with a stillness that was not inertia but readiness, the way a person who is very good at their work stands when they're about to do it.

It was looking at Chen Yufei.

Lin Mushi watched. The Keeper looked at Chen Yufei for approximately fifteen seconds, which was long enough to be deliberate and short enough to avoid being threatening. Then it moved its attention to Lin Mushi, held that for five seconds, and returned it to the aperture in the eastern wall.

It stepped through.

It paused on the other side.

"It wants us to follow," Chen Yufei said. Her voice was entirely level.

"Yes."

"Have you been through there before?"

"No."

She looked at the aperture. He looked at it too. Beyond it was a space he hadn't mapped — darker than the main hall, the Qi field inside it running at a different frequency, denser and more organized in a way that made his channels resonate slightly just from proximity.

"Questions to ask," he reminded himself, and wrote them in the notebook he was already holding: *Strata authority structure. Standing of Chitin Seed practitioners within it. The Keeper's function. The aperture's significance.*

Then he stepped through.

Chen Yufei was one step behind him.

The space inside was smaller than the main hall, roughly circular, with a ceiling that was lower — perhaps ten meters. The walls had a different texture: less smooth, more structured, the hexagonal geometry deeper and more pronounced, almost three-dimensional. At the center of the circular space was a raised platform of the same chitin-composite material as everything else, and on the platform was something he had not expected.

A second Keeper.

Older, by some quality he couldn't yet articulate. The exoskeleton carried more of the dark striations along the joint lines, and the compound eyes were a deeper amber, and the stillness it had was a different kind — not readiness but patience, the specific patience of something that had been waiting long enough that waiting had become its natural state.

He stood in the circular room with Chen Yufei beside him and the two Keepers watching, and the question he'd prepared felt suddenly insufficient for the room he was in.

He asked it anyway.

"I need to understand your authority here," he said, in Mandarin, without certainty that he would be understood. "And what standing I have within it."

The older Keeper turned its head — a slow, precise movement — and looked at him.

Then it made a sound.

Not language, not exactly. A vibration in the Qi field of the room, carrying information the way music carried mood — not word-level specificity but something more direct. He felt it in his channels as a structured signal, not quite translatable but not quite untranslatable either.

He had spent five months learning to read Formican notation.

He understood approximately thirty percent of what the signal said.

He opened his notebook.

He wrote what he'd understood.

He was going to need to understand the rest.
