# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifty-Six — The Visit

He took the train to Leshan on Saturday.

The second visit. The first had been in October, when he'd been six weeks in and the gap between his inner world and what he could say about it had felt absolute. This visit was in December, two months later, and the gap was not smaller — it was, if anything, larger, because he knew more — but it had a different quality. In October the gap had felt like concealment. Now it felt like incompleteness. He was not hiding; he was waiting until what he knew was ready to share.

His mother met him at the station with the assessing warmth of the October greeting — the full-information reading of a person who had been monitoring remotely and now had the complete picture.

"You look better," she said.

"I sleep more."

"Miracle of miracles." She hugged him. She still smelled like the kitchen at home. He perceived the Qi field of her presence the way he'd perceived it in October, the pooled warmth of thirty years of habitation — and registered also, for the first time with his current sensitivity, that it was fractionally different from October. Calmer. As if the back pain had resolved into something settled rather than something managed.

He said, as they walked: "Your back is better."

She glanced at him sidelong. "The exercises. I told you."

"You did. I'm glad."

---

They cooked together for the first time.

He had not cooked with her since he was a teenager — not deliberately avoided it, just never arranged it, the way things you don't actively pursue gradually stop happening. She was making the braised pork and he had been assigned the role of vegetable prep, which he performed with adequate competence and she redirected twice with the specific patience of someone who has their own way of doing things and would prefer you not to improvise.

He followed her instructions. He found this satisfying in a way that was different from the satisfaction of solving a problem — simpler, more bodily, something about the physical ordinariness of standing in a kitchen following instructions about how to cut cabbage.

While he cut cabbage she told him about the tomatoes, and the neighbour's new dog, and the community garden project she'd joined, and the friend who'd had her second grandchild. He listened with his full attention, which now included the Qi dimension — the warmth of contentment in the way she described the garden, the slight wistfulness when she mentioned the friend's grandchild.

She was not lonely. She was engaged in the world in the way of someone who had built a life that sustained them. But she was wistful about some things, and he noted this without interpreting it as something he had created or needed to fix.

---

After lunch he walked through the apartment.

He had his perception at normal operating range — not deliberately scanning, just present in the way he was always present now, aware of the Qi field of every space he occupied. The apartment felt exactly as it had in October: the pooled warmth in the kitchen, the slower movement of energy in the bedroom and study, the particular quality of the light in the northeast corner by the window where she kept her plants.

He stood in the kitchen doorway and felt thirty years of her life in the air of the room, and felt something that he did not try to name but that was in the family of gratitude.

> *Second visit home. December.*
>
> *Observable: she is well. The Qi field of the apartment is settled in a way October's wasn't — the back pain had been creating a mild disruption in the ambient field; it's resolved now.*
>
> *What cooking together showed: she likes company in the kitchen. Not help — company. The distinction matters.*
>
> *What I perceived: contentment in the garden stories. Wistfulness around the friend's grandchild. These are not my business in the sense of requiring action, but they are information I hold as care rather than data.*
>
> *The kitchen is the warmest room. It always has been. Now I know why: three decades of habitation, specifically in that room, specifically in the ritual of cooking and eating and the small domestics of daily life. The warmth is her, distributed through the space like a Qi field that has had thirty years to settle.*
>
> *The Qi landscape of love, observed scientifically.*
>
> *Experienced not scientifically at all.*

He crossed out the last line. Then he wrote it again.

It was true. He was allowed to note things that were true.

He took the evening train back to Chengdu.

The next morning he attempted his first Compression.
