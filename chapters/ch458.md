# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 458 — She Says Yes

She had the kettle on when he arrived, the same as always, and two cups already set out on the table alongside her notebook, closed now, its two weeks of careful questions apparently settled into whatever conclusion waited underneath the cover.

"Sit," she said, pouring the tea before he'd fully taken off his coat. "I've thought about this properly, the way I think about everything that actually matters, and I'd like to tell you my answer the way I organized it in my head, if you don't mind. It came out as a list. I'm too old to pretend I don't think in lists."

"I wouldn't expect anything else," he said, sitting, feeling something in his chest go very still and very attentive, the way it had before every genuinely significant threshold of the past five years.

She opened the notebook, though she didn't look down at it — he suspected she'd simply needed something to hold while she spoke, the way she always needed her hands occupied for the conversations that mattered most.

"One," she said. "I want to see what you become. Not because I need to understand every part of it — I don't think I ever will, not completely, and I've made my peace with that. But I raised a boy who used to ask me why the sky was blue until I ran out of good answers, and some part of me has been wanting to know what happens next in his life since the day he was born. I'm not ready to stop finding out."

He felt his throat tighten unexpectedly, and said nothing, understanding she wasn't finished.

"Two," she continued. "My garden needs more time than I currently have left to give it properly. I've been meaning to redo the whole eastern bed for three years now, and every spring I tell myself next year, and every year my knees remind me why I keep putting it off. I would like enough years to actually finish it."

He laughed, despite the tightness in his chest, the sound arriving before he'd fully decided to let it, and she looked up at him with the particular satisfaction of someone who had planned exactly that reaction.

"That one was for you," she admitted. "I know it sounds smaller than it is. It isn't, actually. But I thought you needed something to laugh about tonight, and I've always been better at making you laugh than at making you cry, even when the moment technically calls for either."

"Keep going," he said, still smiling.

"Three. I have never, in seventy-four years, been afraid of something new. Not when your father and I moved to Chengdu with nothing but two suitcases and no family nearby. Not when I started the shop alone after he died. Not when you told me, five years ago, that you were leaving the convenience store for something you couldn't fully explain yet, and I decided, then, to trust you anyway. I am not going to start being afraid of new things now, at the exact moment my son turns out to be one of the most remarkable new things this world has ever quietly produced."

"Mā —"

"Four," she said, before he could finish whatever he'd been about to say. "My joints hurt, my heart is tired, and if this will help with that, then good. I am practical before I am sentimental, whatever the previous three points might have suggested otherwise. If there is a way to hurt less and do more, I would like to take it."

She closed the notebook, finally, and looked at him directly, her expression carrying the same steady clarity she'd carried through every decision of his entire childhood.

"That's my answer," she said. "Yes. I want the extension. I want to see what you become, I want my garden finished properly, I have never been afraid of anything new, and my knees genuinely do hurt. Is that a complete enough consent for Meen-4's protocol, or does she need something more formal than a mother's list?"

He reached across the table and took her hand, the same hand that had steadied him through every difficulty of his entire life, and found he couldn't speak for a moment, the specific, overwhelming warmth of her answer settling somewhere too deep for words to immediately reach.

"It's complete enough," he managed finally. "It's more than complete enough."

"Good," she said, satisfied, and poured more tea, the ordinary rhythm of the evening resuming exactly the way it always did in her kitchen, regardless of how enormous the conversation happening inside it had actually been.
