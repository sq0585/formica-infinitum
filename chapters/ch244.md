# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Four — The Warning

The call came at 2:07 AM, and he was awake before the second ring finished, the particular instinct two years of crisis had trained into him recognizing the hour itself as a kind of warning before he'd even looked at the screen.

"Construction equipment," Liang Jingzhi said, without preamble, her voice carrying none of its usual measured calm. "Heavy machinery, staged near the Wuhou district coordinates I flagged for you yesterday. I've been running passive seismic monitoring on that location since your message — habit, mostly, I didn't expect anything this fast."

"Show me."

"I'm sending the data now. The crew's mobilization schedule has a soft start logged for early afternoon — staging and equipment checks first, full demolition work beginning once the permits clear final municipal sign-off. Based on the documents I pulled, that puts active demolition at approximately one PM."

He did the arithmetic immediately, the cold clarity arriving the way it always did once a threat moved from theoretical to a number on a clock.

"Just under eleven hours."

"That's my read as well." A pause, the sound of her checking something on her end. "Lin Mushi, the coordinates match the anchor I surveyed three weeks ago almost exactly. This isn't speculative anymore. Someone is specifically targeting that structure, and they're targeting it now."

He was already moving, pulling on clothes with one hand, the phone braced against his shoulder.

"I need everything you can give me," he said. "Structural data, the anchor's exact tolerance thresholds if you have any estimate of them, anything that tells me how much physical disruption it can absorb before something becomes irreversible."

"I don't have precise tolerance data — nobody's ever stress-tested an anchor point deliberately, for obvious reasons. But based on the degradation patterns I've already mapped across the network, I'd estimate this anchor has meaningfully less margin than most. It's older, more central to the broader structure, and it's been carrying additional load since the maintenance gap earlier this year."

"So less time to work with than I'd hoped."

"Significantly less, by my estimate. I'm sorry I don't have anything more precise."

"You have considerably more than I'd have had without you," he said, already composing the alert he was about to send Chen Yufei, Wei Canglan, and every Collective member he could reasonably reach inside the next few minutes. "This is exactly the warning I needed. Thank you."

"Be careful," she said, the careful, professional clip in her voice cracking just slightly around the words. "I've spent weeks mapping how much actually depends on these anchors holding. I don't want to be the person who found the threat in time and then had to watch it happen anyway."

"You won't be," he said, with more certainty than he entirely felt. "I have just under eleven hours, and I'm not going to waste any of them."

He ended the call and sent the alert in the same motion, the apartment dark around him, the city outside still hours from waking, and felt the particular focus settle over him that he'd learned, across two years of crises exactly like this one, to trust more than almost anything else he carried.

Eleven hours. He'd made do with less before. He intended, walking quickly toward the convenience store's threshold through streets still empty enough to run through, to make this one count for every minute it offered.
