# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty — The Northern Peak's Ultimatum

The Northern Peak Court's formal ultimatum arrived three days later, delivered through the same official channel that had carried the original acquisition mandate, eight months earlier, and the formal recognition offer four months after that.

This was different from both. Lin Mushi read it standing at his desk, the document's tone shifting noticeably from the previous two — less diplomatic care, more institutional assertion.

*The Northern Peak Court's Elder Council, in formal session, has determined that the individual operating under the name Lin Mushi represents an unverified and unauthorized cultivation practitioner whose claims of access to source Formican material cannot be independently confirmed through any process the Council recognizes as legitimate.*

*The Council hereby requires that the individual present himself for formal authentication testing, conducted under Northern Peak Court supervision, within thirty days of this notice. Authentication will assess the veracity of claimed Compression capability, communication register fluency, and source material access. Failure to comply within the stated period will result in classification as a rogue cultivation element, subject to whatever measures the Council deems appropriate for managing unverified and potentially destabilizing practitioners.*

He read it twice.

He thought about the word *rogue* — its specific institutional weight, the implicit threat underneath the bureaucratic phrasing, the fact that "whatever measures the Council deems appropriate" had, in Lady Shen's earlier description of her own Court's acquisition mandate, included the specific euphemism *secure cultivation environment.*

He thought about Lady Shen's analysis — the power vacuum, the candidates competing to fill it. This ultimatum, he understood, was the more aggressive faction's answer to that vacuum: assert authority over him directly, force him into a position where either compliance legitimized their ongoing claim to jurisdiction, or non-compliance gave them institutional justification for whatever came next.

He picked up his pen.

He wrote, in the document's margin, in his own small precise hand: *Under whose authority?*

He did not write a formal response.

He set the document aside and went to make tea, the same way he'd handled the first acquisition mandate eight months ago — not with panic, not with immediate reactive strategy, but with the deliberate pause that let him think clearly before acting.

Chen Yufei found the document on his desk that evening, the margin note visible.

"You're not going to respond," she said. It wasn't quite a question.

"Not formally. Not yet." He poured her tea. "I think responding to this ultimatum on its own terms — engaging with the premise that the Northern Peak Council has standing to demand authentication testing from me — concedes something I don't think is true. They don't have that authority. Acknowledging the demand, even to refuse it formally, treats the demand as legitimate enough to require a formal refusal."

"And if you simply ignore it?"

"Then in thirty days, they classify me as rogue, and act accordingly." He sat down across from her. "I don't think silence avoids the confrontation. I think it just determines which terms the confrontation happens on. I'd rather it happen on terms where I haven't validated their framing."

Chen Yufei considered this.

"Lady Shen warned you," she said. "About the vacuum. About someone filling it whether you intend to or not. This ultimatum is exactly that — the Northern Peak Council deciding that if no one else fills the vacuum with explicit authority, they will, by force if necessary."

"I know," he said. "I think the answer isn't to fight them for the vacuum on their terms. I think it's to build something that makes the vacuum question itself less relevant — a structure based on demonstrated capability and genuine community, the way Axis-7 described the Amber Lattice School operating for six centuries. Something that doesn't compete with the Courts for the same kind of authority, because it isn't built on the same foundation."

Chen Yufei was quiet for a long moment.

"That's a much harder thing to build than a counter-ultimatum," she said.

"I know," he said. "I think it's also the only answer that actually solves the problem instead of just winning the current fight."

They sat with the ultimatum between them for a while, untouched, the thirty-day clock already running.

He had work to do, and not much time to do it carefully.
