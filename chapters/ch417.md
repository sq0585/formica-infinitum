# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 417 — The Fifth Anchor Revisited

He decided to tell her directly on an evening that carried no particular significance beyond his own sense that the moment had finally arrived — no crisis prompting it, no review forcing his hand, simply the accumulated weight of something he'd carried quietly for a long time, finally ready to be spoken aloud.

They were in the Archive's outer chamber, the low chitin-ribbed room that had, over the past year, become the site of more consequential conversations than almost anywhere else in the Strata. Meen-4 was reviewing a section of documentation he hadn't asked about, and he let her finish before he spoke.

"I need to tell you something," he said. "About Chapter 350. About what I did with the Anchor Protocol, months before the review even started."

Something in her posture stilled, the particular quality of attention she gave to subjects she'd been anticipating for longer than she'd let on.

"I am aware," she said simply. "I have been aware since you did it."

He felt the sentence land with a specific, quiet weight — not surprise exactly, some part of him had suspected, over the months since, that nothing happened within the Colony Link's structure that Meen-4's own perception failed to notice eventually. But hearing it confirmed directly, after months of neither of them naming it aloud, carried its own particular gravity.

"Why didn't you say anything," he asked. "All this time. Through the whole review. You never once mentioned it, even when it would have been relevant to explain to the Surveyor exactly how many Anchors the Colony Link actually holds."

"I was deciding whether to object," she said.

"And?"

She was quiet a long moment, her antennae angled toward him with an intensity that told him the answer, however simply stated, had not been simply arrived at.

"I did not object," she said finally.

He sat with that, understanding, slowly, the full weight of what those five words actually carried. Not indifference. Not oversight. A deliberate, considered choice, held privately for months, to allow something to continue that she could, at any point, have chosen to end or at least formally question.

"Why not," he asked quietly. "You could have. I would have understood if you had."

"Because the bond functions," she said, and something in her voice carried an honesty he rarely heard from her, unguarded in a way that felt, in the moment, like its own kind of gift. "I have observed it, carefully, for months — the same steadiness the other four Anchors provide, the same mutual benefit, the same considered, careful use rather than exploitation. You did not ask my permission before establishing it. That is a genuine breach of the trust the Protocol is built on, and I want you to hear me say that plainly, because I do not want this conversation to become a comfortable resolution of something that was not, in fact, comfortable when it happened."

"I know," he said. "I've known that the whole time. I should have asked first."

"You should have," she agreed. "But having not asked, and having then watched what you built with it — I found, over months, that objecting to something already functioning well would have cost more than it protected. The bond is stable. It has not weakened you, or me, or the Colony Link's broader structure. It has, if anything, made all three stronger. I chose, eventually, to let the functioning speak for itself, rather than insist on a permission that would have changed nothing about the outcome except the manner of its beginning."

He absorbed that, feeling something in his chest ease that he hadn't fully realized he'd been carrying — months of quiet, private guilt over a decision made in urgency, finally met with an honest, considered response rather than either condemnation or easy forgiveness.

"Thank you," he said. "For telling me now. And for not telling me sooner, in a strange way — I think I needed to sit with not knowing, for a while, to actually understand what I'd asked of you without asking."

"I understood that instinct," Meen-4 said. "It is, I believe, the same instinct that has carried you through every difficult choice in this entire review. You do not simply want forgiveness. You want to have actually earned whatever trust follows a mistake." She paused, something almost gentle entering her tone. "You have earned it, Lin Mushi. I am not merely tolerating the bond. I am, and have been for some time now, its willing fifth Anchor."

He let the words settle, quiet and enormous at once, and found, sitting there in the low chitin-ribbed chamber, that the moment required no further comment — simply the shared, mutual acknowledgment of something that had, in truth, been true and steady for a very long time before either of them finally said so aloud.
