# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Twelve — Dormancy, Three Days

He slept through most of the first day, waking only briefly for water and a bowl of soup Chen Yufei brought without being asked, before the dormancy's heaviness pulled him back under with a gentleness that felt, oddly, like being cared for rather than like illness.

"How do you feel?" she asked, the second time she checked on him, finding him finally sitting upright against the headboard, the room's manufactured daylight filtering through the false window in a way that made the hour genuinely difficult to guess.

"Quiet," he said, and found, testing the word, that it was exactly right. "Not tired, exactly. Just — quiet. Like something that's usually running in the background has actually stopped, for the first time in longer than I can remember."

"Is that supposed to happen?"

"Meen-4 said dormancy after a comprehension threshold could last days. I think this is just what it looks like from the inside."

She sat on the edge of the bed, studying him with the careful attention she brought to anything involving his wellbeing, and seemed, gradually, to relax once it became clear the stillness wasn't distress. "Then rest. Actually rest. I mean that as an instruction, not a suggestion."

---

By the second day, the exhaustion had eased enough that he found himself simply, uncomplicatedly bored — an experience so unfamiliar after two years of constant demand on his attention that it took him most of the morning to recognize it for what it was.

He found, rummaging through a small shelf in the residential wing's common room, a paperback novel someone had left behind — cheap, well-worn, a mystery of some kind judging by the cover, entirely unrelated to anything Formican, Qi-based, or in any way connected to the vast, expanding responsibility that had consumed nearly every waking hour of the past two years.

He read the first page out of simple curiosity, and found, somewhere around the third chapter, that he'd been reading for over an hour without once checking whether Amber-0 had flagged anything, without once extending his Colony Link to check on a resident's wellbeing, without once thinking about Veth-Karun or the Pelagic Archive or the deep-space signal or anything at all beyond the small, contained, entirely fictional problem of who had stolen a diamond from a countryside estate.

He finished the book that evening, the specific, uncomplicated pleasure of it staying with him longer than he'd expected — not because the mystery itself was especially remarkable, but because it had been, for several unbroken hours, the only thing occupying his attention, a luxury he hadn't fully understood he'd been missing.

---

Wei Canglan found him on the third evening, playing cards with Chen Yufei at the small common room table, a game neither of them was taking especially seriously.

"You're losing," Wei Canglan observed, watching the hand play out with visible amusement.

"I'm losing badly," Lin Mushi admitted. "I don't think I've played cards since before Ren Wuji died. I've apparently forgotten how."

"Sit down and I'll show you," Chen Yufei said, already reshuffling. "Best two out of three."

Wei Canglan sat, and the three of them played through the evening with a lightness Lin Mushi hadn't allowed himself in longer than he could easily calculate — no urgency underneath it, no half-attention diverted toward network diagnostics or Colony Link sensing, just the small, ordinary pleasure of losing repeatedly at cards to two people he trusted completely.

"You should let yourself do this more often," Wei Canglan said, midway through the second game, dealing a fresh hand with careful precision. "Not because you're dormant and have no choice. Because it's good for you, independent of whether the Chitin Seed forces the issue."

"I know," Lin Mushi admitted. "I don't know if I'm good at doing it voluntarily, though. Dormancy makes the choice for me. Left to my own judgment, I suspect I'd keep finding reasons to check just one more thing."

"Then maybe that's worth noticing too," Chen Yufei said, laying down a winning hand with visible satisfaction, "separate from whatever the comprehension threshold actually unlocks. Rest that only happens because your body forces it isn't the same as rest you actually choose."

He sat with that as she collected her winnings, the observation landing with more weight than the light, easy game around it might have suggested, and understood, watching Wei Canglan reshuffle for a third round, that whatever emerged from this dormancy in the coming days, he wanted to carry at least this much of it forward deliberately — the simple, uncomplicated discipline of choosing rest sometimes, rather than only accepting it when the Chitin Seed left him no other option.

He lost the third game too, and found, losing it, that he minded considerably less than he once might have.
