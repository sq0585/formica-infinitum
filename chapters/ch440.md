# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 440 — Where the Records End

He decided, in the end, to keep reading — carefully, the same discipline he'd held throughout, but reading nonetheless, because a door left partway open was still, technically, an invitation, and five years of curiosity had never once taught him that stopping short of understanding was the safer choice. Only that rushing was.

The entries thinned as he worked forward through them, and at first he assumed it was simply the natural texture of the record — the crew's earlier, patient decades compressed here into something more urgent, fewer words carrying more weight, exactly as he'd have expected from researchers working against whatever pressure the anomaly had started to represent for them.

Then, gradually, he noticed something else. Not urgency. Absence.

*Observation 396: entry frequency across the flagged section's final weeks drops sharply — from daily observations, sustained across the archive's entire earlier history, to entries several days apart, then over a week apart, then, in the final stretch, nothing at all for a span of time the installation's own internal clock measures with disquieting precision.*

He checked the data three times, the way he checked anything that suggested a conclusion he didn't want to trust on a single pass, and each time the pattern held. Not corruption — he ruled that out carefully, cross-referencing against the archive's internal integrity checks, which reported every section, including this one, as fully intact, uncorrupted, exactly as originally recorded. The record simply stopped accumulating new entries, gradually, the way a habit falls away rather than the way a wall collapses.

*Rule: this is not data loss. This is a diary that ran out of things to say. The distinction matters — corruption implies something happened to the record. This implies something happened to the people writing it.*

He sat with that sentence a long time before letting himself write the next one.

The final entries, when he reached them, were shorter than anything preceding them — not the compressed urgency of researchers racing against time, but something closer to the specific brevity of people conserving effort, writing only what felt essential, the way a person exhausted past their normal limits stops elaborating and simply states the fact.

*Approach vector confirmed,* one read, and nothing more.

*Instrument calibration holding at reduced tolerance,* read another, several days later by the installation's clock, according to his careful reconstruction.

*Departure scheduled,* read the last full entry he could find in the flagged section proper. *Full crew complement. Remote installation to maintain standard operation in our absence.*

And then, after that: nothing. Not a final entry marking an end. Not a closing note, a summary, a formal handoff to whoever came next the way the message at the archive's threshold had so carefully provided. Simply the last recorded thought of someone who had, evidently, expected to come back and write more, and never had.

He set the notebook down, the specific chill of the command chamber settling into him in a way that had nothing to do with the room's actual temperature.

*Observation 399,* he wrote, and found the number itself, arriving so close to a round figure, felt like an unwelcome kind of significance. *The record's final entry describes a departure, not a conclusion. Whoever wrote this did not know it was the last thing they would write. They were still, as far as their own understanding went, in the middle of an ongoing investigation.*

He thought of the message waiting at the archive's threshold — *you will understand why we left when you see what is there* — and understood, now, with a clarity that settled uncomfortably rather than satisfyingly, that the sentence carried a weight he hadn't fully registered on first reading. Not *why we left the installation.* Something closer, he suspected now, to *why we left and did not come back.*

He checked the relay chain — stable, Chen Yufei's last message still sitting unanswered, six hours old now — and found he needed, before going any further, to actually answer it, to hear something ordinary and terrestrial and alive before he let himself finish reading whatever the archive had left to tell him.

*Reading's slower than expected,* he wrote back, honestly, though not yet completely. *Found something that needs more care than I expected. Give me another day.*

Her reply came faster than the relay chain's usual lag should have allowed, as if she'd been sitting with the notebook already open. *Take whatever you need. We're still not going anywhere.*

He held onto that a moment longer than strictly necessary, and then turned back, carefully, to the last thing the record actually had left to tell him.
