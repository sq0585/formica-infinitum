# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Thirty — The Withdrawal

Gou Ping's report reached Lady Shen while she was still inside the Strata, working through governance notes with Lin Mushi in the alcove she'd taken to using for their meetings — a coincidence of timing that meant she learned about her own faction's unauthorized assault in real time, her expression shifting from focused concentration to something colder within the space of a single read.

She set her phone down without speaking for a moment.

"Three of the nine who withdrew early sustained spatial disorientation injuries," she said finally, her voice carrying the careful, controlled flatness he'd come to recognize as the surface over something considerably less controlled underneath. "The remaining five, including Gou Ping, retreated after ninety minutes without reaching any objective."

"That matches what I observed."

"You knew this was happening."

"I had eight minutes of warning before they arrived. Enough to prepare. Not enough to reach you in time without risking that the warning itself might accelerate their timeline." He watched her carefully, gauging how much of her composure was holding and how much was about to give way. "I was going to tell you regardless of the outcome, once I'd had time to confirm everyone involved was safe."

"You didn't trust me to stop it."

"I didn't have time to find out whether I should trust you to stop it. That's a different thing." He paused. "I trust you considerably more now than I did three months ago. I didn't trust the eight minutes I had to test that trust against an organization I don't fully understand the internal politics of."

She was quiet for a long moment, the anger he'd half-expected sitting visibly beneath her surface, controlled but unmistakably present.

"Fourteen of my own people," she said. "Organized under my nose, using channels and loyalty I built myself, to act directly against the position I've spent months establishing. Gou Ping didn't simply disagree with my approach. He organized an operation specifically designed to make that disagreement irreversible before I could intervene."

"What happens now?"

"Now I find out exactly how much authority I actually still have inside my own Court," she said, "or whether the last three months have been an elaborate performance I gave myself the comfort of believing was real."

She stood, gathering the governance notes with hands that were, he noticed, not entirely steady — the same controlled tremor he'd felt in his own hands after the eleven-second fold, the particular physical signature of someone managing more than they were currently showing.

"I need to address this directly," she said. "Today. Before Gou Ping has time to construct a version of events where this looks like reasonable initiative rather than insubordination."

"I understand."

She paused at the alcove's entrance, looking back at him with an expression he hadn't seen from her before — something raw underneath the careful political calculation, the look of someone about to walk into a confrontation whose outcome she genuinely couldn't predict.

"For what it's worth," she said, "I'm glad no one was seriously hurt. I'm glad you handled it the way you did instead of the way you could have."

"I know."

"That doesn't make this less of a problem."

"No," he agreed. "It doesn't."

She left without further comment, the weight of what was coming visible in the set of her shoulders, and he sat alone in the alcove afterward with the quiet certainty that whatever she was about to do would tell him, more clearly than any conversation between the two of them ever could, exactly how far her transformation actually went.
