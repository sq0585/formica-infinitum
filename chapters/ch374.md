# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 374 — Fang Ruxue's Response

Fang Ruxue made the offer in person, which Lin Mushi understood, watching it happen, was itself a kind of respect — the sort of thing that shouldn't be delivered by email, not after what an email had just taken away.

They met in the Collective's administrative chamber again, though this time it was arranged more formally than usual — actual tea poured into actual cups instead of mugs grabbed at random, Fang Ruxue seated rather than standing over a whiteboard, a folder of documents laid out with the deliberate neatness of someone who wanted the gesture to read as considered rather than improvised.

"I'm not here to offer sympathy," Fang Ruxue said, once the tea had been poured and the small talk exhausted. "You've had enough of that this week, I expect, and I doubt it's helped much."

"It hasn't," Dr. Shen Yue admitted, with the dry honesty Lin Mushi had come to associate specifically with her. "But I appreciate you skipping it anyway."

"Then I'll be direct. The Cultivation Research Collective would like to offer you a formal research position. Funded through the Strata City's resource-sharing framework — which means better funded than your university position, not marginally, substantially. Full access to the Archive. Full access to the Chitin Circuit network's data, which is more than any outside researcher currently has, and more than most inside researchers, frankly. You would report to no committee that could revoke your standing over the content of your findings."

Dr. Shen Yue was quiet for a long moment, turning her tea cup slowly in her hands without drinking from it.

"That's a significant offer," she said finally.

"It's meant to be. You're not a consolation hire. You're the person whose two papers made this entire disclosure process credible to institutions that would otherwise have dismissed everything as fringe theory. I'm not offering you this because I feel guilty about what the university did. I'm offering it because it is, by any honest measure, the correct decision for the Collective's research capacity."

"But."

Fang Ruxue's expression shifted, something more careful entering it. "But I would be doing you a disservice if I pretended it was the same thing you built. It's not academic tenure. It's not a title your parents will recognize when you explain it to them. It's not the institution you spent eleven years climbing toward, and it will not, for a long time, carry the outside credibility that institution carried, however unfairly that institution just treated you. I'm offering you something objectively better resourced and subjectively more complicated. I want you to accept it with your eyes fully open to that difference, or not accept it at all."

Lin Mushi, sitting slightly apart from the conversation with the specific instinct to let this decision belong entirely to the two of them, watched Dr. Shen Yue turn the offer over the way she turned over data — testing it from multiple angles before trusting a conclusion.

"Why are you telling me the downside," she asked. "Most people selling an offer don't volunteer its weaknesses."

"Because you'll find them yourself within a month if I don't, and then you'll wonder what else I didn't tell you," Fang Ruxue said simply. "I'd rather you trust every word I say to you for the next decade than have you doubt me over something this small."

Something in Dr. Shen Yue's posture eased slightly at that — not agreement yet, but the particular loosening of someone who had just been handled with more honesty than she'd expected.

"Give me until tomorrow," she said.

"Take until Friday," Fang Ruxue said. "This decision deserves more than an evening."

She left with the same unhurried composure she'd arrived with, and Dr. Shen Yue sat with her tea, now gone cold, staring at the folder of documents without opening it.

"What would you do," she asked Lin Mushi, once the two of them were alone.

"I don't think that's a fair question to ask me," he said carefully. "I built the thing she's offering you access to. I'm not neutral."

"I know. Ask me a different way, then. Not what would you do. What do you think I actually want, underneath the part of me that's still angry about Tuesday."

He considered it honestly, the way he considered any observation that mattered. "I think you wanted, at twenty-two, to spend your life finding true things and telling people about them. I think the university offered you one shape for that, and it worked, until it didn't. And I think this offer isn't the same shape. But I don't think you were ever actually in love with the shape. I think you were in love with the finding and the telling. The Collective can give you more of both than the university ever could again, even if it never gives you back exactly what you lost."

She was quiet a long time after that, and when she finally spoke, her voice had lost some of its earlier brittleness. "That's an uncomfortably accurate thing to say to someone still packing boxes."

"You asked."

"I did." She almost smiled. "Give me until Friday anyway. I want to sit with both truths a little longer before I let either one win."

He nodded, and left her with the cold tea and the unopened folder, understanding that some decisions, even the objectively correct ones, deserved to be arrived at slowly, carried by someone who was allowed to grieve what she was leaving even as she walked toward what came next.
