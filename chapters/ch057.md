# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifty-Seven — What She Knows

He had been on the train back to Chengdu for thirty minutes when his phone buzzed.

His mother, texting, which she had been doing with increasing competence over the past year: *I forgot to tell you something. Call me when you arrive.*

He called from the station.

"I had a health scare in September," she said, without preamble, the way she delivered things she'd decided to deliver. "Chest pain. I went to the hospital. It turned out to be nothing — the doctor said costochondritis, inflammation in the cartilage. He said it was stress-related." A pause. "I didn't tell you at the time because I didn't want you to worry."

He stood at the station exit with people moving around him and processed what she'd said.

September. He had been six weeks into the Codex at that point. He had called her that Sunday and noticed sub-verbal information in her voice — something she was holding back, something that gave the words a different texture than their surface. He had asked about her back. He had not known what else to ask.

"Are you well now?" he said.

"Completely. It was nothing, in the end."

"But in September it wasn't nothing."

"No. In September it wasn't." A pause. "I'm telling you now because you said you were doing something important. I thought — if you're going to do something important, you should know what was happening with me. We should be honest in both directions."

He stood at the station exit and felt something settle.

"Thank you," he said.

"It was nothing. But it didn't feel like nothing at the time."

"I know."

A silence that was not uncomfortable. Then: "Are you eating?"

"Yes."

"The standard noodles or the good ones?"

He paused. "The standard ones."

"Buy the good ones," she said. "Whatever you're doing, do it with good noodles."

---

On the train he wrote:

> *She had a health scare in September. Told me tonight.*
>
> *What I noticed in the October visit: an unevenness in the Qi flow around her lower back that I associated with the back pain she'd mentioned. I assumed the unevenness was the cause. It may have been the residue of the September scare — anxiety held in the body, dispersing over weeks.*
>
> *What I perceived in tonight's call: she was relieved to tell me. The sub-verbal texture of relief. She had been carrying this as something she hadn't shared.*
>
> *She said: we should be honest in both directions.*
>
> *I have not been dishonest with her. I have been incomplete. The distinction is real, but her point is also real: incompleteness is a form of holding back, and holding back is not quite the same as trust.*
>
> *I am not ready to tell her everything. I don't know when I will be.*
>
> *But I need to close the distance further than I have. She moved toward honesty tonight. That calls for something.*
>
> *Noting: she is well. The scare was resolved. The residue has dispersed.*
>
> *Noting also: I was curious about the world before I was curious about her. The Chitin Seed is making me more curious about her. This is one of the things it is for.*

He looked out the train window at the dark outside, the lights of small towns going past in the gaps between the hills.

He had been curious about the world. He was now curious about people. About what they carried that they didn't say.

His mother had been carrying September for three months.

He thought about that all the way home.
