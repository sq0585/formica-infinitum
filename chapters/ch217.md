# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Seventeen — Axis-7 Returns

Axis-7 had been absent from the inner sections for three weeks, long enough that Lin Mushi had started to wonder, without quite admitting the worry to himself, whether something had gone wrong somewhere in the parts of her life he had no visibility into.

She reappeared in the outer hall on a Wednesday evening, no announcement, no change in her bearing that suggested anything unusual had occurred, and addressed him in the communication register before he'd fully registered her presence.

*Report requested.*

He gave her one — the maintenance rotation, Chen Yufei's breakthrough, the Stone Hall surveillance, the slow political churn of three Courts holding position against two that had walked away. She listened, or whatever the Formican equivalent of listening was, without interrupting, her attention precise and unhurried in the way he'd come to recognize as her default mode.

When he finished, she was quiet for a moment longer than usual.

*Assessment, delayed,* she signaled. *Your tactical use of spatial geometry during the Lady Shen confrontation was adequate. Your emotional investment in the outcome was a liability.*

He caught most of it, the technical vocabulary becoming more familiar with practice, though it took him a moment to understand why she was raising something that had happened so long ago.

"That confrontation," he said slowly. "That was months ago. Why now?"

*Observation requires time to resolve into useful conclusion. I do not report incomplete analysis.*

*Confirmed.* The signal that followed was longer, more deliberate. *Lady Shen observed your hesitation when she targeted Chen Yufei.*

He went still.

He hadn't been aware of any hesitation. He remembered the confrontation in fragments — the geometry shifting under Lady Shen's control, the moment Chen Yufei had intervened, his own response to that intervention. He'd assumed his actions in that sequence had been clean, decisive, the kind of response he'd trained for.

"I didn't notice a hesitation," he said.

*You would not. The interval was approximately 0.3 seconds. Your conscious processing does not operate at sufficient resolution to register it. Lady Shen's tactical training does.* Axis-7's signal carried no inflection he could read as either criticism or sympathy, simply the flat, accurate weight of an observation she considered important to deliver regardless of how it landed. *She identified your relationship to Chen Yufei as a structural vulnerability in the first ninety seconds of direct confrontation. She adjusted her approach accordingly. This is why the confrontation nearly succeeded despite your spatial advantages.*

He sat with this for a long moment, turning it over with the same careful, unhappy attention he gave most facts about himself he hadn't asked for and couldn't immediately argue with.

"You're telling me she used Chen Yufei against me."

*I am telling you she identified an exploitable variable and used it efficiently. The variable was not Chen Yufei's presence. The variable was your reaction to threats against her.* A pause, the kind that in a human conversation might have signaled consideration rather than processing delay. *This is not a flaw I am instructing you to remove. Emotional attachment of the kind you carry has produced outcomes in this conflict that pure tactical calculation would not have produced. I am instructing you to be aware of it as a known variable, so that future opponents who identify it cannot use it as effectively as Lady Shen did.*

"Awareness without elimination."

*Correct. The Formican Empire's tactical doctrine did not require the elimination of attachment. It required the conscious management of attachment's exploitable surface area.* Something in the signal's cadence shifted, fractionally, into a register he was beginning to associate with her version of dry observation. *You are not a soldier. You are a Carrier who occasionally requires tactical assessment. I account for the difference.*

He almost smiled, despite the weight of what she'd just told him.

"Thank you," he said. "For telling me directly instead of letting me find out the hard way again."

*The hard way carries higher cost. I prefer efficient information transfer.* She turned, already moving back toward the inner sections, then paused. *Chen Yufei should also know this. The vulnerability exists in both directions, and she does not currently account for it.*

He watched her go, the careful, precise gait that never seemed to waste motion, and sat for a long time afterward with the notebook open in front of him, unable for once to find the right first sentence.

He wrote, eventually, just the fact of it, plain and unadorned, because some things resisted being made more comfortable than they actually were.

> *0.3 seconds. I didn't know. She did. I need to tell Chen Yufei before someone else uses the same gap against either of us.*
