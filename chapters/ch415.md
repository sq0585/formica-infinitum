# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 415 — The Off-Earth Installation

He returned to the dormant signal with the same careful attention he'd once given a single stubborn notation symbol, sitting alone in the Archive's central chamber, letting his awareness extend outward at Planetary Architect scale toward a coordinate he had, for months, treated as a lower priority than everything actively demanding his attention.

It had not moved. It had not changed. It sat exactly where it had first registered, months earlier, a faint signature at the edge of his perceptual range, patient in the specific way only something built to last millennia could afford to be patient.

He worked at it slowly, over several days, extending his perception incrementally rather than forcing the reach all at once — a discipline Meen-4 had taught him early, and one he'd learned, through painful experience, not to abandon even when curiosity urged him toward speed. Roughly eight billion kilometers out, in the outer solar system, far beyond anything the Colony Link's current range had ever attempted to touch. A Formican installation, he confirmed, the geometry unmistakable once his perception grew sharp enough to resolve its structure — still functioning, though at a fraction of whatever power it had once carried, the signal thin and steady, the particular quality of something running on the barest sustainable minimum rather than something actively dying.

"It's not derelict," he told Meen-4, once he'd gathered enough detail to describe it with confidence. "It's dormant. There's a difference. Something built it to survive exactly this — long silence, minimal power, indefinite patience."

Meen-4 studied his description with an intensity he'd rarely seen from her, even accounting for how significant the discovery clearly was. "I believe it holds the stellar-observation records the Stellar Scale curriculum requires," she said slowly. "I want to be careful about how confident I sound in that belief, because I do not have direct confirmation. But the installation's positioning, its power signature, the general category of Formican engineering it resembles — all of it is consistent with an observatory. A structure built specifically to watch stars, from a vantage point Earth's own surface could never provide."

"Why is it dormant, though? If it's this important, why wasn't it maintained?"

"I don't know," she admitted, and there was something in the admission that told him this was not a comfortable gap in her knowledge. "The Archive's records regarding this specific installation are incomplete — deliberately, I suspect, though I cannot yet tell you why. It predates my own active memory considerably. Whatever maintained it once, whoever built it and staffed it and kept it functioning at full capacity, that history is simply not available to me from where I currently stand."

He sat with that uncertainty, feeling the particular pull of an unanswered question settle into him the way it always did — not fear exactly, something closer to the old, familiar curiosity that had carried him through three years of exactly this kind of careful, incremental discovery.

"Can I reach it," he asked. "Not travel to it. Just — extend perception far enough to actually study it directly, the way I've studied everything else."

"Not yet," Meen-4 said. "Your current reach, even at Planetary Architect scale, extends reliably only to the edge of Earth's own gravitational influence. Eight billion kilometers is considerably beyond that. Reaching it would require extending the Chitin Circuit network itself — not physically, obviously, but through the same dimensional folding techniques you've used to grow the Colony Link's range over the past three years. Incrementally. Carefully. The way you have built everything else."

He nodded, already feeling the shape of the next project settle into place — not urgent, not a crisis demanding immediate resolution, but a genuine, patient extension of everything he'd spent years learning to build.

"Then that's what comes next," he said. "Not the Archive alone. Not waiting on the Pelagic request. Reaching that installation, one careful step at a time, until it's close enough to actually study."

"That will take time," Meen-4 warned. "Considerable time, if you insist on doing it as carefully as everything else you've built."

"I've got time now," he said, and found, saying it, that the sentence carried a particular weight it hadn't carried a few months earlier — the quiet, hard-won confidence of someone who had survived the thing that could have taken all his time away entirely, and now, finally, had room to spend some of what remained on a question that had been patiently waiting, eight billion kilometers away, for someone curious enough to come looking.
