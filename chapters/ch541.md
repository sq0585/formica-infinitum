# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 541 — Curious

He sat alone in the pocket dimension laboratory that evening, by choice, the way he had once sat alone the night before Formless Scale first opened, and opened notebook forty-seven to its next blank page.

He sat with the blank page a while before writing anything at all.

He thought of the first blank page he'd ever faced, years ago, a much younger and considerably less prepared version of himself staring at an empty notebook a dying man's final gesture had somehow, inexplicably, made feel urgent. He had not known, that first night, what he was supposed to write. He understood now, sitting with a considerably more experienced hand and a considerably longer history behind him, that the not-knowing had never actually been a problem to solve. It had simply been the correct starting condition for everything that followed.

He wrote one word at the top of the page.

*Curious.*

He held the pen a moment longer, looking at the single word, and felt, looking at it, the specific, unmistakable echo of a stranger's assessment of him at 3:17 in the morning, six years and an entire extraordinary life ago — the same quality Ren Wuji had identified in him before either of them understood what it would eventually cost, or what it would eventually build.

He closed the notebook without adding anything further, and let the word stand alone, exactly as complete as it needed to be.

The morning arrived the way mornings before every major threshold had arrived across the last six years — methodical, unhurried, the specific discipline of careful preparation he had learned, patiently, was not a symptom of fear but a genuine act of respect for what was coming.

He ran the full check, one final time.

Five Anchors confirmed — Chen Yufei's presence warm and ready, arriving from wherever the Collective's pilot program currently had her stationed; Wei Canglan's connection precise, characteristically thorough; Dr. Shen Yue's instruments humming their patient, familiar readiness; Meen-4 present in her corner, ancient and steady; Liang Jingzhi's signal arriving from the dissolution zone, both halves of her, the office and the center, entirely, permanently herself.

Three hundred and twenty-three dissolution zone anchors confirmed, their gradient holding steady, the repair continuing its slow, patient work without requiring his direct attention for even a single morning.

Coherence: 0.99.

The Strata operational — twelve cities, eight thousand residents in Chengdu alone, a mayor who measured her policies against soil and lunch, a chief archivist four records deep into the most eventful year of a forty-million-year career.

His mother, gardening, the best tomatoes of her life still coming in.

Wei Canglan filed a formal objection to the expedition's timeline precisely as he arrived, the folder open this time, the specific unforgiving thoroughness of a man who cared too much to let even a morning this significant pass without full documentation.

"Solar position 7.4-N," Wei Canglan said, reading from the folder's opening line, formal and precise. "Too soon. Insufficient preparation. I want it on the record, one final time, before the day actually starts."

"Received," Lin Mushi said, the same plain acknowledgment he'd offered him a hundred times before. "Noted. Registered."

"You're not going to reconsider."

"No," Lin Mushi said, gently. "But I want you to know the objection is heard properly. Not dismissed. Heard, and unchanged."

Wei Canglan held his gaze a long moment, and then, apparently satisfied by the honesty of the answer if not its content, gave the same small precise nod he gave every closed item on any agenda he had ever kept.

"Then I've done what I can," he said. "The rest is yours."

Lin Mushi looked around the laboratory once more — at five people, at a notebook with one word written in it, at the whole careful, patient structure of a life he had spent six years building specifically so that this exact morning could arrive prepared rather than reckless.

He was ready. He understood, this time, that readiness was not a feeling he needed to wait for.

He closed his eyes, curious, unhurried, and turned his attention toward the edge of everything the Archive had ever left unexplained.

Before the reach completed, he allowed himself one final, private moment — not a farewell, he understood clearly that this was not an ending of any kind, simply a threshold, one more in a long and patient sequence he had learned, across six years, to cross carefully rather than fearfully. He held the shape of the room around him a moment longer: Chen Yufei's steady warmth, Wei Canglan's precise, protective vigilance, Dr. Shen Yue's patient instruments, Meen-4's ancient stillness, Liang Jingzhi's permanent, undivided presence.

Five people. Three hundred and twenty-three more. A single fixed point that had never once needed to grow larger to match whatever scale he eventually reached: 3:17 in the morning, a convenience store, a dying man's hand closing around his wrist.

He held all of it, entirely, the way he had learned to hold everything that mattered, and let the reach begin.
