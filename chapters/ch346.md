# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Six — The Ring Repair Project

The project began the following week, and settled, almost immediately, into a rhythm considerably steadier than the community hall's excited energy had initially suggested it might achieve — three months of patient, unglamorous, genuinely collaborative work, unfolding one careful repair cycle at a time.

Lin Mushi handled the deepest access, his Dimensional Weaving carrying small, rotating teams down to the ocean floor structures no ordinary equipment could reach — a fold opened, held steady, the team working within its protected boundary for hours at a stretch before he closed it again and moved to the next site.

"This is considerably more physically demanding than I expected," he admitted to Chen Yufei, midway through the second week, the specific exhaustion of sustained, deliberate Weaving settling into his shoulders in a way the Strata City's more routine work rarely had.

"You're holding a stable environment against four thousand meters of ocean pressure, for hours, while a team works inside it," she said. "I'd be more worried if it weren't exhausting."

Liang Jingzhi coordinated the geological assessment at each site, her instruments and his own now-mature sensing working in careful tandem to identify exactly where the ancient structures had weakened, and exactly what reinforcement each specific weakness required. Meen-4's Formican construction knowledge shaped the actual repair technique, her four hundred years of engineering discipline meeting a genuinely novel problem — repairing structures built by a civilization that had never anticipated the specific, gradual decay forty million years of continuous operation would eventually produce.

---

"This is unusual work for me," Meen-4 admitted, during one of the project's quieter evenings, the two of them reviewing the day's repairs together. "I have spent four hundred years maintaining, not repairing. The Strata's original construction predates my own tenure considerably. I have never before had the opportunity to actually mend something this ancient, this significant, with my own direct involvement.*

"How does it feel?"

*Meaningful,* she signaled, after a pause long enough to suggest she'd genuinely considered the question rather than offering a reflexive answer. *I have spent considerable time, across recent months, reconsidering my own relationship to this world's history — the Amber Lattice School, the disciplinary action I once believed correct. This project offers something different. Not reconsideration of the past. Genuine, direct contribution to the future. I find I value that considerably.*

He understood, hearing that, exactly how much weight this quiet admission carried, coming from someone who had spent three thousand years — or in Axis-7's case, considerably longer — largely observing rather than directly building.

---

The Circuit team's contribution proved, over the project's second month, considerably more significant than anyone had initially anticipated — Wei Canglan's parallel-fold compression technique, developed months earlier during his three-week struggle with Compression Ratio Four, adapting almost perfectly to the specific structural challenge of reinforcing joints under sustained deep-ocean pressure.

"This is exactly what the technique was built for," Wei Canglan said, watching the joint reinforcement hold steady under Liang Jingzhi's stress testing, something quietly satisfied in his usually composed expression. "I didn't know it at the time. I was just trying to solve a compression problem that had nothing to do with any of this."

"That's how it usually works," Lin Mushi said. "You build something carefully, for its own reasons, and it turns out to matter for reasons you couldn't have anticipated."

By the project's final weeks, the rhythm had become almost meditative — Lin Mushi opening the fold, the team descending, Liang Jingzhi's assessment, Meen-4's careful guidance, Wei Canglan's reinforcement technique, the fold closing, the team surfacing, the next site waiting patiently for its turn.

---

He stood at the twelfth and final node's completion, three months of patient, collaborative labor finally reaching its conclusion, the entire team gathered for what felt, despite no one having planned any ceremony, like a genuine milestone worth marking.

"Twelve for twelve," Liang Jingzhi said, reviewing the final structural assessment with visible satisfaction. "Every node reinforced, stress indicators back within safe operating range across the board. This should hold for considerably longer than either the original structures or our repairs could have managed alone."

*This is good work,* Meen-4 signaled, the phrase she'd first offered him standing in an empty hall two years earlier now settling, once again, into exactly the moment it deserved.

"It's shared work," Lin Mushi said, looking around at the team that had made it possible — geologist, archivist, technician, and himself, three months of patient, unglamorous collaboration finally complete. "I don't think I could have done any single piece of this alone, and I don't think I'd have wanted to, even if I could have."

He wrote about it that evening, the entry carrying the specific, grounded satisfaction of genuine, completed work.

> *Twelve nodes, three months, one team. The Ring will hold for considerably longer now. I don't know yet whether this matters to the Collective's review the way we hope it does. I know it matters regardless — real stewardship, not performed for an audience, offered because forty million years of quiet Formican care deserved, finally, to be returned in kind.*
