# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 366 — First Contact Protocol

The message came through the Tibetan Witness Node at 9:47 that morning, and Lin Mushi felt it arrive the way he'd once felt his phone buzz in his pocket during his convenience store years — a small, specific interruption in an otherwise continuous stream of attention.

He was three thousand kilometers from the Node itself, sitting in the Strata's central chamber with Meen-4 and a printout of the morning's Circuit diagnostics, when the notation began resolving in the part of his mind that stayed permanently threaded through the Node's receiving array. Formican notation, but formal in a register he hadn't encountered before — not the technical shorthand of the Deep Archive, not the conversational cadence Meen-4 used when she was being informal with him. This was structured the way a legal document was structured. Every symbol load-bearing.

Meen-4's antennae stilled before he'd finished parsing the first line. She had received it too, faster than he had, and was already holding very still in the particular way she held still when something required her full processing capacity.

"Read it to me as you get it," he said. "I'll follow along."

"It is formatted as diplomatic notation," she said. "Tier structure I have not used since — " She paused, an unusual hesitation. "Since before you were born. This tier is reserved for contact between the Collective and civilizations under formal review."

The notation continued resolving, phrase by phrase, and he transcribed as she spoke, his handwriting getting worse the faster she went.

*Designation: Surveyor, acting in formal capacity on behalf of the Collective review body assigned to Zone QZ-7.*

*Purpose: to conduct a productive review of Zone QZ-7's status, in accordance with established protocol, commencing upon acknowledgment of this transmission.*

*Request: formal acknowledgment and designation of a primary point of contact for the duration of review proceedings.*

That was all. No threat stated outright. No urgency indicated by tone, because the notation didn't carry tone the way human language did — it carried precision, and precision, Lin Mushi was beginning to understand, could function as a kind of threat all on its own. *A productive review.* The phrase sat in his notebook looking perfectly reasonable and somehow managing to imply, underneath its reasonableness, that an unproductive one was also a possibility, and that the difference mattered enormously to someone.

"What happens," he asked, "if we don't acknowledge it?"

"I don't know," Meen-4 said. "No civilization I have record of has failed to acknowledge a Surveyor's opening transmission. Whether that is because acknowledgment is always the correct choice, or because civilizations that do not acknowledge do not remain civilizations long enough to be recorded — I cannot tell you which."

He set the pen down.

Three years ago, a discrepancy like this would have terrified him in a way that made his hands shake. It still frightened him now, but the fear had changed shape — quieter, more procedural, folded into the same observational habits that had once helped him parse a dying scholar's Codex one stubborn symbol at a time. Observe. Compare. Form a hypothesis. Test.

"Draft an acknowledgment," he said. "Formal register, matching theirs as closely as you can teach me to write it. Point of contact: me. No qualifiers, no hedging — if I'm going to be the one talking to something like this, I should be the one whose name is on the door."

Meen-4 studied him for a long moment with an expression he'd learned, over years, corresponded roughly to something between approval and worry, though she would never phrase it that way herself. "That is either very wise or very reckless," she said. "I have not yet determined which."

"Neither have I," he admitted. "We'll find out at the same rate they do."

They spent forty minutes composing eleven lines of notation. Meen-4 corrected his grammar three times and his tone once, softening a phrase that had, in his draft, come out sounding defensive rather than composed. When it was finished, he read it back to himself in the flat mechanical translation his own imperfect fluency produced, and found it said, essentially: *We received your message. We understand its formality. We are prepared. My name is Lin Mushi, and I will answer for what you find here.*

He sent it through the Node at 11:03 AM.

The reply came back in under four minutes — too fast for anything that had crossed interstellar distance to have actually traveled, which meant the Surveyor was already close, already listening, already positioned exactly where it wanted to be before it had said a single word to anyone on Earth.

*Acknowledgment received. Point of contact designated: Lin Mushi. Formal proceedings to commence at a time and format to be determined by the review body's assessment of Zone QZ-7's readiness. Further contact will follow.*

Seven words in the middle of it stopped him longer than anything else in the message.

*The review body's assessment of Zone QZ-7's readiness.*

Not Earth's readiness. Not humanity's. A zone's — a designation, a boundary on someone else's map, drawn around a world that had never been asked whether it wanted to be inside it. He sat with that for a while, the way he'd once sat with a single untranslated symbol for eleven minutes before the meaning came.

Then he picked the notebook back up and started listing everything he thought the Surveyor might want to know, so that when further contact came, he wouldn't be starting from nothing.
