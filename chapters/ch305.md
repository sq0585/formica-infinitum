# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Five — Formican Consent

The objection arrived through formal channels, which was itself unusual enough that Lin Mushi noticed the form before he'd even read the content — a written notice, submitted through the administrative structure Fang Ruxue had built, rather than the informal conversation that governed most disagreements in the Strata.

*Formal objection: proposed District 4 placement overlaps meditation space currently in continuous use. Filed by: Axis-7.*

He found her that afternoon in the section of the Strata the objection referenced — a quiet alcove near the older archive wing, unremarkable to look at, plain stone and soft ambient light, nothing about it suggesting the three thousand years of use Meen-4 had mentioned weeks earlier.

"You filed a formal objection," he said, settling nearby without intruding on whatever practice she'd been maintaining.

*I did.* She didn't turn from her stillness immediately, finishing whatever internal process she'd been mid-way through before addressing him fully. *The workshop district's proposed western boundary, as currently drafted, extends into this space. I am not opposed to the city's expansion. I am opposed to this specific placement.*

"You could have just told me directly. You didn't need to file it formally."

*I know.* Something in her signal carried a weight he was still learning to read accurately. *I filed it formally because I wanted the objection recorded as a matter of process, not merely resolved through personal conversation with you specifically. If the city is going to have a real governance structure, I want to be among the first to use it as intended, rather than relying on the older habit of simply speaking to whoever holds the most influence.*

He sat with that, recognizing in it something he hadn't fully anticipated when Fang Ruxue's framework had first been drafted — that its value lay not only in resolving disputes fairly, but in giving even the Strata's oldest, most established members a reason to trust the structure over personal appeal.

"That's a good reason," he said. "I'm glad you used it that way."

---

He reviewed the design with Liang Jingzhi and Fang Ruxue the following day, the three of them working through the specific geometry of the conflict, and found, once he actually looked closely, that the fix required considerably less compromise than he'd initially braced for.

"The overlap is narrow," Liang Jingzhi said, tracing the boundary lines against Axis-7's meditation space. "If we shift the workshop district's western edge eleven meters, the geological load distribution still holds. It's not the placement I'd have chosen for other reasons, but it's not a meaningful sacrifice either."

"Then let's shift it," he said.

Fang Ruxue made the note without ceremony, the process working exactly as designed — an objection filed, evaluated, and resolved through evidence and adjustment rather than through anyone's authority overriding anyone else's claim. "I'll update the plan and notify Axis-7 formally. This is precisely the kind of resolution I hoped the framework would produce."

---

He brought the revised plan to Axis-7 himself, wanting her to hear the outcome directly rather than only through the administrative notice.

*This resolves the objection adequately,* she signaled, reviewing the adjusted boundary with the same careful attention she gave everything.

"I wanted to ask you something, separate from the objection itself." He considered how to phrase it. "How many spaces like this exist? That the design process might overlook if no one specifically flags them?"

*Several,* she signaled, after a pause long enough that he suspected she was deciding how fully to answer. *Meen-4 maintains a meditation practice in the eastern archive that predates my own use of this space by considerably longer. Two other Formican residents maintain similar spaces, less ancient but no less meaningful to them. I do not know whether all of them will file formal objections the way I did. Some may simply accommodate the disruption silently, believing complaint to be presumptuous.*

He felt the weight of that land fully — a design process that, however carefully intentioned, could easily have overwritten decades or centuries of quiet, unclaimed use simply because no one had thought to ask before building.

"I'd rather ask directly than wait for objections," he said. "Would you be willing to help identify the other spaces? Not to override anyone's privacy about them. Just so the design process can account for them properly, the way it accounted for yours."

Axis-7 was quiet for a long moment, her attention settling on him with an intensity that felt, for the first time in their long acquaintance, closer to genuine consideration than habitual observation.

*I will ask,* she signaled finally. *I cannot promise everyone will answer. Some of what I am describing has been private for longer than your species has existed. But I will ask, and I will tell you honestly what I learn.*

"Thank you."

*Do not thank me yet,* she signaled, something in the cadence carrying what he was beginning to recognize, across their growing acquaintance, as her particular version of dry amusement. *I filed a formal objection against your design an hour ago. I am not yet certain I have forgiven you for requiring it.*

He found himself laughing, the sound surprising him as much as it seemed to surprise her, and understood, walking away from the quiet meditation alcove afterward, that the city they were building was going to require considerably more listening than any single planning session, however thorough, could ever fully account for on its own.
