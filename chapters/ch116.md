# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixteen — The Surveillance

He mapped her routes over three days.

Not actively — he didn't follow her. The Chitin Circuit network did the passive work, logging every time her Qi signature crossed a circuit's detection radius, and he read the logs each morning and built the map from the data points.

She was methodical. She ran a circuit of the district every day, different timing each day, covering the same ground in different sequences so that the pattern was not obvious unless you had multiple days of data. She was not looking for him at street level — she had his address, she could find him at the apartment if she chose to. She was mapping the area around the entry point, building a spatial model that included the circuit network she couldn't see but was presumably suspecting existed based on how often she seemed to change her approach angle.

She was good at this.

He noted her capability with the combination of respect and concern it deserved: she was a senior Court operative with decades of field practice and she was operating at her own expense, which meant she was personally invested in the outcome. Personally invested people were more persistent than institutionally motivated ones. They were also harder to predict.

On the third day, he changed his own routes.

Not to avoid her — not exactly. He began avoiding the main street-level approaches to the Strata entry point, using the alleys and internal building routes he'd been learning for the past two months. The circuit network's data showed her patterns; he moved differently enough that they rarely overlapped.

He wrote:

> *Surveillance: Ch.116. Lady Shen's route patterns documented. She is mapping the entry point area, not looking for me directly. She knows where I live. She's choosing not to go there.*
>
> *This tells me: she is not here for confrontation. She is here for access. The entry point is the goal. I am, at most, the current holder of the key.*
>
> *She is calculating. She's been calculating since she filed the assessment that revised the Northern Peak Court's mandate. She's here on her own time because she's decided that institutional methods can't get her what she wants and personal methods can.*
>
> *What she wants: entry to the Strata. The archive. The same thing Chen Yufei wanted — the same thing everyone in the cultivation world would want if they knew it was there.*
>
> *The difference: Chen Yufei came to me with questions. Lady Shen is trying to go around me.*

He paused. Added:

> *She hasn't made contact. She could. She knows where I am.*
>
> *She's not ready yet. She's still developing the Compression capability. Another few days, possibly a week.*
>
> *I have that much time.*

He spent it well.

The translation document was finished. He and Chen Yufei spent two evenings reviewing it, checking his notation translations against her knowledge of the Courts' foundational texts to ensure the comparisons were accurate rather than merely defensible.

She read one specific passage three times before she looked up.

"This is the section on the Northern Peak Court's foundational technique," she said.

"Yes."

"You're showing that the Northern Peak's 'high-altitude atmospheric Qi absorption' technique is actually the Standard Protocol's Phase Four breath regulation, modified by successive transmission errors into something fifty percent less efficient."

"Yes."

She set the pages down. "Lady Shen Baoling is going to read this document."

"Probably."

"And she is going to understand that she has been practicing at half-efficiency for her entire career."

"Approximately." He looked at Chen Yufei steadily. "The same thing you understood when I showed you the fire meridian correction."

"It took me three days to process that and I trust you. She doesn't know you."

"No," he said. "But the document is accurate. Whether she trusts me is separate from whether the document is accurate."

She was quiet for a moment.

"You're betting that accuracy is persuasive even without trust," she said.

"I'm betting that a practitioner of Lady Shen's sophistication, when shown accurate information about why her technique works the way it does, will recognize its accuracy regardless of its source." He paused. "The same way you recognized the fire meridian correction even though I'd been in the cultivation world for eight months to your nine years."

Chen Yufei picked up the document again.

"You're right," she said. "I hate that you're right."

"You said that about the meridian architecture too."

"I'm keeping count," she said. She turned the page. "Where does she feature in the distribution list?"

"She doesn't," he said. "Not yet. The forty-seven are people you know to be honest and troubled by contradictions in their tradition. Lady Shen is neither of those things at the moment." He paused. "She becomes part of the conversation when she enters the Strata. Not before."

"What happens when she enters the Strata?"

He thought about Axis-7. About the older Keeper's communication. About the archive's access tiers and what each tier tested.

"She encounters the situation as it is," he said. "Not as she's been imagining it."

Chen Yufei looked at him. "You're going to let her walk in."

"The Strata is not mine to close."

"But you have a plan for when she does."

"I have a response," he said. "Plans imply more control over outcomes than I have."

She put the document down.

"I want to be there," she said.

"Yes," he said. "I was going to ask you to be."

Outside, the Chengdu February night had the specific cold of a city that was still deciding whether winter was over. Lady Shen was out there somewhere in it, running her careful circuit of the district she was about to enter.

He had a few days.

He used them carefully.
