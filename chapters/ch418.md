# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 418 — The Stellar Scale Preview

It happened during a routine relay diagnostic, the kind of ordinary maintenance task he'd performed dozens of times since the off-Earth extension project began, and he hadn't intended, that particular afternoon, to attempt anything beyond the network's already-established range.

He was checking the inner solar system relay's stability, letting his Planetary Architect-scale perception rest lightly against its edge the way he always did, when something in the quality of his own attention shifted — not deliberate, not a choice he consciously made, more like the sensation of leaning against a door that turned out, unexpectedly, not to be fully closed.

His perception extended past the relay's established boundary, past Earth's atmosphere entirely, out into open space in a way he had never once attempted before, and for a disorienting moment he simply let it happen, too startled by the sensation itself to immediately pull back.

What he perceived, in that extended reach, did not resemble anything the Colony Link had ever shown him. Earth's Veritonal field, vast as it had once felt to him, years ago, when he first began sensing it at planetary scale, suddenly registered as something almost intimately small — a single, coherent, densely structured note within a much larger composition. Beyond it, the solar system's Veritonal field unfolded in a structure he had no immediate vocabulary for: layered, immense, organized around a central source of complexity so overwhelming that his attention, reaching toward it instinctively, recoiled before he could fully process what he'd touched.

The Sun.

Not as light, not as heat, not in any of the ordinary registers his human senses understood it through. As a Veritonal presence, a source of complexity that made every structure he had ever built or studied on Earth — the Under-Web, the Pacific Ring, even the Strata's entire accumulated architecture — feel, in comparison, like a single carefully placed stone against the foundation of something too vast to fully comprehend from where he currently stood.

He pulled back, deliberately, the way Meen-4 had taught him to pull back from anything that threatened to overwhelm his ability to hold his own edges, and found himself, back in his own body, in the Archive's central chamber, breathing hard, his hands braced against the floor as though he'd genuinely been physically reaching for something too heavy to lift.

"What happened," Meen-4 asked, arriving within moments, her attention sharpened by whatever change she'd sensed through the Colony Link.

"I touched something," he said, still catching his breath. "Past the relay. Past Earth's atmosphere. I wasn't trying to. It just — opened, somehow, and I reached without meaning to."

He described it as best he could, the layered vastness, the overwhelming complexity of the Sun's Veritonal presence, and watched Meen-4's expression shift into something careful and considering as she absorbed the description.

"That is a preview," she said finally. "Not a threshold crossed. I want to be precise about that distinction, because it matters considerably. You did not achieve Stellar Scale perception just now. You touched, briefly and involuntarily, the edge of what Stellar Scale perception would eventually require you to hold steadily, and your own instinct correctly recoiled from attempting to hold it before you were ready."

"It felt enormous," he admitted. "More than enormous. I don't have a better word for it."

"It is enormous," she said simply. "The Sun's Veritonal field alone contains more structural complexity than Earth's entire planetary field, by a margin I would struggle to express in terms you would find meaningful. What you touched, briefly, is what the Stellar Scale curriculum is actually asking you to eventually hold — not glimpse involuntarily and retreat from, but perceive fully, stably, on purpose."

He sat with the memory of it, still faintly dizzy from the brief contact, and understood, with a clarity that felt almost humbling after months of accumulated confidence, exactly how far the next threshold actually sat beyond where he currently stood.

"I'm not ready for that," he said.

"No," Meen-4 agreed, without hesitation, though there was nothing unkind in the confirmation. "You are not. And that is, I would suggest, exactly the correct thing to recognize right now, rather than the disappointing one. Planetary Architect mastery took you years. What you touched today is not merely a larger version of that same scale. It is something categorically beyond it. You will reach it, I believe, eventually. But not today, and I would strongly advise against attempting to force the timeline simply because you now know, firsthand, what waits on the other side of it."

He nodded, letting the vastness of what he'd briefly touched settle into something he could hold at a respectful distance rather than something he needed to immediately chase.

"Then I'll keep building the foundation," he said. "The relay network. The installation. One careful stage at a time, the way everything else has always worked."

"Yes," Meen-4 said. "That is, I believe, precisely the right instinct to trust."
