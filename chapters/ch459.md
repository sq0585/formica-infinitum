# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 459 — The Protocol

She moved into the Strata City's residential wing for the duration, a small pocket-dimension room not far from the one Xiao Dong had settled into years earlier, and treated the whole arrangement, from the first afternoon, with considerably less apprehension than Lin Mushi had spent the preceding weeks quietly bracing himself for.

"It's very well lit," she observed, on her first walk-through, examining the room's dimensional architecture with the same practical eye she'd have brought to evaluating a rental apartment. "Whoever designs these clearly understands that a person needs a proper window, even if the window technically isn't looking at anything real."

"The light's calibrated to match natural daylight cycles," Meen-4 explained, walking beside her. "We've found people adjust more easily when their bodies aren't asked to abandon every familiar rhythm at once."

"Sensible," his mother said, and that, more or less, set the tone for the three months that followed — his mother approaching an unprecedented Formican medical procedure with roughly the same brisk, unbothered practicality she'd have brought to a longer-than-usual hospital stay.

The protocol itself proceeded exactly the way Meen-4 had described it, weeks earlier, at the kitchen table — careful, monitored, incremental, no single day producing anything dramatic enough to notice, the cumulative effect only becoming apparent across the full span of weeks rather than any individual session. Dr. Shen Yue's instruments tracked her progress alongside Meen-4's own more direct supervision, the two of them comparing notes with the particular collegial ease of two careful professionals who had, over years, learned to trust each other's judgment completely.

"Coherence trending exactly where we'd expect," Dr. Shen Yue reported, roughly a month in, reviewing a week's worth of data with visible satisfaction. "No irregularities. If anything, she's responding slightly better than the historical average — I suspect a lifetime of stubborn good health gave the protocol more to work with than it usually gets."

What surprised Lin Mushi most, though, wasn't the protocol's smooth progress. It was what his mother did with the considerable free time three months of careful, supervised recalibration left her.

She found Meen-4 within the first week, and simply never entirely stopped finding her afterward.

"You mentioned agricultural technique," he overheard her saying, early on, in the Strata's outer garden terrace, where Meen-4 had apparently made the mistake — or, he suspected, the deliberate choice — of mentioning, once, that Formican cultivation included sophisticated approaches to soil management. "I have an eastern garden bed at home that has defeated me for three years running. I would like to understand what I've been doing wrong."

What followed, across the ensuing weeks, was a curiosity Lin Mushi hadn't seen from his mother in decades — not since, he suspected, long before he was old enough to remember her having any time for it at all, the particular delighted attention of someone finally given somewhere unhurried to point a lifetime of practical intelligence that raising a son alone and running a small shop had never quite had room for.

She asked Meen-4 about root structures and nutrient cycling, about the specific Formican understanding of soil as a living system rather than simply dirt, about techniques for encouraging beneficial fungal networks that made her entire eastern bed problem, Meen-4 gently informed her, considerably more solvable than his mother's three years of frustration had suggested.

"She is," Meen-4 told Lin Mushi privately, partway through the second month, something warm and genuinely delighted underneath her usual precision, "one of the most naturally gifted students of applied cultivation ecology I have encountered in centuries, and she has spent her entire life believing her only talent was running a noodle shop and raising a difficult, extraordinary son."

"She's not difficult," he said, though he was smiling.

"She raised you. I have watched you disappear into an unexplored solar system for two months without adequate warning. I stand by my characterization."

He laughed, and found, watching his mother across the garden terrace that afternoon, kneeling carefully beside a bed of experimental soil Meen-4 had helped her design, entirely absorbed in a question about fungal symbiosis, that something in his chest had settled more completely than it had in years — not merely relief that the protocol was proceeding safely, but the specific, quiet joy of watching someone he loved discover, late in a long and generous life, that there had always been more of her left to become.

The three months passed steadily, without complication, exactly the way Meen-4 had promised they would.
