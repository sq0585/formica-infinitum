# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Nineteen — The Circuit Array

He found it by following Axis-7.

Not deliberately. He had been in the archive for his usual session — translation, circuit work, communication register practice with the Keeper station — when the specific quality of Axis-7's presence shifted. Not departure, which he'd come to recognize: the Qi field signature that meant she was moving away from her position. Something more directed than movement. She was going somewhere.

He had, in three weeks of sharing the archive with her, developed enough sense of her patterns to recognize an unusual pattern. He packed his materials. He followed.

She moved north, which was deeper into the Strata's Layer 2 territory than he had previously explored. The vaulted hall extended further than his mapped area — he had been working in the nearest section, the section closest to the entry point. Axis-7 moved with the confidence of someone who had been doing this for much longer than he had, taking corridors he hadn't known existed, passing through archways his perception hadn't identified as significant.

He followed at distance. He kept his Compression at Ratio 2 — the finer perceptual resolution allowed him to track her signature through the ambient field without needing line of sight.

She stopped at a section of wall he had mapped as featureless — nothing distinctive in his surveys, no thicker circuit density, no node markers, no apertures.

She pressed her forelimb to the wall.

The wall lit.

Not faintly. Not the subtle amber of the circuit inscriptions he'd been learning to read. Bright — an entire section of wall, three meters wide by five tall, suddenly running with active Chitin Circuit notation at full power, geometric patterns he recognized from the Codex's technical documents but had not seen in their active state.

The array was dormant.

It had been dormant for a very long time, he suspected, by the way the first activation sequence ran — slightly rough, like an engine turning over after years of standing still.

Axis-7 stepped back.

Lin Mushi, forty meters behind her in the dark corridor, watched the array stabilize.

Then the array sent a pulse.

He felt it through every channel simultaneously — not painful, not alarming, but unmistakable: a structured signal moving outward from the array in all directions, traveling through the Strata's walls and the Under-Web's filament network and, based on the way his Channel-two EM sensitivity registered it, into the ordinary electromagnetic spectrum as well. The pulse had an outgoing quality, a signal with content rather than just energy.

Something receiving that signal would know the Strata was active.

Axis-7 turned and looked at him.

He became aware, in that moment, that she had known he was following her the entire time.

She had wanted him here for this.

He walked forward.

Up close, the array was extraordinary — the notation running across the wall's surface in interlocking layers of unprecedented complexity, describing a function he couldn't fully parse at his current comprehension level but that he read as: *environmental status report. Contents: dimensional stability metrics, Veritonal network health indicators, occupancy status.*

An automated status update. Sent to whoever the Formicans had left to receive it, whenever the station activated.

He had not activated it. Axis-7 had. But he had been present when it happened, and his Chitin Seed's signal would be part of the status report's occupancy data.

Someone, somewhere, had just received a notification that the Chengdu Strata had an occupant with active Chitin Seed integration.

He wrote:

> *The array: a Strata health-monitoring system. Sends automated status to a central Formican network. Axis-7 activated it deliberately, with me present. The ping has gone out.*
>
> *Contents of the ping: dimensional stability, Under-Web health indicators, occupancy status. The occupancy status will include my Chitin Seed signature.*
>
> *Somewhere in the Formican network — possibly much further than Chengdu — something has received this ping and knows the station has an active Seed-carrier.*
>
> *Axis-7 wanted me to see this. She wanted me to know the signal had been sent.*
>
> *Why: because the consequences of the ping are something I'll eventually need to deal with. She's giving me lead time.*
>
> *What happens when a monitoring system receives a ping from a station it thought was dead? Unknown. Timeline: unknown. Could be days, could be much longer.*
>
> *This is the thing that will eventually bring something from much further away than Chengdu.*
>
> *I don't know what that something is yet.*

He looked at Axis-7.

She was watching the array's notation cycle through its activation sequence. Her compound eyes were doing the thing they did when she was reading at speed.

He asked in the register: *Did you need to activate it now?*

She looked at him. The signal she sent back: *Yes. The station's occupancy status was dormant. A dormant station draws no attention. A station with an active Seed-carrier requires acknowledgment from the network. The acknowledgment process begins with the status ping.*

*What acknowledgment?*

*That depends on what the network decides to send.*

He filed this under a new notebook section labeled, with deliberate understatement: *External Consequences.*

The array continued its activation cycle.

He stayed and watched.

It was, he thought, the most beautiful thing he had yet seen in the Strata — the notation running in full operational mode, a system designed by minds that thought in hexagonal geometry and implemented in material that maintained itself over centuries. It was also a system that had just announced his presence to an audience he didn't know.

He had done it by being here.

He had been here by being who he was.

He noted this and moved on.

There was nothing to be done about signals already sent.

There was a great deal to be done about the situation that would follow.
