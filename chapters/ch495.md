# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 495 — Wei Canglan's Final Objection

It arrived the way every one of Wei Canglan's objections had arrived across five years — in writing, formal, precisely worded, delivered through the same administrative channel he used for everything he considered genuinely significant enough to warrant a permanent record rather than a passing conversation.

Lin Mushi found it waiting when he checked the Strata's internal correspondence that morning, filed under a heading that told him, before he'd read a single word of the body, exactly how much weight Wei Canglan had put into composing it.

*Formal Objection — Filed for Record.*

He read it slowly, understanding, from the specific care of the formatting alone, that this was not going to be like any of Wei Canglan's previous objections — not a governance dispute, not a disclosure protocol concern, not any of the dozens of careful, principled challenges that had, across five years, made Wei Canglan one of the most valuable and occasionally most exhausting people in his life.

*I object to the possibility that you will not come back,* it read, *and I note that objecting to it will not prevent it, and I am filing this objection anyway because it is the correct thing to do.*

That was the whole of it. One sentence, plainly stated, no elaboration, no argument marshaled in support, no list of reasons or evidence the way Wei Canglan usually built his objections — simply the fact of the objection itself, filed formally, permanently, because some part of him had apparently decided that the record deserved to show, clearly and for all time, that someone had said this plainly before whatever came next actually arrived.

Lin Mushi sat with it a long while before he found Wei Canglan in person, later that morning, in the same administrative office where five years of careful, essential partnership had unfolded one folder at a time.

"I read your objection," he said.

Wei Canglan didn't look up immediately from the document he was reviewing, though something in his posture told Lin Mushi he'd been expecting exactly this conversation. "I assumed you would."

"I don't have a way to promise you I'll come back," Lin Mushi said honestly. "I want to be clear about that before either of us pretends the objection changes anything about tomorrow."

"I know," Wei Canglan said, finally setting the document down. "I said as much in the objection itself. I am aware that filing it will not prevent whatever happens. I filed it anyway, because I think some things deserve to be said plainly and permanently, regardless of whether saying them changes the outcome. You taught me that, actually, though I doubt you remember the specific conversation."

"Which one?"

"Early on. Arc II, I think, though my memory of the exact chapter has faded. You said something about the Anchor Protocol — that some declarations matter simply because they're true, not because they're strategically useful. I've carried that further than you probably intended, apparently, given that I've now built an entire filing habit around it."

Lin Mushi felt something in his chest tighten at the specific, quiet weight of being reminded of his own words, handed back to him now at exactly the moment he most needed to hear them.

"Thank you," he said, "for filing it anyway."

"You're welcome," Wei Canglan said, and there was, beneath the administrative formality, something considerably more raw than his usual composed precision allowed. "I need you to understand something, and I'm going to say it once, outside the formal record, because I think it deserves saying in both places."

"I'm listening."

"You have built the most careful, most consequential five years of governance I have ever been part of, and I have been part of considerably more than most people ever see. If tomorrow goes wrong, that won't be erased. The Strata exists. The disclosure protocol exists. Three hundred and twenty-three people chose, knowingly, to be part of something enormous, because you built the structure that made that choice safe enough to make. Whatever happens tomorrow, none of that unmakes itself."

Lin Mushi nodded slowly, understanding the specific gift being offered — not false reassurance, not a comfortable denial of real risk, but something considerably more honest and more useful: a plain accounting of what would remain, regardless of what tomorrow cost.

"Noted," he said finally, choosing his own words with the same deliberate care Wei Canglan had chosen his. "Registered. Heard."

Wei Canglan's expression shifted, something almost like a smile finding its way through the careful composure. "That's the correct response to a formal objection," he said. "I appreciate you following procedure, even now."

"I learned from the best," Lin Mushi said, and found, leaving the office a few minutes later, that the exchange had settled something in him he hadn't fully realized still needed settling.
