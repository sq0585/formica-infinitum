# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 429 — What the Mother Knows

She called him on a Tuesday evening, which was unusual enough in itself that he answered immediately, setting aside the relay diagnostics he'd been reviewing without a second thought.

"I've been reading the news," she said, no greeting, no preamble, just the words themselves, carrying a weight he understood immediately, even before she continued.

"I know," he said quietly.

"Is that you?"

He sat with the question for a moment, feeling the particular vertigo of three years of careful protection finally, gently, coming to an end — not through any dramatic reveal, not through the disclosure protocol's formal channels, simply through his own mother, at her kitchen table, reading a physics paper that had upended the world and recognizing, somehow, the shape of her own son inside it.

"Some of it," he admitted.

A long silence followed, the particular quiet he recognized from her, the sound of someone absorbing something enormous without rushing to fill the space with words before she'd fully found the ones she actually wanted to say.

"Are you safe," she asked finally.

"Yes," he said, and meant it, the truth of the word carrying more weight now than it might have months earlier, backed by a review survived, a classification changed, a threat formally, permanently removed.

"Are you eating."

"Yes," he said, and found himself, despite everything the moment carried, smiling faintly at the particular, familiar rhythm of her questions — not what have you been hiding from me, not why didn't you tell me sooner, simply the same practical, grounding concerns she'd always led with, whatever else might be happening underneath them.

"Good," she said, and he could hear, in the single word, something settling in her — not full understanding, not yet, but a kind of provisional peace, the particular trust of a woman who had raised him and had decided, somewhere in the space of that long silence, to extend that trust now to a truth considerably larger than anything she'd previously been asked to hold.

They talked for forty minutes after that, and she never once asked him to explain — not the pocket dimensions, not the Veritonal Energy framework, not the Strata City or the twenty-four hundred people now living inside something he'd built. She asked, instead, about the ordinary things she'd always asked about, folded now around the edges of a truth she'd clearly decided to accept without needing every detail of it first. Was he sleeping enough. Had he seen the new noodle shop three streets over, the one that had opened while he'd apparently been busy with whatever all of this actually was. Did he still remember to visit Xiao Dong, who had, apparently, mentioned to her once that Lin Mushi didn't come by as often as he used to.

"I'll visit more," he said, and meant it.

"See that you do." A pause, and then, gently, the closest she came the entire conversation to actually asking the question underneath all the others. "Whatever this is, whatever you've become — you're still my son. That part doesn't change, no matter how large everything else has gotten."

"I know," he said, and felt the words land with a weight that made his throat tighten unexpectedly. "That's the part I never wanted to lose, no matter what else changed."

"Good," she said again, simply, and he understood, hearing it, that this — the small, familiar word, offered twice now across the conversation — was, in its own quiet way, the fullest acceptance she had to give, and that it was, entirely, enough.

They talked a while longer, about nothing consequential, the ordinary rhythm of a mother and son continuing exactly as it always had, and when the call finally ended, he sat for a long moment in the quiet of his apartment, feeling something settle into place that even the review's successful conclusion hadn't quite managed to settle.

Not safety, exactly. Something steadier than that. The particular, hard-won relief of understanding that whatever he became, however far the Stellar Scale curriculum or the installation or everything still waiting beyond them eventually carried him, there remained one person in the world who would keep asking, simply, whether he was eating, and would keep meaning, underneath the question, exactly what she'd always meant by it.
