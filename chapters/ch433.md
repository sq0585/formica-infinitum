# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 433 — Solar System Space

The first thing he learned, twelve hours into the first segment, was that his body still needed him more than he'd expected.

He'd anchored the physical form in the pocket-dimension workroom, seated, eyes closed, Chen Yufei nearby with a book she wasn't really reading, and let the Weaving carry the working edge of his attention outward past the Moon's quiet shelf toward the first confirmed relay node. The sensation was unlike anything Planetary Architect scale had prepared him for — not the familiar dissolve into a thousand simultaneous human perspectives, but something closer to standing at the edge of a held breath, the body's ordinary signals — thirst, the ache of sitting too long, the small demanding rhythms of being alive — arriving from a distance now, muffled, patient, waiting for him to come back and attend to them properly.

He recorded that first, because the discipline of five years insisted he record the physical cost before the wonder, every time, without exception.

*Observation 341: extended Weaving at this range produces a genuine perceptual split — awareness at the working edge, body maintained at baseline but distant. Distinct from Planetary Architect's simultaneity. This feels less like being in many places and more like standing very far from myself.*

Then he let himself actually look.

The Sun, from a hundred thousand kilometers further out than he'd ever perceived it before, was not merely a Veritonal source. It was the loudest thing he had ever encountered, in any sense the word could plausibly apply — a field so dense and so continuously active that his instruments, calibrated for terrestrial and even planetary work, simply saturated and held steady at their maximum reading, unable to express the actual scale of what they were measuring. He adjusted, recalibrated, adjusted again, and found that no adjustment made the Sun smaller. It only made his own instruments more honest about their limits.

*Rule: the Sun is not a point source. It is closer to a foundation — every other Veritonal structure in the system reads, at this range, as something built on top of it, tuned to it, responding to its rhythm the way a plucked string responds to the frequency of the room it's played in.*

He moved outward, node by node, the way Amber-0 had staged it, and the solar system began to resolve into something he hadn't expected: not empty space punctuated by planets, but a resonant field with planets sitting inside it like instruments in an orchestra none of them had chosen to join, each one reading back a slightly different note depending on mass, composition, distance, the particular geological history compressed into its own rock and metal. Earth's note, when he found it from this distance, was achingly familiar — small, warm, complicated in exactly the way twenty billion years of accumulated life made a thing complicated. He lingered there a moment longer than the schedule strictly allowed.

The asteroid belt, when he reached it after the fourth segment, surprised him more than anything else had. He had expected debris. He found, instead, something closer to a diffuser — millions of scattered bodies, individually unremarkable, collectively performing a function he hadn't anticipated: breaking the Sun's overwhelming field into smaller, more legible fragments before it reached the inner system, the way a lampshade didn't dim a bulb so much as make its light survivable to look at directly.

*Observation 347: the asteroid belt appears to function as passive Qi diffusion architecture, whether by natural formation or ancient design I cannot yet determine. Query for Meen-4 — does Formican cosmology have a framework for this?*

By the sixth segment, past the belt, moving into the outer system's slower and stranger territory, he noticed the field beginning to change in a way that had nothing to do with distance from the Sun.

It grew quieter first — the resonant chatter of the inner planets fading into something closer to silence, the way a room empties gradually as guests leave one by one. And underneath the quiet, faint, patient, unmistakably present: a texture he recognized immediately, with a lurch of recognition that had nothing to do with instruments and everything to do with three years of studying its edges from a much greater distance.

Veth-Karun's echo. Not the event itself — that had happened forty million years ago and thousands of light-years away, closer to the galactic core than anything in this system. But its residual signature, the long slow ripple of a Formless Scale dissolution still propagating outward through the Void Lattice's fabric even now, arriving here as a faint, cold undertone beneath the outer system's ordinary quiet.

He had studied this signature for years in Chengdu, filtered through the accumulated distance of an entire solar system and however many more light-years beyond it. He had never, until this exact moment, felt it directly, unmediated, present in the actual space he was moving through.

*It's colder than I expected,* he wrote, and then crossed out *colder,* because it wasn't a temperature, and reached instead for something closer to accurate. *It's older than I expected. Not threatening. Simply very, very tired, the way a wound stops hurting eventually and just becomes a place that doesn't quite heal the same as the rest of the body around it.*

He held the seventh segment's anchor point longer than the others, letting the sensation settle rather than rushing past it, because some things deserved to be actually felt rather than merely logged.

Then he checked his position against Amber-0's staged route, confirmed the eighth relay node was holding steady eleven hours ahead, and continued outward, one careful segment at a time, toward a machine that had been waiting, patiently, for forty million years, for someone to finally arrive who was ready to read what it had been keeping.
