# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 500 — The Eve

They gathered without ceremony, exactly the way he'd hoped they would, the pocket dimension laboratory settling into the same warm, ordinary light it had held through every threshold of the past five years.

Chen Yufei brought tea, the same small, careful ritual she'd offered him a thousand times before — pouring it slowly, setting a cup in front of each of them, the specific domestic warmth of the gesture doing more to steady him than anything Dr. Shen Yue's instruments could have measured. Wei Canglan brought food, insisting, with the particular dry practicality that had become one of the Strata's most reliable constants, that no one attempted anything this significant on an empty stomach, himself included.

Dr. Shen Yue arranged her monitoring equipment along the far wall, the readings already active, steady, patient, the culmination of a framework she'd spent months building specifically for this exact morning.

Liang Jingzhi was present too, though not physically — extended through the Colony Link from her position deep within the dissolution zone, permanently now, the way she would be every morning for the rest of her life, her signature arriving in the room warm and vast and specific all at once, the particular paradox she'd described to him weeks ago finally, fully understood by everyone gathered to feel it alongside her.

*Still here,* her presence seemed to say, without needing words at all. *Tea's cold. Ready when you are.*

Meen-4 stood in the corner, exactly where she always stood, her stillness carrying the same quiet, formal attention she'd brought to every genuinely significant threshold since the very beginning — a Keeper, and something considerably more than a Keeper, watching the boy she'd first met years ago prepare to become something neither of them could yet fully name.

Lin Mushi looked at them for a long time.

He looked at Chen Yufei, pouring a second round of tea neither of them had finished the first cup of, her hands steady despite everything sitting underneath the morning's careful calm. He looked at Wei Canglan, arranging the food with the same precise, thorough care he brought to every folder, every objection, every careful piece of governance that had held an entire extraordinary city together. He looked at Dr. Shen Yue, checking her instruments one final time, the particular focused stillness of someone who had built, patiently, exactly the tool this moment required. He felt Liang Jingzhi's presence, warm and enormous and specific, holding her portion of an impossible structure steady from a distance no ordinary measurement could properly describe. He looked, last, at Meen-4, ancient and patient and steadfastly present, exactly as she had been through every single day of the past five years.

Five people. Five Anchors. The whole accumulated weight of everything he had built, deliberately, one careful relationship at a time, specifically so that this exact morning would never have to be faced the way Veth-Karun had faced theirs.

He thought of Ren Wuji's opening message, read again just the night before. *I hope you are curious. Everything depends on it.*

He thought of Veth-Karun's closing words, the same account that had cost him more than almost anything else this extraordinary life had asked of him. *I should have held someone.*

He was holding five. He understood, looking around the room one final time, that he had never, not once across the entire span of this improbable life, been anything close to alone.

Chen Yufei met his eyes and nodded, once, the particular steady confirmation that had carried him through every threshold since the very first one. Wei Canglan set down the folder he'd been holding, unopened, exactly as promised. Dr. Shen Yue's instruments hummed their patient, ready confirmation. Liang Jingzhi's presence settled warmer, closer, unmistakably there. Meen-4, in the corner, simply waited, the way she always had.

He set down his own cup of tea, still mostly full, and found that his hands, for the first time in longer than he could easily remember, were entirely steady.

He thought, briefly, of a boy standing behind a convenience store counter on an ordinary night, considerably more curious than prepared, with no idea that a dying stranger's hand was about to close around his and change the entire shape of his life. That boy could not have imagined this room, these five people, the particular warm gravity of a morning built from five years of patient, deliberate care. And yet, somehow, every choice that boy had made — curious rather than cautious, connected rather than alone — had led, one careful step at a time, exactly here.

"Let's begin," he said.
