# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-Eight — What He Really Wants

He wrote it that night, after the meeting, sitting at his desk with the tea gone cold beside him.

He had been circling the question for an hour — turning the Northern Peak Court's offer over from different angles, trying to find the version of his response that was actually true rather than the version that was simply defensible. He had a habit, by now, of distrusting his first articulate answer to anything significant, because the first articulate answer was usually the one built to sound good rather than the one built to be accurate.

He wrote, and crossed out, and wrote again.

What he arrived at, finally, after the false starts:

> *What I want is not protection.*
>
> *I have been thinking about the Northern Peak offer as a transaction — recognition in exchange for partnership, status in exchange for cooperation. That framing makes the decision a cost-benefit calculation, and cost-benefit calculations are the wrong tool for this question.*
>
> *What I actually want: I want things to be right. Not safe. Not advantageous. Right.*
>
> *The Courts have spent eight hundred years building authority on partial understanding. That authority has real consequences — it determines who gets training, who gets resources, who gets to call themselves a legitimate practitioner and who doesn't. People like Chen Yufei spent nine years inside a structure that was, in a specific and correctable way, wrong about itself, and the wrongness cost her efficiency and almost cost her belonging.*
>
> *If I accept recognition from the Northern Peak Court, I am accepting that their authority to grant or withhold legitimacy is valid — even as I correct the basis on which that authority was built. That's not quite a contradiction, but it's close enough to one that it troubles me.*
>
> *If I decline, I am asserting that legitimacy in this domain shouldn't flow from institutions that have been operating on incomplete understanding, however well-intentioned and however genuinely they've now engaged with correction. That's a harder position. It risks isolating me from exactly the kind of partnership that could help the corrected information spread more smoothly.*
>
> *I don't think there's a clean answer here. I think there's only an honest one.*
>
> *The honest one: what I want is not protection, and not even partnership for its own sake. What I want is for things to be right — for the Courts to operate on accurate foundations, for practitioners to get what they've actually earned, for the archive's relationship with the surface to be built on truth rather than convenience. Whether that requires accepting the Northern Peak's recognition or declining it is a question I need to answer based on which choice serves the larger correction, not which choice serves me.*

He read this back.

He sat with it for a long time.

He thought about Elder Sun's unexpected honesty at the end of the meeting — *the offer is not contingent on your answer being yes* — and what that implied about the kind of relationship that might actually be possible, separate from the formal structure of the offer itself.

He thought about Axis-7's observation, weeks ago, that the previous Carriers had operated in isolation and that his approach was different, and that whether different meant better or worse depended on what he did next.

He did not reach a final decision that night. He had two weeks, and he intended to use them properly — talking to Chen Yufei, to Axis-7, to Elder Zhao, possibly even to Lady Shen, whose own trajectory from institutional mandate to personal conviction was itself a kind of data point.

But he had found, in the writing, the thing that mattered more than the decision itself: a clear statement of what he actually wanted, separate from any specific path to it.

*What I want is not protection. What I want is for things to be right.*

He underlined it.

He closed the notebook.

He went to bed, and slept better than he had in several weeks, because the clarity itself was a kind of rest.
