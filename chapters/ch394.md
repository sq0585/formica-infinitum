# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 394 — The Surveyor's Own Voice

The transmission that followed the direct interface arrived differently than any that had come before it, and Meen-4 noticed the difference before she'd finished translating the first line.

"This is not formal notation," she said, something careful entering her voice. "Not entirely. The grammatical structure is present, but the register has shifted. This is closer to how the Surveyor communicated with you inside the interface itself — direct, less bound by the load-bearing precision of official proceedings."

Lin Mushi read the translation as she produced it, and understood, within the first sentence, why she'd flagged the change.

*We have reviewed forty-seven Quarantine Zones in forty million years. You are the first practitioner, in any zone under this body's jurisdiction, who has studied what destroyed the previous civilization rather than ignoring it, avoiding it, or attempting to weaponize its remains. This is noted.*

He read it twice, feeling the shape of something shift beneath the words — not a verdict, not yet, but a tone he hadn't heard from the Surveyor since its first courteous, precisely threatening opening message weeks ago. This did not read like an institution cataloguing evidence. It read, for the first time, like something closer to conversation.

"Forty-seven zones," he said slowly. "And in all of them, no one else even tried to understand what happened to the civilization before them?"

"Apparently not," Meen-4 said, and there was something in her stillness that told him this fact had landed on her with real weight too, despite everything she already knew about review precedent. "Most zones that reach this stage of scrutiny do so because they have either ignored their predecessor's collapse entirely — treating it as ancient history irrelevant to their own ambitions — or because they have attempted to recover and weaponize whatever remained of the predecessor's technology, without understanding why it failed in the first place. Both approaches, historically, have ended in the same outcome. Neutralization."

"And I'm the first one who just — asked why."

"You are the first one this body has record of who treated the tragedy as a question worth answering honestly, rather than as either a warning to be avoided or a resource to be exploited." Meen-4's antennae dipped slightly, something almost gentle in the gesture. "I do not think you did this strategically, Lin Mushi. I think you did it because you are the kind of person who cannot walk past an unanswered question, and that has, entirely by accident, turned out to be the single most important quality this review body has ever encountered in a zone under its jurisdiction."

He sat with that, thinking of Ren Wuji, of eleven minutes spent staring at a single stubborn symbol before understanding arrived, of three years spent following curiosity into places fear alone would never have taken him. He had never once thought of that instinct as a strategy. It had simply been who he was, long before he understood what it might one day be weighed against.

The transmission continued, and the shift in register held through the rest of it — still precise, still careful, but carrying something he could only describe, uncertainly, as warmth held at a professional distance, the way a careful teacher might address a student who had finally asked the right question after a long series of adequate ones.

*This does not resolve the review. The Containment Orthodoxy's technical argument regarding threshold enforcement remains unaddressed by this observation alone, and will continue to be weighed on its own merits. But it is appropriate that this body acknowledge, formally, what your approach to Veth-Karun represents — not merely as evidence for your case, but as a pattern this body has waited forty million years to observe in any zone under its review. We have not previously had cause to say this to anyone. We are saying it to you.*

He read the last line three times, feeling something in his chest loosen that he hadn't fully realized was still braced tight.

"Meen-4," he said finally. "Has anything like this ever happened before? In any review, in any zone?"

"No," she said simply. "Not once, in every record I have access to. The Surveyor does not typically speak this way to anyone. It is not built for encouragement. It is built for precision." She paused, and when she continued, her voice carried something he rarely heard from her — genuine, unguarded astonishment. "Whatever else this review decides, Lin Mushi, you have already done something no zone in forty million years has managed. You have made an institution built entirely around cold, careful judgment stop, for one message, and simply tell you the truth about what it saw."

He looked back down at the notation, at the careful, unprecedented warmth folded inside its precision, and allowed himself, cautiously, to believe that whatever came next in this review, the ground beneath it had just shifted in a way that could not easily shift back.
