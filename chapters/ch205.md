# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Five — The Court Coalition Splinters

Chen Yufei brought the ultimatum to him printed on actual paper, which she only did when she wanted him to understand that something had crossed from rumor into formal record.

"Northern Peak sent this to all five Courts yesterday," she said, setting it on the counter between customers. "It's not subtle."

He read it while she watched. The language was careful, formal, the kind of phrasing built by people who'd spent careers learning how to make an ultimatum sound like a proposal. Stripped of the courtesy, it said one thing: unite behind Northern Peak's investigation and jurisdiction over the Codex matter, or Northern Peak would proceed to claim sole authority and treat continued independence as obstruction.

"Lady Shen isn't sending this," he said. "Not her phrasing."

"Elder Kuo, probably, with her name on it because the coalition still needs to believe she's leading it." Chen Yufei folded her arms. "Either way, it's a test. Whoever doesn't sign loses Northern Peak's protection and gains Northern Peak's suspicion."

"What's the response been?"

"That's why I came in person instead of texting." She glanced toward the door, lowering her voice though the store was empty. "Celestial Compass refused. Formally, in writing, this morning. Fang Ruxue's name is on it."

He thought of the six hours in the outer library, the careful notebook, the four thousand stars she'd asked to see. He hadn't expected the consequence to arrive this quickly, or this publicly.

"And River Jade refused as well," Chen Yufei continued. "Quieter about it — no public statement, just a private message to Northern Peak declining to participate. But my contact there says it's real. They're stepping back from the coalition entirely."

"That's two of five."

"That's two of five who've decided they'd rather risk Northern Peak's anger than sign something built on an investigation they don't trust the conclusions of." She picked the printed page back up, looking at it the way she might look at a wound to assess how deep it went. "Which leaves Northern Peak, Iron Meridian, and Stone Hall. The three Courts least likely to question their own foundations even when the foundations are wrong."

"That's not a coincidence."

"No. It's the harder institutions closing ranks, and the ones with more genuine scholarly tradition deciding they'd rather know the truth than protect the hierarchy." She set the page down again. "Five Courts splitting exactly along the line of who actually cares what's true."

He considered this for a moment, turning it the way he turned most facts he didn't immediately trust — looking for the angle that made it less comfortable than it first appeared.

"Three Courts is still a significant coalition," he said. "Smaller, but harder. Less interested in negotiation."

"That's the part that worries me." She met his eyes. "The Courts who would have argued you down to a reasonable settlement just opted out. What's left are the ones who think force is still on the table."

Outside, the afternoon traffic moved past the storefront in its ordinary rhythm, indifferent to the fact that somewhere in the city, three ancient institutions had just finished deciding that the version of this conflict still available to them was the version with teeth.

He folded the printed ultimatum and put it with the other documents he was keeping — the growing file of things that would matter later, in an order he was only beginning to understand.

"I should talk to Fang Ruxue," he said. "Make sure she knows what she just signed onto."

"She knew before she signed it," Chen Yufei said. "That's the kind of scholar she is."

He didn't doubt it. He still wanted to hear her say so herself.
