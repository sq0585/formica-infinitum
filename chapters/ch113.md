# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Thirteen — Axis-7 Approaches

She had been watching him for three weeks before she said anything.

He was aware of being watched before he could identify the watcher. The awareness arrived through the Colony Link — a specific presence in the archive's ambient field that was distinct from the older Keeper's patient station-keeper quality and distinct from the archive worker who had given him the notebook. Different register. A Qi field that was more active, more internally organized, with the specific quality of something that was paying close attention to something else.

He documented the presence for six days before its position relative to him resolved clearly enough to locate.

East wall, secondary shelving. The presence tended toward the same section every time — the cultivation protocol containers, the ones his dimensional lock technique couldn't yet open, the advanced material he had marked for when his Ratio 3 duration extended enough to meet the access threshold.

She was reading what he couldn't yet access.

He noted this. He noted the Compression signature — higher than his, more precise, without the self-taught irregularity that he was aware his own signature probably had from the outside. She was operating at a ratio he estimated between 2 and 3, switching between them with the ease of someone for whom these were not major transitions.

He continued his own work without changing his behavior. Six more days.

On day seven, he arrived at the archive and found the presence already there, at her usual east-wall section.

On day nine, she was there when he entered and there when he left.

On day fourteen, he looked up from his work and she was sitting at the table across from him.

He had not seen her arrive.

She was approximately the size of a medium-sized dog at her current compression ratio — he estimated Ratio 2.5. The exoskeleton was similar to the archive worker who had given him the notebook, but the striations ran in a different pattern, and the compound eyes had a color he'd been learning to read as a significant variable: slightly more amber than the other Formicans he'd encountered, carrying the amber quality that the Codex's biological records associated with what it called *veteran integration stage.*

She was old. Not elderly — the Codex was clear that Formican cultivation practice significantly extended active lifespan — but experienced in the way that took a very long time to accumulate.

She sat across the table and looked at him.

He looked back.

He opened his notebook. He didn't write anything. He left the page blank and the pen uncapped, the way you leave space for something you expect to receive.

She watched him work for three hours.

He worked, because that was what he was here to do, and being watched while doing it was acceptable. He had learned in the first weeks that the Strata operated on different social protocols than the surface, and one of those protocols appeared to be that sustained observation was neutral information-gathering rather than confrontation.

At the end of three hours, when he was packing up his materials, she spoke.

In the communication register. At full fluency — the signal was complex, nuanced, carrying register layers he could only partially parse. He estimated seventy-five percent comprehension, rising.

He caught:

*Your derivation of Scale Walking Ratio 2 has three errors.*

He stopped packing.

*They have not mattered yet.*

He uncapped his pen.

*They will matter at Ratio 4.*

He looked at her. Her compound eyes were utterly unreadable in any human emotional register and entirely readable in the Qi-field sense — the signal she was broadcasting was flat in the sense of having no hostility or warmth, just the specific quality of precise information being offered without obligation.

Not hostile. Evaluative.

He wrote in the notebook what she'd said, as precisely as he could render it in characters.

Then he looked up.

"Thank you," he said, in Mandarin, because he could say that much verbally and the communication register was slower than spoken language for short exchanges.

She looked at him. The signal that came back was not something he had a full translation for, but the quality of it was the Formican equivalent of: *acknowledgment received.*

She stood.

She left.

He had not gotten her name. He did not know if Formicans used names in the human sense, though the archive's record of individuals was indexed by designation.

He looked at what he'd written.

*Three errors in Scale Walking Ratio 2 that have not mattered yet but will matter at Ratio 4.*

He spent the next two hours finding the errors.

He found two. The third required reading a section of the Scale Walking manual he hadn't yet opened.

He opened it.

He found the third.

He corrected all three in his notes.

He would not have found them without being told they were there.

He wrote at the bottom of the page: *She is right. And she chose to tell me.*

He filed this under *to be understood later* — why she had told him, and who she was, and why now rather than the three weeks of watching that had preceded now.

Those questions had answers.

He was going to learn them.
