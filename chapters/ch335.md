# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty-Five — Chen Yufei Returns

He felt her before he saw her, the Colony Link's familiar texture arriving back into range gradually as her train crossed the last stretch toward Chengdu, a presence he'd learned to recognize as unmistakably hers even at considerable distance — steadier now than it had been three weeks earlier, the specific quality of someone who had actually rested rather than simply left.

He met her at the same platform where he'd said goodbye, and found, watching her step off the train, that the three weeks had changed something in her posture, some accumulated tension he hadn't fully registered as present until he saw it gone.

"You look different," he said, once she'd reached him, her bag set down between them, neither of them rushing the reunion.

"I feel different." She studied him for a moment with the same careful attention she always brought to reading him. "You look like you've been carrying something. Something heavier than the Ring synchronization."

"I have. I'll tell you everything. Not here — somewhere quieter."

---

They walked slowly back toward the Strata, taking the longer route through the city rather than the fastest one, both of them apparently wanting the transition to happen gradually rather than all at once.

"Tell me about Yunnan first," he said. "I want to hear about your three weeks before I hand you mine."

She considered the question seriously rather than deflecting it. "Quiet. Genuinely quiet, in a way I don't think I'd experienced since before any of this started. I stayed with a small guesthouse near a lake, no cultivation networks anywhere nearby, nothing pulling at my attention except the actual, physical world in front of me."

"Did you find what you were looking for?"

"I think so. Not a dramatic breakthrough. Just — clarity. I spent a lot of time thinking about the past two years, about the Anchor bond, about what it's actually asked of me, and I came back understanding something I don't think I understood as clearly before I left."

"What's that?"

"That I can hold this. All of it. Not effortlessly, not without needing rest occasionally, the way I needed it these past three weeks. But I can hold it, and I wanted to come back knowing that clearly, rather than simply hoping it was true."

She paused, something small shifting in her expression. "I also had a small cultivation insight, actually, while I was there. Nothing dramatic. Just a quieter way of holding the Iron Meridian breathing framework that I think I'd been missing for years, buried under all the more complicated technique I've built since meeting you."

"Show me sometime."

"I will. Later. Tonight I mostly want to just be here, with you, before we get back into everything waiting for us."

---

They stopped at Xiao Dong's noodle shop before returning to the Strata, the small, familiar restaurant deliberately chosen for its ordinariness, the two of them settling into a corner table with steaming bowls in front of them, the evening's ordinary bustle of the restaurant around them a welcome contrast to everything that had accumulated in her absence.

"Now tell me," she said, once they'd both eaten enough to slow down. "What's been carrying you these three weeks?"

He told her everything, carefully, the Ring's synchronization first, then the Witness Node's intensifying signal, and finally, more slowly, the discovery about Ren Wuji and Veth-Karun, watching her expression shift through each revelation with the same steady attention she always gave him.

"That's enormous," she said quietly, once he'd finished. "All of it. But especially the last part. I'm sorry I wasn't here for that."

"I needed to sit with it alone first, I think. But I'm glad to finally be telling you now."

"How are you, actually? Underneath the careful summary you just gave me."

He considered the question honestly, rather than reaching for a reassuring answer. "Steadier than I expected to be, given the weight of it. I think Meen-4's honesty helped. I think watching Axis-7 finally join the history project helped too. I don't feel like I'm carrying it alone, even without you here for the worst of it."

"I'm here now," she said, reaching across the small table for his hand. "Whatever comes next with all of it — the Ring, the Witness Node, Ren Wuji's history — you don't have to carry any of it alone again."

He held her hand steadily, the noodle shop's ordinary warmth settling around them, and felt, for the first time in three weeks, the specific, grounded relief of having his fourth anchor point restored — not because he'd been unstable without her, but because some things, he understood clearly now, were simply better carried together than carried well alone.

"Welcome home," he said.

"It's good to be home," she said, and meant it, in a voice steadier and more certain than it had been in longer than either of them had quite noticed until this quiet, ordinary evening finally gave them room to notice it fully.
