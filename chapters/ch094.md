# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Ninety-Four — The Iron Meridian's Inquiry

The Iron Meridian Court contacted Chen Yufei on a Wednesday morning.

Not an official inquiry — a call from Elder Zhao, the senior Chengdu practitioner, who did not make official calls when a personal one would do. Chen Yufei relayed the substance to Lin Mushi that afternoon in the tea house: Elder Zhao had two questions.

First: did the investigation warrant a formal report to the Council?

Second: was the subject cooperative?

Chen Yufei had answered: the investigation had developed significantly, formal reporting was premature pending further information gathering, and the subject was cooperative in ways that exceeded anything the investigation had initially anticipated.

Elder Zhao had said, with the precision of someone who chose words carefully: "Exceeded anticipated."

"She asked what that meant," Chen Yufei told him. "I said it meant the situation was not what the Northern Peak Court's report suggested."

Lin Mushi thought about this. "She'll want to know what it is instead."

"She does." Chen Yufei was rotating her tea cup again, the gesture he'd come to recognize as her processing motion. "I told her I would arrange an introduction."

He looked up.

"I told her," Chen Yufei said, "that the subject was aware of the Court's existence, had expressed willingness to be known, and was managing the situation on their own timeline." A pause. "She asked if you were affiliated. I said no. She asked if you had a teacher. I told her the truth — your teacher died in a convenience store, you've been working from a transmitted archive."

"How did she respond to that."

"She was quiet for about eight seconds. Then she asked how sophisticated the archive was." Another pause. "I told her that was the question I'd been trying to answer for three weeks and that I'd tell her more when I had a better answer."

He thought about Elder Zhao, sixty-three, monthly maintenance cycles on an array she'd been managing for thirty years, a practitioner skilled enough to run a complex Qi system in one of the world's major cities. She had heard: an unaffiliated practitioner, self-taught, inherited archive, working independently in the city she was responsible for. She had not panicked. She had asked two careful questions and arranged an introduction.

This was a person worth meeting.

"When?" he said.

"I suggested next week. She agreed. I told her you'd choose the location."

"I'll choose somewhere with a Chitin Circuit installed."

Chen Yufei looked at him. "So you can monitor the ambient field during the meeting."

"And so she can see the circuit's output if she's interested. Which she will be." He paused. "She's been maintaining an inefficient array for thirty years. If I show her what the Chitin Circuit can tell her about the array's actual performance, she'll be interested."

"You're planning to make her an offer."

"I'm planning to show her something useful and let her decide what to do with it." He looked at Chen Yufei steadily. "That's what I told the Iron Meridian Court I would lied for me."

She stopped rotating the cup.

"Yes," she said. "That is what you told me."

"You lied for me before you had a reason to."

"I told my Court there was no cultivator activity detected. Which was technically—" She stopped. "Which was not entirely accurate."

"Why did you do it?"

She had told him why before — *because I want to know what the notation says before anyone with authority over me does* — but he was asking again because the question now had more context. Seven weeks of context.

She thought about it properly this time. "Because the investigation had found something that didn't fit any category my report form had. And when something doesn't fit the available categories, filing it under the wrong one makes it disappear into the bureaucracy, and I didn't want this to disappear." She looked at the table. "I didn't know what it was. I knew it was too important to file wrong."

"That's a good reason," he said.

"It was an instinct, not a reason. At the time."

"Instincts that turn out to be right have reasons you find later." He picked up his tea. "What's Elder Zhao like?"

"Careful. Experienced. Honest, in the way that people who have held responsibility for a long time are honest — she doesn't have the luxury of self-deception." A pause. "She will want to know if you're a threat."

"I'm not."

"I know that. She doesn't yet." Chen Yufei met his eyes. "What I told my Court, the partial report, the arrangement between us — these protected you for three weeks. They don't protect you from an Elder asking directly."

"I know." He had thought about this. "What would you tell her, if she asked directly, about what I am?"

She thought about this question with the seriousness it deserved.

"I would tell her," she said finally, "that you are probably the most unusual practitioner I've encountered, operating from a source no living human has had access to before, with an understanding of Qi mechanics that makes everything the Courts have built look like an early draft." She paused. "And that you have been, consistently, more careful about what you do with that understanding than most people with half your capability would be."

He was quiet for a moment.

"That's accurate," he said.

"I know." She picked up her tea. "I'm very good at my job."

He had noticed this. He thought she didn't hear it often enough. He didn't say so, because she would find it condescending, but he noted it.

"Next week," he said. "I'll install a circuit in the tea house on Baiyun Street. The Qi density there is good — the Elder will be able to read it clearly."

"I'll tell her."

They paid for the tea and went their separate ways into the December afternoon, which had decided, finally, to produce actual winter instead of its usual damp imitation.

He walked home through the cold thinking about the Iron Meridian Court and Elder Zhao and what it meant to be *known* in the human cultivation world — not hidden, not acquired, just known, on terms that were his own.

He was building something. He wasn't entirely sure what it was yet.

But it was being built on accurate foundations, and accurate foundations were where he always started.
