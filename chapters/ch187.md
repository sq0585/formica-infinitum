# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty-Seven — The Fight

The technique she deployed was a spatial-capture field — a Qi structure he recognized in its broad shape from the Northern Peak's corrected atmospheric framework, though executed with a precision and speed that made clear thirty years of discipline had been applied to it long before any correction reached her.

It expanded outward from her position in a sphere, the boundary visible to his perception as a structured pressure wall, designed to compress and contain anything caught inside its radius before the target could react. He felt the wall's geometry forming a fraction of a second before it closed — the same quality of advance warning the Colony Link gave him in calmer circumstances, now arriving compressed into something closer to instinct than analysis.

He had, at most, a fraction of a second.

He compressed.

Not to his comfortable working ratio — he pushed past it, into Ratio 4, the molecule-scale interface he'd only achieved in careful, controlled training sessions, never under genuine pressure, never with anything like the speed this moment demanded. The transition arrived wrong in every way the manual had warned him it would when forced: no breath sequence, no geometric preparation, just raw need translating directly into compression because the alternative was containment.

It worked.

The capture field closed around the space where his Ratio-1 body had been standing a fraction of a second earlier, and found nothing there to contain — he had compressed out of the field's effective radius before it fully formed, his interface now small enough that the field's binding geometry, calibrated for human-scale targets, had nothing to grip.

He held Ratio 4 for 0.3 seconds — barely long enough to register the transition, the corridor around him resolving briefly into the vast crystalline landscape he remembered from his careful training sessions, now glimpsed in a single disorienting fragment — before the strain of the unplanned, rapid compression forced a re-expansion he didn't fully control.

He came back to Ratio 1 eight meters from where he'd started, off-balance, breathing hard, his legs nearly failing to catch the sudden return to ordinary scale.

Lady Shen turned toward him, her expression carrying genuine surprise for the first time since she'd arrived — and something else underneath it, a flicker he caught even through his own disorientation: the specific strain of someone whose own held capability had just cost her something to maintain through the technique's full deployment.

"That technique," she said, her voice carrying the faintest unevenness she hadn't shown a moment ago. "Ratio 4 evasion, under genuine combat pressure. I did not expect that from your stated capability level."

"I didn't expect it from myself," he admitted, working to steady his breathing. "I've never done that outside controlled practice."

"Then you're more dangerous than the Council's assessment suggested," she said, and reset her stance — though he noticed, even now, the slight hitch in the reset, the half-second longer it took her field to resettle into readiness than it had the first time.

He understood, with the clarity of someone whose options had just narrowed considerably, that he was not going to out-fight a thirty-year veteran of disciplined combat technique, however good his theoretical Compression understanding was. His advantage, if he had one, was not raw capability.

It was two things: the Strata itself, and whatever the forced Ratio 3 was costing her with every minute she held it.

He had spent fifteen months mapping this territory in obsessive, methodical detail. She had been inside it for less than an hour, and she was burning capability she hadn't fully earned just to remain standing in it at this level.

He ran.

Not in panic — with deliberate direction, toward the east corridor's tighter, lower-vaulted passages, territory he had walked hundreds of times, where his Circuit network's density readings told him precisely where the ambient Qi field's character shifted in ways that might matter.

Lady Shen pursued, her own Compression capability easily matching his pace, though he caught, even at a dead run, the faint irregularity in her stride that hadn't been there when she'd first crossed the threshold.

He did not need to outrun her.

He needed to out-navigate her, and buy enough time — both for the situation's actual resolution to arrive from somewhere other than direct confrontation, and for whatever she was holding through sheer will to begin, eventually, to fail her.
