# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Thirty-Four — The Northern Peak Contact

Lady Shen's message arrived less than a day after he'd sent his own, and the timing alone told him something had already gone differently than he'd hoped.

*A contact inside the remaining hard faction reports they've identified the same paper you likely have. They've already approached the author, framed as a private geological research consultancy interested in funding her continued work. She's agreed to a preliminary meeting. I don't have the date yet, but my source suggests it's soon.*

He read it twice, the cold clarity from two days ago returning, sharper now with the added weight of a competing timeline he hadn't known existed.

He hadn't expected to be racing anyone. He'd assumed, naively perhaps, that the paper's discovery was his own quiet problem to manage carefully, on a schedule he controlled. Northern Peak's hard faction — chastened by the censure, but apparently not so chastened that they'd abandoned every independent initiative — had reached the same conclusion he had, considerably faster, and with none of the caution he'd spent two days agonizing over.

A geological research consultancy. He understood immediately what that framing was built to accomplish: present Liang Jingzhi with funding and institutional legitimacy, draw her deeper into a relationship before she had any reason to question who was actually asking, and extract whatever further data or cooperation they needed before she understood enough about what she'd found to negotiate from a position of actual knowledge.

It wasn't violence. It was something quieter and, in its own way, more corrosive — using her ambition and her need for research funding against her own ability to consent meaningfully to what she was being drawn into.

He called Chen Yufei immediately.

"They've already made contact," he said, once she'd picked up. "Framed as a consultancy. She's agreed to a preliminary meeting, soon, no exact date yet."

"How soon is soon?"

"I don't know. I sent her an email less than a day ago. I don't even know if she's read it yet."

Chen Yufei was quiet for a moment, the careful, rapid calculation he recognized from every crisis they'd navigated together. "You need to move faster than you were planning to. If they reach her first — properly, in person, with funding and credentials in hand — you become the second strange message in her inbox instead of the first serious conversation she has about what she's found."

"I know."

"What's your actual plan, if she responds?"

He thought about it, the careful, patient approach he'd built over the past two days collapsing under the new pressure of a competing timeline he hadn't accounted for.

"I check my email obsessively until she answers," he said. "And if she hasn't answered by tomorrow morning, I find another way to reach her. In person, if I have to. I'd rather not — I think it crosses a line I was trying to respect — but I'm not willing to let Northern Peak's hard faction get to her first and shape her entire understanding of this world before she's even heard a version of it that isn't designed to extract something from her."

"That's a reasonable line to be willing to cross."

He checked his email again, the inbox stubbornly unchanged, the cursor blinking in an empty reply field he'd opened and closed three times already that evening.

He thought about the conference footage — her patient, precise explanation to a skeptical professor, the confidence of someone who trusted her own data and her own careful methodology. He hoped, sitting with the uncertainty of a race he hadn't known he was running, that whatever she eventually decided about the strange email in her inbox, she'd bring that same careful skepticism to whoever reached her first.

He checked his email one more time before he finally made himself put the phone down for the night.

Still nothing. The waiting, he understood, was its own kind of cost — one he hadn't budgeted for when he'd convinced himself two days ago that caution and patience were the only things this decision required.
