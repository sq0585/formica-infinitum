# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Four — Notebooks

The notebooks were under the bed.

Not all of them. The current one was on the desk, and the one he kept at the store was in his bag, and the one he'd bought this morning was open beside the phone. But the rest — three years of them, seventeen in total — lived in a plastic storage box that slid under the frame and collected a thin layer of dust on its lid.

Lin Mushi pulled the box out and sat on the floor with his back against the bed.

He was avoiding the phone. He could feel the Codex from across the room the way you feel a warm lamp from across a room — not unpleasantly, but undeniably, a presence with a direction. Turning away from it required something. He turned away from it and opened the oldest notebook in the box.

---

The first entries were from his second year after dropping out of university.

He had told his mother it was a temporary break. He had told himself it was a choice. He had been twenty-two and had spent three months staring at a differential equations exam and feeling, with increasing certainty, that the course was teaching him to operate machinery he did not yet understand. He wanted to understand the machinery first. The department did not offer that option.

He had not gone back.

The notebooks were what he'd done instead.

First pages, first notebook, three years ago:

> *Why does dark matter interact gravitationally but not electromagnetically? Every model assumes it exists because of what it does to visible matter, not because anyone has observed it. We've built a shape in the dark by touching the walls. I wonder what the shape is for.*

He had been reading cosmology papers on his days off. He still did. Manager Tao had once asked what he was reading and Lin Mushi had said "astrophysics" and Tao had nodded with the expression of a man who had decided not to ask follow-up questions.

He flipped forward. A notebook from last year:

> *Ant colonies. The question that keeps returning: how does a colony make a decision that no individual ant initiated? Foraging routes shift to account for new obstacles before any scout has completed a full circuit. The collective responds faster than information should allow. There is a processing layer I don't have a model for.*

That entry had a second line below it, added later in different ink:

> *The collective is not the sum of its parts. The sum of its parts is the infrastructure. The collective is something built on top of the infrastructure.*
>
> *What is built on top of what?*

The ant colony question was underlined. Not once — three times, the pen pressed harder each time, as if at some point he had gotten impatient with himself for not having an answer.

---

He sat on the floor for a while.

Outside the window, two birds were doing something on the power line that seemed to involve intense negotiation. The depth sensation pulsed gently somewhere below the floorboards.

He thought about Ren Wuji's message. *The nearest mind awake enough.*

Not the strongest mind. Not the most trained. Not the youngest or the most promising or the one with the best credentials. The *nearest* mind *awake enough.*

He had been working the night shift. He had been alone with a half-finished paper on dark matter halo formation. He had been doing what he always did between two and four AM: observing time move.

He looked down at the underlined question about ant colonies.

*What is built on top of what?*

He had been asking the right questions his whole life.

He just hadn't known yet what they were questions *about.*

---

He made tea. He reorganized the notebooks by date rather than by which stack they'd been in. He reread a section about tidal locking and why the phenomenon was much stranger than it was usually presented. He found an entry where he had spent four pages trying to work out whether there was a mathematical relationship between the distribution of ley lines in ancient Chinese geography and the placement of early Buddhist temple sites, a question he had ultimately set aside as requiring data he didn't have.

Now he thought about the depth sensation under his desk, and the notion that cities might have been built where they were because something below made that location feel right.

He flipped back to the ley line entry.

He added a note in the margin, today's date:

> *Return to this.*

He made a second cup of tea and sat at the desk and looked at the Codex diagram for a long time without touching it.

Seventeen notebooks, three years of questions, all pointing toward something he hadn't had a name for.

He had a name for it now. He just didn't know what it meant yet.

He turned to a fresh page and began writing a list:

> *Things I already knew, possibly:*
>
> *1. Something exists beneath observable reality. (The Codex calls it 'below.' I have no better term yet.)*
>
> *2. The something has structure. (The diagram. Also: ant colonies. Also: ley lines.)*
>
> *3. Living systems respond to the structure without necessarily perceiving it. (Ant colony decision-making. Human settlement patterns. Possibly: the man who chose me.)*
>
> *4. Perception of the structure can be altered. (This is what the Codex is doing. What it began doing when Ren Wuji's fingers moved.)*
>
> *5. Ren Wuji spent four hundred years in it.*
>
> *Working question: what is 'it'? Not the Codex. Not the app. The thing the Codex is a map of.*

He drew a line under the list.

> *Method: same as always. Observe. Record. Form a hypothesis. Test. Revise. Do not rush.*

He capped the pen.

The depth sensation was still there when he went to sleep that night, patient and wordless, something waiting to be understood.
