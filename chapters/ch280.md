# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty — Shen Yue Enters the Strata

She crossed the threshold the way few people did — not with wonder, not with fear, but with the particular focused stillness of someone whose entire professional life had trained her to encounter the impossible and immediately begin the work of understanding it rather than simply marveling at it.

She stood for perhaps ten seconds, taking in the outer hall's scale — the shelving running past any reasonable architectural limit, the ceiling lost in amber light with no visible source — and then said, in a voice carrying more genuine intellectual vertigo than he'd heard from anyone since Liang Jingzhi's first crossing.

"I need to go back to my office and rewrite everything I think I know about the nature of space."

He waited, giving her the silence she needed.

She didn't take long with it. Within moments, she'd pulled a notebook from her bag and begun, with the same brisk efficiency he'd watched her bring to every dataset across their two prior sessions, taking measurements — pacing the hall's apparent dimensions against what she could verify of the convenience store's actual footprint, noting timestamps, sketching rough notation he suspected only she could fully interpret.

"The volume discrepancy alone should be impossible," she said, half to herself, still working. "Not difficult. Not unlikely. Impossible, by every framework I currently understand space to operate within."

"It's not impossible," he said. "It's simply built according to a different framework than the one your physics currently has access to."

"That's an important distinction." She looked up from her notebook, something sharp and hungry in her expression. "Impossible means the universe broke a rule. Different framework means I've been missing an entire category of rule that was there all along, operating correctly, simply outside my instruments' resolution."

"That's exactly the distinction."

She continued working for nearly an hour, methodical and thorough, asking precise technical questions whenever her measurements produced something she couldn't immediately reconcile, never once losing the careful, professional composure that had defined every interaction he'd had with her so far.

Meen-4 observed from the inner sections, her attention unmistakably engaged despite her usual reticence around new visitors, and Lin Mushi understood, watching the careful, deliberate way she tracked Dr. Shen Yue's work, that something about the physicist's approach had earned a particular kind of respect he didn't see Meen-4 extend easily.

"This is the most significant dataset I will ever have access to," Dr. Shen Yue said eventually, closing her notebook with the particular satisfaction of someone who'd gathered enough initial data to begin real work. "I understand I can't publish the full truth of where it came from. I want to be direct about that constraint before we go any further — I'd rather understand the boundaries now than discover them later, in a way that damages the trust this clearly required to extend to me."

"I appreciate you raising it directly," he said. "The boundary is real, and we'll work through exactly where it sits carefully, together, before you publish anything. I'd rather build that framework collaboratively than hand you a list of restrictions and hope you simply comply with it."

"That's a reasonable approach." She looked around the hall once more, the scale of it, the amber distance falling away toward sections she hadn't yet seen. "I have a great many questions. I suspect most of today's measurements have already generated several papers' worth of follow-up work, even constrained by whatever boundary we eventually agree on."

"I expected they might."

She nodded, something settling into her expression that looked, for the first time since she'd crossed the threshold, less like rigorous professional analysis and more like genuine, unguarded wonder finally breaking through the careful scientific composure she'd maintained throughout.

"Thank you," she said, "for trusting me with this. I understand what it must have cost you to decide I was worth the risk."

"You earned it," he said simply. "Two sessions of careful, honest, rigorous work. That's not a small foundation to build trust on."

She gathered her notebook, the measurements complete for the day, and looked back toward the threshold with the particular reluctance of someone already calculating how soon she could reasonably return.

"I have considerably more work ahead of me," she said. "I suspect this is going to occupy the rest of my career."

"I suspect it might," he agreed, and found, watching her leave, that the careful, patient bridge between his hidden world and the surface science capable of strengthening it had, finally, fully and properly begun.
