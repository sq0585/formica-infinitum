# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Twenty — The Strata City, Opening

The opening happened without fanfare, the way most of the Strata's real milestones had, over two years, quietly stopped needing ceremony to matter.

Lin Mushi stood at the threshold of the newly completed workshop district on the morning the last construction crew finished its work, watching human and Formican residents move through the finished corridors with the unhurried purpose of people settling into a place rather than visiting one, and understood, watching it, that the transition from residential wing to actual city had finally, fully completed itself.

Six hundred and forty residents. Four hundred and twenty Formican, two hundred twenty human, the numbers Fang Ruxue had finalized the week before now visibly, physically true in the corridors around him — the elder Mrs. Tan's daughter stopping to chat with a Formican neighbor over shared gardening advice near the residential wing's entrance, Wei Canglan walking a group of new arrivals through the workshop district's orientation with the same careful patience he brought to everything, the laboratory's eight stations fully occupied for the first time, researchers from both traditions working elbow to elbow on problems neither tradition could have solved alone.

He found Meen-4 near the district's central hub, observing the activity with her usual careful, unreadable stillness.

"What do you think?" he asked.

*I think it is functioning,* she signaled, which he'd learned, across their long acquaintance, was among the highest compliments she offered anything. *Functioning is not a small achievement. Many ambitious structures fail at exactly this stage — beautiful in design, unable to actually sustain the ordinary daily friction of real habitation. This is sustaining it.*

"You sound almost surprised."

*I am not surprised that you built it well. I am observing, honestly, that I did not fully expect to feel what I am currently feeling, watching it.*

"What are you feeling?"

She was quiet for a long moment, her attention moving across the district — Formican elders and human children crossing paths without particular remark, the specific, unremarkable texture of an actual community rather than an experiment.

*Something adjacent to hope,* she signaled finally. *I have observed a great many things across four hundred years in this Strata. I have rarely observed something I wanted, specifically, to continue existing rather than merely to continue functioning correctly.*

He felt the weight of the admission land fully, understanding, hearing it, exactly how significant a statement it actually was from someone who had spent centuries maintaining careful, deliberate distance from most things she observed.

She stayed only twenty minutes longer before withdrawing to her own quarters, the crowd and activity apparently more than her preference for solitude could comfortably sustain, and he let her go without comment, understanding that even this — twenty minutes, voluntarily spent among the noise and motion of a living community — represented something considerable for her.

---

Axis-7's presence, by contrast, he noticed only gradually, over the course of the full four hours the informal opening celebration ran — a distant, watchful figure at the district's edges, never approaching directly, but present, consistently, in every part of the celebration he happened to look toward.

He didn't approach her either, sensing, correctly, that whatever she was doing required the specific dignity of being allowed to do it unremarked. She watched the human children playing near the workshop district's central fountain. She watched Wei Canglan's orientation group settle into their first assigned workspaces. She watched, for a long stretch near the celebration's end, the elder Mrs. Tan sitting with her daughter and several new Formican neighbors, sharing tea in the specific, unhurried way that had become, quietly, one of the city's most reliable daily rituals.

She left sometime near the fourth hour's end, as unremarked as she'd arrived, and he understood, noting her departure, that four hours of quiet, careful witnessing from someone who had spent three thousand years maintaining deliberate distance from exactly this kind of ordinary human warmth represented, in its own way, something every bit as significant as Meen-4's twenty minutes and her admission of something adjacent to hope.

---

He found Chen Yufei near the celebration's close, the two of them standing together at the workshop district's edge, watching six hundred and forty people settle into the ordinary, unglamorous business of building an actual life together.

"We built this," she said, something quietly astonished in her voice, despite having watched every stage of its construction unfold across the preceding months.

"We built this," he agreed.

"It doesn't feel like the beginning of something anymore," she said. "It feels like it's already real. Already here."

He thought about that, about Meen-4's careful, unexpected hope, about Axis-7's four hours of quiet witnessing, about the elder Mrs. Tan's tea and Wei Canglan's steady patience and the eight laboratory stations humming with genuine collaborative research, and understood, standing there with Chen Yufei's hand steady in his, that she was right.

Whatever came next — the Witness Node's patient, unbroken signal, the Collective's unknown intentions, the Pelagic Archive's sealed door still waiting somewhere far beneath the ocean — all of it would have to reckon, now, with the fact that this city, real and populated and quietly, unmistakably alive, already existed.

"Whatever's coming," he said, "it's not coming for an empty plan anymore. It's coming for this."

She squeezed his hand, and together, in the day's fading light, they watched the city they'd built continue, unhurried and unremarkable and entirely real, simply going on being itself.
