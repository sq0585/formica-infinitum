# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Seventy-Four — The Second Anchor

He brought the conversation to Liang Jingzhi carefully, the same deliberate honesty he'd brought to Chen Yufei months earlier, sitting with her in the workspace where she'd spent so many hours now mapping anchor points and Pacific Ring coordinates with the same methodical precision she brought to everything.

"There's something I need to explain to you," he said, "about the Colony Link, and what it would mean if I asked you to become formally part of it."

She set down the survey data she'd been reviewing and gave him her full attention, the particular focused stillness he'd come to recognize whenever she sensed a conversation mattered beyond its surface content.

He explained it the same way he'd explained it to Chen Yufei — the Anchor designation, the structural weight underneath the sensory connection, the genuine consent the mechanism required, the honest acknowledgment that this was asking something considerably larger than ordinary friendship or professional collaboration.

"Chen Yufei is already my first Anchor," he said. "I'd like you to be my second. Not because of any romantic dimension — that's specifically not what this is between us, and I want to be completely clear about that distinction. Because I trust you, because you've already chosen, repeatedly, to stand inside this impossible world with me rather than simply studying it from a safe professional distance, and because I think you deserve to make this choice with full information rather than discovering its weight by accident later."

She was quiet for a long moment, the careful, methodical part of her mind he'd come to rely on working through the implications with the same rigor she brought to any genuinely significant dataset.

"I need a few days," she said finally. "Not because I'm inclined to decline. Because a decision this structurally significant deserves the same careful consideration I'd give any major commitment, and I don't think either of us would respect a hasty yes more than a considered one."

"Take whatever time you need," he said. "There's no urgency attached to this, beyond my own preference for you to understand it before it becomes relevant in some moment that doesn't leave room for careful thought."

---

She came back to him three days later, her expression carrying the particular settled clarity of someone who had genuinely worked through a decision rather than simply waited out a polite interval.

"I've thought about it from every angle I could find," she said. "The structural risk to me is minimal — I'm not the one whose continuity depends on the connection holding. The risk runs the other direction, toward you, toward what happens to your coherence if something happened to me." She paused. "I considered, honestly, whether that asymmetry should make me hesitant. I decided it doesn't."

"Why not?"

"Because I've spent the last several months watching you build something extraordinary — the Pacific Ring network, the Strata's reconstruction, an entire institutional replacement for two thousand years of flawed tradition — and all of it, every piece of it, depends on you remaining coherent enough to keep doing the work. If being your Anchor helps you stay yourself while you become what you're becoming, then yes." She met his eyes directly, her certainty complete. "Clearly yes."

He felt something settle in his chest, the particular gravity of being trusted again with someone's deliberate, fully-considered commitment to his continued existence.

"That's clear and practical reasoning," he said. "And kind, underneath it."

"In that order," she agreed, something dry entering her voice. "I'm a scientist. I arrive at kindness through careful analysis more often than through instinct. I've made my peace with that being how I'm built."

"I don't think that makes it less genuine."

"No," she said. "I don't think it does either."

He explained the designation process to her carefully over the following days, the same patient, methodical work he'd done with Chen Yufei, and felt, completing it, the particular weight of his Colony Link's structural foundation growing steadier with each Anchor formally added.

Two Anchors now. Chen Yufei's quiet, accumulated trust built across nearly two years of shared danger. Liang Jingzhi's clear-eyed, deliberately reasoned commitment, arrived at through careful analysis rather than instinct, no less genuine for the difference in how she'd reached it.

He wrote it into his notebook that evening, the careful accounting he'd learned to keep of every significant development this impossible world handed him.

> *Liang Jingzhi: Anchor 2, formally designated. Her reasoning: clear, practical, kind, in exactly that order. I think that's precisely the kind of foundation I want underneath whatever I'm eventually going to become.*
