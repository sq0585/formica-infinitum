# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Two — What This Means for Lady Shen

She found him three days later, in the Strata's outer hall, her application already submitted and approved through Fang Ruxue's standard process — no special consideration, exactly as she'd asked.

"I wanted to tell you something privately," she said, settling into the chair across from him with the particular formal stillness she still carried into most conversations, even now. "Not for any official record. Just for you to hear directly."

"Go ahead."

"I am doing this," she said, "because what I built is no longer viable, and your structure is the best available alternative." She held his gaze steadily as she said it, no softening, no attempt to dress the admission in nobler language than it actually deserved. "I want you to understand that clearly. This is not loyalty. This is not friendship, though I respect you considerably more than I did a year ago. This is a careful, honest assessment that the world I helped build has ended, and the world replacing it is the one with the best chance of being worth belonging to."

He considered this, the particular weight of her bluntness landing exactly the way she'd clearly intended it to.

"That's honest," he said.

"I thought you'd prefer honesty," she said. "You've never once asked me to pretend I trust you completely, or that I've become someone fundamentally different than the person who once tried to capture you. I think you'd find a performance of transformed loyalty considerably less useful than an accurate accounting of where I actually stand."

"I would," he agreed. "Where do you actually stand?"

She considered the question with the same careful precision she brought to everything.

"I respect what you've built," she said. "I believe, on balance, that it represents something better than what existed before — more honest, more distributed, less dependent on any single person's continued goodwill to remain functional. I don't fully trust you, not in the complete sense that word usually implies. I don't think complete trust is something either of us should expect from the other, given how we began."

"That's fair."

"I will work within this structure honestly. I will contribute what genuine value I have — and I have considerable institutional experience that the Collective currently lacks, regardless of what else either of us thinks about how I acquired it. I will not pretend to be your friend simply because pretending would be politically convenient for either of us."

He sat with this, finding, somewhat to his own surprise, that he respected the answer more than he would have respected something warmer and less honest.

"I don't need you to be my friend," he said. "I need you to be honest about where you stand, the way you just were. That's more durable than friendship, in my experience. Friendship can erode under pressure if it was never built on accurate information to begin with. This won't."

Something in her expression shifted — not warmth exactly, but a kind of recognition, the particular acknowledgment of someone realizing they'd been understood correctly without needing to perform understanding to receive it.

"No friendship," she said. "No complete trust. Honesty, and a shared interest in building something that actually lasts."

"That's enough," he said. "I think it might be more than enough, given where we started."

She nodded once, the careful, formal gesture he'd come to associate with her, and stood to leave, her business in the conversation apparently concluded to her satisfaction.

"I'll see you at the next working group session," she said. "I understand there's infrastructure work that needs doing. I imagine I'll find it familiar."

"I imagine you will."

She left without further ceremony, and he sat alone afterward with the strange, complicated shape of a relationship that had no name he could comfortably apply to it — not enemy, not ally, not friend, but something steadier than any of those categories had ever managed to be.

He found, turning it over, that he didn't mind the absence of a tidy label at all.
