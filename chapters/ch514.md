# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 514 — What Six Years Have Made Her

He asked her, before they began the actual work, to let him simply look.

She agreed without asking why, which he understood, by now, was itself characteristic — Liang Jingzhi had never once, in six years, required a fully explained reason before extending someone the benefit of trusting their judgment. He suspected she understood the request better than he did himself.

He looked.

Not at the node's function, which he had already perceived clearly, the coherent structure she anchored, the gradient it fed. He looked at her — the specific, whole, undivided fact of the person who had made this choice, months ago, before he had the capacity to verify anything she told him about the cost.

She was not diminished.

He had believed her account of this for months, the way a person believes a trusted friend's description of a country they haven't personally visited — fully, but secondhand, with the small unavoidable gap that secondhand belief always carries no matter how completely it's given. He found, looking directly, that the gap simply wasn't there. She was, in every measure his perception could offer, exactly as whole as she had ever been. More, if anything — not larger in any way that mattered, but settled, in a way he recognized because he had spent five years learning to recognize it in himself.

"I told you it wasn't a sacrifice," she said, watching him look, unhurried. "You can actually check now."

"I'm checking," he said.

"Take your time. I'm not going anywhere. That's rather the point."

He took his time. He found nothing that contradicted her — not a fraction of loss folded quietly beneath the surface of her account, not the particular texture of a person who has convinced themselves of a comfortable story because the true one was too heavy to hold. Just Liang Jingzhi, entire, doing exactly what she had told him, from the very beginning, that she wanted to do.

*Observation 514,* he wrote, slower than usual, because he wanted the entry to actually hold what he'd found rather than simply gesture at it. *I have spent months worried, in some quiet unexamined part of myself, that Liang Jingzhi's account of her own choice was generous framing over a genuine cost — the specific, familiar shape of someone choosing to describe an unbearable thing as bearable because the alternative was harder for everyone around her. I was wrong. There is no gap between what she told me and what is actually true. She is not managing a loss. She made a choice she has never once regretted, and the discomfort I have carried about it these past months was entirely mine, not hers, and I owe her the honesty of admitting that plainly rather than continuing to quietly worry on her behalf.*

"You're writing something," she said.

"I'm correcting something."

"Good," she said, and there was real warmth in it, unguarded in a way she rarely allowed even with him. "I spent a long time being the geologist nobody quite believed when she said the fieldwork was going well. I'd rather not spend the second half of my life being the anchor nobody quite believes when she says the same thing."

"You won't be. Not from me. Not anymore."

"Then we're finished with this part," she said, brisk, the particular efficiency she used to close a subject she considered genuinely resolved rather than merely tabled. "Tell me what Formless Scale can actually add to what I've already built. I'd like to hear the mechanism before we start, not after."

He told her, and felt, telling her, the specific pleasure of explaining something technical to someone who would understand every part of it correctly the first time, the way he had once explained circuit efficiency to Chen Yufei in a very different laboratory, a very different life ago, and found her exactly as ready to hear it now as anyone had ever been.

She asked precise questions as he spoke — not skeptical ones, he understood, but the specific kind a careful scientist asks when she genuinely wants to understand a mechanism rather than simply approve it. Where does the amplification actually originate. Does it require continuous attention or can it be sustained passively. What happens to the gradient if his attention is ever needed elsewhere.

He answered each one honestly, including the ones where the honest answer was that he didn't fully know yet.

"You're comfortable not knowing," she observed, something like approval in it.

"I've had practice," he said. "Most of what I've learned in six years arrived exactly this way — building the thing first, understanding the last ten percent of it only after it was already working."

"That's not how geology usually proceeds."

"No," he agreed. "But I suspect it's closer to how you actually made your own decision than the tidy version you've told everyone since."

She looked at him a long moment, and something in her expression — both halves of it, the office and the center — settled into the particular warmth of someone who had just been seen accurately rather than flatteringly.

"Fair," she said. "Let's begin."
