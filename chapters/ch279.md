# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Seventy-Nine — The Decision

He brought the question to the three people whose judgment he trusted most carefully, one at a time, the same deliberate caution he'd brought to every decision this significant since the very first weeks of understanding what he carried.

Chen Yufei's response came without much hesitation.

"You've already decided," she said, watching him lay out the considerations. "I can hear it in how you're describing her. You wouldn't be this careful about the framing if you weren't already most of the way to yes."

"I want to make sure I'm not missing something important before I commit to it."

"What would change your mind?"

He considered this honestly. "Evidence that she'd misuse what she learned. Evidence that her character doesn't match what two careful sessions suggested. I haven't found either, and I don't expect I will."

"Then I think you already know what you're going to do," Chen Yufei said. "I trust your read on people. You've been right about Fang Ruxue, about Liang Jingzhi, about most of the difficult judgment calls this world has handed you. I don't see why this should be different."

Fang Ruxue's response, when he brought the question to her, carried a different but complementary weight.

"She will publish," Fang Ruxue said, the same careful precision she brought to anything structural. "Once she fully understands what she's looking at, a scientist of her caliber will not be content to simply know privately. Publication is how her field validates and builds on discovery. You should understand that consequence clearly before you proceed."

"I do. I've thought about it carefully."

"And you've accepted it?"

He sat with the question, the full weight of what it actually meant settling slowly into something he found, somewhat to his own surprise, he'd already made peace with.

"I think it's time," he said. "Not recklessly — I want to manage the pace and framing carefully, the way I've managed every disclosure so far. But the world this Collective is building can't stay permanently hidden from the people whose work could genuinely strengthen it. Dr. Shen Yue's physics framework, properly integrated, could accelerate our understanding of the Veritonal substrate in ways none of us currently working from cultivation theory alone are equipped to manage."

"That's a significant philosophical shift," Fang Ruxue said. "Moving from careful concealment toward deliberate, managed disclosure."

"It is," he agreed. "I think it's the right one. I think we've reached the point where the hidden world's continued survival depends less on staying hidden and more on building genuine, durable bridges to the parts of the surface world capable of strengthening it responsibly."

---

He brought the question to Meen-4 last, understanding that her perspective carried a weight and history none of the others could fully match.

She was quiet for a long moment after he'd explained the situation, the particular stillness he'd learned to recognize as genuine consideration rather than routine processing.

*She will publish,* Meen-4 signaled finally. *Publications attract attention.*

"Yes," he said.

*You have accepted this?*

He thought about the careful, patient pacing of every previous disclosure — Chen Yufei, then Liang Jingzhi, then the slow, deliberate growth of the Collective itself, each expansion carrying its own particular weight of consequence he'd had to learn to carry.

"I think it's time," he said.

Meen-4 was quiet again, longer this time, and he waited, understanding that whatever she said next would carry the accumulated perspective of four hundred years of careful Formican observation, of a species that had once, catastrophically, failed to manage exactly this kind of expanding visibility.

*The Formican Empire's collapse began, in part, with exactly this kind of decision,* she signaled finally. *Expanding visibility, made carefully, for reasons that seemed sound at the time. I do not say this to discourage you. I say this because you should understand the full weight of the pattern you are choosing to repeat, even carefully, even with better judgment than my own civilization ultimately exercised.*

He sat with this, the gravity of it settling into something he didn't try to dismiss or minimize.

"I understand the weight," he said. "I don't think staying hidden indefinitely is actually safer, in the long run, than careful, managed disclosure. I think the alternative — discovery happening accidentally, without any care or preparation behind it — is considerably more dangerous than choosing the timing and the people deliberately."

*That may be correct,* Meen-4 signaled. *I do not have certainty to offer you either way. I have only the memory of a civilization that made similar choices without sufficient care, and the observation that you, so far, have shown more care than my own ancestors managed.*

"That's not nothing."

*No,* she agreed. *It is not nothing. Proceed, if you have decided. I will not stand in the way of a choice this carefully considered, even one I cannot promise will end well.*

He nodded, the weight of her qualified blessing settling into something he carried forward with full seriousness, and began, the following morning, the careful work of bringing Dr. Shen Yue fully into the world he'd spent two years protecting.
