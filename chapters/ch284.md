# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty-Four — The Third Anchor

He brought the question to Dr. Shen Yue sooner than he'd originally planned, the Dissolution Path's careful, sobering weight pressing the timeline forward with an urgency he couldn't responsibly ignore.

He explained the full mechanism the way he'd explained it to Chen Yufei and Liang Jingzhi before her — the Colony Link's structural function, the Anchor designation's genuine consequence, the honest acknowledgment that this was asking something considerably larger than ordinary collaboration. He added, this time, the part he hadn't needed to explain to the others with quite this urgency: the eighty-kilometer expansion, the texture he could no longer always distinguish from his own feelings, the Dissolution Path's documented, irreversible outcome for practitioners who expanded without sufficient structural support.

She listened without interrupting, her focused stillness carrying the same rigorous attention she brought to any genuinely significant dataset, and when he finished, she sat with it for a long moment before responding.

"You're asking me to help maintain the coherence of something that is going to eventually exist at scales I can't currently imagine," she said slowly, working through the full shape of it the way she worked through any complex problem — methodically, completely, refusing to commit to a conclusion before she'd genuinely understood every variable. "And in exchange, I get access to data that would fill a hundred careers."

"That's correct," he said.

She considered this, her scientist's mind visibly weighing the proposition with the same careful rigor she'd bring to any major research commitment, and then, with a certainty that arrived faster than he'd expected given the gravity of what he'd just described, she answered.

"Yes," she said. "Obviously yes."

He felt something settle in his chest, the particular relief of an answer arriving cleanly, without the hesitation he might have reasonably expected given how recently she'd entered this world.

"You don't want more time to consider it?" he asked, genuinely surprised by the speed of her certainty.

"I've been considering it since the moment you started explaining the mechanism," she said. "I don't need additional time to arrive at a conclusion I already reached partway through your explanation. The risk to me, as you've described it, is structurally minimal — I'm not the one whose continuity depends on the connection. The benefit, both to you and to the work I'm now privileged to be part of, is considerable. That's not a complicated calculation, even accounting for the gravity of what you're asking."

"It's still a significant commitment."

"It is," she agreed. "I'm not minimizing that. I understand exactly what I'm agreeing to — to help hold the coherence of something that's going to grow considerably larger than either of us currently has the framework to fully imagine, in exchange for being genuinely, deeply part of the most significant discovery of my entire career, and of human science generally." She met his eyes directly. "I've spent three years working with incomplete data, certain something larger existed just beyond what I could measure. You're offering me not just access to that larger thing, but an actual structural role within it. I would be a poor scientist, and a poorer person, if I let that opportunity pass simply because accepting it required courage."

He sat with her answer, the particular gravity of her reasoning — clear, deliberate, genuinely considered despite its speed — settling into something he recognized now as a pattern across all three of his Anchors. Chen Yufei's accumulated trust, built slowly across nearly two years of shared danger. Liang Jingzhi's careful, practical analysis, arrived at through methodical reasoning rather than instinct. Now Dr. Shen Yue's rapid but genuinely rigorous certainty, the conclusion of someone who'd spent years already preparing, without knowing it, for exactly this kind of decision.

"Then I'd like you to be my third Anchor," he said. "Thank you for the clarity. And for the courage, since you named it yourself."

"Don't thank me yet," she said, something dry entering her voice, the particular humor he was beginning to recognize as genuinely hers. "Explain the actual designation process to me first. I have a feeling, given everything else about this world, that it's considerably more involved than a conversation and a handshake."

"It is," he admitted.

"Of course it is," she said, and pulled out her notebook, ready, with the same rigorous, eager precision she'd brought to every prior conversation, to understand exactly what came next.

He explained the process carefully, the third Anchor designation settling into place alongside the first two, and felt, completing it, the particular structural relief of a foundation finally growing strong enough to hold whatever this expanding world asked of him next.

Three Anchors now. He wrote it into his notebook that evening, the careful accounting he'd learned to keep of every significant development this impossible world had handed him.

> *Dr. Shen Yue: Anchor 3, formally designated. Her response, characteristically her own: rapid, rigorous, genuinely considered despite its speed. I think the foundation underneath whatever I'm becoming is, for the first time since Meen-4 explained the Dissolution Path, finally stable enough to trust.*
