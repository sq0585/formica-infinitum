# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 503 — Scale Five — Stellar Joins

Stellar Scale did not arrive quietly.

It could not, he understood, even as it happened — the solar system was not a small thing to fold into an already-crowded field of attention, and some part of the difficulty he felt was simply the scale of the addition rather than any flaw in the technique. The Sun's Veritonal field settled in beside the molecular hush of the cushion fibers, beside the walls he could feel without seeing, beside Chengdu's twenty million lives, and did not diminish any of them by its presence. It simply arrived, enormous, and took its place in line.

He felt the asteroid belt's slow diffusion. He felt the outer planets, distant and quiet, still carrying — he noticed this now with a clarity Stellar Scale alone had never quite given him — the faint, forty-million-year echo that ran through the whole system like a hairline crack in old glass, present everywhere, dangerous nowhere in particular.

*Observation 503,* he wrote, the words coming slower now, each one requiring a small, deliberate act of attention he no longer took for granted. *Five scales, simultaneous, foreground. The solar system does not feel larger than the room. It feels exactly as large as it is, and the room feels exactly as large as it is, and neither one is diminished by standing next to the other. I did not expect scale itself to become, in some sense, irrelevant to how much attention a thing requires. Only its own nature matters now. Not its size.*

This was where the Anchors stopped being passive.

He felt the shift in all five of them at once — Chen Yufei's presence, which had held him through every prior threshold with the effortless steadiness of someone pouring tea, tightening now into something more deliberate, more chosen. Wei Canglan's connection, always precise, sharpening further, a conscious act of will rather than the habitual vigilance it had been a moment before. Dr. Shen Yue's instruments registered the change externally, a new cadence in the readings that made her lean forward, wordless, focused entirely on the numbers. Meen-4's signal, from her corner, took on a quality he had rarely felt from her — not surprise, Meen-4 did not experience surprise the way humans did, but something adjacent to it, an ancient patience recalibrating itself to a scale even she had not personally witnessed crossed.

And Liang Jingzhi, from the dissolution zone, went very still.

*This is harder now,* she sent, plainly, no complaint in it, simply the same accurate accounting she gave everything. *Not too hard. Harder.*

*You can say if it's too much.*

*I know I can. It isn't. Keep going.*

He kept going.

The effort was real now, he understood — not the reaching effort of earlier thresholds, but a sustained, conscious labor, five people and three hundred and twenty-three more choosing, moment to moment, to keep holding a structure that had quietly stopped being effortless the instant the solar system joined it. He felt the specific weight of that choice in each of them, distinct, individual, nothing blurring into a single undifferentiated mass of support the way exhaustion sometimes made connections blur. Chen Yufei's effort felt like Chen Yufei's effort. Wei Canglan's felt like his. This, he understood, watching it happen, was the entire discipline in miniature — even under strain, even at this scale, nothing dissolved into anything else.

He held five scales now, and beneath them, five people, awake and present and choosing, again and again, in each successive second, to keep choosing.

He did not write anything further for a while. There was nothing further to write that the moment itself was not already saying more precisely than his hand could manage.

The sixth scale waited at the edge of everything, closer now, its shape sharper than it had been an hour ago. He did not reach for it yet.

He sat instead with what he already held, and let himself feel, plainly, the specific gratitude of a person who understood exactly how much five other people were currently choosing to carry on his behalf, and did not intend to let that fact become background to him, ever, no matter how many scales he eventually learned to hold at once.
