# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 409 — Wei Canglan's Non-Comment

Wei Canglan said nothing about the separation. Not the day the news settled through the Strata's ordinary channels of gossip and observation, not in the week that followed, not in any of the weeks after that. Lin Mushi noticed the silence, and understood, correctly, that it was not indifference.

What changed instead was Wei Canglan's presence.

He began spending more time in the pocket-dimension laboratory — not a dramatic shift, nothing anyone else in the Strata would have particularly noticed, just a gradual increase in the hours he chose to occupy the same space Lin Mushi occupied, working on his own projects with the same methodical, unhurried focus he brought to everything, saying little, asking nothing about the subject everyone else seemed to be carefully avoiding around Lin Mushi entirely.

He brought food, occasionally, without comment — a bowl set down beside whatever Lin Mushi happened to be working on, no announcement, no expectation of thanks beyond a nod. He asked, once or twice a week, about Stellar Scale prerequisites, about the Archive's curriculum, about the technical specifics of whatever project currently occupied Lin Mushi's attention, questions that had nothing directly to do with Chen Yufei or the separation but that filled the space where conversation might otherwise have gone quiet and uncomfortable.

He did not ask how Lin Mushi was doing.

Lin Mushi noticed the absence of that particular question more than he might have noticed its presence, and understood, slowly, over the weeks that followed, that the absence was itself the answer to a question he hadn't needed to ask directly. Wei Canglan's presence — steady, undemanding, simply *there*, evening after evening, in the quiet company of shared work rather than shared conversation — answered the question more honestly than words could have managed.

"You don't have to keep doing this," Lin Mushi said finally, about a month in, watching Wei Canglan methodically calibrate a Circuit relay component that, by now, had been rebuilt and recalibrated more times than seemed strictly necessary.

"Doing what," Wei Canglan said, not looking up.

"Spending every evening in here. I know why you're doing it. You don't have to keep doing it just because you think I need it."

Wei Canglan set the tool down, finally looking at him directly, something in his expression carrying the particular bluntness Lin Mushi had come to rely on more than he ever admitted openly.

"I'm not doing it because I think you need it," Wei Canglan said. "I'm doing it because it's where I actually want to be. The lab work is genuinely interesting to me. You're generally decent company, when you're not brooding, which — for what it's worth — you've been doing less of than I expected." He paused, and something almost gentle entered his voice despite the flat delivery. "If it happens to also mean you're not sitting alone with this every evening, that's not a burden I'm carrying. That's just a good outcome from doing something I'd be doing anyway."

Lin Mushi absorbed that, recognizing, underneath the deliberate understatement, the same careful, undemonstrative loyalty Wei Canglan had shown him for years — the money transferred quietly to his parents years ago, the anger he'd never fully been able to hold onto because it kept colliding with gratitude, the steady presence that had, time and again, arrived exactly when it was needed without ever once announcing itself as help.

"Thank you," he said quietly. "For not asking how I'm doing."

Wei Canglan huffed something that might have been amusement. "You'd have lied to me if I had," he said. "I've known you long enough to know you say 'fine' when you mean something considerably more complicated. This way I don't have to listen to the lie, and you don't have to tell it."

"That's uncomfortably accurate."

"Most things I say are." Wei Canglan picked the tool back up, returning his attention to the relay component with the same unhurried focus as before. "Hand me the smaller calibrator. The one on your left. And eat something — you've been picking at that bowl for twenty minutes instead of actually eating it."

Lin Mushi picked up the calibrator, and then the bowl, and ate, quietly, in the companionable silence of a friendship that had never needed many words to carry exactly what it meant, and found, in the ordinary rhythm of the evening, something that felt, unexpectedly, like being fully taken care of without ever once being asked to explain why he needed it.
