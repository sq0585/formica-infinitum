# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 436 — Stellar Qi Records

The G-type record, once he'd settled into the actual rhythm of reading it, turned out to be less a document and more a discipline — forty million years of a single star's Qi behavior, logged at intervals he couldn't immediately translate into any calendar he understood, building slowly toward a conclusion the installation's long-dead crew had clearly considered foundational.

He worked through it the way he worked through everything difficult, in sessions bounded by the same recovery his body still, stubbornly, insisted on requiring even this far from home — an hour of study, a check on the relay chain, a few minutes simply sitting with what he'd absorbed before reaching for more.

The early entries were what he'd expected, more or less: measurements of the star's own Veritonal output, its density, its cycles, data that mapped, with some adjustment for scale, onto everything he already understood about the Sun from his weeks crossing the solar system to get here. He catalogued it efficiently, confident, the particular satisfaction of finding his existing framework mostly holding.

Then, several hundred entries in, the record shifted in a way that stopped him cold.

*Observation 366: stellar field readings show periodic fluctuation correlated not with the star's own internal cycles, but with the position and activity of neighboring stellar fields, at distances the crew's own notation measures in light-years. This correlation should not exist if stars are isolated Veritonal sources. It exists anyway.*

He sat with the entry a long time, rereading his own words, checking the underlying data three separate times before he let himself trust the conclusion it kept insisting on.

A star was not, it turned out, an isolated thing. He had assumed — reasonably, he thought, given everything he understood about how Veritonal fields worked at every scale he'd previously studied — that the Sun's field, however vast, existed essentially alone, a single enormous source radiating outward into empty space. The installation's forty million years of patient measurement said otherwise. Stellar fields interacted, resonated, dampened and amplified one another across distances that made even the solar system's own scale look small by comparison, connected through Veritonal channels that ran between stars the way the Colony Link ran between people — not physically present in the ordinary sense, but genuinely, measurably real.

*Rule,* he wrote, and felt the particular vertigo of a rule that reorganized everything built on top of it. *A star is not a point source. It is a node. The Sun does not simply exist in the solar system's center, radiating outward in isolation — it participates in a structure that extends across the stellar neighborhood, connected to neighboring stars through channels I did not previously know existed, in a network I am only now, for the first time, beginning to perceive the outline of.*

He set the notebook down and let himself actually feel the size of what he'd just written, the way he always tried to before moving on to the next entry — not rushing past the vertigo, but sitting in it long enough to understand what it was actually telling him.

Everything he had learned about Planetary Architect scale, everything about managing a single planet's Colony Link, had assumed Earth's field existed in relative isolation, one small coherent structure floating in a much larger emptiness. It didn't. It never had. It had simply been too faint a participant in the larger network for him to perceive its connections, the way a single voice in a vast, quiet room might not notice it was part of a conversation until the rest of the room finally spoke loudly enough to hear.

He thought, briefly, of the Colony Link itself — how it had felt, in the earliest weeks, like something entirely new, only to reveal itself later as one more layer in a structure that had always been larger than his first understanding of it. The pattern repeating now, at a scale that made every previous repetition look, in retrospect, like practice for exactly this moment.

*Query for later,* he wrote, underlining it carefully. *If stellar fields interact through channels at interstellar distance, and the Void Lattice itself operates at galactic scale, is the distinction between "stellar network" and "Void Lattice" actually meaningful, or am I looking at the same structure from two different distances?*

He didn't answer the question. He didn't yet have enough to answer it honestly, and five years of careful practice had taught him the specific danger of answering questions before the evidence actually supported an answer.

He checked the relay chain — stable, a short message from his mother relayed through Chen Yufei, nothing urgent, simply *tell him I said hello when he checks in next* — and felt the particular grounding relief of it, an entire stellar network resolving itself into something genuinely larger than he'd imagined, and somewhere eighty million kilometers behind him, his mother still remembering, without fail, to say hello.

Then he turned back to the archive, and kept reading, because the record's next several thousand entries were, according to the crew's own careful organization, about to explain exactly how that interaction actually worked.
