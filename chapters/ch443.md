# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 443 — Meen-4's Memory

Meen-4's reply took longer than usual to arrive — nearly a full day, well past the relay chain's ordinary lag, long enough that he'd started to wonder whether something in the transmission had failed before the message finally resolved, dense and careful, clearly composed after considerable effort rather than dashed off between other obligations.

*I searched the Chengdu Deep Archive's older records,* it began, *and found nothing directly. This did not surprise me — the Deep Archive is comprehensive for Earth-relevant material, but an expedition to a stellar anomaly would not, under ordinary circumstances, have been catalogued there. I then did something I have not done in longer than I am entirely comfortable admitting: I accessed my own oldest memory structures directly, rather than relying on the Archive's indexed records.*

He read that line twice, understanding, faintly, what it must have cost her to write it. Meen-4 spoke rarely of her own memory as something separate from the Archive she maintained — the two had always seemed, in five years of knowing her, functionally identical, a Keeper and the records she kept, indistinguishable in practice even if he understood, intellectually, that she was more than a living index.

*I found a manifest,* the message continued. *Eight Formicans. Designations rather than names in the form you would recognize, though I can render approximate translations if you wish them. The expedition to solar position 7.4-N, dated — by my own reckoning, cross-referenced against events I do remember clearly — to a period I would place at roughly the same era as the installation's final active transmission.*

He sat very still, waiting for the rest, already understanding from the careful, deliberate pacing of her message that she was building toward something she wanted him fully prepared for before he read it.

*Status, for all eight: Unknown.*

*I want to be precise about what that designation means, because I suspect you are about to interpret it more gently than it deserves. Unknown, in Formican record-keeping, is not an administrative placeholder awaiting further information. It is a formal, final classification, applied only when an established protocol for reporting an expedition's fate — return, confirmed loss, recovered remains, any of several other categories our records maintain — has been exhausted without result. Unknown means we received no body. No signal. No explanation. They simply stopped, and nothing in forty million years of subsequent record-keeping has ever added a single further fact to what I am telling you now.*

He set the notebook down, and found, for the first time since arriving at the installation, that he needed to simply sit with something rather than immediately reach for the next observation, the next rule, the next careful line of reasoning.

Eight people. Not an accident, not a single unlucky researcher pushing too far alone — eight, a full expedition, prepared as thoroughly as any team he'd ever read about, walking together toward something they had studied for decades and still did not survive meeting.

*I do not say this to alarm you,* Meen-4's message continued, when he finally returned to it. *I say it because you deserve the complete truth, and because I trust — having watched you make the decision you already made, before I sent any of this — that you do not need me to tell you twice why this remains something we set down rather than chase.*

*I will add one further note, offered honestly rather than as reassurance: in forty million years, no subsequent Formican expedition, to my knowledge, has ever attempted to investigate 7.4-N directly. Not out of institutional fear, precisely. Out of the same discipline you demonstrated on your own, arriving at the same conclusion independently, at considerably greater personal risk, with considerably less institutional memory to draw on than I have just given you.*

He read the whole message through once more, slowly, and then a third time, letting the specific grief of it settle rather than rushing past it the way curiosity would ordinarily have pushed him to.

Eight Formicans, whoever they had been, whatever names the designations in Meen-4's memory actually stood for, had built something extraordinary — a curriculum thorough enough that he was, right now, using it to close a comprehension gap he hadn't known how to close any other way — and had then walked, carefully, deliberately, into the one question their own thoroughness apparently couldn't answer for them.

*Thank you for telling me the truth of it,* he wrote back, finally, once he trusted his hands enough to write clearly. *I'm not going to chase it. I want that on record, from me, not just as a decision I made before I knew the full weight of what I was setting down.*

*Noted,* her reply came, faster this time. *And Lin Mushi — thank you, in turn, for making that decision the way you made it. I have watched you grow for five years. I do not think I have ever been more proud of a choice you made than the one where curiosity, faced with exactly this, chose to wait.*

He closed the notebook, and turned back, finally, to the last portion of the standard classifications still waiting to be finished — the actual reason he had come here, patient and disciplined and, for the moment, deliberately, carefully, complete.
