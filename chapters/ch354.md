# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifty-Four — What He Sees in the Garden

He returned to it two weeks later, the observation he'd deliberately set aside during the visit itself finally demanding the closer attention he'd promised himself he'd give it.

He extended his Colony Link toward Leshan carefully, the planetary field's vast reach making the small, specific location easy to find even at considerable distance, and settled his attention onto his mother's garden with the same patient, careful discipline Liang Jingzhi had spent six weeks teaching him — not sensing the surface, but comprehending what sat beneath it, the accumulated weight of years rather than the passing texture of a single afternoon.

What he found took his breath away.

The garden's soil, unremarkable to any ordinary eye, carried a density of Qi concentration he hadn't expected, hadn't even thought to look for during his physical visit two weeks earlier — not dramatic, not the kind of visible, obvious power that announced itself the way a Circuit node or a Strata boundary did, but real, accumulated, unmistakable once his comprehension actually settled into the timescale required to perceive it.

Thirty years. He understood, extending his sensing more carefully, exactly what he was looking at — thirty years of his mother's devoted, unremarkable attention, tending the same small plot of earth with the same patient care she brought to everything, had produced something his cultivation-trained perception recognized, now, as genuine Qi concentration. Not deliberate. Not cultivated in any technical sense she'd ever have recognized or intended. Simply the accumulated residue of thirty years spent caring, consistently, patiently, about something small and alive and entirely ordinary.

---

He sat with the discovery for a long while, understanding its full weight slowly, the way he'd learned to understand most of this world's largest truths.

His mother had never cultivated anything, in the sense this world had taught him to recognize. She'd never touched a Circuit node, never crossed into a Strata, never once suspected that the impossible life her son now led connected, in any way, to the small garden she tended every morning before work and every evening after.

And yet.

Thirty years of devoted, ordinary attention — watering, weeding, patient correction of whatever went wrong, season after season, without any technique beyond simple, consistent love — had produced something real. Something his own carefully trained perception could recognize now as genuinely, measurably significant, in exactly the terms this impossible world had taught him to measure significance.

He thought of everything he'd learned about cultivation across two years — Compression Ratios, Dimensional Weaving, the careful, deliberate discipline required to reach each successive threshold — and understood, sitting with his mother's garden's quiet, accumulated density, that some of the most significant power in this entire vast, complicated world had apparently been growing, unremarked and unintended, in a small plot of dirt behind an ordinary woman's ordinary house, built from nothing more exotic than thirty years of simply, consistently, showing up.

---

He didn't tell her. He considered it, briefly, turning the decision over with the same careful deliberation he brought to most difficult choices — but concluded, examining his own motivations honestly, that telling her would change something that didn't need changing.

She didn't garden for Qi concentration. She gardened because she loved it, because tending something small and alive gave her a kind of steady, uncomplicated satisfaction that had nothing to do with power or cultivation or anything this impossible world had ever asked of either of them. Telling her what her garden had quietly become would risk turning something pure into something instrumental, and he found, sitting with the choice, that he wasn't willing to make that trade.

Instead, the next time he visited, he simply knelt beside her in the dirt again, tending the garden with her the way he always had, saying nothing about what his perception now understood, letting the quiet, accumulated power of thirty years of love remain exactly what it had always been to her — a garden, ordinary and beloved and entirely, wonderfully unremarkable.

He wrote about it that evening, the entry careful and quiet.

> *My mother's garden carries thirty years of real Qi concentration, built from nothing but consistent, ordinary care. I'm not going to tell her. Some things are more powerful precisely because they were never meant to be power at all. I think this might be the truest lesson this entire arc has tried to teach me, and I nearly missed it, kneeling in dirt I'd visited a hundred times before I finally learned how to actually see it.*
