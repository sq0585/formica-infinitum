# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Nine — Gou Ping's Last Move

The approach came to Lady Shen directly, which told Lin Mushi, the moment he heard about it, exactly how badly Gou Ping had misjudged the woman he was trying to recruit.

She brought him the message herself, printed and annotated, the same careful precision she'd brought to every piece of intelligence she'd shared since her formal dissolution of Northern Peak.

"Gou Ping's faction reached out yesterday," she said, setting the printed exchange on the table between them. "Nine practitioners remain, by their own account — fewer than the Collective's last estimate, which suggests some further attrition I haven't fully tracked yet. Their proposal: if I rejoin them, lend my standing and remaining institutional credibility to their cause, they'll provide intelligence on you in exchange."

He read through the exchange carefully, the careful, almost wheedling tone of someone who had clearly believed Lady Shen's defection was strategic rather than genuine — a temporary accommodation to circumstance that a sufficiently attractive counter-offer might reverse.

"They think you're still playing politics," he said.

"They think everyone is still playing politics, because that's the only framework Gou Ping has ever genuinely understood. He cannot conceive of a version of me that simply decided the old structure was wrong and chose differently because of that, rather than because some more advantageous position presented itself."

"What did you tell them?"

"Nothing," she said. "I forwarded the proposal to you and to Fang Ruxue immediately, without response. I thought you should both see exactly what they're offering and exactly how confident they apparently are that I might accept it."

He sat with this, understanding slowly the full weight of what she'd just done — not simply declining an offer, but demonstrating, through immediate and complete transparency, precisely how far her actual loyalty had shifted.

"You could have used this," he said. "Played along, gathered their plans, fed us information from inside their confidence. That's the kind of move the Lady Shen I first met might have made."

"It is," she agreed. "I considered it, briefly, out of old habit more than genuine intention. I decided against it because deception, even deception in service of a good outcome, isn't the kind of relationship I'm trying to build with this Collective anymore. I'd rather be transparently useless to them than cleverly useful to you through methods that would have required lying to everyone involved, including, eventually, myself."

He found, reading the proposal again, that her choice carried a particular weight he hadn't fully appreciated until she'd articulated the reasoning behind it.

"This tells us something important," he said. "Nine practitioners left, desperate enough to approach someone who'd already publicly dissolved her own institution rather than continue fighting. That's not the posture of a faction with a sustainable plan."

"No," Lady Shen agreed. "That's the posture of people who've run out of better options and are reaching for whatever's left, regardless of how unlikely it is to actually work."

He thought about the careful map he'd been building of Gou Ping's faction — twelve members reduced now to nine, no institutional support, no remaining moderate influence to argue for caution, and now, apparently, attempting recruitment from someone who had already very publicly and very completely chosen a different path.

"I want to use this," he said. "Carefully. If they're this desperate, there might be an opportunity to resolve this without further violence — an offer they'd actually consider, rather than continued conflict neither side particularly wants to keep paying for."

"What did you have in mind?"

He thought about Wei Canglan's framework-first teaching, about the maintenance rotation's slow, patient expansion, about every other moment in this conflict where he'd found a way to make cooperation more attractive than continued fighting rather than simply trying to win through force.

"I want to make them an offer," he said. "Not surrender. Something closer to what you yourself chose — full access to the Collective's resources, verified knowledge, a genuine place in whatever comes next, in exchange for actually putting the conflict down."

Lady Shen considered this, the careful, calculating part of her mind he'd come to respect even when he didn't fully trust its conclusions working through the practical shape of the idea.

"It might work," she said. "Or it might simply give them a clearer picture of exactly how thinly stretched they currently are, which could push the most committed remainder toward something more desperate rather than less. I don't have a confident prediction either way."

"Neither do I," he admitted. "But I'd rather try the door that doesn't require anyone else getting hurt before I close it."

She nodded, the particular respect he'd come to recognize in her settling into her expression.

"Then I'll help you build the offer," she said. "I understand Gou Ping's psychology better than almost anyone currently in this Collective. If there's a version of this that actually lands, I can help you find it."
