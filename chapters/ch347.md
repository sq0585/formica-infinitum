# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Seven — The Deep Ocean Strata

He found it during the Ring repair project's tenth site, an otherwise ordinary reinforcement cycle that turned, in its final hour, into something considerably more significant than routine maintenance.

The structure at this particular node ran deeper than the others, its lowest sections descending into bedrock at an angle Liang Jingzhi's survey data hadn't fully mapped before his Weaving carried the repair team down to examine it directly. He held the protective fold steady while the team worked the upper reinforcement, his own attention drifting, in the quiet moments between active tasks, toward the structure's deeper architecture — a habit he'd developed across months of this work, using the downtime to simply observe rather than let his sensing sit idle.

He felt it before he consciously understood what he was feeling — a subtle shift in the structure's underlying geometry, the Ring node's familiar architecture giving way, at its deepest point, to something that read differently against his Planetary Architect comprehension. Not Ring infrastructure. Something older, denser, folded in a pattern that felt, unmistakably, like the same underlying architecture he'd learned to recognize in the Chengdu Strata itself.

"Liang Jingzhi," he said, his voice carrying the specific urgency that made her look up immediately from the reinforcement work. "There's something beneath this node. Something that isn't part of the Ring's own structure."

She joined him at the fold's edge, extending her own sensing alongside his careful attention. "I'm reading a density anomaly. Consistent with — " she paused, working through the pattern with visible surprise. "Consistent with a Strata boundary. There's another Strata down here, connected to this node."

---

He confirmed it that same evening, back in the Deep Archive, cross-referencing what he and Liang Jingzhi had found against Meen-4's own records.

*You have found the Pelagic Archive,* Meen-4 signaled, once he'd described the location and depth precisely. *Or, more accurately, you have found its exterior boundary. The Ring node's deepest structure connects directly to it — a connection I was not previously aware of, though in retrospect, it is not an unreasonable design choice. The Isolationists who sealed the Pelagic Archive would have wanted its location protected by exactly the kind of ancient, undisturbed infrastructure the Ring provided.*

He sat with the discovery, the weight of it arriving with a specific, careful gravity — not surprise exactly, since he'd known the Archive existed and roughly where, but something adjacent to it, the abstract door he'd carried in his awareness for months suddenly, unexpectedly, made concrete and physically reachable.

"Can I enter it?" he asked.

*Physically, likely yes, given your current capability,* Meen-4 signaled. *The seal the Isolationists placed forty million years ago was built to resist casual access, not Planetary Architect-scale comprehension deliberately applied. I do not believe the barrier itself would meaningfully resist you, if you chose to attempt it.*

"But you're not telling me to attempt it."

*No,* she confirmed. *I told you, when we first discussed this Archive months ago, that I wanted you to think carefully about what you were actually looking for before you opened that door. I do not believe that caution has become less relevant simply because the door is now easier to reach than it once was.*

---

He thought about it carefully over the following days, the discovery sitting alongside everything else the Ring repair project had already asked of him, and found, examining his own motivations honestly, that Meen-4's original caution still held.

He didn't need to know precisely how Veth-Karun's dissolution had unfolded, moment by moment, to understand the lesson it carried. He already had that lesson, carried carefully by Ren Wuji across four hundred years and passed to him with a weight he was still, slowly, learning to hold. Opening the Pelagic Archive now, with an eighteen-month review clock already demanding every ounce of careful attention he could give it, felt, examined honestly, like exactly the kind of unnecessary risk Meen-4 had warned him against from the beginning — curiosity without purpose, reaching toward something dangerous simply because reaching had become possible.

He brought the decision to Meen-4 the following morning.

"I'm not going to open it," he said. "Not now. I know where it is. I know I could reach it. I don't think that's the same as having a reason to."

*I believe that is a wise decision,* Meen-4 signaled, something in the cadence carrying genuine, quiet approval. *The door will remain exactly where it is. Some things are better known and left waiting than opened simply because opening has become possible.*

He nodded, the decision settling into him with the specific, steady confidence of choices made carefully rather than reactively, and wrote about it that evening, brief but deliberate.

> *Found the Pelagic Archive's physical location today — connected to Ring node ten, reachable now in a way it wasn't before. I could open it. I'm choosing not to. Not yet. Maybe not ever, unless I eventually find an actual reason rather than simple curiosity. Some doors are worth knowing the shape of precisely so you can keep choosing, deliberately, not to open them.*
