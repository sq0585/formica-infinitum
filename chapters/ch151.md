# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-One — What Happened to Them

The second Intermediate Archive container held the departure records.

He had not been looking for them specifically. The container's label indicated it held the general student departure archive — final assessments, noted trajectories, known outcomes. He read through it in the same methodical order as the student logs.

Most of the departed human students had returned to the surface. Their departure records noted direction and approximate intent: *returning to Iron Meridian Court with corrected technique framework* or *proceeding to surface life, cultivation practice independent.* These were the practitioners who had come, studied, and left with improved capability.

Some had stayed. The records indicated *ongoing study* or *permanent residency status* for a subset of students across the six hundred years. They had chosen the Strata over the surface — not hiding, apparently, simply finding that the archive offered more than the surface did, and staying.

And some — six, across the full span of the records — had neither returned to the surface nor remained in the outer archive.

They had left through the Veritonal network.

The departure records for these six were unlike the others. More careful. The notation indicated — and here his communication register had to work at near-full fluency to parse the specificity — that the Veritonal network had routes beyond the Earth-local nodes. That the underground network he had been learning to read and repair was one level of a tiered system that extended considerably further out.

That six human students, across six hundred years, had chosen to use those routes.

He wrote:

> *Departure records: six human students left through the Veritonal network's extended routes. Not the local Under-Web. Something beyond.*
>
> *What I know about the Veritonal network: local = Under-Web, node-tributary structure. Station monitoring network = global, Tibetan Witness Node connected. Beyond that: the Galactic Collective's monitoring network, implied by the array ping.*
>
> *Six people walked into the network and didn't come back.*
>
> *Not lost — left. Chose to use routes that go beyond this planet's local network.*
>
> *The departure records don't say where they went. They say: the network's extended routes were accessed, departure confirmed, no return signal.*
>
> *This is foreshadowing in the archive's own record — these practitioners are somewhere in the network that the monitoring array's ping was sent to.*
>
> *I don't know if they arrived anywhere. I don't know if the network beyond Earth is still operational at the level that would be needed.*
>
> *But they went.*

He held the container for a moment before closing it.

Six people, across six hundred years of Amber Lattice School records, who had decided that what was on offer was not limited to Earth.

He thought about the status ping. About what might receive it.

He thought about whether, eventually, he would know where they had gone.

He put the container aside.

He opened the third container.

Historical records from the School's later period — the two hundred years before the disruption Meen-4 had mentioned. The period that included Ren Wuji's departure.

He read carefully.

The records became more sparse as they approached the disruption period. The archival notes had the quality of records that had been partially preserved from something that had reduced the overall volume significantly. There were gaps — not in the sense of missing pages but in the sense of sequences that ended abruptly.

Near the end: a note in notation he recognized as Meen-4's archival style. Not historical record — an archivist's note added after the fact, explaining a gap:

*Records from this period were partially lost during the School's closure. The closure itself was not documented in the surviving records. The following entries represent what was recovered.*

He turned to the next page.

Ren Wuji's departure record, continued from where the first container had left it. But with an addition he hadn't seen in the first container — a notation marked *Archivist's supplement, added 200 years post-event:*

*The student left the School in the year corresponding to [notation he needed to translate, approximately 1620 CE]. His departure followed the School's closure and was precipitated by events during that period. His specific trajectory post-departure is unknown to the archive.*

He sat with *events during that period* for a long time.

The School had closed. Ren Wuji had left.

He had then spent, apparently, approximately four hundred years in isolation on the surface, building the Codex.

What had happened in the School's closure that had taken everything and left only one practitioner walking away with the knowledge?

He thought about Meen-4's careful word: *disrupted.*

He wrote in his notebook: *The Amber Lattice School's closure: not documented in surviving records. Events during that period caused Ren Wuji's departure. He spent 400 years alone after.*

He added: *Ask Axis-7. Not Meen-4. Axis-7 was here 180 years ago, which puts her start at approximately 1840. The School's closure was 1620. She may not have direct knowledge. But she knows more than the archive says.*

He closed the third container.

He looked at the fourth container, which held the foundational sections of the Intermediate Archive's cultivation curriculum.

He would read it tomorrow.

Tonight he needed to sit with what he'd found.
