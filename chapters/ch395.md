# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 395 — Six Months In — Status

He had started calling it, privately, "six months in," though when he actually sat down to count the days on the calendar pinned above his mother's kitchen counter, the number came out closer to eight weeks. It surprised him enough that he mentioned it to Meen-4 the next morning, half expecting her to correct his math.

"It's not a miscalculation," she said. "You are simply used to institutional processes that move at human speed. This one has not."

"Seven months on the clock when the Surveyor first arrived," he said slowly, counting backward. "The formal accounting, the presentation, the counter-submission, the framing vote, the direct interface — that's not seven months of process. That's barely two."

"No," Meen-4 agreed. "It is not."

"Is that normal?"

She was quiet long enough that he understood the answer mattered more than a simple yes or no could carry. "I told you, weeks ago, that I had never seen a first session go this smoothly, or this coldly, and that I could not yet tell you which mattered more. I want to revise that now. What I can tell you, with confidence, is that a review moving at this pace, with this much substantive progress this early, has no precedent in any record I hold. Reviews of this magnitude — sterilization on the table, a zone's entire future weighed — typically extend across years, sometimes decades of Collective time, even accounting for the acceleration interstellar Veritonal transmission allows compared to older methods."

"Then why is this one moving so fast?"

"Because your case is unusually strong, and unusually well-documented, and because the Veth-Karun analysis has apparently generated genuine interest across a body that does not often find genuine interest in anything," Meen-4 said. "I want to be careful here, Lin Mushi, because speed is not automatically good news. A review can move quickly toward a swift, decisive verdict in either direction. But I have never once seen a review accelerate this dramatically toward a negative outcome. Institutions that intend to say no tend to take their time saying it, because the finality of that answer does not require haste. Institutions moving this fast are usually moving toward something they find genuinely compelling enough not to want to delay."

He sat with that, turning over the strange shape of hope it offered — cautious, provisional, but real. "So where does that leave the actual status? If someone asked you right now, plainly, how is Earth doing?"

"Orthodoxy influence is declining, measurably, session by session. The framing vote went against them by a substantial majority. The Surveyor's classification language has shifted — I want to show you this precisely, because the wording matters more than it might appear to." She pulled up the most recent formal notation, the term buried in a subordinate clause he had read past twice without registering its significance. "It has begun referring to this proceeding, internally, not as a 'Zone Review' but as a 'Zone Evaluation with Integration Consideration.'"

"What's the difference?"

"A Review determines a zone's fate. Binary. Continue or cease. An Evaluation with Integration Consideration is a different category of process entirely — it considers not merely whether a zone should be permitted to continue existing, but what its future relationship with the Collective might actually look like, assuming it does. The shift in terminology is not accidental. Nothing in the Surveyor's formal language is accidental."

He absorbed that slowly, feeling the shape of it settle into something he had not quite let himself hope for directly until this moment — not merely survival, but the faint outline of something beyond survival.

"That's better," he said.

"It is considerably better," Meen-4 confirmed. "It does not mean the outcome is decided. The Orthodoxy remains a formally registered party with standing to continue objecting through every remaining stage. But I would be doing you a disservice if I did not tell you plainly: this review is moving faster, and trending more favorably, than any precedent I have access to would have predicted eight weeks ago." She paused, and something in her stillness carried an unusual weight. "I do not know entirely what to make of that. I have spent centuries studying Collective process, and I find myself, for the first time in longer than I can easily measure, genuinely uncertain what happens next. I want you to know that uncertainty is not fear. It is closer, I think, to the same cautious hope you are currently trying not to let yourself fully feel."

He allowed himself a small, tired smile at that — the first in weeks that didn't feel like it cost him something to produce. "Then we're both cautiously hoping at the same time. That's new."

"It is," Meen-4 said. "I find I do not entirely dislike it."
