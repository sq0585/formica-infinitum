# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty-Three — What Meen-4 Says About This

He brought the unanswered question to Meen-4 the next morning, the careful notebook entry from the night before serving as the basis for a conversation he understood, even before starting it, was likely to carry more weight than most of their exchanges.

She listened to his description of the eighty-kilometer range, the texture he could no longer always distinguish from his own feelings, the honest uncertainty about whether the expansion had any natural ceiling at all.

*This is a known pattern,* she signaled, once he'd finished. *Every practitioner in Formican historical record who achieved Colony Link expansion above city-scale required an Anchor network to maintain individual coherence through it. Without sufficient Anchors, the expansion continues unchecked until the individual's distinct selfhood is absorbed entirely into the collective field they're perceiving.*

He sat with this, the cold clarity of the statement landing with considerably more weight than he'd braced for.

"Absorbed how?"

*Gradually, at first. The Carrier begins losing the ability to distinguish their own emotional state from the field's. This is the stage you described experiencing already — surfacing from a session uncertain which feelings belong to you. Left unaddressed, the boundary continues eroding. Eventually, the Carrier's distinct identity dissolves entirely into the broader field, indistinguishable from the collective awareness they were once merely perceiving.*

"That's permanent."

*Yes. The Formican historical record contains several documented cases. None reversed. The selfhood, once fully absorbed, does not reconstitute.*

He felt the particular cold weight of mortality reframed into something stranger than death — not an ending, but a dissolution, the self simply ceasing to exist as a distinct thing while the body, presumably, continued on, animated by something no longer recognizable as the person who had once inhabited it.

"Does this have a name?" he asked, understanding instinctively that something this documented in Formican history would.

*We called it the Dissolution Path,* Meen-4 signaled. *Not a punishment. Not a failure exactly. Simply the predictable outcome of expansion proceeding faster than the structural support required to hold it. Several of our own most accomplished practitioners, across our civilization's history, walked this path — some deliberately, believing dissolution into a larger collective represented a kind of transcendence rather than a loss. Others arrived there through simple neglect, the same way you nearly did, without intending to.*

He sat with the full weight of this, the careful, methodical part of his mind already working through the implications even as a deeper, more unsettled part of him simply needed a moment to absorb what he'd just learned.

"How many Anchors prevent this?" he asked. "Is there a known threshold?"

*The historical record is not precise on this point — individual variation matters considerably, and the relationship between Anchor count and structural stability is not strictly linear. But the pattern across documented cases suggests that three to five committed, properly bonded Anchors provide sufficient stability for Colony Link expansion considerably beyond planetary scale. Fewer than three, and the risk increases substantially the further the expansion proceeds.*

"I currently have two."

*You currently have two,* she confirmed. *And an expansion that has already reached eighty kilometers and shows no indication of naturally ceasing. I would not say this to alarm you unnecessarily. I would say this because you asked the question honestly, and you deserve an honest answer, regardless of how unwelcome the implications.*

He thought of Dr. Shen Yue, of the careful consideration he'd promised himself he'd give her relationship to this world time to develop properly before raising the Anchor question.

He understood, sitting with Meen-4's careful, weighted explanation, that the timeline he'd imagined might need to move considerably faster than he'd originally planned.

"Thank you," he said. "For explaining this clearly, even though I suspect it would have been easier for you to simply let me continue not fully understanding the risk."

*I do not believe in protecting you from understanding,* Meen-4 signaled. *I believe in protecting you from understanding before you were ready to carry it. You are ready now. You asked the right question, at the right time, the way you have learned to do across nearly two years of careful, patient growth.*

He nodded, the weight of the Dissolution Path settling into his understanding alongside every other planetary-scale obligation this strange world had handed him across the past months, and began, with the same careful, methodical discipline he brought to everything, the work of deciding exactly how soon he needed to ask Dr. Shen Yue the question he'd been planning to give considerably more time before raising.
