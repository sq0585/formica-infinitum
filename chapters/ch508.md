# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 508 — What It Is

He spent the next while — he genuinely could not have said how long, and found, testing the thought, that the not-knowing did not trouble him the way it once might have — simply existing inside what he had become, the way a person spends the first morning in a new house walking from room to room, touching things, learning where the light fell.

The solar system was as perceivable to him now as the laboratory he sat in. He could turn his attention from the specific grain of the cushion beneath him to the slow orbital patience of a planet he had never physically visited, and the turning cost him nothing — no gathering, no reaching, no sense of traveling any distance at all, because at this scale, he was beginning to understand, distance itself had become one more local feature of a landscape he now stood entirely outside of.

The Void Lattice's structure was as clear to him as his own hand. He could see — the word was inadequate, every word available to him was inadequate, but it was the closest one he had — the fine, ancient weave of it underneath everything, underneath the solar system, underneath the room, underneath the specific worn grain of the notebook page in front of him, all of it the same fabric at a scale most of the universe had never had cause to notice.

He was in the dissolution zone. He was, simultaneously, entirely present in the pocket dimension laboratory, watching Wei Canglan's hands move restlessly against a folder he had, characteristically, brought and not opened.

He could think, if he chose to, at the speed of Veritonal propagation — thoughts arriving and resolving faster than the concept of sequence generally allowed for. He could also, he discovered, testing it carefully, simply wait. He set a cup of tea beside him, poured fresh this time by Chen Yufei's careful hand, and watched it cool at its own patient rate, and found that watching it cool required exactly the same quality of attention it always had. Nothing about Formless Scale made the tea cool faster. Nothing about Formless Scale made him impatient for it to.

Both were true. Both, he understood now, sitting with the strangeness of it, would remain true for as long as he chose to hold them both — not in conflict, not one waiting to overtake the other the moment his attention wandered, but simply, permanently, coexistent, the way the molecular hush of a cushion and the whole weather of Chengdu had once, five years ago, seemed impossible to hold at the same time and now barely registered as separate.

*Observation 508,* he wrote, slower now, more careful, because he understood he was trying to describe something that no human vocabulary had ever been built to hold. *What Formless Scale actually is, from the inside, is not a state of being infinite. It is a state of being exactly as finite as I have always been, in every particular way that matters — still a person who drinks tea, still curious, still fond of a specific handful of people — while simultaneously perceiving a canvas that has no edges I have yet found. I do not think these two facts are in tension. I think, and I say this cautiously, because I have been wrong about large things before, that they were never actually two facts. I think they were always one fact, and I simply lacked the scale to see it clearly until now.*

He looked up, and found Dr. Shen Yue watching him with the specific stillness of someone waiting for a number rather than a feeling, and gave her both.

"I'm still me," he said. "Entirely. I want that on the record before you tell me what the instruments say."

"Noted," she said, and something in her posture eased, fractionally, the particular unclenching of a scientist who had spent months building a warning system she'd sincerely hoped she would never need to use in earnest. "The instruments agree with you."

"What's the number?"

She looked at the readout a moment longer than the answer strictly required. "I'd rather show you together. Everyone's here for it."

He nodded, and felt, for the first time since the technique had begun, something that was not perception at all — a small, specific, entirely ordinary anticipation, the kind a person feels waiting for a test result they already privately suspect is good news, and wants anyway to hear said aloud, in the room, by people who mattered enough to be the ones saying it.
