# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 492 — Month 54 — Inventory Before the End

He took the inventory on a Sunday, the way he'd taken every inventory before it, sitting alone at the small table above the noodle shop with the notebook open in front of him and a cup of tea going cold beside it, the same quiet discipline he'd first built in the disoriented early weeks after Ren Wuji's death, still holding, still faithful, four and a half years later.

Month fifty-four. He wrote the number first, the way he always did, because everything else in the entry only made sense measured against it — against the specific, ordinary fact that all of this, the Strata and the Colony Link and the three hundred and twenty-eight people now extended alongside him into the largest wound he knew of in the galaxy, had grown out of a convenience store shift that had ended in a stranger's dying hand closing around his own.

He worked through the categories methodically, resisting, as always, the urge to let any single entry run longer than the facts required.

*Stellar Scale: mature.* The solar system no longer an outer boundary but an interior structure, the nearest stars faintly, steadily perceivable, comprehension no longer a gap he was straining to close but a foundation he now stood on comfortably.

*Colony Link: stellar scale.* Extended, node by node, through the dissolution zone's own vast territory, anchored directly against forty million years of accumulated entropy, holding steady, self-sustaining, considerably larger than anything he'd built before it.

*Anchors: five, plus three hundred and twenty-three voluntary dissolution-zone anchors.* He wrote the distinction carefully, the way Meen-4 had taught him to hold it — Chen Yufei, Liang Jingzhi, Dr. Shen Yue, Wei Canglan, Meen-4, the same five who had carried him through every threshold since Colony Link itself, and beside them now, distinct in scale and in kind but no less genuinely given, three hundred and twenty-three people who had chosen, knowingly, to become part of something this large. He added a line beneath the count that fit no category cleanly: *Liang Jingzhi, also, permanently, at the dissolution zone's center — not one of the three hundred and twenty-three, not simply one of the five either anymore. Something the notebook doesn't yet have adequate language for.*

*Strata population: 4,200.* A number that would have seemed impossible five years ago, now simply the steady, accumulating shape of a community that had survived contact with an entire galactic institution and kept, through all of it, quarreling over tool storage and welcoming new children into its residential wing.

*Collective membership: 380.* The Cultivation Research Collective, grown considerably beyond its original modest founding, now the accumulated shape of Fang Ruxue's years of careful, patient governance.

*Chitin Circuit global network: 3,200 nodes, plus off-Earth relay.* Amber-0's steady, patient extension, reaching now from Earth's own surface out through the solar system and into the stellar neighborhood beyond it.

*Dissolution zone repair: 67% complete.* He held that number a long moment before writing the next line, understanding its particular weight — not finished, not yet, Meen-4's own careful caution still holding, but considerably further along than the cautious early weeks had allowed him to hope for.

*Void Lattice coherence in the zone: improving at a measurable rate, independently verified, no longer requiring faith alone to believe in.*

*Formless Scale: ready when he is.*

He wrote that final line slowly, feeling its weight settle into the entry the way every genuine threshold's approach had settled, across five years of careful, patient documentation. He had the mechanism now — Veth-Karun's account, translated at real cost, confirming the exact safeguard he'd built years before he understood why it mattered. He had five people who had said yes before he'd finished asking, and three hundred and twenty-three more who had chosen, knowingly, to extend themselves alongside him. He had a mother who finally knew everything, and a Sunday ritual unburdened, at last, of every careful omission that had once quietly governed it.

*Four and a half years,* he wrote, closing the entry the way he closed every inventory that mattered most — plainly, without ornament. *Since a man died in a convenience store and left a boy who worked there considerably more curious than prepared. I am still curious. I believe, now, that I am finally also prepared.*

He closed the notebook, finished the tea he hadn't drunk, and sat for a long while in the quiet Sunday light, feeling the particular, specific weight of a threshold close enough now to actually see the shape of, waiting, patiently, for him to finally, deliberately, choose to cross it.
