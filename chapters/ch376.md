# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 376 — The Surveyor's First Session

The formal review began on a morning that looked, from the outside, like every other morning that season — grey sky over Chengdu threatening rain it never quite delivered, the Strata humming along beneath the surface exactly as it had for weeks. Lin Mushi had expected, somehow, that a moment this large would announce itself with more ceremony. It didn't. It arrived as a scheduling notice through the Witness Node, precise to the minute, and then, at that minute, it simply began.

He sat alone in the Archive's central chamber for this — Meen-4 present but silent beside him, translating in real time but under strict instruction not to answer on his behalf unless he specifically asked her to. *This is your review,* she had told him the night before. *Not mine. Not the Collective's. I will be your interpreter. I will not be your voice.*

The Surveyor's first question arrived in formal notation, precise and unhurried, and Meen-4 rendered it for him in a low, careful murmur.

*A full accounting is requested of all dimensional activity within Zone QZ-7 since the reactivation of designation Node-17.*

He had prepared for this. He had, in fact, prepared for almost nothing else for the better part of a week, running through every event, every decision, every consequence he could remember since the day he'd first opened Ren Wuji's Codex and felt the world quietly widen underneath him. But preparation and the actual weight of the moment were different things, and he felt that difference now, sitting in a chamber built by a civilization forty million years dead, answering to something that represented civilizations still very much alive.

He began at the beginning, because he could think of no other honest way to begin.

He told it about Node-17's reactivation, about the Codex, about the three years of accumulation that had followed — the Under-Web, the Dissolution Path, the Pacific Ring's stabilization, the Strata's growth from a single hidden chamber to a city of twelve hundred people who had chosen, in one way or another, to build a life inside something extraordinary. He told it about Amber-0, about the Colony Link, about the five Anchors, though he found himself, in the moment, following Meen-4's earlier restraint and leaving her own status unspoken — not a lie, simply an omission he trusted, without needing to ask her, that she would want preserved a while longer.

He told it, too, about the mistakes. The six weeks lost stabilizing nodes he had himself destabilized through strategic overreach. The consequences that had rippled through people who had never asked to be caught in them — Wei Canglan's family, the Court factions torn apart by information he'd released without fully weighing what it would cost the people inside those institutions. He did not soften any of it. Meen-4 had warned him, and Dr. Shen Yue's instinct from weeks earlier echoed in his memory as he spoke — *a civilization that shows you its mistakes and how it fixed them is asking you to trust a pattern.*

The Surveyor listened — if listening was even the right word for whatever process was actually occurring on the other end of the Witness Node's channel — without interruption, for nearly three hours. No questions clarifying his account. No visible reaction of any kind that Meen-4's translation could detect. Just a vast, patient attention, absorbing everything he offered it with the same unhurried precision that had characterized every message it had sent so far.

When he finished, there was a silence long enough that he began to wonder if the session had simply ended without ceremony, the way it had begun.

Then the reply came, brief, and utterly without inflection that Meen-4's translation could render as anything but neutral.

*Accounting received. Complete, as far as verification against independent Collective monitoring data confirms. This is noted.*

That was all. No judgment. No indication of whether completeness counted in his favor or simply against a lower bar than he'd feared. He sat with the flatness of it for a long moment, feeling the particular exhaustion that came not from physical effort but from having held three years of his own life up to something ancient and unreadable and simply hoped it was enough.

"How did I do," he asked Meen-4, once the channel had gone quiet.

"I don't know," she admitted, and there was something almost gentle in the admission, an honesty she didn't have to offer him but chose to anyway. "I have never seen a first session go this smoothly, or this coldly. Both of those things can be true at once, and I cannot yet tell you which one matters more."

He nodded, and looked toward the chamber's ribbed ceiling, thinking of Ren Wuji, thinking of everything three years had built and cost, and found, underneath the exhaustion, something that was not quite relief but was, at least, the particular calm of having told the whole truth to something that would have known anyway if he hadn't.

"Then we wait," he said, "and we keep being exactly what we told it we are."
