# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 368 — The Opening Submission

The Surveyor's second transmission arrived four days later, and it was, in its entirety, a single request: a formal Opening Submission, documenting Zone QZ-7's current state, to be provided at whatever length the zone's point of contact considered adequate, before formal review sessions could commence.

"Adequate," Lin Mushi repeated, reading it over Fang Ruxue's shoulder in the Collective's administrative chamber, a room that had, over the past year, filled up with the ordinary clutter of an actual functioning institution — stacked reports, a whiteboard someone had never fully erased, a kettle that saw more use than anything else in the building. "That's not a number. That's a trap disguised as generosity."

"It's not a trap," Fang Ruxue said, not looking up from the outline she was already sketching. Two years into leading the Collective and she had developed the particular calm of someone who had learned to treat institutional demands as puzzles rather than threats — a calm Lin Mushi envied more than he let on. "It's a test of judgment. They want to see what you consider adequate. That tells them something about you before you've said a single sentence about Earth."

Dr. Shen Yue, folded into the chair by the window with a laptop balanced on her knees, made a small sound of agreement without looking up either. "Underwrite it," she said. "Everything you're not certain matters, include anyway, with a note explaining why you included it despite uncertainty. Let them see you thinking, not just concluding."

Liang Jingzhi, who had joined them an hour earlier straight from the geology department with chalk dust still on one sleeve, was the one who said what Lin Mushi had been quietly worrying about since the request arrived. "How long do we have?"

"They didn't say," he admitted.

"Then we work like we don't have long," she said, "and we stop when it's actually finished, not when we're tired of it."

It took two weeks.

He had underestimated, at the start, how much of Earth's three years of accumulated history — his three years — would need to be rendered into Formican notation with enough precision that the Surveyor's consensus body, drawn from civilizations that had never set foot on this planet, could read it and understand not just the facts but their weight. Meen-4 supervised the translation work directly, correcting his phrasing dozens of times a day, occasionally rewriting entire sections herself when his notation drifted toward something too colored by hope or too colored by fear to remain useful as evidence.

They documented the Strata City's growth from a single chamber to twelve hundred residents. They documented the Chitin Circuit network's eight hundred and forty nodes and Amber-0's management of them, with an honest accounting of the unauthorized contact risk Meen-4 insisted be flagged rather than buried. They documented the Pacific Ring's stabilization work, including the six weeks he'd lost to emergency node repair after his own strategic choices had nearly caused a collapse — Dr. Shen Yue had argued hardest for including that failure, and won. *A civilization that only shows you its successes is asking you to trust a story,* she'd said. *A civilization that shows you its mistakes and how it fixed them is asking you to trust a pattern. Patterns are harder to fake.*

They documented the Colony Link, the five Anchors, though Meen-4 quietly excluded any explicit reference to her own status among them, and he did not question the omission — some things, apparently, even a formal review did not need to know in full.

Three hundred and forty pages, in the end. Two weeks of work compressed into notation dense enough that a fluent Formican reader could parse it in under an hour, though Lin Mushi suspected the Surveyor's consensus body would take considerably longer, weighing every claim against precedent from forty-seven other zones he knew almost nothing about.

He sent it through the Witness Node at 2 AM, alone, because it felt like the kind of thing that should be sent by one person even though a dozen had built it.

The acknowledgment came back within forty-eight hours — fast, by the standards he was learning to expect, and utterly without comment on content. *Opening Submission received. Under review. Further contact will follow.*

He read the four sentences three times, looking for something underneath them, and found nothing but exactly what they said.

"That's it?" Wei Canglan asked, when Lin Mushi showed him the reply the next morning over breakfast Wei Canglan had, without comment, started making enough of for two.

"That's it."

"No reaction. No score. Nothing."

"Not yet." Lin Mushi picked up his chopsticks, and found, to his own mild surprise, that after two weeks of dense notation and sleepless translation, plain rice and plain vegetables tasted like the most honest thing he'd encountered in days. "I think that's the part I need to get used to. It's going to look at us for a long time before it tells us anything about what it saw."

Wei Canglan grunted, which from him counted as sympathy, and refilled Lin Mushi's tea without being asked.
