# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifteen — The Tibetan Witness Node, Active

He returned to the Witness Node's texture two days later, once the initial exhaustion of the Planetary Architect emergence had settled into something manageable, choosing a quiet morning to give the investigation the careful attention it deserved.

He found Meen-4 first, wanting her present for whatever this turned out to be.

"There's something different about the node in Tibet," he said. "I noticed it during the first survey, but I was too overwhelmed to look closely. I want to look closely now."

*I will accompany you,* she signaled, something careful entering her tone that he registered but didn't yet fully understand.

He extended his attention toward the site, the specific location resolving with a clarity his newly-expanded sensing made almost effortless — a structure embedded deep beneath the Tibetan plateau, ancient, Formican in its underlying architecture but distinct, somehow, from every other node in the network.

"It's not just transmitting," he said slowly, working through the texture with careful attention. "Everything else in the Under-Web reads as output — Circuit nodes sending status, Strata locations projecting their quiet ambient presence. This is doing that too. But it's also — "

He stopped, the realization arriving fully formed.

"It's receiving something."

*Yes,* Meen-4 signaled.

He pushed further into the sensation, and found, beneath the node's outward transmission, a second layer he hadn't initially separated from the first — something arriving, not originating from Earth at all, a faint, steady inbound current that had clearly been present for some time, its rhythm too regular and too sustained to be anything other than deliberate.

"How long has this been active?" he asked.

*I do not have precise data,* Meen-4 signaled. *My own monitoring of the Witness Node has been passive for some time — its function predates my tenure as Keeper by a considerable margin, and I have treated its continued operation as background fact rather than something requiring active attention.* A pause, weighted. *Based on what I can now perceive alongside you, I would estimate the inbound signal has been present for approximately three years.*

He felt the number land with specific, uncomfortable weight. Three years. Longer than his entire relationship with this impossible world, longer than every threshold he'd crossed, every relationship he'd built, every careful choice he'd made about what to build and how to build it — something had been arriving, quietly, into a node buried beneath a mountain range, and no one, apparently, had noticed until his own cultivation had finally grown large enough to perceive it directly.

---

"What does the node do with what it receives?" he asked.

*I do not know,* Meen-4 admitted, and something in the admission carried a rare, genuine uncertainty he hadn't often heard from her. *The Witness Node's designated function, as recorded in the general Archive, is observational — it exists to watch, and, apparently, to be watched in turn. I did not previously understand the second half of that function to be active. I understood the node as a historical fixture. Something built, long ago, for a purpose that had since lapsed into dormancy.*

"It's not dormant."

*No. It has, apparently, not been dormant for at least three years, and I did not know.* She was quiet for a long moment, the stillness carrying more weight than words might have. *This requires deeper investigation than either of us has yet given it. I would like to bring this to Axis-7. Her knowledge of the node's original construction predates my own considerably.*

He nodded, understanding, even without full information, that they had found something significant — not a threat exactly, nothing in the signal's texture read as hostile or urgent, but something patient, deliberate, and entirely outside anything he'd previously understood about the shape of the world he'd inherited.

---

He found Chen Yufei that evening, the day's discovery still sitting heavily in his chest, and told her everything, carefully, the way he'd learned to tell her the things that mattered most.

"Someone's answering it," she said, once he'd finished. "Whatever the node's original purpose was, something out there has been responding for three years."

"Yes."

"Do you know what that means?"

"No," he admitted. "Meen-4 doesn't either, not fully. She's bringing it to Axis-7. I think — " he paused, choosing the next words carefully, because they mattered. "I think we've just found something that's been watching this planet for a while now, and I don't yet know whether that's something to be afraid of or something else entirely."

She reached for his hand, the same steady gesture that had carried him through the Planetary Architect emergence days earlier, and he held on, grounding himself in the small, specific reality of her fingers against his while, somewhere beneath a mountain range on the other side of the country, a signal that had been arriving patiently for three years continued, unbothered by his discovery of it, to simply keep coming.
