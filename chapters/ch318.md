# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Eighteen — The Colony Link, Planetary

The full inventory took a week to compile properly, Lin Mushi working methodically through the planetary field's contents with the same patient discipline Liang Jingzhi had spent six weeks teaching him, refusing to let the sheer scale of it push him into hasty, incomplete conclusions.

What he found, once the survey settled into something reliable, was larger than he'd expected even after the initial overwhelming exposure weeks earlier.

The Collective — his Collective, human and Formican, the naming overlap with the vast body now watching from somewhere beyond Earth's boundary still sitting uncomfortably in his chest every time he used the word — had grown to one hundred and eighteen members, the laboratory's expanded capacity and Fang Ruxue's steady governance drawing in researchers faster than he'd fully tracked amid everything else demanding his attention.

The residential wing's population had climbed to three hundred and eighty, families and individuals continuing to settle into spaces built, still, around the same principle the elder Mrs. Tan's room had first established — comfort and dignity before spectacle, homes rather than monuments. The wider city Fang Ruxue's team was still assembling around it — the workshop district, the laboratory's expansion, the Formican section Meen-4 and Axis-7 had helped design — remained, for now, under construction, its own population still to come.

And beyond both of those familiar numbers, the planetary field itself: two hundred thousand Formicans, scattered across the world in patterns he was only beginning to fully map, their presence registering in his expanded sensing as a faint, distant texture, distinct from the human field but woven through it in places where Strata locations sat close enough to population centers to blend the two together.

He held all of it at once now, nightly, the Anchor Protocol he'd begun deliberately practicing — active, conscious connection to Chen Yufei, Liang Jingzhi, and Dr. Shen Yue simultaneously, rather than the looser, more passive awareness he'd previously relied on — becoming, over the course of that week, not an occasional discipline but his actual daily baseline, the steady structural foundation beneath everything else the planetary field asked him to carry.

---

"You're doing it every day now," Chen Yufei observed, one evening, watching him settle into the familiar posture of the practice — reaching deliberately toward her, toward Liang Jingzhi, toward Dr. Shen Yue, holding the three connections steady and conscious rather than letting them remain the background hum they'd previously been.

"I have to," he said. "The field's too large now to hold safely without it. Meen-4's warnings about the Dissolution Path used to feel theoretical. They don't anymore."

"How does it feel? Doing this every day, rather than only during crisis?"

He considered the question honestly, testing his own sense of himself against the three steady points of connection. "Grounding. Genuinely. I thought it might feel like a burden, one more discipline on top of everything else. Instead it feels like the opposite — like these three connections are the reason everything else stays manageable rather than becoming one more thing to manage."

She smiled at that, something quietly pleased in her expression, though he also caught, beneath it, a faint trace of the same watchfulness she'd carried since the pauses had first become visible weeks earlier.

"Is it working?" she asked. "The pauses — are they better, with this as a daily practice?"

He checked honestly, running the same accounting she'd taught him to run. "Better. Not gone. But better. I think holding you deliberately, every day, rather than only reaching for you when something else demands my attention, has changed the shape of it somewhat."

"That's something," she said. "I'll take something."

---

He brought the full inventory to Fang Ruxue and Meen-4 the following morning, the numbers laid out plainly: one hundred and eighteen Collective members, three hundred and eighty residents in the growing residential wing, two hundred thousand Formicans sensed across the planet, the Pacific Ring's twelve nodes stable and monitored, the Tibetan Witness Node's inbound signal continuing, unbroken, its patient rhythm unchanged since the discovery three weeks earlier.

"This is a considerably larger world than the one we started planning the city around," Fang Ruxue observed, reviewing the numbers with her usual careful precision.

"It's a considerably larger world than the one I started this whole journey in," he said. "I don't think I fully appreciated, until this week, exactly how large it had actually become."

*The scale is accurate,* Meen-4 confirmed, cross-referencing his figures against her own records. *I would note one thing. The Collective membership figure — one hundred and eighteen — has grown steadily but not dramatically since your last formal count. I would encourage continued growth, deliberately, rather than allowing the pace to stall now that other priorities are demanding more of your attention.*

"I'll make sure Fang Ruxue's recruitment efforts keep pace," he said, and Fang Ruxue nodded, already making the note.

He sat with the full weight of the inventory for a while after the meeting ended, alone, the planetary field humming steadily at the edge of his awareness — vast, layered, held now through a daily discipline he'd built specifically because he'd finally understood, fully, what it cost to hold something this large without deliberate, structural support.

He wrote it into his notebook that evening, brief, the way his most important entries had increasingly become.

> *Anchor Protocol, daily now, not occasional. One hundred and eighteen in the Collective. Three hundred eighty in the residential wing, with the wider city still rising around it. Two hundred thousand Formicans across a planet I can finally, actually feel in full. Something is watching all of it. I don't know yet what that means. I know that holding this steadily, every single day, is the only way I have any chance of meeting whatever comes next as myself, rather than as something the scale has quietly dissolved.*
