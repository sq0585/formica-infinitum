# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Thirty-Two — The Node Goes Quiet

He noticed it on a Wednesday.

The filament network beneath the store had always run with what he'd come to think of as a characteristic brightness — the ambient luminosity of a tributary under moderate flow. Not brilliant, not the dense concentration he'd perceived at the main current, but present and consistent, a reliable background that he'd stopped consciously noting because it was always there.

On Wednesday it was dimmer.

Not critically dimmer. Not extinguished. But the drop was visible in a way it hadn't been before — the way a lamp's dimming is invisible until it suddenly isn't, the change having been gradual until the eye adjusted enough to register the cumulative effect.

He stood at the counter at 1:30 AM with no customers in the store and measured what he was perceiving against his memory of how it had looked on Day 17, when the overlay had first come in and the filaments had been new to him. He had drawn them then, in the spatial survey notebook, with a particular density of line to indicate their relative brightness.

He opened the notebook. He compared.

The filaments were measurably dimmer.

---

He had documented the battery anomaly. He had documented the depletion rate in his energy dynamics notebook, noting that it was slow but real, noting that an external monitoring system might detect it, noting that he had been drawing from the node for six weeks without fully understanding what that meant for the node's long-term capacity.

He had not put a number on it. Now he did.

He sat in the stockroom on his break and estimated, as carefully as he could, the ratio between what he'd perceived on Day 17 and what he was perceiving now. It was not a scientific measurement; he had no calibration standard and no instrument. But he was the instrument, and after six weeks of practice he trusted his calibration more than he had.

Roughly sixty percent. The node was at roughly sixty percent of the capacity he'd observed on Day 17 — but Day 17 was itself not a true baseline. The depletion had almost certainly begun the moment the man died and the Codex transferred. Whatever the node's original capacity had been, it was higher than what he'd first measured.

He didn't know what the original figure was. He might never know. What he could do was track the rate from Day 17 forward and extrapolate.

Approximately three to four percent per week, based on six weeks of observation.

---

He wrote in the energy dynamics notebook:

> *Node capacity estimate: ~60% of Day 17 baseline. Day 17 baseline: not original capacity — some depletion had already occurred before I could perceive the network. Original capacity: unknown, likely 15–25% higher than Day 17 reading.*
>
> *Depletion trend: continuing, rate approximately 3–4% per week by rough observation.*
>
> *Consequence: at current rate, full collapse would occur in approximately 15–20 weeks from now. But the rate is not fixed. The coffee-buying practitioner's own scanning adds a small draw each visit. If his visits increase in frequency, or if a senior investigator is deployed with wider-range perception, the draw accelerates.*
>
> *The coffee-buyer appears to be a junior observer — he documents, he reports, he does not act independently. He is building a case for someone above him in whatever hierarchy sent him. That senior figure has not yet arrived. When they do, their own perception will interact with the node's field and the depletion rate will increase.*
>
> *This is the mechanism by which the four-month estimate could be accurate even as my own rate estimates seem faster: the final acceleration comes from the investigator's own presence, not just from my usage.*
>
> *Revised timeline estimate: eight weeks until external discovery becomes highly probable under current conditions. If a senior investigator is deployed, that window shrinks sharply.*

He added a final note:

> *Across the city tonight, someone is looking at the coffee-buyer's reports and noting a consistent decline in node output. This person will draw a conclusion: whatever is causing the depletion is either accelerating its activity or the node is entering terminal decline.*
>
> *Either conclusion leads to the same action: escalate. Send someone with more capability.*
>
> *I need to be findable on my own terms before I am found on theirs.*

He read this back. He starred it.

He didn't know yet how to execute on it. But the problem had changed shape, and he needed to think about the new shape.

He went back to the counter. The fluorescent light hummed. The filaments ran dimmer than yesterday.

He started thinking.
