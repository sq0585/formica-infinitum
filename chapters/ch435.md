# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 435 — The Message

The translation resolved slowly, the way the oldest notation always did, each symbol yielding its meaning only after several minutes of patient cross-reference against everything Meen-4 had taught him and everything the installation's own archive offered by way of context. He worked through it the same way he worked through everything difficult: without rushing, without assuming, testing each interpretation against the ones around it before trusting any single piece in isolation.

When it finally resolved into something he could read as a whole, he sat with it a long moment before writing a single word of his own.

*To whoever arrives,* it began, and something in the phrasing — deliberately open, addressed to no one in particular and therefore, somehow, to him specifically — settled into his chest with a weight he hadn't expected from four words.

*If you have reached this place, you are ready to see what we saw. The distance alone confirms it — nothing unprepared survives the reaching. We do not know who you will be, or what civilization sends you, or how long from now you will stand where we once stood. We know only that you arrived, and that arrival is itself the qualification we could not otherwise verify.*

*The stellar records are organized by classification, oldest first. Begin with the standard G-type field — our own system's star, and the pattern from which every stellar comprehension we achieved was ultimately built. Study it fully before proceeding. What follows will not make sense without that foundation.*

*End with the anomaly at solar position 7.4-N.*

*You will understand why we left when you see what is there.*

He read the final line three times, and each time it settled a little differently, the way a stone settles further into soft ground with each additional weight placed on top of it.

*Observation 361: the message is not merely informational. It is structured as instruction — sequence specified, foundation before anomaly, deliberately paced. Whoever composed this understood exactly what they were leaving behind, and exactly how it should be approached by someone with no other context.*

He set the notebook down and let himself simply sit with it for a while, the command chamber's quiet cool settling around him, the relay chain's faint steady hum the only other presence in a room that had been waiting, motionless, for longer than human civilization had existed in any recognizable form.

*You will understand why we left.*

Not why they died. Not why they disappeared. Why they *left* — a word choice that implied intention, a decision made rather than a fate suffered, and he found himself turning the distinction over carefully, the way he turned over every word that seemed to have been chosen with unusual precision.

He thought, briefly, of Ren Wuji's own message, left in a dying man's hand five years and a lifetime of scale ago. *I hope you are curious. Everything depends on it.* Two messages, separated by forty million years and an entire civilization's rise and vanishing, both built on the same quiet faith: that whoever eventually arrived would be curious enough, careful enough, patient enough, to actually deserve what had been left waiting for them.

He did not yet know what waited at solar position 7.4-N. He suspected, from the message's own careful pacing — foundation first, anomaly last, an explicit instruction not to skip ahead — that he wasn't meant to know yet either.

*Rule,* he wrote, and underlined it the way he underlined every rule that felt, on first writing, like it might cost him something later to keep. *Whatever waits at 7.4-N, I follow the sequence the way it was left for me. I do not skip ahead because curiosity is impatient. The message itself is evidence that the crew who wrote it understood exactly how dangerous skipping ahead could be.*

He checked the relay chain once more — stable, Chen Yufei's check-in six hours old now, a brief message waiting: *Take your time. We're not going anywhere* — and felt something in his chest ease slightly at the specific, ordinary kindness of it, forty million years and an unimaginable distance away from the small apartment above the noodle shop where all of this had actually started.

Then he turned back to the archive's first section, the standard G-type stellar field the message had specified, and began, patiently, the long work of learning to read what an entire vanished expedition had spent presumably decades learning to read before him.

The anomaly could wait. He had been taught, by every teacher who had ever mattered to him, that the things worth understanding almost always could.
