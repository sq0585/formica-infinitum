# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Seven — The Weight of the Planet

He sat alone in the pocket dimension laboratory that night, the notebook open, the weight of what Meen-4 had told him sitting in his chest with a particular density he hadn't experienced from any previous discovery this strange world had handed him.

He wrote slowly, choosing each word with more care than usual, because some thoughts needed to be gotten exactly right the first time or not written at all.

> *The Formicans built infrastructure to stabilize Earth's tectonics approximately forty million years ago and never decommissioned it. Without active maintenance, Earth's volcanic and seismic activity would increase to extinction-event levels within decades — not centuries, decades, a timeline that fits inside a single human lifetime with room to spare.*
>
> *I am now responsible for maintaining planetary infrastructure.*
>
> *I want to note that I did not ask for this.*

He read the last line back, and found, despite the genuine gravity of everything underneath it, something close to the particular dry humor he'd relied on across two years of impossible developments — not to diminish the weight of what he'd just taken on, but to keep himself functional underneath it, the way he'd learned, slowly, that humor and seriousness could coexist without either one canceling the other out.

He kept writing.

> *Practical questions, in order of urgency:*
>
> *1. How much of the maintenance burden is currently being carried by structures operating without correction, and how close to failure is the system actually running?*
>
> *2. What does maintenance actually require — Compression-stage practitioners, like the Under-Web nodes, or something at a scale only I currently have access to?*
>
> *3. Who else needs to know this? The Collective's working capacity is finite. This is not a problem I should attempt to carry alone, the way I nearly didn't survive the anchor demolition by trying to hold too much without help.*
>
> *4. How do I tell anyone this without sounding like I have lost my mind?*

He sat with the fourth question longer than the others, the particular absurdity of it landing with more weight than its phrasing suggested.

Forty million years of patient Formican engineering. Forty years of remaining margin before catastrophic failure. A responsibility that had simply arrived, unbidden, the moment his own network had finally grown sophisticated enough to detect what had always been there, quietly holding the planet's surface together while eight billion people went about ordinary lives with no idea any of it existed.

He thought about his mother, asleep in her apartment across the city, entirely unaware that somewhere beneath the Pacific Ocean, structures older than human civilization itself were the only thing standing between her ordinary, comfortable life and a geological catastrophe neither she nor anyone she knew would ever have the context to understand.

He thought about Chen Yufei, about Wei Canglan, about Fang Ruxue and Liang Jingzhi and the sixty-seven members of a Collective he'd spent the past year painstakingly building — all of them, suddenly, carrying a stake in something considerably larger than the political conflict that had occupied most of his attention until tonight.

He closed his eyes for a moment, simply sitting with the scale of it, allowing himself the particular discipline he'd learned across two years of crises: feel the weight fully, honestly, without either minimizing it or letting it become paralyzing.

He opened his eyes and picked the pen back up.

> *I don't have answers to most of these yet. I have the beginning of a plan: understand the system's current state before deciding anything else. Bring in the people who need to know, carefully, in the order that makes sense rather than all at once.*
>
> *I am twenty-one years old. Two years ago I was working the night shift at a convenience store, mostly bored, occasionally restocking the cooler. I am now responsible, in some genuine and undeniable sense, for whether the Pacific Rim continues to be survivable.*
>
> *I am going to need a considerably larger notebook.*

He closed the current one, the small joke at the end doing exactly what he needed it to do — not erasing the weight of what he'd just taken on, but making it possible to carry, one careful, methodical step at a time, the way he'd carried everything else this impossible world had handed him so far.
