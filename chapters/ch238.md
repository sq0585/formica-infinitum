# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Thirty-Eight — Dimensional Weaver, The Expansion

Liang Jingzhi's surveys gave him something he hadn't had before: a prioritized map of exactly where the Strata's structural attention was needed most, instead of the careful guesswork he'd been operating on. He used it to plan the largest reconstruction project he'd attempted since reaching Dimensional Weaver stage.

Thirty percent additional habitable area, built from collapsed sections that had sat untouched since long before he'd first crossed the threshold — not patched or stabilized, but genuinely rebuilt, new geometry grown from the ruins of structures that had failed centuries before he was born.

Meen-4 worked alongside him for most of it.

She had never offered to assist directly with construction before, beyond the occasional unsolicited correction, and he understood, watching her settle into the work without ceremony on the first morning, that something had shifted in how she regarded the project — or perhaps in how she regarded him.

They worked largely in silence. Her guidance, when she offered it, was precise and technical, focused entirely on Formican construction protocols he hadn't yet encountered in his own translation work — load-bearing relationships between adjacent folds, the proper sequencing for reconstructing collapsed sections without destabilizing the structures around them, small details that would have cost him weeks of trial and error to discover alone.

He found, somewhere around the second day, that the silence between them had stopped feeling like the careful distance of an observer tolerating a student and started feeling like something closer to companionship.

"You haven't corrected anything in four hours," he said, on the third day, midway through stabilizing a particularly complex section of collapsed shelving.

*Correction was not required,* she signaled. *Your work has been acceptable.*

"That's not what I asked."

A pause, the particular cadence he'd learned to read as her considering whether to say more than the strictly necessary minimum.

*I have worked alongside few practitioners in this manner,* she signaled finally. *The Archive's prior carriers worked alone, or with limited collaboration. This arrangement is — *

She paused again, longer this time.

*Satisfactory,* she finished.

He understood, watching her return immediately to the work without further elaboration, that this was as much as she intended to say, and that the word she'd chosen was carrying considerably more weight than its plain meaning suggested.

---

By the end of the second week, the expansion was complete — new corridors, new alcoves, new rooms grown from collapsed ruin into stable, livable space, the Strata's total habitable area larger than it had been at any point since he'd first crossed into it.

He stood in the largest of the new spaces on the final evening, a wide hall he'd built with deliberate emptiness, no specific purpose assigned to it yet, simply room held open for whatever the community eventually decided it needed.

Meen-4 stood beside him, surveying the work with the same precise, unreadable attention she brought to everything.

*This is good work,* she signaled.

"I had good guidance."

*You had adequate guidance. The work itself was yours.* A pause. *I did not expect, when you first crossed the threshold, that I would spend four hundred years observing one carrier and then choose to build alongside this one.*

He considered the weight of that admission, the centuries of solitary observation she was implicitly comparing this collaboration against.

"I'm glad you did," he said simply.

*As am I,* she signaled, and turned, without further ceremony, back toward the inner sections — leaving him alone in the wide, empty hall he'd built, the quiet companionable silence of the past two weeks settling into something he understood, slowly, as one of the more significant relationships this entire impossible world had given him.

He wrote it into his notebook before he left for the night, simply, without trying to add more weight to it than it already carried on its own.

> *Thirty percent more habitable space. Meen-4 worked alongside me the entire time, without distance, without the careful observation she usually maintains. She called the arrangement 'satisfactory.' I think, from her, that's an extraordinary thing to say.*
