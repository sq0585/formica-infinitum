# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Thirty — The Experiment

Phase Three. The bilateral exchange.

He attempted it on his break at 3 AM, in the back room of the store where the shelving units and the cleaning supplies and the spare till rolls lived, because the back room was private and it was the only private space available during a night shift, and a man sitting cross-legged in the stockroom was easier to explain than a man sitting cross-legged behind the register.

He sat against the shelving unit. He ran through Phases One and Two as calibration — perception open, directionality confirmed, the north-northwest heading steady and clear. The filament network ran through the floor and the walls, luminous and patient, doing what it had always done.

Phase Three as described in the document: *direct attention to the nearest primary flow, sustain perception without attempting influence, and introduce a structured signal — not a direction, not a request, but an identification. Make known that a Chitin Seed interface is present and active.*

He wasn't entirely sure what *introduce a structured signal* meant in practice. The document's rhythm markers indicated it should take approximately four breath cycles, which he was now measuring in the protocol's cadence: four counts in, two hold, six counts out, two hold. He would use breath and attention together. He would perceive the nearest primary flow clearly and then — he had no better word for it — *be present to it*. Not announce himself. Just be evident.

He closed his eyes. He found the nearest primary flow with his attention — a thicker thread of the filament network that ran directly beneath the stockroom's east wall, presumably heading toward the main current he'd mapped as north-northwest. He settled his attention on it without moving toward it or away from it.

He breathed. Four in, two hold, six out, two hold.

Nothing happened on the first cycle. He kept breathing.

On the third cycle, the flow changed.

---

It didn't change dramatically. The thread didn't surge or pulse or do anything visible. But its quality shifted in a way he recognized from the protein superconductor paper — a change in the coherence of the transmission, like a signal clarifying when the receiver tunes to the right frequency.

The thread *oriented toward him.*

Slightly. He would have said infinitesimally except that it was completely clear. The flow, which had been running along its fixed path without reference to his presence, shifted its angle by perhaps two degrees in a direction that was specifically toward him. A lean. An acknowledgment.

He held the perception carefully, not reacting, just receiving.

A warmth moved through his hands that was different from the usual baseline — not more intense, but *richer*, as if the single note of the usual warmth had been joined by its harmonics.

He sat with it through two more breath cycles. Then the quality normalized. The flow returned to its fixed path. The harmonic warmth faded to the usual single note.

He opened his eyes.

The stockroom was the stockroom. The spare till rolls were where he'd left them.

He got out his notebook.

> *3:07 AM. Phase Three attempt. Location: stockroom, east wall.*
>
> *Method: sustained perception of nearest primary flow, breath regulation (4-2-6-2), attention without intervention.*
>
> *On third breath cycle: flow response. Orientation shift of approximately 2 degrees toward my position. Sustained approximately 8 seconds. Subsided naturally.*
>
> *Simultaneous: harmonic warmth in hands. Not intensity increase — qualitative change. As if the baseline signal acquired additional frequencies. Duration matched the orientation shift.*
>
> *Interpretation: Phase Three successful. The 'bilateral exchange' is not symbolic. The network responded to my presence with a measurable change in behavior.*
>
> *What this means:* the Under-Web is not passive. It responds to Chitin Seed interface. The exchange is functional, not ceremonial.*
>
> *What I felt: the flow didn't come toward me. It noticed me. That's the right word: noticed.*
>
> *New question: what does it do with that information?*

He heard the front door chime. He put the notebook in his apron pocket and went back to the counter.

A university student bought an energy drink and paid with his phone and left.

Lin Mushi stood at the counter for the rest of his shift with the harmonic warmth still faintly present in his palms, a new frequency that hadn't been there before the experiment.

The network had noticed him.

He had introduced himself to the Under-Web, and the Under-Web had introduced itself back.

He had no idea yet what that meant. He was going to find out.

His left hand felt different from his right.

He checked the meridian map in his notebook.

He started making notes about which channel that implied, and whether the response had come through the same pathway as the wrist healing, and what that would tell him about the sequencing of the Standard Protocol.

The fluorescent light hummed. Outside, Chengdu held its nighttime quiet.

He wrote until his shift ended.
