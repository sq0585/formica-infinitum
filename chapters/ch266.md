# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty-Six — What Meen-4 Knows

He found her in the inner sections, working through a section of catalogued texts he'd never had reason to access, and laid the survey data out for her without preamble — the deep structures, the Ring of Fire configuration, the unmistakable signature of something still active after what he now understood must be millions of years.

She went still.

He had learned, over nearly two years of working alongside her, to read the particular quality of her stillness — the difference between routine attention and genuine surprise, between considering a question and recognizing one she'd been waiting for. This was something else entirely, a silence that stretched on far longer than anything he'd experienced from her before.

He counted, almost without meaning to, the way he'd once counted seconds on his first stable fold.

Four minutes. Then twenty seconds more.

*We knew,* she signaled finally, the cadence carrying a weight he'd rarely heard from her. *We chose not to tell you because you were not ready for the implications.*

He felt the cold clarity arrive, the particular sensation of a question forming before he'd consciously decided to ask it.

"What are the implications?"

*That if those structures fail,* she signaled, *Earth's geological stability decreases catastrophically within approximately forty years.*

He sat with this, the weight of it settling slowly and completely, considerably larger than anything he'd braced himself for during the walk to find her.

"Decreases how?"

*The structures you detected stabilize tectonic activity across the entire Pacific Rim — the region your geologists call the Ring of Fire precisely because it concentrates the planet's most volatile volcanic and seismic activity. The Formican engineers who built this infrastructure, approximately forty million years ago by your calendar, did so to make Earth's surface survivable for sustained habitation, both their own and, eventually, for whatever life evolved here afterward.*

He thought about every earthquake, every volcanic eruption he'd ever read about in passing — events that had always seemed, to his understanding before this moment, like simple geological inevitability, the planet's own restless nature operating without any external hand shaping it.

"The structures are managing that. Actively. Right now."

*Continuously. The Ring of Fire's current activity level, severe as it sometimes appears from the surface, is the managed, stabilized version of what the underlying geology would otherwise produce.*

"And without maintenance, the structures fail within forty years."

*Approximately. The Formican calculation accounted for gradual degradation absent active correction — the same principle as your Under-Web maintenance crisis, at a scale considerably larger than anything you have yet managed.*

He sat with the full shape of what she'd just told him, the careful, methodical part of his mind already beginning the work of understanding exactly what this meant, even as a deeper, more human part of him simply needed a moment to absorb the scale of it.

"Why didn't you tell me sooner? When you first understood I could detect this, or even before — surely the Archive's records have always contained this information."

*You were not ready,* she signaled again, the simple repetition carrying more weight than elaboration would have managed. *A Carrier who learns of planetary-scale obligation before establishing sufficient personal stability often fails — not through incapacity, but through being crushed by scope before developing the capacity to carry it. You needed the Strata, the Collective, the network of people and institutions you have built across two years, before this particular weight could be added without breaking something essential underneath it.*

"And you decided I was ready now."

*You detected it yourself, through your own network, without my prompting. That is the readiness test. Had you not been ready, you would not yet have built sufficient infrastructure to notice the structures at all.*

He understood, slowly, the particular logic underneath her careful, patient withholding — not deception for its own sake, but a kind of protective pacing, the same principle the Codex itself had always operated by, comprehension locks gating each new scale of understanding until he'd genuinely earned the capacity to hold it.

He picked up his notebook, the weight of forty million years of patient Formican engineering and forty years of human urgency settling, finally, onto a page where he could begin, methodically, the work of understanding exactly what came next.

"I need to think about this," he said. "Carefully. Before I decide what to do with it."

*That is correct,* Meen-4 signaled. *I would be concerned if you did otherwise.*
