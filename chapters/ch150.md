# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty — The Intermediate Archive

He opened the first Intermediate Archive container on a Friday afternoon.

Meen-4 had placed four containers at his usual table — he had arrived to find them there, with a brief notation on the topmost container that he read as the Formican equivalent of *start here.* He arranged them in order, set out his annotation notebook, and opened the first.

Historical records. The Amber Lattice School's student logs, beginning six hundred years ago.

He read carefully.

The Amber Lattice School had been — he read this from the context rather than a specific description — a Formican institution that had maintained a practice of accepting human students alongside Formican ones. Not many humans: the records showed one to three human students at any given time across the six hundred years of logs he was reading. Always self-selected, always practitioners who had found their way to the Strata through their own capability development, always assessed by the Keeper station before being introduced to the School's curriculum.

The records were methodical. Each human student had an entry describing their arrival, their Compression capability at time of entry, their cultivation tradition background, their areas of focus during study, and — if available — their eventual departure.

He read through them in order. Forty-seven human students across six hundred years. Some stayed for months. Some for years. A few for decades.

He reached the container's final section.

The entry was dated four hundred years ago. The notation at the top of the entry said, in the communication register: *Student record — Human, surface tradition, Iron Meridian lineage — extended study.*

He read the description.

*The student in question demonstrated unusual facility for Scale Philosophy comprehension at time of entry. Initial capability: Ratio 2 with theoretical framework that was—* The notation cluster here he had to parse carefully: *self-derived but substantively correct. Significant.* 

*The student remained at the School for thirty years, during which he developed Ratio 4 capability and preliminary Dimensional Weaving theory. His approach to all material was methodical, documentation-oriented, and focused on constructing frameworks that would be transmissible to practitioners outside the School's direct instruction environment.*

He stopped at this sentence.

He read it again.

*Transmissible to practitioners outside the School's direct instruction environment.*

Ren Wuji had spent thirty years here. And he had specifically spent those thirty years building materials that could be transmitted to people who wouldn't have access to the School.

He looked at the Codex on his phone.

The transmission format — the audio files, the notebooks, the graduated document releases — that wasn't something Ren Wuji had improvised at the end. He had been building it for thirty years. The Codex was the transmission vehicle he had designed during his time in this school.

He wrote with a hand that was not quite steady:

> *Ren Wuji's record in the Intermediate Archive, third tier. Key finding: he was a student here for 30 years. His specific focus: building transmissible frameworks. He designed the Codex format during his tenure here.*
>
> *The Codex is not a found object — it is a deliberately constructed pedagogical tool designed by a practitioner who had thirty years to think about how to teach someone who wasn't physically present in the school.*
>
> *He was teaching me before I was born.*
>
> *The notation describing his departure: incomplete. The record ends with a notation indicating the student left the School for the surface. There is no record of what happened after.*

He looked at the entry's final notation. Meen-4 had said the records were partial because the School had been — disrupted.

He turned to the next container.

He read for the rest of the afternoon.

He read the way he sometimes did when something was important enough to set everything else aside for — not fast, not slow, with the pace of someone who is taking in each sentence and allowing it to situate before reading the next.

When he left the archive that evening he stood in the parking structure for a moment, Chengdu's spring evening around him, and thought about a man who had come to this place four hundred years ago and spent thirty years building the tools that had found Lin Mushi behind a convenience store counter.

He thought: *you knew it would need to work without you being there. You built for that.*

He did not go home immediately.

He walked for an hour through the city.

He had known intellectually, from the first audio file, that Ren Wuji had prepared carefully and with deliberate care for someone he'd never meet. He had read it in the files, the notes, the graduated complexity of the Codex's document releases.

He had understood it.

He was understanding it differently now.

He went home and ate dinner and went to bed and lay in the dark thinking about thirty years of building something you'd never see used.

He understood why Ren Wuji had been warm about it. In the audio files, underneath the precision and the dry humor, there had always been warmth.

Thirty years of building something for someone you'd never meet was either the loneliest thing or the most hopeful thing, and Ren Wuji had clearly decided which it was.

Lin Mushi decided the same thing.
