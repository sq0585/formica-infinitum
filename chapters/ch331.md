# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty-One — The Weight of the Connection

He didn't tell anyone for two days, the discovery sitting in his chest with a weight that resisted every attempt he made to translate it into something he could actually share.

He went through the motions of his ordinary routine carefully, deliberately, aware that his silence was itself unusual enough that people were beginning to notice. Wei Canglan asked, on the first evening, whether something was wrong, and he'd deflected with an answer general enough to be technically true without revealing anything — *processing something, not ready to talk about it yet* — and Wei Canglan, with his usual careful respect for other people's timelines, had let it go without pressing further.

He spent most of the two days in the small pocket dimension he used for meditation, not actively cultivating, simply sitting with the fact of it, turning the discovery over from every angle he could manage.

Ren Wuji had taught Veth-Karun.

He tried, repeatedly, to reconstruct what that must have meant — a teacher, watching a student reach for something too large, too fast, without the comprehension necessary to survive reaching it. He thought about his own relationship to power, the careful, deliberate discipline Meen-4 had insisted on at every threshold, the specific caution she'd built into every lesson since the day she'd first explained the Dissolution Path to him. He wondered whether Ren Wuji had tried to teach Veth-Karun that same caution, and whether it simply hadn't been enough, or whether something else entirely had happened, something the clinical Archive record hadn't captured.

He thought, too, about the obscured notation, deliberately blurred by Ren Wuji's own hand rather than by any external censorship, four hundred years of careful, patient work spent teaching someone he'd never meet — and never once mentioning, in any recording, any note, the single most significant thing that had apparently happened to him.

---

On the second night, he finally wrote about it, the notebook entry taking him considerably longer than any he'd written before, each sentence arriving slowly, carefully, as though the weight of the thing required its own deliberate pace.

> *Ren Wuji's student was Veth-Karun. I don't know yet what that means, fully. I know it means he carried something for four hundred years that he never once told me about, not in any recording, not in any note, not even obliquely in the careful, patient way he told me almost everything else.*
>
> *I keep trying to imagine what those thirty years must have looked like — teaching someone, watching them grow, and then watching whatever happened next. I don't have enough information yet to know if he failed to prevent it, or tried and simply couldn't, or wasn't even present when it finally happened. I don't know if he blamed himself. I think, given everything else I know about him, that he probably did.*
>
> *He didn't just leave me the knowledge. He left me the responsibility for what the knowledge cost. I don't think that was intentional — I don't think he expected me to find this, or if he did, I think he expected considerably more time to pass before I was ready to carry it. But it's mine now, whether he intended it or not, and I don't know yet what to do with the weight of it.*

He set the pen down, the entry longer than anything he'd written before, and sat with it a while longer in the quiet dark of his room.

---

Chen Yufei's absence sat differently now, sharper, the specific ache of wanting to tell her something this heavy and having no way to reach her that didn't feel like an intrusion on the distance she'd asked for. He thought about calling anyway, and decided, carefully, that this wasn't the kind of weight that belonged in a phone call meant to interrupt someone's deliberate solitude.

He would tell her when she returned. He would carry it, until then, the way he'd learned to carry most of the heaviest things this world had handed him — alone at first, deliberately, giving himself the space to sit with it honestly before asking anyone else to help hold it.

On the third morning, he finally sought out Meen-4, understanding, with the same clarity that had guided him toward the discovery itself, that she was the only person in this world who might actually understand the full shape of what he was carrying.

He found her in the circular chamber, and before he'd said anything at all, she looked up, her attention settling on him with unusual intensity.

*You have found something,* she signaled. Not a question.

"I have," he said. "I need to tell you. I think you're the only person who might understand what it means."

She was quiet for a long moment, something careful and braced entering her posture, as though she'd been anticipating this conversation for longer than he'd realized.

*Tell me,* she signaled.

He sat down across from her, and began.
