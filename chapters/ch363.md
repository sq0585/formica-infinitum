# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Sixty-Three — The Disclosure Question

He requested a second meeting with Lady Shen three days later, understanding, after sitting with her counsel, that the conversation deserved a continuation rather than being left to settle unresolved.

"I want to ask you directly," he said, once they'd settled into the same quiet meeting room. "Not for advice this time. For your honest read on where I actually stand, from your perspective, watching me across these two years."

She considered the question with the same careful precision she brought to everything, studying him for a long moment before answering. "You've built something genuinely remarkable. I don't say that lightly, given how our history began. But I don't think you've faced this specific question — disclosure, deliberate and chosen rather than reactive — because facing it means accepting that the world as it currently exists, contained and hidden, cannot continue indefinitely. I think some part of you has been hoping, quietly, that you might manage both indefinitely — the hidden world's continued protection, and its inevitable, eventual visibility, without ever having to choose between them."

"That's an accurate read," he admitted, the honesty costing him something even as he offered it.

"The paper is the beginning of a disclosure process you haven't formally decided to initiate," she said again, the same phrase from days earlier now landing with renewed weight. "You should decide."

"I know," he said.

"What are you going to do?"

He sat with the question, genuinely, rather than reaching for any answer that might satisfy the moment's demand for resolution. "I don't know yet."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the honest one," he said again, meeting her eyes directly, refusing to let the discomfort of genuine uncertainty push him toward a false clarity he didn't actually possess.

---

She studied him for a long moment, something shifting in her expression — not disappointment, he understood, watching her carefully, but something closer to genuine respect for the honesty itself, however unsatisfying its content.

"You have eight months before the Collective review's conclusion," she said finally. "You should decide before that."

He nodded slowly, accepting the weight of the deadline without flinching from it. "I understand the urgency. I want to be honest with you about something else, though — I don't think I should decide this alone, and I don't think I should decide it quickly, simply because the timeline demands speed. This is the kind of choice that deserves the same careful, collaborative process everything else important in this world has gone through. Fang Ruxue. Chen Yufei. Meen-4. Everyone who's helped build what exists now."

"That's reasonable," Lady Shen said. "I'd expect nothing less careful from you, given everything else I've watched you build. I only want to be certain the deadline is genuinely understood, rather than allowed to slip past unattended while other, louder priorities absorb your attention."

"It won't slip past unattended," he said. "I promise you that."

---

He left the meeting carrying the full weight of the unresolved question, understanding, walking back toward the Strata City through the ordinary evening streets, that this was, perhaps, the first genuinely large decision of his entire impossible journey that he couldn't approach through careful, methodical technique — no Compression Ratio to master, no threshold to cross through patient cultivation, simply an enormous, human choice about how, and when, and whether, to let an entire hidden world become visible to the one it had always quietly shared its air with.

He wrote about it that night, the entry honest and unresolved.

> *Eight months. Lady Shen is right that I've been avoiding this. I don't have an answer yet, and I don't think forcing one prematurely would actually serve anyone well. I know I need one before the review concludes. I know this isn't a decision I should make alone. Beyond that, tonight, I have more questions than answers, and I think that's simply the honest place to sit, for now, before the careful work of finding a real answer actually begins.*

He closed the notebook, the weight of the unresolved question settling into him alongside everything else this arc had asked him to carry, and understood, sitting in the quiet of his own room, that whatever came next would require every ounce of the patient, careful judgment this entire impossible world had spent two years teaching him to hold.
