# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 416 — Preparing for Off-Earth Reach

The work began, as most of his largest projects began, with something small and unglamorous — a single test relay node, constructed in the pocket-dimension laboratory, designed to extend the Chitin Circuit network's dimensional folding technique beyond Earth's atmosphere for the first time.

"We are not sending anything physical," he reminded Wei Canglan, who had joined the project with the particular focused intensity he brought to any problem that let him work with his hands and his precision at once. "This isn't a rocket. It's an extension of the same folding the Colony Link already uses — Veritonal relay, not matter moving through space."

"I understood that the first time you explained it," Wei Canglan said, not looking up from the relay component he was calibrating. "I asked twice more anyway because I wanted to make sure I hadn't missed something that would get us both killed. Some habits don't go away just because the project sounds elegant on paper."

The first stage was modest by design: extending the network's reach only to Earth's own orbit, a distance trivial compared to the eight billion kilometers that remained, but significant as a proof of principle. Lin Mushi worked at it the way he worked at everything now — methodically, testing each incremental extension before trusting it to hold, letting Meen-4 catch errors in his notation before they became errors in the structure itself.

It took nine days to stabilize the orbital relay. He remembered, distantly, that reaching Dimensional Weaver maturity years earlier had felt like an enormous undertaking in its own right, and found himself faintly amused, watching this new project unfold, at how much more patient he'd become since then — willing, now, to spend nine careful days on a distance that would have once felt almost insultingly small compared to the ambition of the project as a whole.

"It's holding," Meen-4 confirmed, once the orbital node's signature stabilized fully. "Stable, low power draw, no degradation over the testing period. This is a solid foundation."

"Then we keep going," he said. "Inner solar system next. Incrementally, the same way we did the orbital stage."

The work stretched across weeks that followed, each stage building carefully on the one before it — Earth's orbit, then the inner solar system's edge, the relay network extending outward in patient, deliberate stages that mirrored, in miniature, the entire philosophy that had built the Strata itself. Infrastructure, always, before ambition. Foundation before reach.

He found the rhythm of the work oddly restorative, in the months following the review's conclusion and the separation's careful adjustment — a project with clear, incremental milestones, no existential stakes attached to any single day's progress, simply the steady, patient accumulation of reach that had characterized the best parts of the past three years.

"You seem lighter, doing this," Chen Yufei observed, during one of their now-routine Anchor maintenance sessions, watching him review the latest relay diagnostics with something closer to genuine enjoyment than the grim determination that had characterized most of his work during the review.

"I think I forgot, for a while, that building could feel like this," he admitted. "Not a crisis to survive. Just — a good problem, solved a little more each day."

"I'm glad you found it again," she said, and something in her voice carried real warmth, uncomplicated by anything the separation had changed between them. "You were always better at this than you gave yourself credit for. The patient part. The part that doesn't need everything solved today."

He thought about that, watching the relay network's slow, steady extension continue across the following weeks — inner solar system fully stabilized, the reach growing incrementally toward distances that had, only months earlier, existed for him only as an abstract signature at the edge of his perception.

The installation itself remained far out of reach still, the full eight billion kilometers still mostly ahead of him. But the foundation was holding, extending, growing exactly the way everything he'd ever built had grown — slowly, carefully, one stable stage at a time, until one day, he trusted, the distance that once felt impossible would simply be the next ordinary stage in a project he'd already proven he knew how to build.
