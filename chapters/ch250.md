# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty — The Cultivation War, Arc III Midpoint

The seminar had been Fang Ruxue's idea — the Collective's first genuinely public session, hosted in an ordinary university conference room, open to any practitioner curious enough to attend without needing to navigate the careful, half-secret channels that had governed every prior gathering.

"It's time," she'd told him a week earlier. "We've spent months building this quietly, carefully, among people already inclined to trust the project. If the Collective is going to become the genuine replacement structure it needs to be, it has to be willing to stand in an ordinary room and explain itself to strangers."

He'd agreed, with the particular unease he brought to anything that traded careful control for genuine openness.

The room was three-quarters full when Gou Ping's faction arrived.

There was no warning this time — no eight minutes from the Circuit network, no careful days of preparation. The attackers simply walked through the conference room's ordinary door, eight cultivators moving with the coordinated, practiced speed of people who had planned this specific moment carefully, combat techniques already half-formed before anyone in the room had fully registered what was happening.

Lin Mushi felt the cold clarity arrive instantly, his own response automatic before conscious thought caught up to it — but automatic wasn't fast enough, not in a civilian space crowded with people who had never trained for violence and had no defensive technique to fall back on.

The first strike landed before he could intervene.

A young Celestial Compass scholar near the front, caught in the spreading edge of a technique meant for someone else entirely, crumpled with a cry that cut through the room's sudden chaos. A second member — one of the recently resigned Iron Meridian practitioners, still adjusting to a Collective membership barely a week old — took a glancing hit attempting to shield the people nearest him.

Lin Mushi moved.

He didn't have the careful architecture he'd built for the Strata's defense, didn't have days to prepare a maze that would scatter eight committed attackers without anyone getting hurt. What he had was raw Dimensional Weaving, applied fast and crudely, enough to disrupt the room's geometry just enough that three of the eight lost their footing entirely, their coordinated assault fracturing into confused, isolated motion.

It wasn't clean. It wasn't the careful, harmless containment he'd managed against Gou Ping's first attempt. People were getting hurt in the chaotic seconds before his intervention took hold, and he understood, even as he worked, that he wasn't going to be fast enough to prevent all of it.

Chen Yufei reached the room seconds later, drawn by the alert he'd sent the instant the attackers appeared, her own intervention adding to the chaos in ways that finally, slowly, began tipping the confrontation toward containment rather than continued assault.

By the time the eight attackers withdrew — disoriented, several injured themselves in the scramble his crude Weaving had created — the conference room looked like exactly what it had become: a civilian space violated by people willing to hurt uninvolved bystanders to make a political point.

Two Collective members lay injured. The young scholar's arm hung wrong, the particular angle that told him, even without close examination, that something had broken. The Iron Meridian practitioner sat against the wall, pale and shaking, blood from a shallow cut along his temple.

Lin Mushi stood in the wreckage of a room that had, an hour ago, simply been a place for curious strangers to learn something new, and understood, with the cold, complete clarity of someone who could no longer pretend otherwise, exactly what kind of conflict this had actually become.

This was not a political dispute anymore. These people would hurt anyone, regardless of involvement or innocence, to protect a claim to authority that no longer had any legitimate foundation underneath it.

He knelt beside the injured scholar, helping Chen Yufei stabilize the broken arm, and felt the particular, cold resolve settle into him that he recognized, distantly, as the moment something in this conflict had permanently changed.

His response to this would define everything that came after it. He understood that clearly, kneeling in the wreckage, and began, even as he worked to help the injured, the careful process of deciding exactly what that response needed to be.
