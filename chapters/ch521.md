# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 521 — What Liang Jingzhi Says, After

Axis-7 left the way she had arrived — once, briefly, without ceremony, the room settling back into its previous shape around the space she'd occupied, though not quite all the way back, the way a room never quite settles back to its exact prior configuration once something significant has passed through it.

Dr. Shen Yue's figure remained unshared a while longer, patient, unbothered by the delay. He understood she would offer it when the room was ready, and not a moment before, and found himself, for the first time all morning, entirely content to let a number wait.

He turned his attention back to the dissolution zone, to the gradient he still held woven together with Liang Jingzhi's own permanent structure, and felt her presence there, steady, unchanged by everything that had just happened around her — the reunion, the residual's response, the small permanent pattern it had settled into, Axis-7's arrival and the lineage she had, carefully, only partially resolved.

She had been present, in some form, through all of it.

"You held all that," he said, "and never once mentioned it."

"You were doing the parts that needed mentioning," she said. "I was doing the part that needed holding. I've found, over the past several months, that those are usually different jobs, and it rarely helps to confuse them."

He considered that, and found, considering it, that it was exactly the kind of thing she would say, and exactly true.

"How does it feel," he asked, "to have been the hinge the whole resolution turned on? Six months of holding a permanent extension, alone, before anyone else had the scale to even see what you were carrying. And now this — the residual, the record, Axis-7, all of it resting, in the end, on the one fixed point you gave the Lattice back before any of us understood what it would eventually cost you to keep giving it."

She was quiet a moment, the specific quiet he had learned to recognize as her deciding whether a question deserved a considered answer or a plain one.

"It feels like my job," she said finally, the same three words he had heard from her, in one form or another, since the very first time she'd explained her decision to him, months and a lifetime ago. "I'd like some tea now, if anyone's making it."

He laughed, the sound arriving easy, unguarded, and felt something in the room laugh with him — Chen Yufei's presence warming, Wei Canglan's sharpening with something that was, for him, unmistakably fond amusement, Meen-4's ancient stillness carrying, for once, something almost light.

"Someone's making it," he said. "Chen Yufei's been refilling that pot since before this technique started. I don't think it's stopped once."

"Good," Liang Jingzhi said, both halves of her present at once, the office and the center, indistinguishable and entirely, permanently herself. "Then I'll have some. Properly, this time. Not through a report."

He held the connection a moment longer, feeling the gradient's whole structure settle into the quiet, patient work of continuing to heal at a pace he no longer needed to worry over — three hundred and twenty-three volunteers, one permanent anchor, and now, woven alongside them, whatever he himself could offer at a scale finally large enough to actually matter.

*Observation 521,* he wrote, closing the entry the way he closed most things now, plainly, without embellishment. *The residual has found somewhere to rest. Ren Wuji's student has been addressed, and the record does not respond, and the saying of it was never for the record to hear. Axis-7 has offered what honesty allows and no more. And Liang Jingzhi, asked to account for the weight of six months' quiet sacrifice, would like some tea.*

*I think that is, in the end, the correct scale for almost everything.*

He closed the notebook, and turned his attention, gently, back toward Dr. Shen Yue, who was still waiting, patiently, for the room to be ready to hear whatever number she had been holding all this while.

"All right," he said. "Tell us."

She turned the display, and the figure sat there, plain, unadorned, exactly the kind of number that would once have seemed impossible and now simply seemed, to everyone gathered, earned.

He read it, and felt the room absorb it together — Chen Yufei's hand finding the teapot again, Wei Canglan's careful, precise nod of a man closing an item on an agenda he had never once doubted would eventually close correctly, Meen-4's ancient stillness settling into something that, for the first time in five years, he recognized without needing to ask her to name it.

Liang Jingzhi's presence, both halves of her, remained steady through all of it, unhurried, exactly where she had always said she would be.

"Tea," she said again, into the quiet. "I did ask."

Chen Yufei laughed, warm and real, and poured a cup nobody in the room physically needed and everybody, in the specific way that mattered most, genuinely wanted.
