# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Sixteen — The Wrist

It was not a serious injury.

Lin Mushi established this to his own satisfaction in approximately four seconds: the joint moved, the pain was sharp but not acute, the swelling when it came would be mild. He had sprained his wrist before, in secondary school, and knew the specific quality of the hurt — localized, clean, the kind of thing that resolved in two or three days with ice and rest and some deliberate not-using-it.

He had been lifting a heavy stock crate from the wrong angle. He knew as soon as he did it that he'd done it wrong. The crate had gone down safely. His wrist was the tradeoff.

He stood in the back corridor at 11:40 PM, holding his right wrist with his left hand, and made a practical assessment of the situation: he still had six hours of shift left, there was no one to call in at this hour, the pain was manageable. He would ice it with a bag from the freezer case and get on with things.

He went to the freezer case. He got a bag of frozen edamame because it was the right shape and he was always going to pay for what he took. He pressed it against his wrist and stood at the counter feeling the cold spread through his palm.

The warmth in his hand was still there. Lower, now, pulled back by the shock of the cold, but present when he concentrated. A baseline that had been there for seventeen days.

He did not make a decision about what happened next. It was more that his attention moved toward his wrist, toward the specific location of pain, toward the joint and the structures around it — and the warmth followed his attention the way water follows a slope. Not dramatically. Not in a way he was directing. His awareness moved inward, toward the injury, and what had been diffuse became focused.

He felt the warmth pool at his wrist.

---

At 2:47 AM he realized he had put the edamame back in the freezer at some point without noticing, and his wrist, when he turned his attention back to it, did not hurt.

Not "didn't hurt as much." Did not hurt.

He pressed on the joint. Nothing. He rotated it slowly in both directions. Nothing. He made a fist and held it and released it. Nothing.

He looked at his wrist.

It looked like his wrist. No swelling that he could see. No residual tenderness.

He had sprained it. He was certain of that. He had felt the specific quality of the injury and known it immediately from prior experience.

Now he had no injury.

He stood at the counter for a while, turning his right hand over, pressing the joint from different angles, looking for evidence of the thing that had been there an hour ago.

He found none.

---

He wrote carefully:

> *11:42 PM: wrist sprain, right hand, moderate. Standard presentation. Applied ice.*
>
> *12:?? AM: attention moved inward toward injury. Warmth followed attention toward the site. Not a deliberate decision.*
>
> *2:47 AM: pain absent. Zero tenderness. Full range of motion. Swelling: none.*
>
> *Time elapsed from injury to resolution: approximately 3 hours. Typical resolution for moderate sprain: 48–72 hours.*
>
> *What happened: I don't know exactly. The warmth — the energy the Codex has been building in me — was directed to the injury site and appears to have accelerated healing. Appears to. I cannot rule out that I underestimated the injury initially.*
>
> *But I didn't underestimate it. I know what a sprained wrist feels like. This was one.*
>
> *Tentative conclusion: the energy can be directed. The direction followed my attention. The direction produced a biological effect.*

He paused. He added:

> *Unintended consequence, possibly: I drew from the local filament network to do this. I don't know how much. I don't know the protocol. I borrowed something I haven't been given permission to borrow, from a system I don't fully understand, without knowing the impact.*
>
> *Note: find out if this was a problem. Find out how to do it properly. Or find out if doing it at all was correct before trying again.*

He capped the pen.

The rest of the shift passed without event. A student came in at 3:15 for an energy drink and looked at Lin Mushi's right hand, perhaps because he was holding it oddly, and Lin Mushi put it in his pocket and rang up the purchase and said good night.

He walked home in the early light with both hands in his pockets. The wrist moved normally. It would, probably, move normally for the rest of his life, and he would never have evidence beyond his own memory of what the baseline had felt like at 11:42 PM.

He did not find this troubling. He had always known his own perceptions were the primary instrument. The instrument was all he had.

He did find himself thinking, on the walk home through Wuhou District in the pale October morning, about the filament network he'd been drawing from for seventeen days without realizing it. He had been leaking. Now he had pulled deliberately. The scale was small but the principle was the same: he was using something that had existed before him and would exist after him, and he had been doing it without a clear understanding of the cost.

He needed to understand the cost.

He took the long route home, past the construction site where the depth sensation had once surged so hard it had made him stumble. The rebar frame of the new building caught the early light. He walked past without stopping, hands in his pockets, thinking about permission and borrowing and the difference between finding a door open and being invited through it.

Somewhere in the city, a monitoring system that he did not know existed had just logged an anomaly.

He did not know that yet.

He bought a fresh notebook on the way home and labeled it *Energy Dynamics* before he'd even unlocked his door.
