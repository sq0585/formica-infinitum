# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Twenty-Two — The Night Shift Regular

The man first appeared on a Wednesday.

He came in at 2:07 AM, bought a black coffee from the machine by the door — the cheapest option, no milk, no sugar, exact change from a wallet that he replaced in his left coat pocket with the economy of someone who had done this many times — and left without looking at anything else in the store.

Lin Mushi rang the sale through and watched him go.

There was something different about his Qi signature.

Not dramatically different. Not alarming. But the man's signature had a quality that Lin Mushi had not encountered in a regular customer before: a kind of internal organization, a structural coherence in the energy he gave off that was distinct from the ambient Qi of ordinary people. Where a normal person's Qi was diffuse and unconcentrated — like the pooled warmth in his mother's kitchen, present but undirected — this man's Qi had edges. It ran in specific channels.

Lin Mushi wrote in his notebook after the man left:

> *2:07 AM. New customer. Male, late 30s, black coat. Bought coffee, exact change, left quickly. Qi signature: structured. Internal organization consistent with deliberate cultivation — not the self-reinforcing warmth of habitation or emotional charge, but something that has been worked on intentionally.*
>
> *He is a practitioner of something. Unknown what.*
>
> *His attention during the transaction: correct. He looked at the machine, at the counter, at me briefly. He was not surveying the store in the way someone would if they were looking for a specific thing. He was conducting a routine.*
>
> *Routine suggests regular visits. Wait.*

---

The man came back on Friday. Then the following Monday. Then Wednesday again.

The pattern established itself over two weeks: three visits per week, always between 1:45 and 2:30 AM, always the same coffee, always the same coat, always the same efficient transaction.

Lin Mushi catalogued his Qi signature across seven appearances and confirmed: the internal organization was consistent. The channels were real. The man was a trained practitioner using some kind of deliberate cultivation method. His level was hard to assess — Lin Mushi didn't yet have a good frame of reference for comparative ability — but the signature had a quality of settled practice, something built over years rather than weeks.

He also confirmed, on the fifth visit, something more specific: the man's attention on leaving was directed north-northwest.

The same heading as the main current beneath the store's floor.

He was not just buying coffee. He was checking something.

---

> *Week 3, night shift regular update:*
>
> *The man is monitoring the node. Not looking at it with visual Qi perception — his resolution doesn't seem fine enough for that, or he'd be reacting differently to what the node looks like now. He is measuring ambient Qi output from the street, using a method I'd describe as broad-spectrum sensing.*
>
> *He hasn't found me because he isn't looking for a person. He's looking for a geographic anomaly.*
>
> *The node's depletion signature: I looked at this again tonight. It is visible from street level as a low-output zone — the ambient Qi in the area around the store is measurably reduced compared to a control point two blocks north. Anyone with sufficient sensitivity would notice this as a local dead spot.*
>
> *He's noticed the dead spot. He's here to document its progression.*
>
> *He doesn't know what's causing it.*
>
> *I am what's causing it.*
>
> *Assessment: this man is a junior observer, not an investigator. His capability is real but limited in range. He gathers and reports. Whatever organization monitors this node, he is not its senior representative — he is the one who has been sent to do the preliminary work before someone more capable is deployed. The distinction matters for timeline planning. He is not the threat. He is the indicator of the threat.*

Lin Mushi capped the pen and served the man his coffee on the next visit with the same neutral pleasantness he extended to every customer, and the man thanked him with a nod and left, and Lin Mushi stood at the counter and listened to his own heart and found it was steadier than he would have expected it to be three weeks ago.

He was being monitored. He was monitoring his monitor. This was information. This was manageable.

He updated the timeline in his notebook.

*Four months* had been the man's estimate. It was now six weeks after the second message, which meant he had roughly eleven weeks remaining. This man was low-level — gathering data, not investigating. The real investigator, if there was one, would come later.

He had eleven weeks to understand enough not to be found helpless.

He had things to learn.

He turned his attention back to the notation notebook he'd been keeping under the counter and kept working on the grammatical layer problem until 4 AM, when Xiao Dong arrived early for a handoff and mentioned, in passing, that he was saving up for something — he waved off the question of what with a gesture that meant *not yet* — and then Auntie Zhang came in for her soy milk and told him about her sister's disagreement with a neighbor about a shared wall.

He listened to the whole story.

Some things were just life.
