# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Seventy-Eight — Chen Yufei Returns

He was waiting around the corner from the 7-Eleven at 10:10 AM.

She appeared at 10:14, walking from the east with the focused attention of someone who had been to this location before and knew what she was looking for. She went inside. He watched her through the Qi field — she spoke with the morning clerk, briefly, then moved toward the northwest corner as if something there was drawing her attention.

She could feel the threshold. Not at his resolution, but as a local anomaly — he'd been able to feel it himself long before he could read its structure. Eight years of practice gave her sensitivity his five months hadn't had at the start. She wasn't going to identify it as a threshold specifically, but she was going to know something was there.

She came back out.

He stepped around the corner and fell into step beside her.

She did not startle. She had felt him coming. She turned to look at him.

"You were waiting for me," she said.

"Yes."

"This is twice now."

"Yes."

She studied him for a moment. "You knew I'd come back to the store."

"You felt something in the northwest corner. You came to verify it wasn't an artifact of the node collapse."

"It wasn't," she said. "There's something there." She looked at him. "What is it?"

He said: "A threshold into the Strata below Chengdu. I've crossed it nine times. Last time I met a Formican keeper and was shown the library."

She was quiet for a long time.

"The library," she said.

"Yes."

"The Amber Lattice Library, specifically."

He stopped walking. "You know that name."

"The Iron Meridian's foundational texts reference it as a legendary site. Lost architecture of the Amber Lattice School. The school that developed the original cultivation theory." She looked at him. "You found it."

"It was below the store I worked at."

Another long silence.

"You've been in there," she said.

"Nine times. I have notes."

She made the same assessment she'd made at the tea shop — rapid, thorough, arriving at a conclusion he couldn't read from her expression. Then she said: "I need to see it."

"I know," he said. "That's why I was waiting for you."

"You need something from me."

"Not exactly. I have something to tell you that you'll find difficult. I think it's better to tell you with evidence in front of you rather than asking you to take my word for it."

"What do you need to tell me?"

"That the Iron Meridian's foundational texts are approximately sixty percent accurate descriptions of a system they didn't build and don't fully understand. That the remaining forty percent is interpretation error. That you have been practicing correctly for eight years, in a framework that works for the wrong reasons, and that I can show you why it actually works, and that showing you will probably change your relationship to everything you've been trained in."

Chen Yufei looked at him.

The Chengdu December morning was gray and cold and smelled of traffic and distant cooking and the city getting on with its business.

She said: "Take me in."

"Tonight," he said. "After midnight. I'll need to explain the entry method. You'll need eight channels minimum. Do you have eight?"

She looked slightly startled. "Yes."

"Then tonight." He took a notebook from his bag — he always had a notebook — and wrote his number in the back cover, tore the page out, and handed it to her. "Message me when you're ready."

She took the page.

He walked away.

He heard her say behind him, quietly, to herself or possibly to him: "Who are you?"

He had answered this before. He didn't turn around to answer it again.

She would find out tonight.
