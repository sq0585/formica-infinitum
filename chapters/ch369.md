# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 369 — Lady Shen's Intelligence

Lady Shen Baoling arrived at the Strata unannounced, which was, by now, simply how she arrived, and Lin Mushi had stopped being surprised by it two years ago. What had not stopped was the particular alertness she triggered in him every time — not fear exactly, not anymore, but the specific attention he gave to a variable whose exact value he had never fully solved for.

She found him in the pocket-dimension chamber where he reviewed Circuit diagnostics most mornings, and she did not sit before she spoke, which told him before she said a word that this was not a social visit.

"There is a Collective faction you have not accounted for," she said. "The Surveyor represents consensus. It does not represent unanimity. There is a difference, and the difference is currently being weaponized against you."

He set down the diagnostic tablet. "Tell me."

"They call themselves the Containment Orthodoxy. I learned the name eleven days ago, through channels I am not going to detail, and I have spent those eleven days confirming it rather than bringing you speculation." She finally sat, across from him, with the particular economy of movement he'd come to recognize as her version of restraint. "They have filed a pre-emptive recommendation with the review body. I have not seen the full document. I have seen enough of it summarized to know its conclusion."

"Which is."

"Earth's removal from the zone of active contamination risk."

He turned the phrase over, the way he turned over any piece of notation whose surface meaning felt deliberately smoothed. "Removal," he repeated. "That's not a word that means what it sounds like it means, is it."

"No." She did not soften it, which he had learned, over years of dealing with her, was its own kind of respect. "It is a euphemism doing a great deal of work. I have not confirmed the exact mechanism they are proposing. But I have confirmed the category of outcome they are proposing it toward, and I would be doing you no service by letting you hear it from someone gentler."

Something cold moved through him, quiet and clarifying rather than panicked — the same sensation he remembered from years ago, standing in Ren Wuji's small apartment, understanding for the first time that the Codex he'd inherited was not a curiosity but a responsibility with teeth.

"Sterilization," he said.

"I believe so. Yes."

He sat with it. Outside the chamber's dimensional walls, he could feel — distantly, the way he felt everything now, a low continuous hum at the edge of awareness — the Strata going about its ordinary morning. Someone cooking. Children he didn't personally know running somewhere they weren't supposed to. Twelve hundred lives that had chosen, in one way or another, to exist inside something he had built, none of whom had any idea that an institution older than human civilization was, at this exact moment, weighing whether their existence constituted an acceptable risk.

"Why are you telling me this," he asked. Not suspicious — he had stopped being reflexively suspicious of her information two years ago, even when he remained careful about her motives. Genuinely curious.

"Because information withheld from you has cost lives before, and I have grown tired of being the reason," she said, which was as close to something personal as she ever let herself get with him. "And because I want you to win this review. Whatever else is true about how I have engaged with you, that has never not been true."

He believed the first half of that sentence completely. The second half he filed away, the way he'd learned to file away everything Lady Shen said that sounded almost, but not quite, like the whole truth.

"What do they actually believe," he asked. "Not the euphemism. The reasoning underneath it."

"I don't fully know yet. But institutions like this rarely believe themselves cruel. They believe themselves careful." She stood, smoothing a fold from her sleeve that didn't need smoothing, the gesture of someone giving her hands something to do while her mind moved faster than her body. "I will have more within the week. In the meantime, do what you have always done well. Prepare for the worst possibility without becoming the kind of person the worst possibility would be right about."

She left the way she always left — without ceremony, the chamber's air resettling behind her like water closing over a stone.

He sat alone for a long moment before he reached for the notebook, and wrote, at the top of a fresh page, four words that he suspected would define the shape of everything that followed.

*Containment Orthodoxy. Find out why.*
