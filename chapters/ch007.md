# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Seven — Day Two — Warmth

Nine days after the man died, Lin Mushi began noticing what the walls were doing.

Not all walls. Not consistently. But at corners — specifically at corners, where two walls met the ceiling in a right angle — the plaster looked slightly wrong. Not wrong in a structural sense. Wrong in the way a photograph looks wrong when the color calibration is slightly off: technically fine, but disagreeing with how you expected it to look.

In certain corners, at certain angles of light, the wall looked like it was one layer too thin.

He recorded this at 3:48 AM on a Thursday, crouched in the back of the store examining the junction of the wall behind the drinks case:

> _Observation 3: corners. Specifically where two vertical surfaces and one horizontal surface meet. Plaster appears translucent at these junctions — not visually translucent, I can't see through it, but the perception of solidity is reduced. Like touching something and being less certain it's fully there._
>
> _Alternative explanation: sleep deprivation is a known cause of perceptual distortion. I am averaging 5.5 hours. This may be the explanation._
>
> _Counter-note: I have averaged 5.5 hours since I was eighteen. This has never happened before._

He also noticed the floor.

Not in his apartment this time — he had been noticing the depth under his apartment floor since the second morning. The store was different. The depth here ran in a different direction: not simply downward, like a hollow below the foundation, but _oblique_, as if the hollow were tilted, angled through the earth at a trajectory that didn't align with any of the building's axes.

He stood very still behind the counter at 4:03 AM and tried to determine which direction the depth ran.

North and slightly down. He was almost certain. The sensation had a direction to it the way sound had a direction — not visible, but locatable if you paid attention.

He turned ninety degrees and paid attention again.

Still north. The depth had a consistent heading regardless of which way he faced.

He wrote:

> _Observation 4: depth sensation (store floor) is directional. Heading: approximately N, with a downward pitch of perhaps 20–30 degrees. Does not shift with my orientation._
>
> _This is inconsistent with a self-generated perceptual distortion. A distortion caused by my own neurology would reorient when I reorient. An external phenomenon with a fixed trajectory would not._
>
> _Revising explanation from (possible sleep deprivation) to (perceptual anomaly, cause unknown, likely related to the Codex)._
>
> _Next test: is the heading consistent across nights, or does it drift?_

He added one more line after a pause:

> _The warmth from the phone is less today. Not absent — less. As if something that was previously being established has now stabilized._

He did not know what that meant yet.

He reread the message on his break, as he had reread it every day since the night he'd first been able to read it. _The world you live in is a surface. Beneath it is everything that has ever mattered._

North and slightly down.

He thought: so that's where it is.

He closed the notebook and went to check the drinks case inventory, because the inventory still needed checking regardless of what was beneath the floor, and because doing ordinary tasks well felt like a form of argument against the possibility that he was losing his mind.

The fluorescent light hummed. The store held its nighttime quiet.

In the corner where the drinks case met the wall, the plaster was one layer too thin.
