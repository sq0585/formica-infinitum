# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Two — Chen Yufei's First Entry

She walked faster than him.

He had not expected this. He'd assumed she would move carefully through the Strata — she was experiencing it for only the second time, the scale was still registering — but Chen Yufei had the forward motion of someone who had been building up to this for weeks and had finally been given permission to stop holding back.

She reached the outer library forty seconds ahead of him.

He found her standing in the middle of the outer hall with both hands pressed flat against the shelves, the way you might press your hands against something you needed to confirm was physically real.

"Nine years," she said, without turning around. "Nine years in the Courts. And they've been *looking for this.*"

He stopped where he was and let her have the moment.

She was perceiving the archive at whatever resolution her channels gave her — eight primary, all running at Iron Meridian efficiency levels that had improved by a third since he'd corrected the posture and the breathing framework. He could see her Qi field adjusting to the material of the shelves, the same recognition response the floor gave him but slower, the Strata reading her interface geometry as familiar but not native.

"The documents in those containers," she said. "The Courts have spent two hundred years and considerable resources trying to access things like this. The Celestial Compass School alone has funded twelve research expeditions into underground sites across Sichuan looking for Formican material. And the whole time—"

"It was under a 7-Eleven in Wuhou District."

She turned to look at him. The expression was not quite amusement — something adjacent to it, the slightly incredulous recognition of a specific kind of absurdity.

"Under a 7-Eleven in Wuhou District," she repeated.

"Where a twenty-two-year-old worked nights." He walked toward the shelves. "The Courts were looking in the right general area. They just didn't have the entry mechanism."

"The Compression technique."

"And the Chitin Seed. Without both, you can feel the boundary but you can't cross it." He ran his hand along a shelf, feeling the familiar warmth of the active circuit inscriptions. "The Courts have practitioners who can feel the boundary. None of them can do Ratio 1.4."

"Yet." She turned back to the shelves and started moving along them slowly, reading container labels with the sixty-percent comprehension she'd developed from his shared notation materials. "Can you teach Compression to someone without a Chitin Seed?"

He had been thinking about this since the question first formed. "The Codex doesn't address it directly. The Standard Protocol assumes Seed integration as a prerequisite. But the protocol's mechanism is geometric — the interface compression follows from understanding the geometry, and the geometry is learnable." He paused. "I don't know if the Seed is what makes the geometry workable or if it's an efficiency multiplier for a geometry that could work without it."

"You don't know."

"I'm eight months in. I don't know a great many things."

She looked at him with the expression she was developing for when he said something that was modestly accurate about a situation that was far from modest. "What do you know, specifically, that I need to know about this space before I start reading things?"

He appreciated the directness. He told her: the archive's access tiers, which containers were locked and which weren't, the dimensional lock mechanism he was working toward opening, the communication register and what he could and couldn't currently parse, the Keepers' role and the older Keeper's specific communication about the station's maintenance function.

He also told her about the archive worker who had given him the container.

She stopped walking. "It gave it to you."

"Placed it in my hands. Container labeled *Notes on Being a Carrier, Volume 1.*"

"And you haven't opened it yet."

"Not yet."

She looked at him with something that was assessment and something that was understanding and something in between that he didn't have a label for. "Why not?"

"Because I need to understand more of what I'm entering before I read what the previous Carrier learned from entering it." He paused. "I don't want to read it and accept their experience as mine before I've had my own."

She was quiet for a moment.

"That's," she said carefully, "either very wise or very stubborn."

"Possibly both."

She turned back to the shelves. He watched her begin reading container labels, her index finger moving slowly along the notation the way a new reader's finger tracked text, not as a reading aid but as a spatial anchor.

Her excitement was genuine and it was creating a vulnerability he needed to note — not to exploit, never that, but to understand. She had spent nine years in pursuit of exactly this kind of access. She was here now. That history would shape every decision she made in this space, and he needed to know its shape.

He wrote in his notebook while she read:

> *Chen Yufei's first library entry. Observation: genuine excitement creating forward motion. She went to the shelves before she oriented to the space. This is useful — she will engage with the content more directly than I did at first.*
>
> *Vulnerability: nine years of Courts orientation. She has frameworks for this kind of material that were built without the Codex's foundation. She will need to keep translating — from the Iron Meridian category system into the Formican one.*
>
> *My job here: not to direct her reading. To answer questions accurately when asked. To be available. To not be condescending about what she doesn't yet know, because what she knows that I don't is substantial and important and she is using it every time she helps me.*
>
> *She said: 'This is what they've been looking for.' She meant the Courts. But she also meant herself.*
>
> *This is what she has been looking for.*

He closed the notebook. He went to his own section of the shelves and kept working on the dimensional lock geometry.

He could hear her reading — the soft sounds of someone wholly absorbed.

He let her be wholly absorbed.

That was what the Strata was for.
