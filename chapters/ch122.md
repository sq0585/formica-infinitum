# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Twenty-Two — Notes on Being a Carrier, Volume 1

He read it on a Sunday.

Not in the archive — at home, at his desk, with the Chitin Circuit network running in the background and the February rain doing what Chengdu February rain did. He made tea, he ate properly first, he aligned his notebooks in their usual order.

Then he opened the container and began.

The predecessor had not written in Formican notation. They had written in Classical Chinese, with occasional Formican notation for technical terms that didn't have adequate Chinese equivalents. The script was a form of running hand that would have been legible to a reasonably educated reader of two hundred years ago and was legible to him with some effort.

The first page was a date: a year from the Jiaqing period of the Qing dynasty. Approximately 1800 CE.

Then:

*If you are reading this, you have been where I have been. You have stood in the hall that goes further than you expected. You have learned that the floor remembers your passage. You have found the archive.*

*I am leaving these notes because I spent my first year doing this alone, and alone was inefficient. The solitude was not the inefficiency — solitude is often correct. The inefficiency was in the errors I made in the first year that took me years to correct, because no one was there to tell me they were errors.*

*These notes are the correction I would have liked.*

He read slowly. He read carefully. He was aware, reading, that he was doing something specific: receiving the experience of someone who had been in exactly his position two hundred years ago, who had thought carefully about what they wished they'd known, and who had made the decision to leave it behind.

Volume 1 covered months one through twelve of what the predecessor called *the Receiving Period* — the phase of initial integration, perception development, and framework-building that corresponded to everything Lin Mushi had done in his first year.

What the predecessor wished they'd known, that they hadn't:

*The Keepers are not decorative. They are functional staff with specific roles and specific authority. Treating them as background will cause you to miss significant communications they are trying to give you. I lost approximately three months to this error.*

He thought about the months he'd spent before the circular room visit when the older Keeper had first communicated with him. He had been treating the Keeper as a guide rather than a communicant. He had been faster to engage than the predecessor had been, partly because of the Codex's decoded communication register.

*The Colony Link develops faster than the Codex predicts in practitioners who have extensive prior experience with group dynamics and distributed systems. If you have this background, the Colony Link will outpace your other capabilities and you will need to develop filters before you develop range.*

He thought about the group-scale Colony Link awareness that had developed in the past month — rooms full of people suddenly legible in their aggregate emotional state. He had been documenting it without a framework for what it was going to become.

*The Formican community in Layer 3 is aware of you. They will decide in their own time how to engage. Do not interpret the absence of engagement as absence of knowledge. They know you are here from the first day.*

He thought about Axis-7's report to the Elder Council. He had not known about that when he was in the first weeks. He had known there were Formicans below but had not known they were tracking him from day one.

He wrote in the margins at each key note, his own cross-reference: *confirmed,* or *also experienced, but later,* or *this was different in my case because—*

The predecessor had reached the archive at month seven. He had reached it at month eight. The predecessor had opened the first dimensional lock at month ten. He had opened it at month nine.

He was slightly ahead of the predecessor's pace, which he attributed to the Codex's decoded notation system having more material available to him than had been available to someone working from scratch in 1800.

Volume 1 ended with an entry from month twelve:

*One year. The archive contains more than I will process in a lifetime. The Formican community below has made contact — or rather, a specific member of it has. I am calling her the Scholar because I do not yet have the register fluency to understand her designation and she reads with a scholar's absorbed attention.*

*I have been learning this work for one year. I expect I will spend the rest of my life on it.*

*If you are reading this in the future: the rest of your life is sufficient. Do not rush. The archive will be here.*

He sat with the last page for a while.

Then he wrote in his own notebook:

> *Notes on Being a Carrier, Volume 1: read. Date: February, month nine.*
>
> *The predecessor was here two hundred years ago, in almost exactly the same situation. The errors they described: I made most of them, but earlier and recovered from them faster.*
>
> *They called Axis-7 (presumably) 'the Scholar.' This is apt.*
>
> *Their final note: 'do not rush. The archive will be here.' I have been rushing because the external timeline has been pressing. But their point holds: the work itself doesn't require urgency. Only the specific situation requires urgency.*
>
> *The situation is specific to me. The work is universal.*

He put Volume 1 back in its container.

He looked at Volumes 2 through 12 in the archive.

He would read them in order, at the pace they deserved.

He was in month nine. Volume 2 covered months thirteen through twenty-four.

He had a few months before he'd need it.

He went back to the translation document.

He had forty-seven recipients to prepare for.
