# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fourteen — Day Seventeen

The change came at 4:44 AM.

He knew the time because he checked — not from the register, which he wasn't near, but from the clock on his phone, which he pulled from his pocket at 4:43 to see how long until the morning deliveries. He looked at the screen. He put it back in his pocket. He turned around to straighten the snack display he'd been restocking when everything became different.

Not in a way he could have described first. Later, yes. But in the moment it was simply: everything was different, and he stood very still, because moving seemed like exactly the wrong thing to do.

---

The floor had changed.

Not structurally. The lino was still lino, scuffed where the trolley had worn through the surface finish, slightly sticky near the drinks case from a spill two weeks ago that he'd cleaned but that had left something adhesive in the grout. The floor was the floor.

But now he could see what was beneath it.

Not through it — his eyes still told him the normal story, lino and grout and below that concrete and below that the building's foundation. But there was a second layer of information arriving that was not coming through his eyes, and it was arriving with a clarity and detail that made the first layer of information feel like a rough sketch.

Beneath the 7-Eleven's floor ran filaments.

Luminous was the closest word, but it wasn't quite right — they didn't produce light exactly, they had a quality of *being bright* that was distinct from emitting photons. They ran in geometric patterns: not random, not organic, not the branching of mycelium or the threading of roots. They were structured. Angles precise. The geometry hexagonal at their intersections, the lines between intersections running true.

He had known, from seventeen days of documentation, that there was *something* below the floor. He had had a heading, a rough direction, a sense of depth.

He had not known it looked like this.

---

He stood frozen for — he checked his phone later — eleven minutes.

He did not move deliberately. He stood because movement seemed like it might disturb something, though there was no evidence for this. He stood because his body had made a decision before his mind caught up, which was occasionally useful.

He looked at the filaments.

They were not all the same weight. Some ran lighter, thinner, with a quality of suggestion — the way a pencil line looks before it's gone over in ink. Others were dense and bright at their core, tapering as they extended, suggesting that they had sources, points of higher concentration from which they flowed outward.

The heavy nodes.

He recognized the pattern from the Codex diagram. The same distribution of emphasis, the same relationship between a concentration point and the lines extending from it. He was looking at the physical referent of something he'd spent twelve days studying as abstract notation.

The diagram was not a diagram of an idea.

It was a map of something real, and the real thing was under the floor of a convenience store in Wuhou District.

---

At 4:55 AM, a delivery truck pulled up outside.

Lin Mushi heard it and moved. He straightened the snack display with hands that were slightly unsteady and walked to the back to unlock the delivery door and sign for the morning stock and exchange four sentences with the driver about the cold and exchange another four sentences about the regional distribution backup that had meant late deliveries all week.

He did this correctly. He received the stock. He locked the door. He went back to the floor.

The filaments were still there.

He had half-expected them to be gone — an artifact of darkness and tiredness and the particular quiet of 4 AM that sometimes made the mind show you things that weren't there. They were not gone.

He got out his notebook.

---

> *4:44 AM, Day 17. New perceptual layer active. Appears to be a visual or quasi-visual overlay that renders the energy structure (Codex notation: likely the "Under-Web" — translating provisionally) directly as spatial information.*
>
> *Description: luminous filaments, hexagonal geometry, variable density. Heaviest near north-center of the store floor. Extend beyond the building's footprint in all directions.*
>
> *Relationship to Codex diagram: direct. The heavy nodes in the diagram correspond to concentration points in the spatial structure. The connecting lines correspond to the filaments. The diagram is a map. I am now standing in the territory.*
>
> *Duration of perception: ongoing. I have been perceiving this for 17 minutes as I write.*
>
> *Effect on function: none observed. I can see the filaments and also see the floor. The two layers of information are present simultaneously without interference.*
>
> *Emotional response: yes. Filing separately.*

He stared at the last line. He had never before written "filing separately" to indicate a response he wasn't yet ready to put words to.

He wrote one more line:

> *I am going to need a new notebook.*

He capped the pen, took his phone out of his pocket, opened the Codex, and held the diagram up alongside his view of the floor.

The correspondence was exact.

He had been studying a map for seventeen days. Now he was standing in the place the map described.

The city was beginning to wake up outside — the first sounds of delivery scooters, a distant bus, someone raising a shutter a block away. Five more minutes until his shift ended.

He closed the Codex. He put the phone in his pocket.

He walked to the back of the store and found the last three notebooks he kept in his supply bag, still in their packaging. He tore the plastic off the top one, wrote the date on the first page, and labeled the cover in the margin:

*Spatial Survey — Notebook 1.*

Five minutes. He had five minutes before Xiao Dong arrived for the morning shift.

He began sketching the geometry he'd seen, his pen moving fast, the lines going down with the accuracy of someone transcribing rather than imagining.

He had a map.

Now he could start the survey.
