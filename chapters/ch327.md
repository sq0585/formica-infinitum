# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Twenty-Seven — What He Notices Without Her

The difference didn't announce itself. It arrived, the way most of his significant discoveries did now, in the accumulated weight of small decisions made over several days, none of them individually remarkable enough to notice until he stepped back and looked at the pattern they'd formed together.

It started with the workshop district's power allocation, a routine decision he'd have ordinarily talked through with Chen Yufei over dinner before finalizing. Without her, he made it alone, faster than usual, weighing the workshop's needs against the residential wing's without the specific counterbalance her perspective usually provided.

He noticed it that same evening, reviewing the decision in his notebook, and found himself uncertain, for the first time in a long while, whether the choice he'd made was actually the right one, or simply the one that had felt right in the absence of anyone to test it against.

"You're doing that thing," Wei Canglan observed, two days later, watching him hesitate over an otherwise straightforward Circuit routing decision. "Second-guessing something you'd normally decide without a second thought."

"I think I am," Lin Mushi admitted. "I didn't expect her absence to change how I make decisions this much. I expected to miss her. I didn't expect to actually decide differently."

"How differently?"

He considered the question seriously, running back through the past several days' choices with the same careful accounting he brought to everything. "Faster, for one thing. She usually slows me down — not because she disagrees often, but because explaining a decision to her forces me to actually examine it before committing. Without that, I'm moving through choices more quickly than I used to, and I'm not entirely certain the speed is an improvement."

"What else?"

"I think I'm weighting things differently. Not worse, necessarily. Just — different. She tends to notice the human cost of a decision faster than I do on my own. Without her raising it, I catch myself defaulting toward efficiency more than I used to, and I don't always notice until after I've already committed."

---

He tested the observation deliberately over the following days, tracking his own decisions with the same careful discipline he'd once brought to teaching Amber-0 to formalize its judgment, and found, uncomfortably, that Wei Canglan's read had been accurate.

He wasn't making worse decisions without Chen Yufei present. Most of them held up fine on their own merits. But they were different decisions — the specific texture of her perspective, accumulated across two years of shared judgment, absent in a way that changed the shape of his reasoning even when the reasoning itself remained sound.

He raised it with Liang Jingzhi during one of their now-regular check-ins, curious whether her own experience as an Anchor gave her insight into the dynamic.

"That's the Anchor effect, functioning exactly as it should," she said, unsurprised by the observation. "You're not simply connected to us for stability during moments of crisis. The connection shapes your ordinary reasoning, continuously, in ways that are probably too subtle to notice under normal circumstances. Her absence removes a piece of that shaping. You're seeing, for the first time, how much of your everyday judgment was actually a collaboration rather than something you generated entirely on your own."

"That's a strange thing to realize," he admitted. "I didn't think of myself as making decisions collaboratively, most days. I thought I made them and then discussed them afterward."

"I don't think that distinction holds up as cleanly as it sounds," Liang Jingzhi said. "Discussing something afterward still shapes how you approach the next decision. The collaboration doesn't need to happen in real time to be genuine collaboration."

---

He wrote about it that evening, the entry longer and more careful than most of his recent ones, trying to capture something that felt, even as he wrote it, larger than its small, unglamorous examples suggested.

> *Three days without Chen Yufei, and I'm making different decisions — not worse, but different. Faster. Less attentive to human cost by default. I didn't know how much of my ordinary judgment was actually shaped by her presence until the presence was gone. I think this is what the Anchor bond actually is, underneath the crisis-management framing Meen-4 first explained it through. It's not a safety net for emergencies. It's a continuous, quiet collaboration, running in the background of every decision I make, whether I notice it happening or not.*

He closed the notebook, the residential wing quiet around him in a way that felt, now, distinctly, like something was missing, and found himself counting the days until she'd return with a specificity he hadn't quite allowed himself before tonight.

Eighteen more days. He could manage eighteen more days. But he understood now, more clearly than he had a week ago, exactly how much of what he built each day wasn't his alone, and had never quite been his alone, for longer than he'd fully appreciated.
