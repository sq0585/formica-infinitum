# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 474 — Building the Solution for Veth-Karun

He spent the following week doing the work he did best — turning an enormous, theoretical shape into something he could actually build, one careful requirement at a time.

The plan, once he'd assembled every piece of it, resolved into something he could finally hold in a single coherent structure, spread across the notebook's pages in the particular dense, methodical handwriting he reserved for problems he considered genuinely solved rather than merely understood.

*The solution,* he wrote, heading the entry the way he'd headed almost nothing else across five years of careful documentation. *Extend the Colony Link — Earth's coherent, sustained pattern of connected, distinct consciousnesses — to stellar scale, and beyond it, into the dissolution zone itself. Anchor the extended pattern directly against the wound Veth-Karun's failure left in the Void Lattice's fabric. Maintain individual coherence throughout the entire process using the Anchor Protocol — the exact mechanism Veth-Karun's account confirms was the one thing missing from every prior attempt.*

He read the summary back, satisfied with its clarity, and then sat with the specific, practical problem it immediately raised.

Five Anchors had carried him through every threshold so far — Dimensional Weaver, Colony Link, Planetary Architect, Stellar Scale itself, each crossing steadied by the same five careful, devoted people whose presence had, without his fully understanding it until Veth-Karun's account named the mechanism plainly, kept him coherent while everything else expanded.

This was not going to be the same scale of crossing.

*Observation 476,* he wrote, working the math through carefully, cross-referencing against everything the installation's stellar curriculum had taught him about how much perceptual and structural weight a single anchoring point could reasonably bear. *The dissolution zone spans a volume of space thousands of light-years across. A reference pattern sufficient to give the Lattice something genuinely coherent to reinstantiate against cannot be anchored through five points alone, however devoted those five points are. The structure requires distribution — many anchoring points, spread across the zone's own vast territory, each one holding a portion of the pattern's coherence rather than five people attempting to hold the entire weight centrally.*

He thought of Liang Jingzhi's steady, practical mind, of Dr. Shen Yue's careful instrumentation, of Chen Yufei's unwavering presence through every previous crossing, and understood, with a specific ache he hadn't expected, that this project was going to ask something of considerably more people than the five who had carried him this far.

*This requires more Anchors than five,* he wrote, finally, the sentence landing with more weight than its plain phrasing suggested it should. *Not replacing the five — nothing about this diminishes what Chen Yufei, Liang Jingzhi, Dr. Shen Yue, Wei Canglan, and Meen-4 have built with me across five years. But the dissolution zone's scale demands a distributed structure the five of them cannot reasonably provide alone, however willing they would be to try.*

He brought the completed plan to Meen-4 the next morning, laying it out methodically, the way he'd laid out every difficult problem across the past year.

"You understand what you're describing," she said, once she'd worked through the full structure. "Not five volunteers. Considerably more — enough distributed anchoring points to actually cover a zone this vast."

"I know."

"And you understand what you're asking of them, if they agree. Extending Colony Link anchoring to stellar scale, into direct proximity with a forty-million-year-old dissolution event. This is not a small commitment, Lin Mushi. This is, potentially, one of the most significant commitments anyone in Collective history has been asked to make."

"I know that too," he said quietly. "I don't have another version of this plan that costs less. I looked. Veth-Karun's failure was solitary preparation, and I'm not going to solve it by asking a handful of people to quietly carry a weight built for hundreds."

Meen-4 was silent for a long moment, studying him with the particular careful attention she reserved for decisions she considered genuinely significant.

"Then I believe," she said finally, "it's time to ask the Cultivation Research Collective properly — not five people this time. Everyone who might understand what you're offering, and might be willing to accept it."

He nodded, and felt, sitting with the shape of the request ahead of him, the specific weight of a project considerably larger than anything he had personally attempted before — not a threshold he alone would cross, but one he was about to ask an entire community to consider crossing beside him.
