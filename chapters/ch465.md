# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 465 — Preparing for the Pelagic Archive

The Pacific Ring's monitoring data gave him the Archive's precise coordinates within an hour of searching — four thousand seven hundred meters down, in a trench system he'd flown over a hundred times in his life without ever once suspecting what lay folded beneath it, dimensionally compressed the same way the Strata itself was compressed, a pocket of space considerably larger on the inside than the ocean floor above it would ever suggest.

He spent the better part of two days studying the lock system alone, the way he'd studied every unfamiliar Formican threshold since the very beginning — patiently, without assuming his existing fluency would simply carry over.

*Observation 456: the Pelagic Archive's dimensional lock is considerably older than anything I've encountered outside the installation's own architecture. The notation style shares structural features with both the Chengdu Deep Archive's grammar and the older curriculum I catalogued at the edge of the solar system. This suggests continuity — a single evolving tradition, rather than isolated, disconnected systems.*

The lock, once he understood its underlying logic, required something closer to Compression than to Dimensional Weaving proper — the same fundamental technique the Formicans had used, across their whole civilization's long retreat from the surface world, to fold vast spaces into forms small enough to survive discovery. He tested the principle carefully against the lock's structure, cross-referencing it against everything he'd learned building the Strata's own pocket-dimension architecture, and found, to his relief, that his current capability held it comfortably.

*Rule: I can open this door. The technique required is within my range. What waits on the other side of it is the actual unknown.*

He built the Circuit relay next — a careful extension of the Chitin Circuit network down through four thousand seven hundred meters of open ocean, anchored to the seafloor at a point directly above the Archive's compressed pocket, giving him a stable, monitored connection back to the Strata and everyone in it regardless of how deep the actual descent eventually carried him.

Amber-0 handled most of the technical construction, efficient and precise as always, though it flagged one detail he hadn't fully anticipated.

*Relay integrity at this depth will be lower than terrestrial or even off-world configurations,* the system reported. *Seawater's Veritonal attenuation is considerably higher than vacuum's. I recommend treating any relay signal from within the Archive itself as unreliable beyond simple status confirmation — yes, no, still present. Detailed communication may not be possible once you're inside.*

He absorbed that soberly, understanding what it meant in practice: once he crossed the threshold, whatever happened inside the Pelagic Archive, he would largely be facing it alone, with only the thinnest possible thread back to the people who loved him.

He briefed his Anchors that evening, gathering all five in the pocket dimension laboratory the way he had before the Stellar Scale crossing, laying out the plan plainly — the Archive's location, the lock's mechanism, the Surveyor's designation as the Collective's chosen witness, the relay's limited reliability once he was actually inside.

"You're doing the deliberate thing again," Chen Yufei observed, once he'd finished. "Telling us before, instead of after."

"I'm trying to make it a habit," he admitted. "I don't know yet what waits inside. I know it's the most sensitive document in Formican history, and I know an entire civilization considered it dangerous enough to seal for forty million years. I'd rather you all know exactly where I am and why, even if I can't promise a steady connection the whole way through."

Liang Jingzhi studied the relay diagram carefully, her practical attention settling on the specific gap in coverage. "If something goes wrong down there and we lose the signal entirely — what's the plan?"

"Wait," he said honestly. "The same plan as the installation, the same plan as every threshold before it. I don't have a version of this where you can follow me in. This one, I have to cross alone. I just don't want to cross it without you knowing, this time, exactly what you're waiting for."

Dr. Shen Yue was already adjusting her own instruments, calibrating something for the specific conditions of deep ocean transmission. "I'll have monitoring active at the relay's terrestrial end the whole time," she said. "Whatever thread we've got, I intend to read every bit of signal it gives us."

He nodded, grateful, and looked around the room at five careful, steady faces, understanding, the same way he'd understood it before every genuinely enormous threshold of the past five years, that whatever waited in the Archive's ancient dark, he was not, in any way that actually mattered, facing it as alone as the ocean's crushing depth might otherwise have suggested.

Tomorrow, he would go down.
