# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 412 — What the Other Zones Mean

The offer went out three days later, drafted with the same careful precision that had gone into every formal Collective communication Lin Mushi had sent since the Surveyor first arrived — a proposal to share Earth's Opening Submission structure, the strategic shape of its Veth-Karun presentation, and a candid account of what the Containment Orthodoxy's threshold argument had actually looked like from the receiving end, offered without cost or condition to any zone whose development trajectory suggested it might, eventually, face a similar review.

The response, when it came, was more complicated than he'd expected.

The Collective itself did not prohibit the offer — Meen-4 confirmed this within the first day, reading through the formal acknowledgment that arrived through the Witness Node with the particular flat neutrality that seemed to characterize most of the Surveyor's own communications. *Offer noted. No formal objection raised by the review body. Zones matching the specified development pattern will be informed of the offer's availability, at their own discretion whether to accept.*

The Orthodoxy's response was less neutral.

"They've filed a formal objection," Meen-4 said, translating the notation with a dryness that told him she found the timing almost predictable. "Not to the review's outcome — that battle is settled. To this specifically. Their argument: that a zone sharing its own review strategy with other developing zones constitutes a form of coordination the Collective's structure was never designed to accommodate, and that it sets a precedent for zones under scrutiny to organize collectively against the review process itself."

"Is that a fair reading?"

"It is not an unreasonable one, from their perspective," Meen-4 admitted. "Zones have historically been reviewed in isolation, each case considered entirely on its own evidence. What you are proposing is, in a small way, a genuine structural novelty — one zone, having survived its own reckoning, actively assisting others still approaching theirs. I do not believe this has happened before, in any record I hold."

"And the objection — does it stop the offer?"

"No. The Surveyor's response already noted it. Noted, not acted on. The objection exists in the formal record, available for the Orthodoxy to reference later if it ever becomes relevant to some future dispute, but it carries no immediate force to prevent what you've already offered."

He sat with the shape of that — an institution that had lost the argument it cared about most, now registering its displeasure through a smaller, procedural channel, unable to stop the thing it objected to but determined, still, to make its objection part of the permanent record.

"They're not going to let this go, are they," he said. "Not really. Not the sterilization argument, not this, not anything that touches what they actually believe."

"No," Meen-4 said simply. "I do not think they will. But I want you to understand something about what just happened, because I think it matters more than the objection itself." She paused, choosing her words with visible care. "You have, through this single offer, begun to change what a Monitored Development Zone can mean within the Collective's structure. Not merely a civilization being watched, cautiously, for signs of danger. A civilization capable of contributing something to the wider structure it exists inside — not just surviving scrutiny, but actively helping others survive theirs. That is not a small thing, Lin Mushi. Whether or not any zone accepts your offer, the offer itself has already changed how this body understands what a zone like Earth might eventually become."

He thought about that, about the two unnamed zones somewhere across the vast distances the Collective spanned, developing quietly toward a reckoning he hoped, now, they might face slightly less alone than he had faced his own.

"I hope someone accepts it," he said quietly. "I don't need credit for it. I just don't want another world to have to build the whole defense from nothing, the way we did, if there's anything at all I can do to make it easier."

"That," Meen-4 said, "is, I believe, precisely why the Orthodoxy objected in the first place. Not because the offer is dangerous. Because it is exactly the kind of thing they never expected a zone under their scrutiny to choose to do."
