# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 485 — Lady Shen's New Role

She appeared at the Collective's next formal review session without warning, seated among the neutral observers rather than among any faction Lin Mushi could immediately place her within, and he felt the old, familiar wariness surface before he'd fully registered why.

"Lady Shen," he said, once the session's opening formalities had concluded and the room's attention had briefly shifted toward informal conversation. "I didn't expect to see you here."

"I imagine not," she said, her expression carrying none of the cold calculation he remembered from Arc III, nor the honest, difficult vulnerability of their last real conversation in Arc V. Something else now — careful, guarded, deliberately unaligned. "I've been asked to consult on the dissolution zone repair's political dimensions. My knowledge of both human cultivation traditions and Collective political structures makes me, apparently, a useful resource, regardless of how either side currently feels about employing me."

"Which side asked you?"

"Neither, exactly. Both, in a sense." She studied him carefully before continuing. "I am not your ally, Lin Mushi. I want that understood plainly, before either of us wastes time pretending otherwise. I still do not fully trust what you're capable of, or what you might become if the current careful discipline you've maintained ever slips. But I am also, I've found, no longer willing to align myself with anyone actively opposed to you. The Containment Orthodoxy's continued objections strike me, increasingly, as institutional stubbornness rather than genuine caution. I find I cannot, in good conscience, lend my expertise to a position I no longer believe."

He absorbed that, understanding the specific, uncomfortable honesty of it — not reconciliation, not the clean narrative arc a simpler story might have offered, but something considerably more true to the actual texture of five years of complicated history.

"What does consulting on the political dimensions actually involve?" he asked.

"Translating what the repair effort means, in terms the wider Collective's more cautious factions can actually evaluate honestly," she said. "I understand the Orthodoxy's genuine concerns better than most, having once shared several of them myself. I can help the Surveyor's reports land with an audience that might otherwise dismiss them as one interested party's favorable spin."

"That sounds like it would actually help."

"It might," she said. "It might also simply give me a formal reason to keep watching you closely, which I confess remains part of my motivation regardless of how useful my consultation turns out to be for anyone else." A faint, dry note entered her voice, the closest thing to humor he'd heard from her in years. "I am aware of how that sounds. I am choosing to be honest about it anyway."

He found himself, despite everything, appreciating the honesty more than he'd have appreciated an easier, more comfortable reconciliation. "I'd rather have you watching closely and telling me honestly what concerns you," he said, "than have you pretending trust you don't actually feel."

"That," she said, "is possibly the first thing you've ever said to me that I agree with entirely."

They didn't speak further that session, though he noticed her, across the following weeks, present at every subsequent review, her contributions careful and precise, occasionally sharply critical in ways that made him reconsider details he might otherwise have taken for granted, never once softening her skepticism into easy support.

"She's not becoming an ally," he told Meen-4, after one particularly pointed exchange where Lady Shen had raised concerns about the 323's long-term wellbeing that even his own careful screening process hadn't fully anticipated.

"No," Meen-4 agreed. "I don't believe she ever will, fully. But I think you're beginning to understand something about her that took me considerably longer to learn myself — a distrustful ally who tells you the truth is, in many circumstances, considerably more valuable than a trusting one who tells you only what you want to hear."

He nodded, watching Lady Shen across the room, still guarded, still unconvinced, still — in her own complicated, reluctant way — genuinely useful to a project she had never once claimed to fully believe in.

Some relationships, he was learning, simply never resolved into anything cleaner than exactly this.

He found, walking home that evening, that he had stopped minding it. Five years ago, an unresolved distrust like Lady Shen's would have sat in him like an open wound, something he felt compelled to eventually heal or explain away. Now it simply sat there, honest and unfinished, one more genuine relationship among the many this extraordinary life had handed him — not every thread needed to end in reconciliation to still, somehow, matter.
