# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 498 — The Night Before

He asked to be alone that final night, and Chen Yufei, understanding immediately without needing the request explained further, kissed his forehead and left him to it, the pocket dimension laboratory settling into a stillness he hadn't let it hold in longer than he could easily remember.

He didn't work. There was nothing left to prepare that hadn't already been prepared, no observation left to record that would meaningfully change what tomorrow required of him. He simply sat, for a while, in the warm, familiar light, and let himself, finally, feel the full weight of everything the past four and a half years had built toward this exact, quiet evening.

He found the notebook's very first pages eventually, the ones he rarely returned to anymore, the handwriting there younger and less certain than anything he'd written since, a boy still learning the shape of a Codex he didn't understand, copying down a dying man's final message with hands that had, he recalled now, been shaking considerably harder than they were tonight.

*I hope you are curious,* Ren Wuji's message read, exactly as it always had. *Everything depends on it.*

He read it slowly, the way he hadn't allowed himself to read it in years, and found the words carried a different weight now than they had at eighteen, terrified and alone in a convenience store with a dying stranger's hand still warm against his own. He understood now what Ren Wuji had actually been asking for — not merely intellectual curiosity, not simply the willingness to learn, but the whole enormous, patient discipline that curiosity properly held eventually became: the choice to keep asking questions even when the questions grew large enough to frighten him, and the equally important choice to sometimes, deliberately, set a question down until he'd earned the right to actually face it.

*I was curious,* he thought, setting the page down carefully. *I still am. I think that's the truest thing I can say about myself, at the end of all of this.*

He reached next for the translation of Veth-Karun's account, the copy he'd made for himself after the Pelagic Archive's cold, patient chamber had first given him the words. He read it again, slowly, the way he'd read it aloud in that ancient room months ago, letting each line settle exactly as heavily as it deserved to settle.

*I did not have anything I refused to release. I had only knowledge. I should have held someone.*

He sat with the sentence a long while, and then, finally, let himself do the thing he'd been quietly building toward the entire evening without fully naming it: he reached, gently, toward the five presences that had carried him through every threshold since Colony Link itself. Chen Yufei, steady and warm, asleep now in the next room but present, still, in whatever way the Anchor Protocol had always made her present. Liang Jingzhi, extended even now, permanently, into the dissolution zone's vast, patient territory, holding the deepest part of an impossible structure with the same plain competence she brought to everything. Dr. Shen Yue, her instruments humming quietly somewhere nearby, already watching for tomorrow's first readings. Wei Canglan, his formal objection filed permanently into the record, exactly where it belonged. Meen-4, patient and ancient and steadfastly present, the same as she had been since the very first confused weeks of this entire extraordinary life.

*I am holding five people,* he thought, and found the thought arrived not as strategy, not as technique, but simply as fact — the plain, grounding truth of a life he had built, deliberately, one careful relationship at a time, specifically so that this exact moment would never have to be faced the way Veth-Karun had faced theirs.

He opened the notebook to a fresh page, the pen resting lightly in his hand, and found, for the first time in four and a half years of careful, faithful documentation, that he didn't need to write anything at all.

Some things, he understood, sitting in the quiet, patient dark, simply didn't require a record. They only required, finally, the courage to be lived.

He closed the notebook, the blank page still blank, and let himself, at last, rest.
