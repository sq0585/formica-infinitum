# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 464 — The Pelagic Archive Approval

The notification arrived through the Witness Node on an otherwise unremarkable morning, formatted in the same dense diplomatic notation he'd learned to read, slowly and carefully, across the whole of Arc V's long review.

Meen-4 brought the translated document to him herself, and something in the particular briskness of her arrival told him, before she'd said a word, that whatever it contained mattered considerably more than the ordinary administrative traffic that usually crossed her desk.

"The Pelagic Archive review has concluded," she said, setting the document down. "Sixty days, as promised, though I confess I'd stopped counting precisely, given everything else occupying both of us recently."

He read through it carefully, the notation resolving into meaning the way it always did now, fluent rather than effortful, five years of practice finally paying its full dividend.

*Petition reviewed. Access to the Pelagic Archive, submitted by petitioner Lin Mushi in connection with ongoing Void Lattice repair research, is granted conditionally. Access is limited to the petitioner and one designated observer of the Collective's choosing. The designated observer for this access is the Surveyor.*

He read the final line twice, feeling something shift in his chest that wasn't quite surprise.

"The Surveyor," he said aloud. "Not a committee. Not a specialized researcher. The Surveyor itself."

"That is significant," Meen-4 said, settling into the chair across from him with the particular stillness she reserved for genuinely important developments. "The Surveyor's role in Earth's review formally concluded at Arc V's close. It has no standing obligation to remain involved in anything concerning this system any further. Its designation here means the Collective specifically considers this access — and by extension, your entire Veth-Karun research — significant enough to warrant its personal, continued attention."

"Is that good news or concerning news?"

"Both, likely, in the proportions the Surveyor's involvement has always carried." She considered the document further, her expression thoughtful. "It is not malevolent. You know this already, from the review itself. But it is thorough, and it does not attend anything it considers unimportant. Its presence at the Pelagic Archive means the Collective as a whole will be watching what you find there considerably more closely than a routine access approval would ordinarily warrant."

He set the document down and let himself sit with the weight of it — the Pelagic Archive, sealed since long before the disclosure protocol, before the Surveyor's arrival, before any of the past year's careful, hard-won progress, finally, formally opening, with the single most significant entity in his entire relationship with the Collective standing beside him when it did.

"What do we actually know about what's inside it," he asked. "Beyond Veth-Karun's account, I mean. Meen-4, you accessed part of it once before, didn't you — Arc IV, before I understood what any of it meant."

"I confirmed its existence and its general contents," she said. "I did not read the account itself. That required a level of authorization neither of us possessed at the time, and frankly, I was not certain either of us was ready for what it might contain. Some doors, as you've learned yourself more than once, deserve to open on their own schedule."

He nodded, recognizing his own hard-won discipline reflected back at him, and felt, underneath the anticipation, a specific thread of apprehension he hadn't quite expected. Forty million years sealed. The most sensitive document in Formican history, by the Collective's own careful classification. Whatever Veth-Karun had left behind, in the final 0.7 seconds before a dissolution that had scarred the galaxy's own fabric, was not going to be small.

"When can we go," he asked.

"The approval specifies no deadline, but I would not delay unnecessarily," Meen-4 said. "The Archive is located four thousand seven hundred meters beneath the Pacific Ocean floor. You will need to study its dimensional lock system before attempting entry, and I would strongly recommend briefing your Anchors fully before you go — this is not a threshold to cross without people who love you knowing exactly where you've gone and why."

"I'll start today," he said, already reaching for a fresh page in the notebook, the specific, familiar momentum of a genuine breakthrough's aftermath settling into him — not the vertigo of the discovery itself, but the steady, disciplined work of actually preparing to meet it properly.

Somewhere beneath an ocean he'd crossed countless times without ever once suspecting what waited under it, a door forty million years sealed was, finally, formally, ready to open.
