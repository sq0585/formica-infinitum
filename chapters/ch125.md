# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Twenty-Five — The Sunday Call

His mother called at 10 AM.

He had known she would — the Sunday call had not missed a week in over a year — and he had set his Strata work aside and was sitting at his desk with tea prepared when the phone rang.

"You sound different," she said, after the usual opening exchanges.

"How?"

"Like someone who has a lot going on but is managing it." A pause. "Not stressed. Occupied."

"That's accurate," he said.

"The research?"

"Yes. Things have developed significantly in the past month." He paused. "I have people working with me now. On different parts of it."

"You have colleagues?"

He thought about Chen Yufei in the archive, reading Formican agricultural records with the same absorbed attention she brought to cultivation technique. He thought about Gao with his notebook at his own desk two blocks away. He thought about Elder Zhao's careful questions at the tea house.

"Some of them are colleagues," he said. "Some are more complicated than that."

She was quiet for a moment. "Is complicated bad?"

"No. Complicated is what happens when things matter." He picked up his tea. "How's the garden?"

They talked about the garden for ten minutes — the early spring preparations, the decision about whether to try tomatoes again this year or accept that the container drainage was never going to be adequate. She had opinions about this, well-researched opinions, and he listened and asked questions because her expertise in the domain was genuine and the domain was not one he had knowledge of.

This was, he had realized over the past several months, one of the things he found genuinely satisfying about the Sunday calls: his mother knew a great deal about things he knew nothing about, and that ratio was different from most of his current relationships. Chen Yufei he was teaching. Elder Zhao was, to some degree, deferring to him. Gao was a peer in capability but less informed about the specific situation. His mother talked to him as someone who had nothing to prove to him and was simply interested in the conversation.

It was restoring in a way he hadn't found a label for.

"The cousin's wedding," she said, transitioning with the ease of someone who tracked multiple threads simultaneously. "Mei is already worried about the food."

"The food?"

"The caterer canceled. Three weeks out. She's trying to find a new one." A sigh. "Your aunt is not handling it well."

"The wedding is still happening."

"Of course it is. It just requires — management." Another sigh, this one lighter. "You'll come and be a calming presence."

"I'm known for being a calming presence," he said.

She laughed — the warm sound that had been a constant of his childhood. "You're known for being very quiet and then saying something so accurate that everyone stops arguing." She paused. "That counts."

He thought about Chen Yufei describing his involvement in their alliance: *he negotiates like someone who knows his leverage.* He thought about Axis-7's first three weeks of observation before she spoke. He thought about the forty-seven people who were going to receive a document that said, accurately, that the foundations of their institutions were built on misunderstanding.

"I'll come," he said. "It's—" he checked the calendar — "three weeks from Saturday."

"Yes. I'll save you the good noodles."

"Standard," he said.

"I know you've been eating standard. I sent you money."

He looked at the message notification he'd been ignoring for a week. She had, in fact, sent money with a note that said: *for the good noodles.*

"You didn't have to do that," he said.

"I know." The warmth in her voice was the specific warmth of someone who had decided something and was comfortable with having decided it. "Buy the good ones. You're working hard."

He was quiet for a moment, with the full perception of what was in her voice — the care, the slight worry underneath the care, the decision to express care through practical action rather than direct statement. He had been reading this clearly for months now. He did not find it intrusive. He found it, in the way of things that had always been true and that he was only now able to perceive precisely, simply right.

"Thank you," he said.

They talked for another twenty minutes.

After she hung up, he sat at his desk and held the phone for a moment.

He wrote, briefly, in the observation notebook:

> *Sunday call. She sent money for noodles. I will buy the good ones.*
>
> *The Colony Link emotional field in phone communication: I can now read it with the same clarity I read it in person. The medium doesn't significantly attenuate it.*
>
> *What was in her voice: care, low-level worry, satisfaction with the practical action of sending the money, contentment with the garden preparations.*
>
> *What was not in her voice, notably: anxiety about the upcoming weeks. She doesn't know what the upcoming weeks look like for me.*
>
> *This gap between what I'm managing and what she knows is something I've been carrying for nine months. It's getting heavier.*
>
> *Not a plan to close it yet. Just noting: it's getting heavier.*

He put the notebook aside.

He went to the shop and bought the good noodles with his mother's money.

He ate them standing over the sink at noon, tasting the better broth, and found that this was, in the middle of everything, exactly the right thing to be doing.
