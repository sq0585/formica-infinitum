# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Ninety-Nine — Wei Canglan's Anger

It came out the way Chen Yufei had warned him it eventually would — not through any deliberate investigation, but through the ordinary, unglamorous mechanics of a small family's finances eventually being examined closely enough that an anonymous transfer became visible.

Wei Canglan found him in the archive on a Thursday evening, his expression carrying a controlled intensity that Lin Mushi recognized immediately as something different from his usual careful composure.

"You transferred money to my parents," Wei Canglan said. No greeting, no preamble.

Lin Mushi set down the Circuit design he'd been working on.

"Yes," he said. He did not deny it, did not pretend confusion, did not try to soften the admission with immediate explanation.

"I found out today," Wei Canglan said. "My mother mentioned the account balance was higher than it should have been, given her wages and the rent. I looked into it. The transfer traced back to an account Chen Yufei helped set up, which traced back to you."

Lin Mushi said nothing. He had decided, weeks ago, accepting the risk Chen Yufei had named, that if this moment came, he would not defend the choice preemptively. He would let Wei Canglan say what he needed to say first.

"You should have told me," Wei Canglan said, and now the controlled intensity was sharpening into something closer to genuine anger.

"Why?" Lin Mushi asked. Not defensively — a real question, wanting to understand the actual shape of what Wei Canglan needed him to hear.

"Because I needed to be angry at you," Wei Canglan said, "and I can't be angry at someone who did that."

The words landed with a specific, unexpected weight — not the accusation Lin Mushi had braced for, but something more complicated and more honest.

"Tell me more about that," Lin Mushi said quietly.

Wei Canglan was silent for a long moment, the anger visibly working against something else underneath it — gratitude, maybe, or the specific discomfort of holding two contradictory feelings at once without either one canceling the other out.

"I've spent two months," he said finally, "carrying this complicated mix of things. Gratitude that you took the document seriously when I asked. Respect for how you handled the verification, the public statement, all of it. And underneath that — resentment, that my decision, which I made with clear eyes, still cost my family everything it cost them. I needed somewhere to put that resentment. I'd been quietly directing some of it at you, in a way I hadn't fully admitted to myself, because you were the catalyst even though you never told me to act."

He paused.

"And now I find out you've been quietly paying my mother's rent gap for two months, anonymously, specifically so I wouldn't have to know and feel obligated, specifically so my parents wouldn't have to perform gratitude to someone connected to the reason they lost their home in the first place." He looked at Lin Mushi directly. "I can't be angry at you for that. I want to be. I don't have anywhere to put it anymore."

Lin Mushi held his gaze.

"I'm sorry," he said. "Not for the document, or for the truth in it. For the fact that you needed somewhere to put something difficult, and I took that place away from you without asking whether you wanted me to."

Wei Canglan looked at him for a long moment, something in his expression finally settling, the sharp edge of the anger softening into something more like exhaustion.

"You couldn't have known I needed it," he said. "I didn't know I needed it until just now, saying it out loud."

They sat with this together, the archive's amber light steady around them.

"Thank you," Wei Canglan said eventually, quietly. "For my parents. And — I think — for not telling me, even though it complicated something I needed to feel."

Lin Mushi nodded.

It was, he understood, the most human moment of the entire saga — not a political victory, not a successful counter-document, not a confrontation survived. Just one person's inability to hold both gratitude and resentment cleanly, and the quiet, difficult honesty of saying so out loud.
