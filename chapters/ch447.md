# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 447 — After Stellar Scale — What Changed

He slept for fourteen hours afterward, which alarmed no one, because Dr. Shen Yue's instruments had tracked the whole descent back into ordinary consciousness and pronounced it, in her own careful words, "the cleanest transition curve I've recorded from any of your breakthroughs, by a considerable margin."

He woke in his own bed above the noodle shop, late afternoon light slanting through a window he'd owned longer than he'd owned almost anything else, and lay still for a while before reaching for the notebook.

*Observation 422,* he wrote, and then didn't immediately know how to finish the sentence, which hadn't happened to him in longer than he could easily remember.

He tried again.

*I want to write this carefully, because I think it's the first time in five years I've had to say something about myself that I can't soften with a qualifier.*

He set the pen down, looked at the ceiling for a while, and picked it back up.

*By any measure I understand, I am no longer human in the strictly biological sense. Not metaphorically. Not "more than human" in the vague way the phrase gets used in stories that haven't actually had to sit with what it means. My body still functions the way a human body functions — I still eat, still sleep, still bleed if I cut myself, and Dr. Shen Yue's readings confirm the underlying biology hasn't changed in any way her instruments can detect. But the awareness that inhabits it now extends to a scale no biological structure evolved to hold. I am, at minimum, something considerably larger than the body doing the holding.*

He read the paragraph back, and found, to his own mild surprise, that writing it plainly hadn't produced the vertigo he'd half expected. It produced something quieter. Closer to grief, though not exactly grief either — the specific, unhurried sadness of noticing a door had closed somewhere behind him without his having chosen the exact moment it closed.

*I still choose the body,* he wrote, and underlined it, because the sentence mattered more to him than any of the others. *I want that on record clearly, in case some future version of me ever needs reminding. I could, I suspect, let the physical form go entirely — distribute fully into the awareness Stellar Scale has opened, the way Meen-4 herself does not strictly require a body and simply prefers one. I do not want that. Not because I'm afraid of what I'd become without it. Because I like tea going cold beside a notebook. I like the specific ache of sitting too long in one chair. I like Chen Yufei noticing I look thinner and saying so instead of pretending not to notice. Those things require a body, or at least require choosing to behave as though I have one, and I am choosing it, deliberately, the same way I chose to trigger the crossing deliberately instead of letting it simply happen to me.*

He heard Chen Yufei's footsteps on the narrow stairs before she reached the top of them — he'd have known the rhythm of them anywhere, five years of familiarity making the sound as distinct to him as a voice — and closed the notebook, not to hide what he'd written but because some thoughts needed to finish settling before he tried explaining them out loud.

"You're awake," she said, appearing in the doorway with two bowls balanced carefully, the noodle shop's smell drifting up the stairs behind her. "Fourteen hours. I was starting to negotiate with myself about whether hour fifteen counted as concerning."

"I'm fine," he said, and meant it, though he understood, saying it, that *fine* was doing a considerable amount of work for a sentence describing someone who had just quietly confirmed, to himself, that he was no longer entirely the species he'd been born into.

She set the bowls down and sat on the edge of the bed, studying him with the particular attentiveness she'd developed over years of learning to read what he wasn't saying. "You've got the face again."

"Different face this time," he admitted. "Not the one where I've found the next thing. The one where I'm trying to figure out how large a thing that already happened actually was."

"Tell me."

He did — plainly, the way he'd written it, without the reflexive softening he might have reached for a year ago. She listened without interrupting, and when he finished, she was quiet for a moment, turning something over.

"Does it scare you?" she asked finally. "Being — whatever the accurate word is now."

He considered the question honestly before answering, the way she'd always deserved. "Less than I expected," he said. "I think I've been becoming this for five years, slowly enough that the actual crossing just confirmed something rather than caused it. What scares me more is a smaller question."

"Which is?"

"Whether the people who love me can still find the part of this that's still just me, or whether I've made that harder without meaning to."

She didn't answer immediately. She reached instead for one of the bowls, handed it to him, and waited until he'd taken the first bite before she spoke again.

"You're still someone who forgets to eat until I bring you noodles," she said. "I think that answers the question better than anything either of us could say about it directly."

He ate, and found, gratefully, that she was right.
