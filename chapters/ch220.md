# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty — Consent Protocol

The appendix, once he actually read it, took him most of the night and gave him less comfort than he'd hoped for.

Document 9's supplementary section described the Colony Link's expansion in clinical, unhurried Formican prose — the same register the Codex used for everything, as though the dissolution of privacy among a community of distributed minds was simply another engineering parameter to be specified rather than a question with any moral weight attached to it. The Formicans, he understood slowly, working through the notation, had never built privacy into the Colony Link's architecture in the first place. They hadn't needed to. Their entire civilization had been organized around the assumption that shared awareness was a feature, not an intrusion.

He was not building a Formican colony. He was asking human individuals, each with their own private interior life, to live inside a structure that had never been designed with their kind of privacy in mind.

The appendix did, eventually, describe a filtering mechanism — a way for an individual within range to signal a specific Qi pattern that the Carrier's interface could learn to recognize and exclude. Opt-out, in practice, though the documentation never used language that direct.

He spent the next two weeks building it.

The technical work was slower than he'd expected, mostly because the filter had to be learned individually for each person who wanted it — a unique pattern, deliberately generated, distinct enough from ambient noise that his interface could reliably distinguish *I am choosing not to be sensed* from simple emotional quiet. He tested it first on himself, then asked Chen Yufei to try it, refining the recognition threshold across a dozen failed attempts before it held reliably.

He announced it the moment it worked, without waiting for it to be polished further.

---

Three people opted out.

Su Lan was the first, unsurprising given how carefully she'd always guarded her own interior — she learned the pattern in a single afternoon and used it immediately, and Lin Mushi felt the specific, deliberate quiet of her absence settle into the edge of his awareness like a door closing gently rather than slamming.

One of the independent cultivators followed within the week. The third was someone Lin Mushi hadn't fully expected — Wei Canglan, who'd been the one to force the disclosure in the first place, and who explained his choice with the same blunt honesty he brought to most things.

"It's not about trust," he said, when Lin Mushi asked. "I trust you more now than before, actually, because you built this instead of just apologizing and hoping I'd forget about it. I just don't want anyone reading my feelings continuously, even someone I trust. That's not a statement about you. It's a statement about what I want for myself."

"That's a completely sufficient reason."

"I know. I'm not asking permission. I'm explaining, because you went to the trouble of building something that lets me choose, and I think you deserve to know why I'm using it instead of just disappearing from your awareness without comment."

Lin Mushi considered this — three people, out of nine, choosing the quiet door rather than the open one, and found that he respected the choice more than he regretted the gap it left in his perception.

He wrote it into his notebook that evening, the rotation schedule for ley-line maintenance sitting on the facing page, two separate kinds of infrastructure he was learning, slowly, to build with the same careful attention.

> *Three opt-outs: Su Lan, one independent, Wei Canglan. The filter works. It's not perfect — I suspect there are edge cases I haven't found yet, moments of strong enough feeling that might leak through the pattern recognition before it catches them. I'll need to keep testing.*
>
> *Wei Canglan called it insufficient, before he used it. He's probably right that it is. But insufficient and present is still better than absent entirely, and I'd rather build the imperfect version people can actually use than wait for a perfect one that exists only in theory while everyone's privacy stays unprotected in the meantime.*
>
> *Note: ask the others, periodically, whether the filter is still working for them. Don't assume silence means consent. Check.*

He closed the notebook, the hall around him quiet in the particular way it was quiet now — three small, deliberate absences sitting at the edges of an awareness that otherwise still held everyone else, a structure built not to solve the problem completely but to make sure, at minimum, that nobody was forced to live inside it without having been asked.

It would have to do, for now. He suspected, turning the page toward tomorrow's maintenance schedule, that it would not be the last time this particular lesson found him again in some new and unfamiliar shape.
