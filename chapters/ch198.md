# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Ninety-Eight — The Alliance Offer

Three independent cultivators arrived at the thin section's entry point on a Tuesday evening, having reached out through the assembly's shared channel two days earlier with a request that was, in its way, more significant than anything the political maneuvering of the past month had produced.

Chen Yufei had vetted them carefully before extending an invitation to meet in person — three practitioners, each from a different tradition, each exiled from their original Court or sect specifically for the crime of publicly verifying the documents' claims when their institutions had moved toward suppression. A former Verdant Stream Collective ecologist whose public confirmation of the cosmological notation had led to quiet expulsion from her regional network. A Void Mirror Sect practitioner — rare enough that his presence alone was noteworthy, given how little anyone understood about that sect's internal workings — who had simply disappeared from his sect's records after voicing support for the document's accuracy. And a younger Iron Meridian practitioner, one of the three Elder Zhao had mentioned months ago, who had finally reached the end of what quiet institutional pressure could be tolerated and had chosen to leave rather than continue absorbing it.

They stood in the outer hall, having crossed the threshold with Chen Yufei's assistance, and Lin Mushi watched them take in the scale of the space with the same absorbed stillness everyone experienced on first entry.

"Thank you for meeting us," the Verdant Stream practitioner said, once the initial adjustment had passed. Her name, she'd told them in advance, was Su Lan. "We didn't come to ask for shelter, exactly, though I won't pretend the practical situation isn't part of it."

"Tell me what you came to ask," Lin Mushi said.

She glanced at the other two, a quick check that seemed to confirm shared resolve rather than seek permission.

"We'd like to join you," she said. "Not as disciples. We're not looking for a new master to replace the ones who failed us. We're looking for colleagues — people who understand what we've lost and what we've chosen, who are building something we'd like to be part of building rather than simply receiving."

The Void Mirror practitioner, who had said almost nothing since arriving, spoke next, his voice quiet but precise. "I have spent eleven years inside a sect that taught me half of what I now understand to be a single, complete technique, fragmented and obscured for reasons no one in my sect could explain even when pressed. I do not want to spend the next eleven years inside a different institution that does the same thing, however well-intentioned."

Lin Mushi nodded slowly, understanding the specific weight of what was being asked — and what was specifically not being asked.

"What I can offer," he said carefully, "is access. To the archive, to corrected technique, to the genuine understanding this entire situation has been built around. What I can't offer is a guarantee of anything beyond that — not institutional protection, not certainty about what structure eventually emerges from the assembly's loose beginning, not a promise that joining this means joining something stable."

"We understand that," the younger Iron Meridian practitioner said. "I think — speaking only for myself — that's actually part of why we're here rather than somewhere else. We've had enough of institutions promising stability they didn't actually have."

Lin Mushi looked at the three of them for a long moment.

"Then yes," he said. "Welcome. Not as disciples. As colleagues, exactly as you've asked. We'll figure out the rest of the structure together, the same way the assembly has been trying to."

Su Lan looked relieved in a way that went beyond the words exchanged — the relief, Lin Mushi understood, of someone who had spent weeks uncertain whether the choice they'd made would land anywhere at all, finally finding that it had.

That evening marked, quietly and without ceremony, the first time the Strata held intentional residents beyond Lin Mushi himself — the first voluntary community formation in a space that had, until recently, been simply his own solitary project.

He noted this in his notebook before he slept that night.

> *Three new colleagues. Not disciples. The distinction matters, and I intend to keep holding it carefully as this grows.*
>
> *Something is being built. I don't fully know its final shape yet. I am increasingly certain it's the right direction.*
