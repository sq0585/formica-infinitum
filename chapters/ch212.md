# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twelve — The Stone Hall Sect

The Circuit network flagged the anomaly at 6:42 in the morning, a small persistent signature sitting at the edge of the district's monitored range that hadn't been there the night before.

Lin Mushi checked it from his apartment, the readout simple enough on his phone screen: three Qi signatures, stationary, positioned roughly a hundred meters from the convenience store's entrance, holding steady in a pattern that suggested deliberate surveillance rather than passing presence.

He recognized the signature profile within minutes of comparing it against his notes. Stone Hall Sect — the harshest and least subtle of the three remaining coalition Courts, their cultivation style built around raw structural force rather than the kind of refined control the Celestial Compass or even Iron Meridian practitioners favored. He'd catalogued the profile weeks ago, during the early days of trying to understand who he was actually dealing with.

He finished his tea before he did anything about it.

There was no urgency in three stationary signatures watching an entrance from a hundred meters away. Urgency was for threats already in motion. This was reconnaissance, patient and clumsy in roughly equal measure — clumsy enough that his network had caught it within minutes of activation, patient enough that whoever had sent them expected to wait.

He routed around them on his way to his shift, taking the longer path through the back alley that put two buildings between his approach and their line of sight, and arrived at the store eleven minutes later than usual without ever entering the watchers' field of view.

He logged the detour in his notebook anyway. Some habits were worth keeping even when the immediate threat was minor.

---

Chen Yufei confirmed it that evening, having run her own check through channels he didn't have access to.

"Stone Hall, confirmed," she said. "Three mid-rank cultivators, rotating in eight-hour shifts. They've been there since before dawn."

"Surveillance, not interdiction."

"For now." She set her phone down on the counter, her expression careful. "That's the part that should bother you more than it seems to. Surveillance is what you do before you decide interdiction is worth the cost. Three Courts coalition, watching the only entrance they know about, building a picture of your movements."

He considered this. The watchers hadn't approached. Hadn't attempted contact, hadn't tried to follow him past the alley detour, hadn't done anything beyond simply being present and recording what they could observe from a hundred meters out.

"What happens when watching stops being enough for them?"

"That's exactly the question," Chen Yufei said. "Right now they're gathering information because information is cheap and doesn't require justifying to anyone above them. The moment someone in that chain decides watching isn't producing useful results fast enough, the next step is closer contact. Following. Maybe attempting to intercept you somewhere less public than the store."

"Or attempting to find another way into the Strata."

She went still at that, the implication landing visibly. "Can they find it? Without you?"

"I don't think so. Not easily. The threshold isn't marked in any way that would be obvious to someone who didn't already know what they were looking for, and I've never seen evidence the Courts mapped it precisely before I started using it." He thought about Axis-7's earlier warnings, the careful tactical assessments he'd been collecting without fully appreciating their weight. "But I don't actually know that for certain. And I'd rather not find out by being wrong."

"So what do we do?"

"For now, nothing different. I'll keep routing around them. The network will keep logging their presence and rotation patterns — that's useful information too, in its own right." He looked at the readout still glowing faintly on his phone screen, three small steady points a hundred meters from where he stood. "But I want to start thinking seriously about what happens if watching stops being the worst thing they're willing to do."

Chen Yufei didn't argue with the caution. She simply nodded, the careful nod of someone filing the conversation away under a heading she suspected they'd return to sooner than either of them wanted.

Outside, the three signatures held their positions, patient and unhurried, watching an entrance that had already, quietly, stopped being the only thing worth watching.
