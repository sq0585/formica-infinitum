# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 421 — The World Begins to Know

The paper became the most-read document on the internet within seventy-two hours, a fact Lin Mushi absorbed through Meen-4's careful monitoring of global attention rather than through any direct sense of it himself — the Colony Link did not extend to public sentiment, only to the Strata's own carefully bounded network, and the sudden, overwhelming scale of the wider world's reaction remained, in a strange way, something he had to learn about secondhand, like weather happening somewhere he couldn't directly feel.

Discussion of it appeared in parliaments within a week — not formal debate yet, mostly confused, urgent questions raised by legislators who had clearly not expected to spend their morning briefings parsing a physics paper describing pocket-dimension habitation and hidden cultivation networks beneath a major Chinese city. Three governments formally requested contact within two weeks of publication, their inquiries arriving through official diplomatic channels that Fang Ruxue, with her years of careful institution-building, found herself scrambling to actually receive and process.

"We don't have a protocol for this," she admitted to him, four days after the first formal inquiry arrived, a stack of translated diplomatic correspondence spread across the administrative chamber's table in a disorder that felt, to Lin Mushi, uncharacteristically messy for a woman who prided herself on careful order.

"I know," he said. "We need one."

It was not triumph, watching the world absorb the truth Dr. Shen Yue had handed it. It was, instead, an enormous, sprawling complexity that seemed to multiply faster than either of them could organize a response to it. Scientists demanded access to verify the claims independently. Governments demanded briefings. Ordinary people, by the millions, flooded every available channel with questions ranging from the deeply technical to the frankly absurd, and somewhere in the middle of all of it, actual fear began to surface — not universal, not even majority, but present, loud, and impossible to simply reassure away with a single careful statement.

He watched footage, gathered by Meen-4 from public broadcasts, of news anchors trying to explain Veritonal Energy to audiences who had, three days earlier, never heard the term. He watched clips of protests forming outside government buildings, some demanding transparency, others demanding the entire thing be shut down and suppressed, contradictory crowds shouting past each other in the specific chaos of a species trying to metabolize something too large to process quickly.

"This is what I was trying to avoid," he said quietly, watching one particular clip a third time — a crowd, genuinely frightened, chanting something about hidden threats and government cover-ups that had nothing directly to do with the Strata but drew, unmistakably, on the same fear the disclosure had unlocked.

"I know," Chen Yufei said, sitting beside him, watching the same footage with the same careful attention. "But I don't think it was avoidable forever. You built something extraordinary, Lin Mushi. Extraordinary things don't stay contained indefinitely, no matter how carefully you manage the release."

"Do you think she was wrong to do it this way?"

Chen Yufei considered the question honestly, the way she considered most things that mattered. "I think she was right that people deserved to know," she said finally. "I think the timing was hers to choose, not yours, and I think the chaos following it doesn't retroactively make her decision wrong. Chaos isn't the same as harm. It's just chaos. People are frightened and confused and asking a lot of questions all at once. That's uncomfortable to watch. It's not the same as catastrophe."

He held onto that distinction, turning it over in the days that followed, watching the initial wave of panic and confusion slowly, gradually, begin to settle into something more like sustained, difficult, genuine public conversation — still chaotic, still far from resolved, but not the catastrophic unraveling he'd spent months carefully trying to avoid.

"We're not going to control this the way we controlled everything else," he told Fang Ruxue, once the first wave had begun to settle enough to actually think clearly. "It's out. It's not going back. All we get to control now is how honestly we respond to what people are asking."

"Then let's respond honestly," she said, already reaching for the stack of diplomatic correspondence still waiting on the table. "Starting with whoever sent this first inquiry. They deserve a real answer, not a delay."

He nodded, and reached for the papers alongside her, understanding, with a clarity that felt both frightening and oddly settling, that the world he'd spent three years protecting from itself had, finally, begun the long, difficult work of learning what it actually was.
