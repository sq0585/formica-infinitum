# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Thirty-Five — Peng Shaohua's Report

The Iron Meridian recall order arrived on the same day as the document release.

Not a coincidence, but not coordination either — the recall order had been building for two months, since the senior Iron Meridian practitioners had noticed Chen Yufei's accelerated progress and had begun asking questions she hadn't answered satisfactorily. The document's release on Tuesday simply meant the order arrived in the same news cycle as everything else.

Chen Yufei called at 7 PM.

"The recall order is formal," she said. "Signed by three Elders including Elder Ma." A pause. "Elder Ma specifically is disappointing."

He had heard Chen Yufei mention Elder Ma before — the practitioner who had spent fifty years correctly developing her channels, who had told a twenty-two-year-old Chen Yufei that she trusted her channels to do what she needed. The one whose technique had been accurate for reasons neither of them had understood.

"The terms?" he asked.

"Report to the Iron Meridian's review committee within thirty days. Provide full disclosure of my activities and associations since my independent operational status began. Failure to comply: formal expulsion and Qi-seal." A pause. "The Qi-seal is the part that's concerning."

A Qi-seal was something he'd read about in Chen Yufei's earlier briefings on the Courts: an imposed restriction on a practitioner's channel access, achieved through a specific Qi manipulation technique that the senior courts had developed and maintained as institutional authority. It was not permanent — a sealed practitioner could unseal if they complied with the institution's conditions — but it was effective, and it was an assertion of the institution's right to restrict access to Veritonal cultivation.

"Can they actually apply a Qi-seal without your cooperation?" he asked.

"Not at this distance. The technique requires physical proximity and a significant capability advantage." She paused. "If they sent Elder Ma, yes. She's more capable than me in the traditional framework."

"But not in the corrected framework."

A silence. Then: "That's a significant point."

It was. The fire meridian correction had increased her effective Qi output by forty percent over three months. She had been working with the corrected technique for those three months while her Court colleagues were still working with the suppression error. The capability gap between her and Elder Ma had been real when the order was signed. It was narrower now, possibly negligible.

"The person who filed the report that triggered the formal order," he said. "The document says Peng Shaohua."

"A Court member who has been watching my activity for the past two months. I knew someone was watching. I didn't know it was Peng specifically." A pause. "He's thorough. The report will be accurate in its observations and incomplete in its interpretation."

"The interpretation will say: unauthorized independent research, unknown partner, insufficient reporting."

"Yes."

He thought about this.

The Iron Meridian recall order had been sent because Chen Yufei had been doing exactly what she was doing. The order was a predictable institutional response to an institutional challenge. He had known this would come. Chen Yufei had known it would come.

"What do you want to do?" he said.

The question mattered. He had learned, working with Chen Yufei, that she had been making her own calculations throughout this process — about her institution, her tradition, her identity. He was not going to make this decision for her.

She was quiet for a moment.

"I don't want to stop," she said. "That's the most important thing. Everything else is downstream of that."

"If you don't report within thirty days, you're expelled."

"Yes."

"And the expulsion is on the record. Affecting how the cultivation world sees you."

"Yes."

He waited.

"I've been an Iron Meridian practitioner for nine years," she said. "My practice was real. My channels are real. What I've built in nine years doesn't disappear when the institution says it does." A longer pause. "What do I lose if I'm expelled? Institutional access — the Court's node network, their research library, their community. Institutional protection — which has been conditional on my value to them and is now apparently not worth more than the discomfort my work is causing."

She paused again.

"What do I keep?" she said, and she was answering her own question. "My channels. My capability. My access to the Strata. You. Elder Zhao, who is not the same as the Court." A pause. "And the document is already out. The information I wanted to know first — I know it."

He was quiet.

"Tell me if I'm missing something," she said.

"You're not missing anything," he said. "You've thought about it clearly."

A long silence.

"Then I'm not going to report," she said.

He didn't say anything. He didn't need to.

"I need to tell Elder Zhao before it becomes formal," she said.

"Yes. She should hear from you."

"Tomorrow morning."

"Good."

She paused. "How do you feel about this?" she asked. "Being the reason someone loses their institutional standing."

He thought about this honestly.

"I feel responsible," he said. "Which I am. I introduced you to the archive, the correction, the information. The consequences of that introduction are real." He paused. "I also believe you made an autonomous choice at every step. You could have reported in October. You chose not to."

"Yes."

"The responsibility is distributed. I'm not absolved by your autonomy. But your autonomy exists." He paused. "What I can do is make sure that what you gain from this is worth what you're paying for it. That's the obligation."

She was quiet for a moment.

"It already is worth it," she said.

"I know," he said. "But the obligation continues."

She hung up.

He sat at his desk and wrote in the margins of the day's notes:

> *Chen Yufei will not comply with the recall order. She will be expelled. This is a consequence I caused, that she chose, that serves a purpose I believe in.*
>
> *The obligation: make sure what she gains is worth what she pays. Ongoing.*
>
> *The work this week: everything happening simultaneously. This is going to require more careful management of attention than I've ever needed.*

He turned to the next blank page.

He kept working.
