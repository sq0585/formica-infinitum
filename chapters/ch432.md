# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 432 — The Off-Earth Extension

Amber-0 had been extending the off-Earth Circuit network patiently for the better part of a year, long before Lin Mushi had a specific reason to care how far it had gotten.

He found the full report waiting for him that afternoon in the small pocket-dimension workroom that had, over five years, quietly become his actual office — the Strata's administrative spaces handled Strata business, but this narrow, chitin-walled room handled everything that didn't fit anywhere else. Fourteen hundred Circuit nodes now, the system's summary read, up from the eight hundred and forty he remembered from somewhere around Arc IV's close. Twelve hundred of them terrestrial. The remaining two hundred distributed outward, in a slow, deliberate chain, through low orbit, past the Moon's quiet gravitational shelf, out toward the asteroid belt's scattered rubble.

*Relay integrity: 94% across the full off-Earth chain,* the report noted. *Six percent loss attributable to distance-based Qi attenuation, within expected tolerance. Full chain operational.*

He read the number twice, feeling something shift into place that had been loose for months.

"You built this without waiting for me to ask," he said, to the room generally, because Amber-0 didn't require a body to be addressed and had stopped needing him to specify that months ago.

*I extended the network according to the standing directive: maintain and expand off-Earth relay capability toward the installation coordinate, priority secondary to Earth infrastructure needs.* The response arrived the way Amber-0's responses always did — text resolving directly into his awareness through the Colony Link's administrative channel, no voice, no affect, simply information arriving exactly shaped. *I did not anticipate you would need it urgently. I am gratified the timing aligns.*

He allowed himself, briefly, something close to a laugh. Five years in, and he still hadn't entirely settled the question of whether Amber-0 experienced anything close to gratification, or had simply learned, from watching him and Meen-4 for long enough, which words made a conversation feel less like reading a status report.

"Show me the chain," he said.

The workroom's far wall dissolved into a projection he'd built specifically for this kind of work — not the flat diagrams of Arc IV's early Circuit maps, but something closer to the way he actually perceived it now, a nested structure of nodes and connective threads rendered at a scale his eyes could hold even though his perception, unassisted, still couldn't quite reach the far end of it.

Twelve hundred terrestrial nodes, dense and steady, the accumulated infrastructure of five years' patient work. Then the chain thinning as it left the planet — lunar relay, stable; three points along a slow orbital transfer path, each one a small miracle of dimensional folding rather than physical construction, since nothing about the Chitin Circuit's chitin-based architecture survived vacuum and radiation the way conventional materials would have. Then the belt itself, sparse, distant, the last confirmed node sitting at a distance that made the number in the report — a thousand hours of Dimensional Weaving to traverse — feel, for the first time, concrete rather than abstract.

"That's further than I thought," he said quietly.

*It is further than most terrestrial-scale perception is built to comfortably hold,* Amber-0 replied. *I would recommend a staged approach. Full Dimensional Weaving across the entire distance in a single continuous session carries risk I am not equipped to fully quantify, given that no prior data exists for a Weaving effort of this duration.*

"How would you stage it."

*Segment the traversal along existing relay nodes. Anchor at each confirmed point before proceeding to the next. This converts one thousand hours of unbroken effort into a series of shorter sessions, each bounded by a known, stable reference point.*

He looked at the projection a long while, tracing the chain with something that wasn't quite his eyes, the way he'd learned to trace the Strata's own architecture in the early years before the perception became automatic. The gaps between nodes were larger than he liked. But they were gaps with known shape, mapped and measured, rather than the total unknown the installation had represented five years ago.

"Meen-4 should know I'm doing this," he said.

*She has been informed. She requested that I relay a message: she will be present for the first departure, and she expects regular check-ins through the relay chain, and she used the phrase "I am not asking, Lin Mushi" in a tone I am reasonably confident was not administrative.*

He smiled at that — the particular, specific accuracy of Meen-4 finding a way to worry through five years of increasingly formal Collective diplomacy without ever once softening the actual demand underneath it.

"Tell her yes," he said. "Tell her I'll check in at every node."

*Confirmed. I am also obligated to note: Chen Yufei submitted an identical request through a separate channel four minutes earlier. I have merged both into a single monitoring protocol to avoid redundant notification traffic.*

He sat with that for a moment — two people, independently, in the space of four minutes, making sure he understood he wasn't allowed to disappear quietly into a thousand hours of dark — and found it settled something in his chest that the relay chain's careful engineering hadn't quite managed on its own.

"Start the staging sequence," he said. "I'll begin tomorrow morning, first light. I want one full day to prepare properly — tell Chen Yufei and Meen-4 both, and make sure the Strata's day-to-day administration doesn't need me for anything that can't wait a month."

*Preparing the staged route now. Estimated total traversal time, accounting for the segmented approach: eleven hundred and forty hours, distributed across an anticipated six to eight weeks depending on your session endurance.*

Six to eight weeks. He wrote the number in the notebook, underlined once, and did not let himself think too hard about everything at home that would simply have to wait — his mother's Sunday calls continuing without him physically present for them, the Strata's ordinary business running on Wei Canglan's steady administration, the entire terrestrial world holding itself together while he spent two months reaching, one careful segment at a time, toward a place no living person had touched in forty million years.

He closed the projection, finished the last of his tea, and went to find Chen Yufei, because there was exactly one conversation left before tomorrow that mattered more than any relay chain, and it wasn't with Amber-0.
