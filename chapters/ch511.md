# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 511 — The Acceleration

He had expected, before he actually attempted it, that accelerating the repair would resemble the technical work he was most practiced at — a matter of applying force correctly, the way he had once inscribed circuits or folded pocket dimensions, effort translated efficiently into outcome. He understood, reaching toward the gradient's thin edges, that this instinct was exactly wrong, and understood it early enough, this time, not to make the mistake before correcting for it.

The Void Lattice was not a structure waiting to be pushed. It was already repairing itself, patient and ancient, following a logic that had existed since long before Veth-Karun's dissolution ever gave it something specific to repair. Forcing it would not speed the process. It would, he suspected, do to the Lattice roughly what forcing had once done to every young, impatient cultivator he had ever read about in the older, less careful texts — introduce exactly the kind of instability the discipline existed to prevent.

He did not force it.

He offered, instead, reference pattern — the same coherent shape three hundred and twenty-three anchors had been offering for months, extended now into the places their collective reach had not quite covered. Not new instruction. Continuation, in the Lattice's own native grain, of a pattern it had already, patiently, decided to trust.

*Observation 511,* he wrote, slower than usual, each word chosen with the specific care he reserved for conclusions he did not want to overstate even to himself. *The correct verb here is not force. It is collaborate. The Lattice already knows what repaired looks like — it has always known, the way a wound knows, on some level, the shape it is trying to close back into. What was missing was sufficient coherent reference across the whole gradient, not sufficient force at any single point. I am providing reference where the existing coverage runs thin. That is the entire intervention.*

He felt the shift almost immediately — not sudden, not dramatic, nothing that resembled the language of breakthrough he had grown used to at every prior threshold. A gradual, patient quickening, the Lattice's own self-repair function finding, at the gradient's thin edges, a clarity it had been reaching toward without quite arriving at on its own.

Dr. Shen Yue's voice reached him from the laboratory, careful, methodical, the specific cadence she used when she wanted a number understood rather than merely heard. "The projected timeline is moving. I want you to see the actual figures before either of us gets ahead of what they mean."

He looked.

Twelve million years, the original estimate had said, months ago, back when the three hundred and twenty-three had first extended themselves into the wound and given the Lattice something coherent to work with. Twelve million years, revised downward with every subsequent month of careful, patient tending, though never by very much — the kind of number that made the work feel, some days, like tending something that would outlast every person currently tending it, several times over.

The new figure was smaller. Considerably smaller. He looked at it twice before he trusted it enough to write it down, and even then he wrote it with the specific restraint he had learned, across five years of Wei Canglan's careful institutional caution, to apply to any number that looked, on first encounter, too good to be entirely believed.

*New estimate,* he wrote. *I will not record the figure here until Dr. Shen Yue has verified it against a second measurement window. I have been wrong about timelines before, usually in the direction of optimism, and I would rather be right slowly than wrong quickly, especially about something this many people gave this much of themselves toward.*

"You're being careful," Dr. Shen Yue said, watching him write, something warm underneath the clinical observation.

"I'm being Wei Canglan," he said, and felt, through the Anchor Protocol, Wei Canglan's answering pulse of something that was almost, for him, delight.

*Accurate,* Wei Canglan sent. *Also, for the record, correct.*

He smiled, faintly, and turned his attention back to the gradient — to the slow, patient quickening of a wound that had waited forty million years for exactly this kind of company, and found, watching it continue, that he was in no particular hurry to rush past the feeling of simply watching it work.
