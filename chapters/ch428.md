# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 428 — Preparing for Stellar Scale

The off-Earth Circuit network reached the inner solar system's outer edge on an otherwise unremarkable afternoon, the milestone arriving not with drama but with the same quiet, incremental confirmation that had characterized every stage of the project since its beginning.

"Stable," Meen-4 confirmed, reviewing the relay diagnostics alongside him. "No degradation, consistent power draw, holding at the projected range. This is, I believe, the furthest any Circuit-based structure has ever reliably extended from Earth."

He sat with the number for a moment, feeling its scale the way he'd learned to feel every genuinely large accomplishment now — not with immediate triumph, but with the slower, more grounded satisfaction of watching patient work finally arrive somewhere significant. The installation itself remained still further out, the majority of the eight billion kilometers still ahead of the relay's current reach. But the trajectory was proven now, stable and repeatable, each stage building reliably on the one before it.

The Archive's Stellar Scale curriculum, meanwhile, had grown considerably more accessible in the months since he'd first encountered it — not because the underlying material had changed, but because his own steadily accumulating understanding, combined with Meen-4's patient guidance, had gradually unlocked sections that had once resisted his attempts to parse them.

"Eighty percent readable now," he told Chen Yufei, during one of their Anchor maintenance sessions, showing her the curriculum's slowly resolving structure. "I still can't attempt any of the actual techniques — the stellar observation prerequisite is still out of reach until the installation's fully accessible. But I can at least understand what I'm eventually going to be asked to do."

"How does it feel," she asked. "Reading something that far beyond where you currently stand?"

He considered the question honestly. "Humbling," he admitted. "The preview I got, touching the edge of the Sun's Veritonal field by accident — that's still the most vivid reminder I have of how far beyond Planetary Architect scale this curriculum actually reaches. But it doesn't feel impossible anymore, the way it did the first time I opened the section. It feels like the next long project. Not a crisis. Just — the next thing to build toward, patiently, the same way everything else got built."

Something had shifted, too, in the daily rhythm of his own attention — a change he noticed gradually, over the weeks following the installation project's steady progress and the Strata's continued, stable growth. Planetary Architect scale, once a capability requiring constant, careful active management, had settled, finally, into something closer to mature, background competence. The Colony Link's twenty-four hundred connections, the Circuit network's fourteen hundred nodes, the Under-Web's ongoing maintenance — all of it continued to function, steadily, without requiring the same constant, exhausting vigilance it once had.

For the first time since he'd first opened Ren Wuji's Codex, three years earlier, he found himself with genuine room to think.

Not urgent room, carved out of crisis. Actual, unhurried space — the particular luxury of a mind no longer consumed entirely by immediate survival, free, for stretches of an afternoon at a time, to simply sit with a question and let it unfold at its own pace rather than racing against some external deadline.

"What are you thinking about," Meen-4 asked, one such afternoon, finding him sitting quietly in the Archive's central chamber, the Stellar Scale curriculum open in front of him but his attention, clearly, somewhere further away than the page.

"The installation," he admitted. "What's actually inside it. Why it's dormant instead of maintained. What it might mean, once I finally reach it, for everything the Stellar Scale curriculum is asking me to eventually become." He paused, turning the thought over more fully. "And what happens after that. Whatever comes after Stellar Scale. I never used to have room to think that far ahead. Everything was always about surviving whatever was directly in front of me."

"And now?"

"Now I think I finally have room to actually wonder," he said, "instead of just reacting to whatever crisis arrives next."

Meen-4 studied him for a long moment, something quiet and considering in her stillness. "That," she said finally, "may be the most significant change this entire year has produced. Not the review's outcome. Not the disclosure. This — the simple, hard-won luxury of genuine curiosity, unhurried, finally given room to breathe."

He nodded, and let himself sit with the thought a while longer, the vast, patient shape of the Sun's Veritonal field still lingering somewhere at the edge of his memory, no longer purely terrifying, now, but something closer to a promise — distant, enormous, and, for the first time, genuinely his to eventually reach toward, on his own unhurried timeline.
