# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Forty-Nine — Month Twelve

He sat with tea on a Thursday morning, one year after Ren Wuji had come through the door of the Wuhou 7-Eleven at 3:17 AM and not left the way he had come in.

He was not at the archive. He was at his desk.

The circuit network was running its quiet monitoring. The Chitin Circuit on his windowsill was glowing its amber-gold. His notebooks were stacked in their usual order. The city outside was doing what Chengdu did on a Thursday morning.

He had told himself he would take an hour and sit with this.

He was not going to make it a ceremony. He was not particularly given to ceremony. But the year deserved a pause — not to mark an ending, there was no ending in this work, but to be briefly present to what a year was and what it had accumulated.

---

He thought about what he had known a year ago.

At 3:17 AM one year ago, he had known: he worked a night shift that was ending in two weeks. He was behind on reading. His mother had called the previous Sunday. He had been thinking about an article on quantum tunneling in protein chains that a physicist had posted online, which he'd been trying to understand because it seemed relevant to something he'd observed in the store's environment that he hadn't been able to explain yet.

He had not known about the Chitin Seed. He had not known about the Strata. He had not known about the Colony Link, or the Under-Web, or the Compression technique, or the Formican community below the city he'd been living in for two years, or the predecessor's archive, or Axis-7, or Meen-4, or Chen Yufei, or Gao's particular quality of steady witness.

He had been carrying, without knowing it, the latent Veritonal layer of his nervous system, which had been waiting for activation since before he was born.

He thought about what Ren Wuji had been at 3:17 AM. Four hundred years old. The last thirty years of that four hundred having been spent building toward a transmission that had to happen in four minutes because his time had run out.

He thought about Ren Wuji's voice in the audio files — the warmth, the dry humor, the care that had been present in every preparation, in the choice of what to say and what to leave for the notebooks to show.

*I hope you are curious. Everything depends on it.*

He wrote in his observation notebook:

> *Month 12. One year since the Scholar's death.*
>
> *I don't think I'll understand this year fully in another 365 days. I think that's fine. Understanding it later is not the same as not understanding it now.*
>
> *What I know now that I didn't know a year ago: the list is very long and I'm not going to write it all out. The summary is: the world has more layers than I knew, the layers are inhabited, the inhabitants have been here longer than I have, and the work of understanding this is the most important work I have found to do.*
>
> *What hasn't changed: I keep notebooks. I try to understand things before deciding what to do with them. I eat properly when I remember. My mother sends noodle money. Gao picks up the phone.*
>
> *Ren Wuji: I want to say something about you. The year has given me language for things I didn't have language for when I first heard your voice. You prepared something very carefully for someone you'd never met, over a period I can't fully comprehend, and then it worked. It worked, and I'm here, and the work is continuing.*
>
> *I don't know if you knew it would work. I think you hoped.*
>
> *I hope too, about things that are ahead of me now the way the archive was ahead of me then.*

He read this back.

He did not revise it.

He poured the last of the tea — it had gone cold, which he hadn't noticed.

He sat for another few minutes.

Then he opened his notebook to the annotation work.

He had an exchange arrangement with Keeper Meen-4 and four containers waiting for review.

He went to work.

One hour, as promised to himself.

Then work.

The work was the year's real commemoration.
