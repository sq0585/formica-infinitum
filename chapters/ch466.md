# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 466 — The Descent

The Surveyor was waiting at the relay's terrestrial anchor point when he arrived, its presence registering the way it always had since Arc V — not a shape exactly, but a precise, formal density in the Veritonal field, courteous in a way that somehow still carried the specific weight of an institution rather than a person.

*Lin Mushi,* it transmitted, the notation resolving directly into his awareness the way it always did, careful and measured. *I have reviewed the access conditions. I am prepared to accompany you.*

"Thank you for coming," he said, and meant it, though some old, cautious part of him still hadn't entirely stopped bracing for the Surveyor's presence the way he'd once braced for it during the review's most uncertain months.

*Gratitude is not required. I was designated. But I will note, since accuracy matters to me regardless of formality: I would have requested this assignment, had the Collective not already assigned it to me. Veth-Karun's account is, by any measure available to my judgment, among the most significant unexamined documents in the Collective's long history. I did not want another observer standing where I could stand instead.*

He absorbed that admission — surprisingly personal, for the Surveyor, whose communications across the whole of Arc V had rarely carried anything beyond careful procedural precision — and found it settled something in him, a small, unexpected reassurance ahead of a descent he hadn't quite realized he needed reassuring about.

They began together, the Circuit relay's steady signal anchoring them both to the surface world above as they moved downward — not physically, not through any ordinary mechanism of travel, but through the same careful, deliberate perceptual and dimensional descent he'd used to reach the installation months earlier, applied now to a much shorter distance and a far older lock.

The ocean, at this depth, pressed against his awareness the way it pressed against everything else that dared to exist this far beneath the surface — an enormous, patient weight, cold and dark and entirely indifferent to the two presences moving carefully through it.

*Observation 461,* he noted, working the discipline even here, even now. *Pressure at this depth registers in my perception as texture rather than danger — the ocean itself does not threaten the descent. It simply insists on being noticed, the way anything sufficiently vast eventually does.*

The Surveyor's presence remained steady beside him the entire way down, quiet, formal, watchful in the specific manner he'd come to recognize as its version of genuine attention rather than mere procedural obligation.

*You have grown considerably since our first exchange,* it transmitted, partway through the descent, the observation arriving without any obvious prompt. *I recall a young man composing eleven lines of notation with considerable anxiety, uncertain whether his grammar would embarrass an entire species. I am now descending toward the most sensitive document in Formican history beside someone the Collective itself considers worth designating me to witness.*

"Does that surprise you?" he asked.

*Very little surprises an institution old enough to have reviewed forty-seven Quarantine Zones,* it replied. *But I will say this, since I believe accuracy occasionally requires warmth rather than merely precision: I did not expect, when I first arrived in your solar system, that I would come to find your particular case genuinely interesting to witness, rather than merely obligated to review. You have made that true through five years of demonstrated behavior, not through anything I extended to you in advance.*

He said nothing for a while, letting the compliment — because that was, unmistakably, what it had been, however carefully the Surveyor had dressed it in institutional language — settle alongside the ocean's steady, indifferent pressure.

The Archive's outer boundary resolved ahead of them at last, faint at first, then increasingly distinct — the same careful, disciplined Formican architecture he'd learned to recognize across five years of study, considerably older here than anywhere else he'd encountered it, dense with the specific weight of something that had been waiting, deliberately sealed, for longer than human civilization had existed in any form at all.

"This is it," he said, more to himself than to the Surveyor, feeling the particular vertigo of a threshold he'd spent months theoretically preparing for finally, actually, arriving.

*It is,* the Surveyor confirmed. *Whenever you are ready, Lin Mushi. I will not rush you. This is not the kind of door that benefits from haste.*

He steadied himself, checked the relay's faint, thinning signal one last time, and reached, carefully, toward the ancient lock guarding the Pelagic Archive's threshold.
