# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-Six — The Northern Peak's Hard Faction

Chen Yufei's network contact called instead of texting, which had become, over the months, a reliable signal that whatever followed wasn't meant to sit in writing.

"Master Gou Ping," she said, once Lin Mushi had picked up. "Northern Peak, senior enforcement rank. He's been gathering support inside the Court for weeks — quietly, the kind of organizing that doesn't show up in formal channels because it isn't formal yet."

"Gathering support for what?"

"For the position that Lady Shen's approach has failed. That negotiation and patience have produced nothing but Northern Peak losing two-thirds of its coalition and watching its own foundational legitimacy come under threat from a single unaffiliated cultivator who should, by his account, have been neutralized months ago." She paused. "He believes you should be confronted directly. Overwhelming force, not further diplomacy."

Lin Mushi sat with this, turning it over against everything he understood about Northern Peak's current internal politics — Lady Shen's careful, deliberate shift toward cooperation, her growing willingness to expose her own tradition's flaws rather than defend them blindly.

"Does Lady Shen know?"

"That's the part that worries me most. My contact says Gou Ping has been organizing carefully outside her direct oversight — using channels and personal loyalty built over years of shared service, not the formal Court structure she still nominally controls. He's recruited fourteen cultivators. All willing, all convinced this is the necessary correction to a strategy they believe has failed."

"Fourteen cultivators, organized outside her authority, planning to do what exactly?"

"I don't have full operational details. But the framing my contact heard was direct and unambiguous. A confrontation. At the Strata's known entry point."

He felt something settle in his chest, cold and procedural, the particular clarity that arrived whenever a threat moved from theoretical to concrete.

"When?"

"Soon. Days, not weeks, is my contact's assessment. Gou Ping isn't the patient type, and he's worried that further delay just gives Lady Shen more time to consolidate a position he disagrees with."

Lin Mushi thanked her, ended the call, and sat for a long moment with the Circuit network's readout glowing softly on the table in front of him — the same quiet monitoring system that had caught Stone Hall's surveillance weeks ago, now carrying a different and considerably heavier kind of warning.

---

He told Chen Yufei in person an hour later, watching her expression shift from concern into the careful, focused calculation she brought to genuine threats.

"Fourteen cultivators is significant," she said. "Not an overwhelming force by Court standards, but more than enough to cause real damage if they reach the entry point with intent to use it."

"Lady Shen doesn't know."

"Are you going to tell her?"

He considered this carefully. Telling her gave the hard faction's plan a chance to be intercepted from within Northern Peak before it ever reached him directly — the kind of clean resolution he would have preferred, if it were available. It also risked, if Gou Ping's people had any awareness of surveillance, accelerating a timeline that was already moving faster than he wanted.

"Not yet," he said finally. "If I tell her and it tips them off, they might simply move sooner, with less warning than I currently have. I want to prepare first. Then I'll tell her, regardless of what happens — she deserves to know what her own faction is doing without her authorization, whether or not it changes anything by the time she hears it."

Chen Yufei nodded, the decision matching her own instinct closely enough that she didn't argue it.

"How do we prepare for fourteen cultivators?"

He looked toward the Strata's distant threshold, the entry point that had carried him through countless ordinary crossings over the past two years, and thought about everything he'd learned in the months since the shoebox-sized fold had first held for eleven seconds.

"Not the way they're expecting," he said. "I'm not going to fight fourteen people directly. I'm going to change the space they're trying to fight in."

He pulled out his notebook and began, with the same careful, methodical attention he brought to every problem this world had handed him, to plan exactly how.
