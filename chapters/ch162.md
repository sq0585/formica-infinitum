# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixty-Two — Night Before

The document was finished on a Thursday afternoon.

He had read it through one final time, alone, checking each section against the source material a last time before release — not because he doubted the work, but because the habit of final verification was too deeply ingrained to skip, even at month fourteen, even after everything that had led to this point. Chen Yufei had reviewed it that morning and pronounced it ready. Axis-7, when he'd shown her the relevant Codex cross-references the previous week, had confirmed the translations were accurate to the source.

Everything was ready.

The release was set for the following morning — 6 AM, the same hour as the first document, sent this time to all five Courts' formal communication channels simultaneously, with no curated recipient list, no careful sequencing. Direct, complete, unmistakable.

He did not go home that evening.

He went to the Strata, alone.

He told Chen Yufei he needed the evening to himself, and she had understood without needing it explained — she had her own version of this need, he suspected, the kind of solitude that came before a threshold rather than after one. She had not asked to come. He had not invited her.

He walked through the threshold, through the vaulted hall, past the library door where Axis-7 was, as always, reading at the east wall. She looked up as he passed.

He sent, briefly: *I need to be in the library alone tonight.*

She considered this, then sent back something he hadn't quite expected: *Understood. I will work in the circular room instead.*

She left. The library was empty.

He sat at his usual table.

He opened his notebook.

He thought about Ren Wuji, four hundred years old, sitting somewhere in this same archive system — or one like it, or this very room, he didn't know — preparing the materials that would eventually find him at 3:17 AM behind a convenience store counter. He thought about the predecessor before that, two hundred years of solitary documentation, the careful notes left for whoever came next. He thought about the six students who had walked into the network's deeper routes and never come back, and the forty-one who had returned to the surface changed, and the handful who had simply stayed.

He thought about tomorrow morning, and what five Courts simultaneously receiving a document that corrected and undermined centuries of foundational doctrine would actually produce — politically, socially, in the lives of thousands of practitioners who had built their identities around traditions that were about to be shown, with full documentation, to be partially wrong.

He thought about Chen Yufei's word: *catastrophe.*

He thought about his own word, offered back to her: *correction.*

He thought neither word was wrong, and that tomorrow would decide which one history eventually settled on, and that the deciding would not be entirely in his hands.

He held the pen.

He did not write anything.

The amber light of the archive held steady around him — the maintenance circuits running their patient, ancient cycles, the Layer 3 community's collective field a warm constant beneath the floor, the shelves full of fourteen thousand years of accumulated understanding that had been waiting, in various states of access and concealment, for someone capable of carrying it back to the surface correctly.

He sat with the notebook open and the pen uncapped for two hours.

He did not write a single word.

This was not failure to find words. He had words — he always had words, eventually, for everything he turned his attention to. This was something else: a deliberate choice to simply be present, without converting the presence into documentation, without turning the last quiet evening before everything accelerated into another entry to be filed and cross-referenced.

Some things did not need to be written down to be real.

He had learned this, slowly, across fourteen months — in the silence with Chen Yufei three nights ago, in the wedding's warm aggregate field he hadn't fully documented, in his mother's voice on Sunday calls that he listened to rather than analyzed.

He let this be one of those things.

At some point past midnight, he closed the notebook — still blank on its current page — and sat for a while longer simply existing in the space that had become, over fourteen months, something closer to a second home than he had expected an alien archive beneath a convenience store to become.

Then he stood.

He walked back through the vaulted hall.

He crossed the threshold, back into the ordinary 7-Eleven, back into the ordinary Chengdu night.

He went home.

He slept four hours.

At 6 AM, he sent the document.
