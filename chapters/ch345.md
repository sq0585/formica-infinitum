# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Forty-Five — Liang Jingzhi's Contribution

She presented to the full Strata City council on a Thursday evening, the community hall Fang Ruxue's governance framework had established months earlier filled beyond its usual capacity, word of the presentation's subject having spread through the city faster than any formal announcement could have managed.

Liang Jingzhi stood at the front of the room with the same unhurried, precise confidence she brought to every piece of fieldwork, a wide display behind her showing the Pacific Ring's full structure in a level of detail Lin Mushi hadn't previously seen assembled in one place.

"I want to walk everyone through something that's been part of this city's foundation from the beginning, but that I don't think most residents fully understand yet," she began. "The Pacific Ring — twelve ancient Formican structures along the ocean floor, maintaining, as far as my research over the past two years has established, the geological stability that makes this entire region of the planet habitable at all."

She walked the room through the data methodically — seismic patterns the Ring's structures visibly influenced, habitability metrics that correlated, with striking precision, to the Ring's continued function, the specific, unglamorous mechanics of exactly how ancient Formican engineering had been quietly protecting human civilization for longer than any human record could account for.

"Here's the part that matters most for tonight," she continued, the display shifting to show a series of structural stress indicators across all twelve nodes. "The Ring is aging. Not failing, not urgently, but showing wear patterns consistent with forty million years of continuous operation without maintenance. If nothing changes, our best models suggest meaningful degradation within the next several centuries — not our lifetime, but well within the lifetime of the city we're building here."

---

A murmur moved through the room, the specific quality of an audience recognizing, for the first time, the true scale of what Liang Jingzhi's careful survey work had actually revealed.

"What does that mean, practically?" a resident asked, one of the newer Collective members Lin Mushi recognized but didn't yet know well.

"It means the obligation to maintain the Ring can't remain something only a handful of us quietly track," Liang Jingzhi said. "It has to become something this entire community understands and shares responsibility for. Not because it's urgent this year, or even this decade. Because forty million years of Formican engineering kept this planet habitable without ever asking anything of us in return, and I think we owe it more than passive gratitude now that we understand what it actually requires."

She looked toward Lin Mushi briefly before continuing. "I've also come to believe this matters for reasons beyond simple maintenance. The Ring's function — protecting Earth's habitability — is precisely the kind of careful, deliberate stewardship I think we need to be able to point to clearly, going forward. Not just that we haven't broken what the Formicans left behind. That we're actively choosing to care for it."

---

Fang Ruxue spoke from the audience, her question carrying the specific practical focus she brought to every serious proposal. "What would an actual repair project require? Realistically."

"I've been developing a proposal," Liang Jingzhi said, pulling up a new display. "Three months of dedicated, collaborative work — my own geological survey expertise, the Circuit team's engineering capability, Meen-4's Formican construction knowledge, and Lin Mushi's Dimensional Weaving to actually access and reinforce the structures at depths no ordinary equipment can reach."

She detailed the proposal further, the room's earlier murmur settling into the focused, engaged quiet of a community genuinely considering a real, significant commitment rather than simply reacting to alarming news.

By the presentation's end, the council's response was clear enough that a formal vote felt, to Lin Mushi, almost redundant — the Strata City, unanimously and without significant dissent, choosing to take on the Ring's repair as shared, communal work rather than leaving it to any single expert's quiet, individual vigilance.

---

He found Liang Jingzhi afterward, the community hall slowly emptying around them.

"That was extraordinary," he said. "You didn't just present a problem. You gave this entire community a reason to actually own it together."

"I've been thinking about this for a while," she admitted, something quietly satisfied in her expression despite the evening's exhausting intensity. "Ever since our fieldwork together, honestly. I kept coming back to the same realization — a structure this significant shouldn't depend on one person's private survey data. It deserves the kind of shared, deliberate stewardship a real community can actually provide."

"You did more than propose a repair project tonight," he said. "You gave the review case something genuinely important. Careful, communal stewardship of exactly the infrastructure that makes this world worth protecting."

"I hadn't fully thought of it in those terms," she admitted, "but I think you're right. Sometimes the most important contribution to a large, abstract problem is simply doing the smaller, concrete work correctly, and trusting that it matters more broadly than it might first appear to."

He nodded, understanding, walking out of the community hall into the evening's cool air, that the eighteen-month case was accumulating exactly the kind of evidence Meen-4 had described weeks earlier — not performance built for an audience, but genuine, careful stewardship that would have mattered regardless of who was watching.
