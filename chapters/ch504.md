# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 504 — Scale Six — The Void Lattice Threshold

The Pelagic Archive had never given it a number.

He understood why, now, arriving at the edge of it — the five scales he already held were each, in their own way, a place. Micro Scale was small places. Compression was his own place, folded tighter. Dimensional Weaver was built places. Planetary Architect was one enormous place, and Stellar Scale was many places joined into a field. Every threshold so far had asked him to perceive more territory, more richly, at greater distance.

This was not a place.

He had read the Archive's description of it perhaps forty times over the preceding months, in the careful, methodical way he read everything that mattered, and every one of those forty readings had left him with the same honest gap between the words and what they were pointing at. The text called it, in the closest translation Meen-4 could offer, *the medium in which places exist* — not a location, not a distance to be crossed, but the substrate underneath every location and every distance, the fabric that made the concept of "underneath" possible in the first place.

He understood, reaching the actual edge of it, exactly why no number had ever been assigned. A number implied a place in a sequence. This was the sequence's own foundation, and foundations do not sit inside the things they support.

*The Void Lattice,* he wrote, and set the pen down, because writing at this particular threshold had become its own small act of discipline — proof, moment to moment, that he remained a person capable of holding a pen rather than a perception without a body attached to it. *This is where Veth-Karun crossed. This is where Veth-Karun did not survive crossing. I am about to do the same thing they did, at the same threshold, with — I hope — the one thing they did not have.*

He felt the Anchor Protocol tighten, deliberately, all five and three hundred and twenty-three more, a single coordinated act of will that he could feel arriving from Chengdu offices and Strata council chambers and a dissolution zone thousands of light-years distant, all of it converging on him at once, none of it losing its individual shape in the convergence.

He engaged Scale Six.

There was no sensation of falling, no rush, nothing that resembled the dramatic language the earliest cultivation texts he'd ever read as a much younger, much less careful person had led him to expect from a threshold like this. There was only, all at once, everything — not everything within some enormous but bounded field of perception, the way Planetary Architect had made a planet everything and Stellar Scale had made a solar system everything, but everything in the sense the word was not usually permitted to mean. The medium beneath the medium. The fabric that the fabric of every place he had ever perceived was woven from.

He felt it the way he had, at nineteen, once felt the specific vertigo of understanding, for the first time, that the Earth was round and moving and he had simply never noticed because he had never needed to — except multiplied past any scale that comparison could honestly hold, and arriving, this time, with no solid ground anywhere beneath the feeling to stand on while he adjusted to it.

He held the Anchor Protocol.

Five people. Three hundred and twenty-three more. And beneath all of them, underneath the whole structure, the same single fixed point he had carried into every threshold before this one, unchanged since he had first learned to name it: 3:17 in the morning, a convenience store on a street he could still walk without thinking, a stranger's hand closing around his wrist with the specific, deliberate weight of someone choosing, in his final seconds, exactly who to trust with what came next.

He did not dissolve.

He held the sixth scale, the medium beneath every place he had ever perceived, and remained, unmistakably, still the particular person doing the perceiving — a fact so quietly, thoroughly true that for a moment he almost missed how enormous a thing it actually was, buried as it was beneath the sheer scale of everything surrounding it.

Dr. Shen Yue's instruments, behind him, produced a reading she did not say aloud yet. He felt her decide not to, the way he had learned to feel small human choices even at this scale, and understood, without needing the number, that whatever it said, it had not yet told her to be afraid.

He stayed there, at the threshold Veth-Karun had crossed and never returned from, and let himself simply exist in it a while, curious, careful, and — improbably, given everything the moment could reasonably have made him feel instead — calm.
