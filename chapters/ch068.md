# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Sixty-Eight — The First Compression Attempt

At 3 AM in his apartment, he attempted Compression.

Not in the Strata — the Codex specified that first Compression attempts should be made in familiar environments where the ambient Qi field was well-mapped and any instability could be quickly assessed. His apartment was well-mapped. He had been living in it for months with his perception active. He knew every filament, every threshold, the cold room's now-correctly-routed flow, the slight accumulation of his own habitation in the northeast corner where he worked.

He sat cross-legged on the floor, channels in full harmonic, the spatial geometry derivation present in his attention like a blueprint he was preparing to fold.

He held the geometry.

He let the geometry compress.

---

For 1.7 seconds he was at Ratio 1.5 — he had been there before, it was the threshold-entry compression, he knew this territory. He pushed further. He let the geometry reduce past 1.5.

The world changed.

Not dramatically. Not in a way that was visible to anyone who might have been watching. But from the inside: the apartment's Qi field, which he knew at Ratio 1 as luminous threads in a specific geometric arrangement, resolved into something denser and more detailed. The threads were not threads. They were structures, with internal architecture, containing processes that moved at a scale he had not previously been able to resolve.

He was at approximately Ratio 2.

His own hands, visible in the faint luminosity of the compressed-scale Qi field, were not hands in the ordinary sense. They were complex systems — the protein channels visible as bright pathways through the tissue, the Chitin Seed's crystalline integration visible as a different quality of brightness at the nerve junctions.

He was looking at the machinery of himself.

Then the geometry collapsed.

---

He was back at Ratio 1 before he could choose it, the Compression failing in the way an unpracticed physical skill fails — correctly executed and then suddenly not, the body defaulting to the easier state before the harder state had stabilized.

He was gasping. Not from pain — from the shock of the change. The perceptual shift from Ratio 2 back to Ratio 1 was like a bright room suddenly going dim, and his nervous system had registered it as an emergency even though it was not an emergency.

He sat on the floor and breathed until his heart rate normalized.

Then he reached for the notebook.

> *3:17 AM. First full Compression attempt. Achieved Ratio 1.5 — familiar, stable. Pushed to approximately Ratio 2.*
>
> *What I saw at Ratio 2: internal structure of the filament network — threads are complex systems, not simple flows. My own hands: protein channels visible as luminous pathways, Chitin Seed integration visible at nerve junctions.*
>
> *Duration: 1.7 seconds before geometry collapse. Not failure — instability. The geometry was correct; I couldn't hold it yet.*
>
> *Physical response: gasping, elevated heart rate. Involuntary Ratio 1 restoration. Not painful — startling.*
>
> *Immediate response: tried again.*

He had, in fact, tried again immediately, because the 1.7-second glimpse had shown him something important enough to want to hold for longer than 1.7 seconds.

The second attempt: 2.1 seconds.

The third: 2.3.

He tried four times in one hour and each time held it slightly longer before the geometry collapsed. By the fourth attempt he was beginning to understand the specific failure mode: not the compression itself failing, but a subtle misalignment in how he was maintaining the compressed state. He was trying to hold it the way you hold a physical object, by clenching — but clenching introduced tension that disrupted the geometry. The correct approach was more like balancing, not holding. Presence, not force.

> *Attempt 4 note: maintenance failure is muscular, not structural. I'm clenching. The geometry requires balance, not grip.*
>
> *Same insight as Phase Three — you don't direct the bilateral exchange, you attend to it. You don't hold the Compression, you inhabit it.*
>
> *Next session: practice the quality of balance rather than the duration.*

He made tea at 4:30 AM. He sat at the desk with the notebook and the tea and the aftermath of four Compression attempts and thought about what it meant to inhabit a geometry rather than hold it.

He was tired in a way that felt earned.

He drank his tea.

He went to bed at five and slept until noon, which was the most he'd slept in one stretch in several months.

When he woke, the Chitin Seed was warm and the apartment felt slightly different — as if the act of briefly perceiving it at a finer scale had updated something in his baseline understanding of the space.

He checked the northeast corner.

The flow was still correctly routed.

He opened the notebook and tried again.
