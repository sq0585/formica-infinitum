# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Nine — Fang Ruxue's Proposal

Fang Ruxue brought a written proposal, three pages, typed and printed with the kind of formal care he associated more with academic grant applications than cultivation politics.

"I've been calling it, provisionally, the Cultivation Research Collective," she said, setting the pages on the table between them. "Independent of all five Courts. Open access to verified Formican-derived knowledge, compiled and shared rather than hoarded. I would chair it. I would need you as Archive interpreter — not as a figurehead, as working participant. The translations have to be accurate, and right now you're the only person alive who can verify that."

He read through the pages while she waited, the same patient stillness she'd brought to the convenience store doorway weeks ago.

The structure was careful. Membership open to any verified practitioner regardless of Court affiliation, governed by a rotating council rather than a single authority, funded through contribution rather than tribute, with explicit provisions against any single faction accumulating disproportionate control over the archive of verified material. She'd clearly thought about the failure modes — the ways an institution meant to replace the Courts' worst habits could quietly grow the same habits if no one built against it from the start.

"This is good," he said, and meant it. "Better than anything I'd have drafted on my own."

"I've had thirty-one years inside an institution to learn what I'd build differently if I ever had the chance." She looked at him directly. "You should lead it."

"No."

"You're the only person with full access to the Archive. The only person who can verify translations against the original notation. Practically speaking, the Collective's credibility depends entirely on your participation regardless of who holds the title."

"That's exactly the problem," he said. "If I lead it formally, everything it does becomes a referendum on me — on whether people trust me, distrust me, want to align with me or position against me. The work gets buried under the politics of my name being attached to it. You don't have that problem. You have thirty-one years of credibility that exists independent of anything happening right now."

She considered this, turning it the careful way she turned everything, and didn't argue further. "I disagree," she said. "I think the institution would be stronger with you visibly leading it. But I understand the reasoning, and I respect it enough not to keep pushing against it."

"I'll participate fully. Translations, verification, whatever the Archive work requires. I won't chair it."

"Then I'll chair it." She picked the pages back up, already making a small notation in the margin — adjusting the draft in real time, he realized, the habit of someone who treated every conversation as a working session rather than a formality. "I'll need your input on the governance structure as well. Particularly the provisions around archive access. I don't fully understand what I'm trying to protect against yet, and you do."

"I'll cause problems at scale," he said. "I want to be honest about that before you build something around the assumption that I won't. I see angles other people miss, and sometimes I act on them before I've explained myself well enough for anyone else to follow the reasoning. That's not a flaw I'm planning to fix quickly."

Something in her expression suggested she'd already anticipated this, and had simply wanted to hear him say it himself.

"Noted," she said. "I'll build the governance structure to account for it rather than around the assumption you'll change."

He found, watching her make another small notation in the margin, that he trusted this version of the plan more than he'd trusted any version where his own name sat at the top of it.

"When do you want to announce it?" he asked.

"Soon. The coalition's fracturing gives us a narrow window — Courts and independent cultivators alike are looking for something to align with that isn't simply picking a side in Northern Peak's investigation. If we move before that window closes, the Collective becomes the obvious alternative instead of one option among several."

"How soon is soon?"

"I was thinking," she said, looking up from the page, "two weeks."

He thought about Lady Shen's quiet, careful interest in becoming part of something that lasted. About the maintenance burden the ley-line crisis was already threatening to dump on him. About a structure still being drafted on three pages of paper that was, somehow, already becoming the most important thing he was building.

"Two weeks," he agreed. "I'll have the governance input to you by the end of the week."

She gathered the pages, satisfied, and for a moment — just a moment — looked less like a scholar negotiating an institutional founding and more like someone who had spent half her life waiting for a reason to build something instead of simply studying what other people had already built.

"Then I suppose," she said, echoing herself from the library weeks before, "we have a great deal of work ahead of us."
