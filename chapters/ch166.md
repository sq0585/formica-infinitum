# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixty-Six — Verification

The Celestial Compass School's response arrived three days after Kuo's visit, and it was different in character from everything that had come before.

The sender was a scholar named Dr. — though she didn't use the title formally within the cultivation world — Lin Wenqing, who Chen Yufei's contact had described months ago as someone who had spent fifteen years trying to reconcile Qi flow patterns with conventional astronomy and had been troubled by unexplained gaps. Her message was longer than anything else he'd received, methodical in a way that read as genuinely scholarly rather than political.

*I have spent three days comparing your document's notation against fragments my school has preserved for approximately four hundred years,* she wrote. *These fragments were inherited from an unknown source — our founding records describe them only as "recovered material, origin uncertain, treat with reverence." We have always been taught that the notation was symbolic. Ceremonial. A representation of cosmic principles rather than a literal technical language.*

*I have now confirmed, character by character, that the fragments are not symbolic. They are literal. They are excerpts — badly damaged, partially transcribed by someone who did not fully understand what they were copying — from the same source material your document translates. The astronomical alignment charts we have used for ceremonial purposes for four centuries are, in fact, a functional cosmological mapping system, degraded by repeated copying into something closer to superstition than science.*

*I do not know how to fully process this yet. What I can tell you, with confidence: your document's claims about the Formican source material are verifiable, and I have now verified them independently, against materials your document did not have access to and could not have known about.*

*I would like to understand more. Not as an institutional representative — the Celestial Compass School has not yet decided what its official position will be. As a scholar who has found, after fifteen years, an answer to questions I had begun to believe were unanswerable.*

Lin Mushi read this twice.

He thought about what it meant — independent verification, from a source he hadn't selected, hadn't curated, hadn't even known had the relevant fragments. The document's claims were not simply asserted. They were now confirmed by someone with no reason to confirm them except that the confirmation was true.

He wrote back carefully, answering her specific technical questions about the cosmological notation, including several cross-references to Document 6 that he thought would help her reconcile the fragments she had with the fuller system.

He also wrote: *Thank you for verifying this independently and for telling me what you found, including the parts that must have been difficult to receive. I understand what it costs to discover that four hundred years of inherited practice was built on a misreading. I've watched someone close to me go through a version of the same discovery. It doesn't get easier quickly. It does get easier.*

She replied within the hour, briefly: *Thank you. I will be in touch as I work through the rest of the material.*

He showed the exchange to Chen Yufei that evening.

"This is significant," she said, reading it carefully. "Not because of what she said about the document. Because of who she is. Lin Wenqing is respected across multiple Courts for her rigor. If she states publicly that she's verified this independently—"

"It changes the credibility calculus," he finished.

"Significantly." Chen Yufei looked up from the message. "The hostile responses can dismiss your document as an unaffiliated practitioner's claim. They can't as easily dismiss independent verification from someone with her reputation."

He thought about this.

"I didn't engineer this," he said. "I didn't even know the Celestial Compass School had those fragments."

"I know," Chen Yufei said. "That's what makes it valuable. It wasn't manufactured. It was found." She set the message down. "This is the first real crack in whatever unified opposition Kuo was worried about. It won't be the last."

He hoped she was right.

He found out, over the following weeks, that she was.
