# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Sixty — After the Ambush

He told Chen Yufei the moment he reached home, and watched her composure crack in a way he'd rarely seen from her — not panic, but the particular fear of someone confronting, for the first time with full concreteness, exactly how close she'd come to losing him.

"You should have called me," she said. "The instant it started."

"There wasn't time, and once the geometry held, calling for help would have meant breaking concentration I needed to maintain. I made the choice that kept the situation contained rather than the choice that would have felt better to you in the moment."

She didn't argue with the logic. She simply held onto him for a long moment, the fear working its way through her before settling into something steadier.

"What do we do about this?" she asked finally.

He'd been turning the same question over during the walk home, and found, by the time he'd reached his own front door, that he already knew the answer he wanted to pursue.

"Fang Ruxue's been building something," he said. "A dispute resolution process for the Collective — formal mechanisms for addressing exactly this kind of conduct, rather than each incident simply becoming another isolated act of violence that nobody answers for through anything but more violence. I want to use it."

"A formal complaint. Against an attempted assassination."

"Yes. Not because I think it'll fully satisfy what Gou Ping's faction deserves, but because I think the alternative — answering force with force, escalating until someone eventually gets seriously hurt or killed on either side — is exactly the trap I've spent two years trying not to fall into. The Collective needs to prove its institutional mechanisms actually function, even against something this serious. Especially against something this serious."

---

He filed the complaint the next morning, providing the Circuit network's recorded data — Qi signatures, timestamps, the alley's coordinates, everything the monitoring system had captured of the encounter — along with his own detailed account.

Fang Ruxue, her arm still healing but her judgment as sharp as ever, convened the dispute resolution panel within three days: herself, Lady Shen, and two senior independent members chosen specifically for their distance from either side of the conflict.

The hearing was careful, methodical, conducted with a formality that surprised him given how informal most of the Collective's other proceedings had remained. Gou Ping did not attend, predictably, but two of the four attackers — apparently less committed to the cause than their leader, and considerably more frightened of formal consequence than he was — submitted written statements that, while careful to minimize their own culpability, confirmed the essential facts of what had happened.

The ruling came five days after the hearing began.

Gou Ping's faction was formally expelled from any legitimate cultivation community structure recognized by the Collective — not merely censured, the way Northern Peak had once handled his initial suspension, but barred entirely, the ruling distributed to every Court and independent network the Collective maintained contact with, a clear, public record of exactly what had been attempted and exactly how the new institutional framework had responded.

It didn't stop Gou Ping. Lin Mushi understood that clearly, reading the ruling's careful language. A man who had already organized an assassination attempt wasn't likely to be deterred by formal expulsion from a structure he'd never fully respected to begin with.

But it mattered anyway.

It meant the Collective's dispute resolution process — built carefully, tested now for the first time against something genuinely serious — had functioned exactly as Fang Ruxue had designed it to function. Evidence gathered, a fair hearing convened, a clear ruling issued and publicly recorded, all without anyone needing to answer violence with more violence.

He sat with Chen Yufei that evening, the ruling printed and sitting between them on the table, and felt something he hadn't quite expected to feel in the aftermath of someone trying to kill him.

Cautious hope.

Not that the danger was over — he understood clearly that it wasn't, that Gou Ping remained exactly as dangerous as he'd been before the ruling, perhaps more so now that formal channels had closed against him entirely. But hope that the structure he'd helped build, slowly and carefully across the past months, was strong enough now to hold something real, to answer genuine violence with something other than more violence in return.

"It worked," Chen Yufei said, reading the ruling over again.

"This part of it did," he said. "I don't think we've seen the end of Gou Ping. But I think we've proven something important about what kind of world we're actually trying to build in his place."

She nodded, and reached for his hand, and the two of them sat together in the quiet evening, the day's careful institutional victory settling into something that felt, despite everything, genuinely worth protecting.
