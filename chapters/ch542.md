# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 542 — What He Finds

He extended his awareness toward solar position 7.4-N the way he had learned, across six careful years, to extend it toward anything that mattered — not reaching, not forcing, simply turning his attention the way a person turns toward a sound they've finally decided they're ready to listen to properly.

The distance closed the way all distance closed for him now, instantly, without effort, the specific privilege of a scale that had stopped treating space as an obstacle sometime around the moment he'd first learned to hold seven perceptions at once and remain, through all of it, unmistakably himself.

He found the installation first — silent, exactly as every prior report had described it, its structure intact, its systems patient and dormant in a way that had never quite matched the language of abandonment the Archive's oldest records had used to describe it. He held that observation a moment, filing it carefully, understanding already that this alone was worth the six years of deliberate patience he'd spent not rushing toward it.

And then, at the edge of the installation's dormant field, he found the thing itself.

He held it in his perception a long time before attempting to describe it, even to himself, understanding, from six years of careful practice, that the first attempt to name an unfamiliar thing too quickly was usually the attempt that got it wrong.

It was not dangerous. He was certain of that much almost immediately — not through any absence of power, which the thing carried in a quantity he found genuinely difficult to measure against anything he'd previously encountered, but through the specific, unmistakable absence of the particular texture danger always carried, the coiled readiness of something oriented toward harm. This was not that.

It was not hostile. He held that certainty alongside the first, testing it carefully, the way he tested every conclusion that mattered. There was no aggression in it, no defensive posture, nothing that resembled the guarded stillness of a thing bracing against an intrusion.

It was something else. Something the Archive's entire taxonomy, exhaustive as it was across forty million years of careful Formican observation, had simply never had occasion to classify — not Formican, not human, not anything the Collective's considerable catalogue of known civilizations had a category prepared to receive. New, in the specific, rare sense the word almost never honestly earned.

*Observation 542,* he thought, though he did not yet reach for the notebook, understanding that some things needed to be simply witnessed before they could be properly written. *What waits at 7.4-N sits outside every classification system the Archive, the Collective, and six years of hard-earned understanding ever gave me. I do not know what it is. I am not currently afraid of not knowing.*

He sat with it a long time — he could not have said how long, and found, testing the thought, that the not-knowing did not trouble him here any more than it had troubled him at any of the thresholds before this one. He simply existed alongside it, curious, patient, extending nothing but attention, asking nothing but to be permitted to understand.

It did not answer him, not in any language he could yet parse. But he felt, holding the moment, something that was not quite communication and was not quite its absence either — the specific, unmistakable sense of being noticed in return, considered, met with a curiosity that mirrored his own.

Eventually, he opened his eyes.

He was in the pocket dimension laboratory again, primarily, the way he chose to be primarily anywhere now — five people around him, a city beyond the walls, a wound at the edge of everything still healing, patiently, on schedule.

He picked up notebook forty-seven, and turned to the page that held a single word, written the night before.

*Curious.*

He picked up the pen, and began to write — the same act the novel had opened with, six years and one whole extraordinary life ago, a night-shift clerk with an empty page and no idea yet what any of it would eventually mean.

He still did not know everything. He understood now, with a clarity that felt less like an ending and more like the correct, permanent shape of things, that he never would — that the not-knowing was not a gap waiting to be closed, but the actual, ongoing condition of a curious mind meeting a universe considerably larger than any single lifetime could exhaust.

He was, unmistakably, still himself.

He began to write.
