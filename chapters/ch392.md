# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 392 — The Surveyor — Direct Meeting

The request arrived through the Witness Node in a format Meen-4 studied for a long time before she would explain it, her antennae held in the particular stillness that meant she was choosing her words with unusual care.

"The Surveyor is requesting a direct dimensional interface," she said finally. "Not notation exchanged through the Node. A meeting. Your perception, its presence, in the same folded space, without translation delay or formal document structure between you."

"Is that normal?"

"No." She said it flatly, without hedging. "I have no record of a Surveyor requesting direct interface with a zone's point of contact at this stage of a review. Historically, it has occurred, in rare cases, only near a review's conclusion, when the consensus body has narrowed its remaining questions to something that formal notation cannot adequately carry. Requesting it now, still in the middle of active proceedings, is unprecedented in every record I have access to."

He sat with that, feeling the particular vertigo that had become familiar over the past weeks — the sense of watching precedent bend, again and again, around a case that refused to fit neatly into the review body's accumulated forty million years of experience.

"What does it require," he asked. "Logistically. Is this something I can simply agree to?"

"Not simply. A direct interface of this kind requires the Orthodoxy's consent as a formally registered party to the review — a procedural safeguard meant to ensure no zone can arrange a private, unwitnessed conversation with the Surveyor that circumvents the review's transparency. They will have to agree to it."

"Will they?"

Meen-4 was quiet a moment. "I believe they will grudgingly consent," she said finally, "not because they want to, but because refusing would look, to the rest of the Collective's membership, like fear of exactly the transparency they claim to be fighting for. Refusing costs them credibility they cannot currently afford to lose, given how badly the framing vote went for them."

The consent came through two days later, exactly as Meen-4 had predicted — terse, formal, and unmistakably reluctant, the Orthodoxy's notation carrying, even through translation, a quality that felt distinctly like gritted teeth.

The interface itself, when it happened, was unlike anything Lin Mushi had experienced in three years of steadily expanding what his perception could reach. Meen-4 guided him through the preparation — a folding of his own awareness inward and outward simultaneously, a technique closer to the deepest meditative states he'd practiced during his earliest cultivation training than to anything in the Formican notation he'd since built his life around.

"Do not try to control what you feel there," she told him, the morning before, in the Archive's outer chamber. "Every civilization that has attempted a direct interface unprepared has made the same mistake — treating the Surveyor's presence like an opponent to be managed, rather than a scale to be witnessed honestly. You have spent three years learning to stay yourself while holding a planet's worth of awareness. That practice is what will carry you through this. Not strategy."

"And if I lose the thread of myself in there," he asked, "the way I nearly did with the Colony Link two weeks ago?"

"Then you will find your way back the same way you did then," she said. "By locating something singular and steady, and measuring outward from it. I cannot go with you into the interface. But you are not going in with nothing. You are going in with three years of exactly this kind of practice already behind you."

He held onto that instruction the following morning, sitting in the Archive's central chamber, Chen Yufei's steady presence in the Colony Link deliberately kept close at the edge of his awareness — not a crutch, but the fixed point Meen-4 had described, the singular thing he could measure everything else against if the vastness ahead of him threatened to blur his own edges the way the Strata's fear once had.

He did not travel anywhere, exactly. His body remained in the Archive's central chamber, seated, breathing steadily. But some other part of him — the part that had, over three years, learned to hold a planet's worth of Circuit nodes and a Colony Link's worth of distributed lives all at once — extended into a folded space that felt, for the brief duration of the interface, less like a place and more like the inside of an argument being carefully, deliberately constructed around him.

The Surveyor's presence there was not a shape. It was closer to a pressure, vast and patient, a quality of attention so total that he understood, immediately and without needing to be told, why no civilization under active review had ever requested this contact lightly.

*You are here,* the presence communicated, not in notation now, but in something closer to direct meaning, unmediated by symbol. *This is unusual. I want you to understand that I have made an unusual request, and that I do not make unusual requests without cause.*

"I understand," he said, or thought, or some combination of both that the interface didn't seem to distinguish clearly. "I'm ready for whatever you need to ask."

*Good,* the presence returned, and something in the texture of it — vast, unhurried, and for the first time, faintly, almost curious — settled over him like the beginning of a conversation neither of them yet knew the ending of.

*Then let us begin.*
