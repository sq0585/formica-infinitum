# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 453 — The Extension Protocol Exists

Meen-4 listened to the full shape of the question without interrupting, the way she always listened to things that mattered, and when he finished, she was quiet long enough that he wondered whether he'd asked something that troubled her more than he'd expected.

"You want to know if the protocol can be safely applied to your mother specifically," she said finally. "Not generally. Specifically."

"Yes."

"Then let me answer that carefully, because the general precedent and the specific case are not quite the same question, and I would rather you understand the difference before you decide anything."

They sat in the Archive's outer chamber, the same low-ceilinged room where she'd once explained the Surveyor's nature to him years ago, and she worked through it methodically, the particular precision she brought to anything she considered genuinely consequential.

"The mechanism itself is well understood," she said. "Fang Ruxue's extension proceeded without complication, and the underlying protocol has existed in Formican practice for considerably longer than either of us has been alive. It does not transform the body dramatically. It extends functional biological years — repairing accumulated wear at a cellular level, recalibrating the body's own maintenance processes to something closer to what they operated at several decades earlier. Your mother's joints, her cardiovascular strain — both fall well within the range the protocol reliably addresses."

"And the risk?"

"Lower than you likely fear, and higher than zero, which is the honest answer for anything worth taking seriously." She held his gaze steadily. "The protocol requires three months of careful, supervised administration. Complications are rare but not unheard of — in perhaps one case in several hundred, historically, the body's own systems respond adversely to the recalibration, requiring the process to be halted and reversed. Fang Ruxue's case had none of this. I would expect your mother's case to proceed similarly, given how closely her presentation matches the profile the protocol handles most reliably. But I will not tell you the risk is nothing, because it isn't, and you deserve the accurate number rather than the comforting one."

He absorbed that, weighing it the way he weighed anything with real stakes attached. "What about precedent — has this been done before, for someone with no cultivation background at all? Fang Ruxue understood what she was consenting to years before she actually decided. My mother would be starting from nothing."

"That," Meen-4 said, "is the actual question underneath your question, and I think you already know the answer, or you would not have asked it so carefully." She paused, considering her own words before offering them. "The protocol itself does not require cultivation background. It is biological, not technique-dependent — your mother's body does not need to understand Qi to benefit from the recalibration, any more than a patient needs to understand pharmacology to benefit from medicine."

"But consent does require understanding."

"Yes. And that is where your actual difficulty lives, not in the protocol's mechanism." She folded her hands, the particular stillness she carried when she was about to say something she considered important enough to deliver without any softening. "You cannot meaningfully explain what you would be asking her to accept without explaining considerably more than the protocol itself — the Codex, the Strata, everything you have built and everything you have become. A seventy-four-year-old woman with no context for any of it cannot give informed consent to a biological intervention she cannot situate within any framework she currently possesses. If you want her genuine, informed yes, Lin Mushi, the disclosure has to come first, complete and unhurried, and only afterward does the extension protocol itself become a question she is actually equipped to answer."

He nodded slowly, understanding that she had simply confirmed, with far more precision, the exact conclusion he'd already been circling for days.

"How long would the full disclosure reasonably take," he asked, "for someone starting from nothing?"

"That depends entirely on her, not on any protocol I can specify," Meen-4 said. "Fang Ruxue built her understanding over months, working alongside the Cultivation Research Collective the entire time. Your mother would need something far more compressed and far more careful — a conversation, not a curriculum. I cannot tell you how long that conversation should take. I can tell you it should take exactly as long as she needs, and not one day less, regardless of how urgently you want her joints and her heart addressed."

He sat with that, the specific patience of the instruction settling into him alongside everything else he'd absorbed. "Thank you for being honest about the risk. I think I needed the real number, not the reassuring one."

"I know," Meen-4 said, something gentler entering her usual precision. "That is, I believe, exactly why you asked me first, rather than simply deciding on your own and presenting her with a conclusion already reached. You wanted to be certain, before you asked her to trust you with something this large, that you had actually earned the right to ask."

He left the Archive with much more information than he'd arrived with, and exactly one decision still, entirely, his own to make.
