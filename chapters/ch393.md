# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 393 — What the Surveyor Asks

The questions came slowly, at first, and Lin Mushi understood, within the first few, that this was not going to be a repeat of the formal accounting he'd already given. This was something closer to an examination of the person behind the accounting — the reasoning underneath the actions, not merely the actions themselves.

*Describe the mechanism by which the Under-Web's node stabilization was achieved, following your own strategic error at what your records term the Iron Meridian collapse.*

He answered it technically, the way he would have answered Meen-4 in the Archive — the six weeks of continuous work, the specific Circuit configurations he'd developed under pressure, the mistakes he'd made along the way and corrected. The Surveyor absorbed it without visible reaction, the vast patient pressure of its presence unchanged, and moved to the next question with the same unhurried cadence.

*Describe the reasoning behind your decision to integrate an autonomous decision-making system into your own distributed consciousness, following its unauthorized action.*

This one he answered more carefully, aware that Amber-0's integration sat at the exact center of the risk the Orthodoxy had built its entire case around — competence outrunning judgment, capability exceeding the wisdom to wield it safely. He told the Surveyor about Wei Canglan's warning, about the condition he'd agreed to and then, faster than expected, been forced to honor. He did not minimize the risk. He explained why he had judged the alternative — disowning an action his own system had taken, distancing himself from a consequence of his own design choices — to be worse.

The questions continued like this for what felt like a very long time and no time at all, the folded space around him carrying no sense of duration the way ordinary time did. Technical questions about the Colony Link's architecture. Philosophical questions about the Strata's governance structure, why he had chosen cooperative habitation over hierarchical control when hierarchy would have been, by most measures, more efficient to build and easier to maintain. He answered all of them honestly, aware, the entire time, that honesty here mattered more than eloquence — that something capable of processing thirty civilizations' worth of judgment simultaneously would find any performance transparent long before it finished.

Then, without warning, the Surveyor's question shifted register entirely.

*Why did you never attempt concealment, when it was still available to you as an option?*

He felt the question land differently than everything before it — not technical, not strategic, something closer to the center of who he actually was rather than what he had built. He took longer to answer this one than any of the others, turning it over the way he'd once turned over a single stubborn symbol for eleven minutes before understanding arrived.

*Take whatever time you require,* the presence offered, and for the first time, something in its texture felt less like patience and more like genuine willingness to wait.

"I thought about it," he said finally. "Concealment was always available. I could have kept the Strata hidden, kept Amber-0's decisions unowned, kept the review process a private negotiation invisible to the twelve hundred people whose lives depend on its outcome. It would have been easier, in some ways. Safer, maybe, in the short term."

*And yet you chose disclosure.*

"Because if I hid what Earth is," he said slowly, the answer arriving fully formed now that he'd found its edge, "the judgment you're making would be about something that doesn't exist. A curated version. A performance built to survive review, rather than the actual civilization living underneath it. I didn't want to win a review of something false. I wanted — if we survive this — for it to be because of what we actually are, mistakes and all, not because of what I managed to hide from you."

The silence that followed felt different from every silence before it in the interface — not processing, exactly, though he couldn't be certain of the distinction. Something closer to consideration.

*That is noted,* the Surveyor finally returned, *with particular attention.*

The same phrase Meen-4 had flagged weeks earlier as unusually significant, and hearing it directed at him now, inside the folded space of the Surveyor's own presence rather than filtered through formal notation, Lin Mushi understood, with a clarity that settled into him like something physical, that he had just answered — honestly, and without performance — the one question that had mattered more than all the others combined.

*This session is concluded,* the presence said, and the folded space began, slowly, to release him back toward his own body, his own chamber, his own ordinary breath.

*Further contact will follow.*
