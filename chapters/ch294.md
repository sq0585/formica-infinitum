# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Ninety-Four — The Colony Link, City Scale

The shift happened during ordinary meditation, which was, he was learning, how the largest changes in his cultivation tended to happen now — not during crisis, not during deliberate effort, but in the quiet accumulated moments when he wasn't straining toward anything in particular.

He'd settled into his evening practice in the small pocket dimension he kept for exactly this purpose, letting the Colony Link's familiar awareness spread outward the way it did every night — Chen Yufei's steady presence nearby, Wei Canglan's evening training in the hall two corridors over, the elder Mrs. Tan's quiet, contented stillness in her room, the accumulated small emotional texture of a Strata full of people going about their evenings.

Except tonight, the texture didn't stay texture.

The eighty-kilometer range had been his for months now — the whole metropolitan sprawl sitting at the edge of his awareness as a distant, diffuse weather system, present but soft-focus, something he had to lean toward deliberately to read with any clarity. Tonight, without any deliberate reaching on his part, the softness resolved. The distant weather stopped being distant. It arrived, all at once, as a single continuous field he no longer had to lean toward at all — every district he'd only ever sensed as blurred aggregate mood suddenly present with the same immediate, ambient clarity he usually reserved for a resident three corridors away.

He opened his eyes, the meditation broken not by discomfort but by simple astonishment.

Chengdu. The entire city, its full twenty million people present in his awareness not as individuals, not even as the broad neighborhood-scale texture he'd grown accustomed to over recent months, but as a single, unified, continuously legible field — its center a dense, overwhelming knot of accumulated human feeling that made his own breathing, for a moment, feel unsteady in his chest.

He sat very still, letting the field settle rather than reaching to expand or contract it, testing the boundaries of something that had apparently sharpened into full coherence overnight rather than growing gradually enough to notice.

---

He found Chen Yufei in the residential wing's common room an hour later, still working through the specific disorientation of having felt an entire city's emotional weather resolve from familiar background texture into a single, seamless, unavoidable field all at once.

"I need you to check something," he said, and told her, as precisely as he could manage, what had happened.

She listened without interrupting, her expression shifting from curiosity to something more careful as the scale of what he was describing became clear.

"City-scale," she said finally. "That's — Lin Mushi, that's not a small jump from where you were."

"I know."

"Do you know what it means, functionally? Not just that you can feel it. What it costs to hold."

He thought about the question honestly, running the same careful accounting he'd learned to trust across two years of expansions that had each, in their turn, demanded something new of him. "I don't fully know yet. It didn't feel like strain, exactly. It felt like — noise. A lot of noise, all at once, before I learned how to let it settle into something I could actually parse rather than just be overwhelmed by."

"That's the part that worries me," she said. "Not the noise. The learning to let it settle. What happens to you if you get good at that? If twenty million people's emotional weather stops being something you lean toward and just becomes background, the same way individual conversations became background months ago?"

He didn't have an immediate answer, and found, sitting with the question, that its absence was itself informative — a gap he hadn't yet filled, sitting exactly where Meen-4's warnings about the Dissolution Path had always pointed.

"I don't know," he admitted. "I think that's exactly the question I need to figure out how to keep asking, rather than assuming I'll remember to ask it once the noise has already become quiet."

---

He began the dedicated meditation practice that same week — not the general cultivation work he'd always done, but something more specific, more deliberately structured, built around a single discipline he set for himself with Chen Yufei's help: every evening, before letting the Colony Link's awareness expand outward, he would first locate himself. Not the city. Not the Strata. Himself — breath, body, the particular weight of being one person sitting in one room, established firmly enough that everything he felt afterward, no matter how vast, would have something solid to return to.

It felt, the first few nights, almost absurdly simple compared to the scale of what it was meant to counterbalance — five minutes of attention to his own breathing before opening himself to twenty million people's accumulated feeling.

But he kept at it, night after night, understanding, even without being able to fully articulate why, that this small, unglamorous discipline was going to matter considerably more, over the months ahead, than any single dramatic breakthrough in cultivation ever could.

He wrote it into his notebook on the seventh night, the entry brief, the way his most important entries had started to become.

> *Colony Link: city scale. Chengdu, in full, as a single field. It doesn't hurt yet. That's the part I need to watch. The nightly practice — five minutes, just breath, just this body, before anything else — isn't a technique. It's an anchor I'm building for myself, on purpose, before I need it rather than after.*

He closed the notebook and sat for a while longer in the quiet of his own single, located breath, the city's vast, distant weather waiting patiently at the edge of his awareness for whenever he chose, deliberately, to let it back in.
