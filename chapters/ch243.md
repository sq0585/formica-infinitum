# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Three — Iron Meridian's Last Move

The Collective's externalized Weaving announcement landed harder than he'd expected, and the silence that followed from Iron Meridian's direction worried him more than any of the louder reactions had.

Celestial Compass had responded with careful scholarly excitement, three new working-group applications within a day. River Jade had sent a cautious but genuinely curious inquiry about licensing terms, the first time they'd used language that implied a future where the device existed as ordinary infrastructure rather than a threat. Even Stone Hall, characteristically blunt, had issued a public statement calling it dangerous and irresponsible — loud, predictable, ultimately just noise.

Iron Meridian said nothing at all.

Chen Yufei noticed the silence too, and didn't trust it any more than he did.

"My old contacts inside the Court have gone quiet," she told him, four days after the announcement, her expression carrying the particular tightness she reserved for genuine concern rather than routine caution. "Not hostile-quiet. Absent-quiet. The kind of silence that usually means a decision's already been made and nobody wants to be the one who leaks it before it's executed."

"What kind of decision?"

"I don't know. That's what worries me. Every other reaction to the announcement has been proportional — excitement, caution, public objection, the things you'd expect from institutions processing a genuine shift in their world. Iron Meridian's silence isn't proportional to anything. It's the silence of people who've stopped debating and started planning."

He sat with this, the cold, procedural unease settling slowly into something closer to genuine worry.

"They've lost the most from this," he said, thinking it through out loud. "Celestial Compass and River Jade already had scholarly reasons to want closer cooperation. Northern Peak's reorganizing from the inside regardless of what the external Courts do. Stone Hall's too unsophisticated to mount anything beyond loud objection. Iron Meridian's the only Court left with both the institutional cohesion and the desperation to actually do something drastic."

"Drastic like what?"

"I don't know yet. That's the part I don't like." He thought about Document 7's warnings on anchor disruption, the structural fragility he'd read about months ago and filed away as theoretical. "But if negotiation and political maneuvering have both failed them, and the Codex itself can't be argued out of existence, there's a narrower category of options left. Most of them involve trying to damage something physical instead of trying to win an argument."

Chen Yufei's expression sharpened at that, the same direction her thoughts had clearly already been moving.

"The anchor points," she said.

"Possibly. I don't have evidence yet. Just the shape of what's left once everything else has been tried and failed."

"Should we warn Liang Jingzhi? Have her watch the survey data more closely?"

"Yes," he said. "Today. If anyone notices unusual activity near an anchor point before I do, it's going to be her instruments, not my own perception. I want her watching closely, and I want to know immediately if anything changes."

He sent the message himself, that evening, asking her to flag any unusual seismic or construction activity near the anchor coordinates without further explanation of why — not wanting to alarm her unnecessarily before he had anything concrete to be alarmed about.

He went to bed that night with the particular unsettled awareness of someone who had identified the shape of a threat without yet knowing where or when it would actually arrive, and slept poorly, the silence from Iron Meridian's direction sitting heavier in his thoughts than any of the louder threats the past months had already handed him.
