# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 499 — The Morning

He woke before dawn, the way he always did on mornings that mattered, and made himself, deliberately, eat a proper breakfast first — his mother's instruction from years of Sunday mornings surfacing unbidden, *you can't think clearly on an empty stomach, and today of all days you'll want to think clearly.*

Chen Yufei had rice porridge waiting when he came downstairs, and he ate all of it, methodically, the way he was about to approach everything else the day required.

*Observation 516,* he wrote, once the meal was finished, the notebook open beside the empty bowl. *Breakfast: complete, properly, as instructed. Beginning full systems check before proceeding.*

He worked through it the way he'd worked through nothing else in the entire notebook's four and a half years — not the urgent, improvised checking of a crisis, but the slow, exhaustive, deliberate confirmation of a man who understood that today's margin for oversight was smaller than any day that had come before it.

*Anchor check.* He reached for each of the five in turn, feeling for their steady, familiar presence the way he'd learned to feel for anything that mattered most. *Chen Yufei: present, confirmed, here in the room. Liang Jingzhi: present, confirmed, extended into the dissolution zone, her signature steady and warm even at that impossible distance. Dr. Shen Yue: present, confirmed, monitoring equipment already active. Wei Canglan: present, confirmed, administrative channels clear for the day. Meen-4: present, confirmed, standing exactly where she always stood when something enormous required her attention.*

*Three hundred and twenty-three: confirmed, individually, through Amber-0's careful census — every single anchor point accounted for, every signature steady, no withdrawals overnight, no coherence failures across the entire distributed network.*

He moved through the infrastructure next, the same careful thoroughness applied to every system that had, across five years, grown into something considerably larger than he'd ever planned for it to become.

*Chitin Circuit network: stable, 3,200 nodes plus off-Earth relay, full integrity confirmed. Amber-0: running, responsive, standing by. Strata City: fully operational, four thousand two hundred residents going about an entirely ordinary Monday, most of them, he suspected, with no particular awareness that today was different from any other. The Ring structures: holding, load-bearing capacity within normal parameters. Liang Jingzhi's position within the dissolution zone: confirmed stable, her own report arriving calm and characteristically plain — "Still here. Tea's cold. Ready when you are."*

He allowed himself a small, genuine smile at that, the particular steadiness of her humor doing more to settle his nerves than any technical confirmation could have managed alone.

*Dr. Shen Yue's monitoring framework: fully active, coherence signature baseline established, threshold model calibrated and ready.*

He read back through the entire entry once it was complete, the most exhaustive, most methodical accounting the notebook had ever held, and found, reading it, that the sheer thoroughness of it had done exactly what he suspected some quiet part of him had built it to do — not merely confirm readiness, but create it, the specific discipline of careful preparation manufacturing, through its own patient completeness, the steadiness he hadn't been entirely certain he'd feel this morning otherwise.

Wei Canglan arrived at the laboratory just past seven, carrying a folder he set down without opening. "Nothing requires your attention today that can't wait," he said. "I made certain of that personally, starting three days ago."

"Thank you."

"The objection stands," Wei Canglan added quietly. "I wanted you to know that, on the record, one final time, before the day actually starts."

"Noted," Lin Mushi said again, the same plain acknowledgment he'd offered before. "Registered. Heard."

Dr. Shen Yue arrived shortly after, her instruments already humming with the particular readiness he recognized from every threshold before this one, and Meen-4 arrived last, exactly as she always did, settling into the corner she'd claimed years ago without ever formally announcing it.

He looked around the laboratory, gathering slowly with the people who mattered most, and understood, checking the notebook one final time, that every system, every person, every careful piece of five years' accumulated preparation, stood exactly where it needed to stand.

The morning was ready. He closed the notebook, and turned to wait for Chen Yufei, and for whatever the day's actual gathering would finally, formally, begin.
