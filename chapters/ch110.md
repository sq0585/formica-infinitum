# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Ten — The Investigator's Visit

Elder Kuo Tianming came to see Chen Yufei on a Friday.

He arrived at her apartment at 10 AM with a paper bag containing two soy milk drinks from a street stall — a gesture that had been calculated precisely, she recognized, to be warm without being conciliatory. He had been doing this for twenty years. He was very good at it.

She had known he was coming since Thursday. She had told Lin Mushi. They had discussed what she would say.

"Your progress reports have been thin," Kuo said, settling into the chair across from her like a man who had earned the right to be comfortable in any room he entered. He was in his late fifties, precise in the way of people who had been precise for long enough that it had become effortless. "Three months of investigation notes that say, essentially, 'situation developing.'"

"The situation has been developing," she said.

"Into what?"

She had thought carefully about what she would give him. Not because she was afraid of him — she wasn't — but because the question of *what Kuo deserved to know* and *when* was exactly the conversation she'd been having with Lin Mushi for three weeks.

"The node's depletion was caused by an individual practitioner who was unaware that the node existed or that it was monitored," she said. "The practitioner has since been made aware. The depletion was not malicious and has been acknowledged as a harm that the practitioner is actively working to remediate."

Kuo looked at her. "Is this practitioner willing to engage with the Northern Peak Court?"

"The practitioner is engaging with me. The Northern Peak Court's investigation created conditions that weren't conducive to broader engagement." She held his gaze. "Your operative, Elder Kuo. Lady Shen arrived in Chengdu with an acquisition mandate."

"She has been recalled," he said, which was news. "The Council revised the protocol after receiving her updated assessment."

She absorbed this. Lady Shen's assessment had apparently been sufficiently alarming to cause the Northern Peak Court to change its approach. "What did the updated assessment say?"

"That the situation is more complex than a standard unaffiliated-practitioner acquisition." He paused. "That the practitioner has ties to... something she declined to name in the official report. Which is unusual. Lady Shen names everything in reports."

She said nothing. Kuo watching her while she said nothing was a well-practiced exchange between two people who were both good at it.

"She didn't name the Strata," she said finally.

Kuo's expression, which was generally controlled, moved fractionally. "She mentioned an underground structure in the vicinity of the original anomaly. She did not access it."

"No. She didn't." Chen Yufei put her soy milk down. "Elder Kuo, I'm going to be direct with you. The practitioner in question has access to materials that make the Northern Peak Court's acquisition mandate not only impractical but counterproductive to everything the Court actually cares about. I'm working to establish a framework for information sharing that would benefit the Courts without requiring anyone to be acquired."

"Who is this practitioner?"

"Someone you've already met." She held his gaze. "You spoke with his former manager. You had his name from the background inquiry."

A long silence.

"Lin Mushi," Kuo said.

"Yes."

"The night shift clerk."

"Former night shift clerk."

Kuo picked up his own soy milk. He was quiet for nearly a minute, which for Kuo was an extended period of visibly recalibrating rather than just being quiet.

"What does he have?" he said finally.

"A complete Formican cultivation framework from primary source material." She paused. "Not fragments. Not fragments interpreted through centuries of transmission errors. The original system."

Another silence.

"I see," Kuo said.

"He has been, consistently, more interested in sharing accurate information than in protecting access. What he's been protecting is the process — making sure that when the information reaches people, it reaches them in a form they can actually use." She paused. "He's been working with me for three months on what that process should look like."

"And the Northern Peak Court—"

"Would be part of what the information reaches. If the Court's approach shifts from acquisition to dialogue." She met his eyes. "Lady Shen's recall suggests the Council is reconsidering."

Kuo set down his soy milk.

He looked at her with the assessment of a man who had spent a career reading rooms and knew when he was being read in return.

"This conversation," he said, "should not have happened without Lin Mushi present."

"I know," she said. "He knows too. This is what we agreed I would tell you."

He was quiet again. Then, with the quality of someone arriving at a conclusion he'd been building toward for the past fifteen minutes: "Arrange a meeting."

"He'll choose the time and place."

"Of course he will," Kuo said, without rancor. He picked up his paper bag and stood. "Tell him I'm not coming as an investigator."

"What are you coming as?"

Kuo looked at her. "Someone who has spent thirty years managing the stewardship of a system he's only beginning to understand he didn't understand." He moved toward the door. "That's a different conversation than an acquisition."

She watched him go.

She sat for a few minutes after the door closed, holding her soy milk.

Then she messaged Lin Mushi: *Kuo wants to meet. Not as investigator. Wants to talk.*

He replied in forty seconds: *Expected. I'll choose the location by tomorrow.*

Then: *Did he lie?*

She thought about the conversation. The calibrated warmth, the paper bag, the precisely chosen words. She thought about *someone who has spent thirty years managing the stewardship of a system he's only beginning to understand he didn't understand*, which was either genuine or an extremely good imitation of genuine.

She replied: *I don't think so. Verify with the circuit readings at whatever location you choose.*

His reply: *Already planning to.*

She finished her soy milk.

The situation was developing, as she'd told Kuo.

Into something she had not predicted when she'd started the investigation.

Into something that might, if handled correctly, be good.
