# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-Nine — Chen Yufei and Lin Mushi

He didn't talk to her about the Northern Peak offer that evening.

He had planned to — it was on the list of conversations he needed to have during the two-week consideration period, and Chen Yufei's judgment was, by now, something he weighted seriously in any decision that touched the Courts. But when she arrived at the archive around eight, carrying a thermos of the noodle shop's broth because she'd noticed he'd been skipping dinner, the evening took a different shape than he'd planned, and he let it.

She settled at the north table with the agricultural records she'd been working through for two weeks — she had developed, over the months, a genuine scholarly absorption in Formican farming theory that had nothing to do with cultivation technique or institutional politics, and that he found himself increasingly glad existed, separate from everything else between them.

He sat across the room with a Chitin Circuit he was redesigning — a sixth-generation unit, smaller, more efficient, intended eventually for the surface district network's expansion.

Neither of them spoke for a long time.

The library held its particular quality of ambient sound — the faint hum of the archive's maintenance circuits, the occasional soft shift of Axis-7's presence at the east wall, the distant warmth of the Layer 3 community's collective field at the edge of his perception. He worked. She read. The amber light held steady.

At some point — he didn't track exactly when, because tracking it would have changed what it was — he became aware that three hours had passed.

He looked up.

She was still reading, her expression the absorbed, slightly furrowed concentration she brought to dense material. The thermos sat between them, mostly empty — he had eaten without quite registering it as a separate event from the work.

She looked up at the same moment, as if his attention had reached her through some channel that didn't require the Colony Link to explain.

"What time is it?" she said.

"Past eleven."

She looked faintly surprised, the way people look surprised when absorption has done its work properly. "I should go soon."

"You should."

Neither of them moved for another few minutes.

"This crop rotation system," she said, finally, gesturing at the container in front of her. "Fourteen thousand years old. Still more sophisticated than anything currently used in Sichuan agriculture. I keep thinking — somewhere on the surface, there's a farmer who would find this genuinely useful, and there's no path for it to reach them."

"Add it to the list," he said. "Things that need a path."

"The list is getting long."

"It is."

She closed the container carefully, the way she handled everything in the archive — with a specific respect that he'd noticed from her first entry and that had never diminished. She stood, stretched, gathered her things.

At the door, she paused.

"This was good," she said. Not elaborating on what *this* was — the three hours, the silence, the shared work, the thermos of broth she'd thought to bring without being asked.

"Yes," he said.

She left.

He sat alone in the archive for a few more minutes, the Chitin Circuit half-redesigned in front of him, Axis-7 a quiet presence at the east wall, the Layer 3 field a steady warmth beneath everything.

He did not write anything about the evening in his notebook. Some things, he had learned, did not improve from being documented. They existed more fully in not being pinned down.

He packed up his materials and went home.

He thought, on the walk back through the late Chengdu night, that there were certain kinds of comfortable presence that accumulated without either person naming what they were accumulating toward, and that this was, he suspected, exactly as it should be — not rushed, not analyzed, simply allowed to continue being what it already was.

He did not mention the evening to anyone.

Neither did she.

But both of them noted it, in the private way that mattered more than any notebook entry could.
