# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 422 — The First Government Contact

The inquiry that mattered most arrived on official letterhead, translated and forwarded to Fang Ruxue through a diplomatic channel neither of them had used before, and Lin Mushi read it three times before he fully trusted that its tone was as carefully, deliberately measured as it appeared to be.

China's Ministry of Science and Technology did not demand. It did not threaten, and it did not, to Lin Mushi's quiet relief, treat Dr. Shen Yue's paper as either hoax or hostile act. It requested, in language so precise and so carefully neutral that Fang Ruxue remarked, reading it, that whoever had drafted it clearly understood exactly how much diplomatic weight every word would carry, formal clarification regarding the claims made in the published research, and an invitation to establish a direct line of communication between the Cultivation Research Collective and appropriate government representatives.

"It's polite," Lin Mushi said, reading it again. "Genuinely polite. Not a trap dressed as politeness."

"It's also unanswerable, in its current form," Fang Ruxue said, setting the letter down with a particular weariness he recognized — the specific exhaustion of someone facing a problem their considerable experience had never actually prepared them for. "We don't have a protocol for this. I mean that literally, not as a figure of speech. There is no established framework for how a hidden civilization responds to a formal government inquiry asking, politely, whether it exists and what it wants."

"I know," he said again, the same two words he'd offered days earlier, when the weight of the problem had first become fully clear. "We need one."

They sat with the letter between them for a long while, turning over the shape of what an answer might even look like — not evasion, neither of them wanted that, and not the kind of overwhelming full disclosure Dr. Shen Yue's paper had already forced into the world without either of their careful staging. Something else. Something new, that neither of them had a template for.

"What do we actually want from this relationship," Fang Ruxue asked eventually, the question landing with more weight than its simplicity suggested. "Not what are we obligated to give them. What do we want, going forward, from a government that now knows we exist and clearly intends to keep knowing?"

Lin Mushi thought about it carefully before answering, aware that whatever he said now would likely shape decades of whatever came next. "I don't want to be governed by them," he said slowly. "The Strata has its own structure, its own way of making decisions, built by the people who actually live there. I don't want to hand that away just because a government asked nicely."

"Agreed."

"But I also don't want permanent hidden conflict," he continued. "Years of the Strata operating in secret defiance of a state that knows we're here and resents not being consulted. That's not sustainable, and it's not honest, given that we've just spent months arguing to an entire galactic institution that honesty is the foundation everything else should be built on."

"So somewhere between full submission and permanent secrecy," Fang Ruxue said slowly, testing the shape of it. "A relationship. Negotiated. Ongoing."

"Something like that. I don't know exactly what it looks like yet. But I think that's the direction the answer needs to point, even if the specifics take time to work out properly."

Fang Ruxue nodded slowly, already reaching for a fresh sheet of paper, the particular brisk competence returning to her posture that Lin Mushi had come to rely on across years of watching her turn overwhelming problems into manageable, structured plans.

"Then let's draft a response," she said, "that says exactly that, carefully, without promising specifics we haven't actually worked out yet. An acknowledgment. An openness to dialogue. A clear statement that the Strata intends to remain self-governing, while genuinely welcoming a working relationship built on mutual transparency rather than either submission or secrecy."

"That's a lot to fit into a single response."

"Then we'll need more than one response," she said, already writing. "This is going to take weeks, Lin Mushi. Possibly months. We are, effectively, drafting a treaty framework from nothing, with a government that has never negotiated with anything like us before, because nothing like us has ever previously existed for them to negotiate with."

He picked up a pen of his own, settling in beside her, understanding, with the particular steady resolve he'd learned to bring to every genuinely difficult project of the past three years, that this — patient, careful, unglamorous negotiation — was simply the next long project the world now required of him.
