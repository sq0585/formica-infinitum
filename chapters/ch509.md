# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 509 — The Coherence Check

Dr. Shen Yue's framework had activated the instant Formless Scale was reached, the readings arriving in the same quiet, patient cadence they had carried since Ch.496 first brought the instrument online — the same 0.0 to 1.0 scale, the same warning-system caution she had built into it from the beginning, the same careful refusal to call it anything grander than what it actually was.

She turned the display so the whole room could see it.

*0.97.*

No one spoke for a moment. He felt, through the Anchor Protocol, the particular shape of five different silences — Chen Yufei's, warm and relieved; Wei Canglan's, characteristically searching the number for a flaw before allowing himself to trust it; Meen-4's, ancient and considering; Liang Jingzhi's, arriving from the dissolution zone steady and unsurprised, the way she greeted most things.

"Not perfect," Dr. Shen Yue said, breaking the silence herself, the same plain accuracy she brought to everything. "I want to be precise about that, because I think precision matters more today than reassurance does."

"What would perfect look like," he asked.

"I don't know. I've never seen a reading above 0.99, and that was you, six years ago, on an entirely ordinary Tuesday, doing nothing more extraordinary than making tea." She studied the number a moment longer. "I consider 0.97, under these specific circumstances, considerably more than acceptable. I would go further. I consider it remarkable."

"You built the framework not to be impressed," he said, gently. "You told me that yourself. A warning system, not a celebration."

"I did say that." She allowed herself, briefly, something that was almost a smile. "I'm allowed to be both things at once. The framework isn't compromised by my also being pleased with what it's telling me."

Wei Canglan had been quiet through the exchange, his attention still fixed on the number the way it fixed on any figure he had not personally verified, and Lin Mushi felt the question arrive through the Anchor Protocol before Wei Canglan spoke it aloud, the specific texture of it unmistakably his — careful, procedural, the question of a man who had built an entire second career out of making sure no one in the Strata was ever caught unprepared by a number they hadn't planned for.

"What happens if it drops," Wei Canglan asked.

"I'll tell him immediately," Dr. Shen Yue said, without hesitation, the answer clearly one she had rehearsed for exactly this question, months ago, long before there had been an actual reading to attach it to.

"And then?" Wei Canglan pressed, and Lin Mushi understood, hearing it, that this was not really a question about mechanism. It was the same question Wei Canglan had been quietly, persistently asking in one form or another since the day he'd first sided against Elder Kuo and lost everything for it — *what is the plan when the plan stops being enough* — and he understood, too, why the answer that came next mattered considerably more to Wei Canglan than any single number ever would.

"And then," Dr. Shen Yue said, simply, "we figure it out."

It was not an elaborate answer. It was not, Lin Mushi thought, meant to be. It was the same answer the whole last five years had ultimately rested on, stated plainly enough that even a man who trusted very little without seeing its full architecture first could recognize it as sufficient: not a guarantee, not a mechanism engineered against every possible failure, but five specific people, and one dissolution zone full of volunteers, and a shared, working agreement that whatever came next, it would be met together rather than alone.

Wei Canglan considered this for a moment, and then, apparently satisfied, gave the same small precise nod he gave every closed item on every agenda he had ever kept.

"Acceptable," he said.

Lin Mushi felt something in the room ease, all five Anchors at once, the particular collective exhale of people who had spent the morning holding something enormous and had just been told, in the plainest possible terms, that it had held.

He looked around the laboratory — at Chen Yufei's tea, cold again; at Wei Canglan's unopened folder; at Dr. Shen Yue's instruments, steady at 0.97; at Meen-4, still and watchful in her corner; at the particular warm, vast, specific presence of Liang Jingzhi holding her portion of everything from a distance no ordinary measurement could properly describe.

He was Formless. He was, by every measure that actually mattered, still entirely himself.

He picked the pen back up, and wrote nothing more that morning beyond the number itself, because some facts, he had learned, were better left exactly as plain as they arrived.
