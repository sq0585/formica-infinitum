# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 444 — The Stellar Scale Breakthrough — Conditions

He finished the standard classifications eleven days later, working through the remaining stellar types with the particular steady momentum of someone close enough to a goal that the final stretch of it stops feeling like effort and starts feeling, instead, like simple continuation.

He knew, before he'd even finished writing the last entry's summary, that something had shifted.

*Observation 418: reached for the solar field's edge again, unprompted, the way I've been testing myself every few days since the resonance principle first clicked. It resolved almost immediately this time. Not fully — there's still a gap, still texture I can sense but can't yet parse completely. But it's a narrow gap now. Narrow enough that I suspect the next genuine comprehension event closes it entirely.*

He sat with that sentence a long while, the specific, familiar weight of a threshold he recognized from every previous scale advancement — Dimensional Weaver, Colony Link, Planetary Architect, each one arriving eventually at exactly this feeling, the sense of standing close enough to a door that he could, if he wanted, simply reach for it directly.

He did not reach for it directly.

*Rule,* he wrote, and found, writing it, that the decision had actually already been made somewhere beneath conscious thought, days ago probably, and he was only now catching up to articulating it properly. *Every previous breakthrough has arrived the same way — alone, unannounced, in whatever ordinary moment happened to be occurring when the comprehension finally resolved. Dimensional Weaver, in a stairwell. Colony Link, during meditation I wasn't even trying to push. Planetary Architect, in circumstances I still don't fully understand the mechanics of. Each one has cost something — a dormancy event, a period of dangerous instability, hours or days of aftermath no one was prepared to help me through because no one knew it was coming.*

*This one, I have the rare privilege of seeing arrive before it does. I am not going to waste that.*

He thought of Liang Jingzhi's report, still years off in a future he hadn't reached yet but could already sense the shape of — *I am here and I am also forty million years of absence, and it is not painful, it is very large* — and understood, distantly, that whatever came after Stellar Scale would demand exactly this kind of deliberate preparation, magnified considerably. Better, he thought, to practice the discipline now, at a scale he could still reasonably manage, than to arrive at something larger having never once chosen his own threshold on purpose.

He checked the relay chain, composed a longer message than his usual check-ins, and sent it to all five of them at once — Chen Yufei, Liang Jingzhi, Dr. Shen Yue, Wei Canglan, Meen-4 — a single transmission carrying the same request to each.

*I'm close. Close enough that I think the crossing could happen almost any time now, without warning, the way every other one has. I don't want that this time. I want to come home, finish the last of the preparation properly, and do this deliberately, with all of you present, in the pocket dimension laboratory, so that whatever it costs, someone is actually there to help me through it. I know that's a lot to ask, coordinating five schedules around a moment I can't predict precisely. I'm asking anyway.*

The replies arrived faster than the relay chain's usual lag should have allowed, as if all five of them had been sitting, in their separate corners of an ordinary Tuesday, already half-expecting the message.

*Name the day and I'll be there,* Chen Yufei wrote, no hesitation in it at all.

*I would attend regardless of the specific timing,* Liang Jingzhi added, with the particular practical warmth he'd come to recognize as entirely characteristic of her. *This is not an imposition. This is what the word Anchor was always supposed to mean.*

Dr. Shen Yue's reply carried, alongside her confirmation, a note about equipment she wanted to bring — monitoring instruments, she explained, built specifically to track exactly this kind of transition, data that might matter considerably later even if it changed nothing about the moment itself.

Wei Canglan's message was characteristically brief and characteristically thorough. *Confirmed. I'll clear the administrative calendar. Also — for what it's worth — this is, I think, the first time in five years you've asked us to help with something before it became an emergency. I'd like that noted somewhere.*

Meen-4's response came last, and carried, underneath its usual precision, something he recognized immediately as pride. *I will be present. And Lin Mushi — this decision, choosing to arrive at a threshold deliberately rather than being carried across it by accident, is itself a form of the comprehension you have spent months building. You are learning to hold the crossing the same way you learned to hold everything else. Carefully. On purpose. With people who love you standing close enough to catch whatever falls.*

He read all five replies through twice, feeling something steady and warm settle into the space where anxiety about the coming crossing might otherwise have lived, and began, finally, the long segmented journey home.

The distance had not gotten any shorter. But for the first time since leaving Earth, he found himself genuinely looking forward to what waited on the other side of it — not merely the comprehension he'd come to find, but the five people who had, without exception, said yes before he'd even finished asking.
