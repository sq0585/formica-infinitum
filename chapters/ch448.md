# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 448 — The Return to Earth

The technical problem, once he'd slept enough to think about it properly, turned out to be more interesting than the emotional one, and he was grateful for it in the specific way he was always grateful when a difficult feeling came packaged with a difficult problem he could actually work on.

Stellar Scale awareness did not turn off. He'd suspected as much within hours of waking, and two days of careful testing confirmed it: the solar system's architecture, the faint nearby stars at the edge of perception, even the far distant weight of Veth-Karun's dissolution zone, all of it remained present, constant, a layer of awareness sitting quietly underneath everything else the way Planetary Architect scale's planetary hum had settled, years ago, into something he no longer had to consciously manage.

The question wasn't whether he could still access it. The question was where he chose to let his primary attention actually live.

*Observation 425: tested three configurations over the past two days. Configuration one — primary attention at Stellar Scale, Earth-scale awareness as background — produces excellent comprehension but measurably degrades ordinary interaction. I answered Chen Yufei's question about dinner eleven seconds after she asked it. She noticed. I noticed her noticing.*

*Configuration two — primary attention at Earth scale, Stellar Scale fully suppressed — is not actually possible. I tried for approximately six hours. The stellar awareness does not suppress; it simply waits, the way Colony Link waited during the early meditation discipline, present regardless of whether I'm attending to it directly.*

*Configuration three — primary attention at Earth scale, Stellar Scale held at a deliberate, managed distance, available but not intrusive — worked. This is, I think, going to have to be the answer, the same way it was the answer for every scale before this one.*

He thought, working through the third configuration's specific discipline, of the five-minute practice he'd built for himself years ago, in the early disoriented weeks of Colony Link — five minutes of breath, five minutes of simply being one located person, before letting the vast field back in each evening. He rebuilt the same discipline now, scaled up considerably, and found, to his relief, that the underlying principle held even at this much larger distance. Attention was a choice. It had always been a choice. The scale of what waited to be chosen had simply gotten larger, again, the way it always eventually did.

He tested it properly at dinner that evening, sitting across from Chen Yufei at the small table with the window's evening light fading behind her, and made himself, deliberately, entirely present — not performing presence, not merely suppressing the stellar awareness by force, but genuinely choosing, moment to moment, to let Earth-scale attention hold the foreground.

"You're here," she said, partway through the meal, something in her tone suggesting she'd noticed the difference from the past two days.

"I'm here," he agreed. "It took some deliberate work to get back to it. I wanted you to know that — not because it should have been automatic, but because I think it matters that it wasn't, and I chose it anyway."

She considered that for a moment, setting down her chopsticks. "Is this going to keep being a choice? Every time, for the rest of however long we have together?"

"I think so," he admitted. "I don't think it stops being a choice. I think Formless Scale, whenever I actually reach it, is going to ask the same question at a size that makes tonight's version look almost easy by comparison — whether I come back to a body, to a room, to people, or whether I let the awareness simply keep expanding because expanding is, at some level, always going to be easier than choosing to stay small enough to still be someone's dinner companion."

"And you'll choose to come back."

"Every time," he said, and found, saying it, that it wasn't merely reassurance offered for her benefit. It was, as far as he could currently tell, simply true. "It's not a retreat. I want to be careful about that word, because I think it's the wrong one. It's not smaller than the scale I could hold instead. It's just — this is where the things I actually love happen to live. The rest of it is real. This is what matters."

She reached across the table and took his hand, not saying anything further, and he let the stellar awareness settle back into its managed distance, present and patient, while the ordinary, specific warmth of an evening meal with someone he loved held, deliberately, the whole of his attention.

He would make the same choice again, he understood, though he didn't yet know how much larger the choosing would eventually have to become before the story asked it of him one final time.
