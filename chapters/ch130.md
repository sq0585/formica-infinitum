# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Thirty — Lady Shen Approaches

She crossed the threshold on a Monday at 2:13 AM.

He knew the precise time because he had a Chitin Circuit installed in the parking structure above the entry point, and the circuit's log showed a structured Qi signature at Ratio 1.4 entering the boundary layer at 2:13 and not emerging.

He was in the archive when it happened.

Not by accident. He had been monitoring the circuit data for three days and had predicted, from her approach pattern's increasing frequency near the parking structure, that Monday or Tuesday would be the crossing. He had arranged to be in the Strata before she crossed, specifically so that he would be present in the space when she arrived rather than arriving after her.

Position mattered.

He was sitting at the archive table with his notation work, Axis-7 at her usual east-wall position, when his Chitin Circuit app showed the signature crossing.

He sent a message to Chen Yufei: *She's in.*

Chen Yufei's reply in thirty seconds: *On my way.*

He put the phone in his bag.

He kept working.

---

She entered the outer library twenty-three minutes after the crossing, which was consistent with having entered at the thin section and moved carefully through the unfamiliar main hall. He heard her — or rather, perceived the shift in the ambient Qi field's organization as she moved through it. The field organized around her compression signature the way a stream organized around a stone.

She stopped at the library door.

He looked up.

She was in the doorway, at Ratio 1.4, looking at the archive. At Ratio 1.4 she was not much smaller than her normal scale — the ratio reduction wasn't dramatic at this level — but the Strata's scale made everything look slightly different anyway. She looked like someone who had been told they were about to see something significant and had arrived and was receiving the information that *significant* had been an understatement.

He gave her the silence.

She had compound interest in this moment. He was not going to interrupt it.

After approximately ninety seconds, she looked at him.

He said: "You should probably come in."

She came in.

He pulled a chair back from the table. He set it across from him, which was the position that said *we are going to talk* rather than the adjacent position that said *we are going to work side by side.*

She sat.

He looked at her.

She was fifty-two by his estimate, with the posture of someone who had maintained a physical practice for decades. The Qi signature he'd been reading from circuit logs was cleaner in person — more precisely organized than Elder Kuo's, more intricately constructed than Chen Yufei's Iron Meridian framework, with an architecture he was now familiar enough with to recognize as Northern Peak's atmospheric cultivation structure. She had been practicing her tradition at full commitment for at least thirty years.

She was looking at the archive shelves.

"Preserved," she said.

"Yes. The chitin-composite material has active maintenance inscriptions. The archive has been running its own preservation protocols without external intervention."

She looked at him. "For how long?"

"The oldest material in this section: Formican-age original, by the material quality. Many millions of years." A pause. "The curation — the organization, the access tiers, the dimensional locks — that's more recent. Approximately two thousand years by the layering."

She was quiet for a moment.

"You've been here for months," she said.

"Since October."

"You have access to all of it."

"Not all of it. The access tiers are tied to capability thresholds. I can access the outer library and the Secondary Archive. The Intermediate Archive requires Meen-4's permission, which I've been earning through an exchange arrangement. The inner archive: I don't have access yet."

"What determines access?"

"Comprehension. Compression ratio. Communication register fluency." He met her gaze. "The archive tests whether you understand enough to receive what it contains. Not your credentials — your comprehension."

She looked at the shelves. He let her look.

Then, without looking away from the shelves: "What do you want?"

It was the question she'd had since the beginning. He had been waiting for her to ask it directly, because the direct question deserved the direct answer and the direct answer was the only one that was useful here.

"What I want," he said, "is for things to be right. For the cultivation world to understand what it's been practicing and why it works. For the Formican community below to have a human relationship with the surface that isn't based on concealment. For the Under-Web to be maintained by people who understand what they're maintaining." He paused. "I want the knowledge in this archive to be accessible to people who have developed enough to use it well."

She was quiet.

"And me," she said. "What do you want from me specifically?"

He thought about this.

"I don't know yet," he said honestly. "You're here on your own time, with your own purpose. What you want matters. I can tell you what I have, and you can tell me what you want, and we can see if there's an honest arrangement that serves both."

She turned to look at him.

The Colony Link gave him a faint reading of her emotional field — not at the group-scale resolution he'd been developing, but the close-range individual sensitivity he'd had for months. What was in her field: a quality he read as *assessment* layered over something that might have been *relief,* or might have been the quality of someone who had been braced for a very different conversation and was recalibrating.

He had not offered her terms. He had offered her honesty.

She had come here expecting leverage. He had offered exchange.

She was deciding what to do with the difference.

He waited.
