# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-Nine — The Ambush, Response

He compressed to Ratio 4 in the half-second he had, deeper than he'd ever needed on an ordinary street, the kind of depth he usually reserved for careful, deliberate work rather than split-second survival.

The world's texture shifted around him immediately, the alley's molecular architecture suddenly available to his perception in a way that gave him options his attackers almost certainly hadn't accounted for. The incoming technique, calibrated for an ordinary human silhouette occupying ordinary space, found nothing where it expected a target — he felt it pass close enough to register as pressure against his Compression field, close enough that a fraction of a second's difference in his timing would have ended this encounter before it began.

It never connected.

He didn't counterattack. He'd promised himself, across the careful work of the past weeks, that this wasn't going to become an exchange of harm if he had any way to prevent it — even against people who had just demonstrated, unambiguously, that they intended exactly that kind of harm toward him.

He reached instead for the principle underneath the Spatial Anchor Array, adapted hastily for a confrontation he hadn't had days to prepare for. Not the full boundary technique, which needed setup the moment didn't allow. Something narrower and cruder, born of necessity rather than careful architecture: a non-Euclidean folding of the alley's coherent geometry, deliberate disorientation rather than elegant containment.

He began folding the space around him.

The second attacker's strike went wide, aimed at a position he'd already left. The third lost his footing entirely, the alley's subtly wrong angles disrupting a stance built for ordinary, predictable ground. He felt the fourth attacker's confusion arrive as a hesitation in their approach — the particular uncertainty of someone realizing, mid-engagement, that the fight they'd planned for no longer matched the fight they were actually standing inside of.

He retreated to the alley's far wall and held there, Compression steady at Ratio 4, the improvised geometry holding around him with considerably less elegance than his more careful work but with enough effectiveness that four trained, coordinated attackers found themselves unable to locate him with any reliability at all.

He sat down against the wall.

It was, he understood even as he did it, an unusual choice in the middle of a fight for his life — but the geometry didn't require him standing, didn't require anything from him except sustained attention, and sitting cost him considerably less energy than maintaining a combat stance through however long this needed to last.

He waited.

Gou Ping's voice reached him first, sharp with frustration, calling instructions to the others that the alley's confused architecture scattered into fragments before they could coordinate any meaningful response. Footsteps moved past him, close enough to hear clearly, oriented toward a version of the alley that no longer matched the one he occupied.

Ten minutes passed. Then twenty.

He felt the attackers' coordination fracture further with each passing minute, frustration giving way to genuine uncertainty, the particular psychological toll of operating inside a space that refused to behave the way trained instinct insisted it should.

At the thirty-minute mark, he felt one of the four — not Gou Ping — break off entirely, footsteps retreating toward what they clearly hoped was the alley's exit.

By the fortieth minute, all four had withdrawn, Gou Ping's signature the last to go, carrying a frustrated, simmering intensity that told Lin Mushi clearly this wouldn't be the last attempt, however cleanly this particular one had failed.

He let his Compression ease back toward ordinary baseline once the last signature crossed beyond his sensing range, the careful, improvised geometry releasing along with it, the alley settling back into its ordinary, unremarkable shape around him.

He sat against the wall for a few minutes longer, his hands steady, his breathing controlled, and felt something that wasn't quite triumph and wasn't quite relief.

He had survived an assassination attempt, alone, on an ordinary street, without harming a single person who had come specifically intending to kill him.

He thought that mattered. He thought, sitting there in the quiet aftermath, that it mattered more than almost anything else this conflict had asked of him so far.

He stood, finally, and continued the walk home, the night around him ordinary again, indifferent, exactly as it had been before four people had tried, carefully and deliberately, to end his life inside it.
