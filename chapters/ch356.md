# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifty-Six — The Strata City, Community Issues

The dispute reached him secondhand, the way most ordinary community friction now did, filtered through Fang Ruxue's careful governance process rather than arriving at his door directly the way conflicts once had during the Strata's earlier, smaller years.

"Workspace allocation," Fang Ruxue explained, during their regular check-in, her tone carrying the specific, weary patience of someone handling something genuinely mundane rather than genuinely alarming. "Two research groups, both wanting the same pocket-dimension workspace for overlapping hours. Neither is willing to compromise on timing. It's been escalating for about a week."

"Do you need me involved?"

"No," she said, without hesitation. "That's actually why I'm telling you about it — not to ask for your intervention, but so you understand the panel is already handling it properly, through the process we built specifically for this kind of thing."

He felt a small, familiar instinct rise in his chest — the old reflex to step in, to resolve things directly, the habit of two years spent being the person everyone eventually brought their problems to.

He caught the instinct, deliberately, and set it aside.

"Tell me how the panel's approaching it," he said instead, choosing curiosity over intervention.

"Rotating panel, exactly as designed. One human resident, one Formican resident, one neutral party — currently me, though we're still working toward transitioning that role to the broader community. They're gathering usage data from both groups, timing patterns, actual necessity versus preference. It's slow. It's occasionally tedious. It's also working exactly the way I hoped it would when I drafted the framework."

---

He found himself, over the following days, deliberately resisting the urge to check on the dispute's progress more closely than the situation actually warranted, understanding, each time he caught the impulse, that the discipline itself mattered as much as the outcome.

He mentioned it to Chen Yufei one evening, working through the specific, unfamiliar sensation of watching something important happen without his direct involvement.

"It's strange," he admitted. "I keep wanting to just solve it. It would take me twenty minutes, probably. Talk to both groups, find a compromise, move on."

"Would that actually be better?"

He considered the question honestly. "No. I don't think it would. If I solve it, everyone learns that the fastest path to resolution is getting my attention, rather than trusting the structure Fang Ruxue built specifically to handle this. That undermines the whole point of the framework."

"So you're just going to let it be slow and occasionally tedious."

"I'm going to let it be exactly what it needs to be," he said, something settling into the decision as he spoke it aloud. "A real community, working through a real disagreement, using a real process, without me stepping in every time something gets uncomfortable."

---

The dispute resolved itself, eventually, over the following two weeks — not dramatically, not through any single decisive intervention, but through the panel's patient, methodical work, a compromise schedule finally emerging that satisfied neither group completely but that both, ultimately, accepted as fair.

Fang Ruxue reported the resolution with the same understated efficiency she brought to everything, no particular fanfare attached to what had, in its own small way, represented a genuine milestone.

"It held," she said. "The framework worked exactly as designed, start to finish, without requiring either of us to intervene."

"How does that feel?" he asked.

"Satisfying," she admitted, something warm entering her usually measured tone. "I spent thirty-one years watching institutions fail because they couldn't handle exactly this kind of small, unglamorous friction. Watching this one succeed, even at something this minor, means more to me than I probably let show."

He nodded, understanding, sitting with her quiet satisfaction, that this — a workspace dispute, patiently and unremarkably resolved through structure rather than authority — represented something considerably more significant than its small scale suggested.

He wrote about it that night, brief but deliberate.

> *A workspace dispute resolved itself this week, without me. That sounds small. I don't think it is. I think it might be the clearest proof yet that this city has become a real place — not an extension of me, not something that depends on my constant attention to function, but a genuine community, capable of its own ordinary, unglamorous friction, and its own ordinary, unglamorous resolution.*
