# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 506 — The Dissolution Temptation

It arrived, as he had expected it might, without announcing itself as anything at all.

There was no sensation of danger. No warning he could have written down beforehand and handed to himself as preparation, because the entire nature of the thing, he understood in the instant it began, was that it did not feel like a threat. It felt like an idea occurring to him, gently, reasonably, the way a good idea occurs — the sense, arriving from nowhere in particular, that the boundary he had been so carefully, deliberately maintaining was, on reflection, a small and somewhat arbitrary thing to keep insisting on.

*You could let this go,* the thought did not quite say, because it was not really a thought, not a voice, not anything with the courtesy to identify itself as separate from him. It was simply there, woven into the same fabric he was currently perceiving directly, the way a slight undertow is woven into water that otherwise looks perfectly calm from the shore. *The perception would be complete. You are the only thing narrowing it.*

He held.

It was not, he would understand later, a single continuous refusal. It was many small ones, arriving faster than he could count them, each one requiring the same specific act of insistence Meen-4 had described a chapter of his life ago as *refusal* — not strength, not power, simply the repeated, unglamorous choice to remain a located, particular person rather than the considerably vaster, considerably calmer thing the undertow kept quietly suggesting he could become instead.

Chen Yufei's connection intensified — not a rescue, he understood even as it happened, but a fact she was offering him, the plain physical reality of someone who existed as herself, wholly, undiminished, on the other side of the pull he was resisting.

Wei Canglan's presence arrived beside it, characteristically, absurdly specific: irritated, in this exact moment, about something entirely mundane — a Strata council vote he'd lost that morning over drainage easements in the eastern district, the small, petty, wonderfully ordinary irritation of a man who cared, deeply, about infrastructure nobody else found interesting. It was not comfort exactly. It was evidence. Proof that somewhere on the other side of the undertow, a specific, irreplaceable person was annoyed about drainage.

His mother's garden arrived next, uninvited, unbidden — the particular smell of the soil she turned every spring, the tomatoes she'd been proud of last year, the exact quality of her laugh when he'd once told her, badly, some fraction of what he actually did with his days.

A blank page.

He held all of it — not as a wall, not as a fortress, but the way a person holds a handful of small, specific, irreplaceable things while a current tries, patiently, persistently, to convince him that letting them scatter would feel like relief.

*Observation 506,* he would write later, once the twelve seconds had passed and he trusted his hand enough to hold a pen again. *Twelve seconds. I know the exact figure because Dr. Shen Yue's instruments logged it precisely, and I have looked at the number several times since, the way a person checks a wound after the fact to confirm it actually happened. Twelve seconds is not long. It felt considerably longer while I was inside it, and I suspect that discrepancy is itself the entire danger — that dissolution does not need very much real time to accomplish what it accomplishes. It only needs a person to stop counting.*

*I did not stop counting.*

He came back to himself the way a swimmer comes back to shore — not with any single dramatic motion, but with the accumulating, unglamorous fact of solid ground reasserting itself beneath feet that had, for twelve seconds, forgotten ground was something he required.

Dr. Shen Yue's voice reached him, careful, deliberately even. "The reading dipped through the event. It's climbing back now. Steadily."

"I'm here," he said, out loud, for the first time in some while, and felt how strange the sound of his own physical voice had become, small and specific and entirely, wonderfully particular against the backdrop of everything else he currently perceived.

"I know," she said. "The instruments told me before you did."

He almost laughed. He did laugh, a small, real sound, and felt Chen Yufei's connection warm at it, and Wei Canglan's sharpen with something that was, underneath the characteristic irritation, unmistakably relief.

Twelve seconds. He held the number the way he held everything now — specifically, deliberately, and without letting it become, in his own accounting of the morning, anything larger than exactly what it had been.

The sixth scale remained open around him. The seventh waited beyond it, closer now than it had been an hour ago.

He picked the pen back up, and did not, this time, need to remind his hand of anything at all.
