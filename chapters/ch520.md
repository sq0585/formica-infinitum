# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 520 — The Lineage, Understood

He had carried the discrepancy for six years without ever quite letting himself examine it directly.

Veth-Karun's dissolution was forty million years old. Every record the Archive held, every account the Collective's own careful, ancient bureaucracy had ever filed, agreed on the figure without exception. Ren Wuji had died in a Chengdu convenience store roughly four hundred years before this exact morning, a man who had lived, by every measure Lin Mushi had ever been able to verify, an unusually long but recognizably human span of time. And Ren Wuji had taught the person who became this.

He had known, since Arc IV, that the two facts did not obviously fit together. He had chosen, deliberately, not to force them into fitting.

"You want the mechanism," Axis-7 said, watching him consider the question he hadn't yet asked. "I understand the wanting. I do not think I can give it to you, and I want to be honest about that before I say anything further, rather than let you spend the next several minutes hoping for something I cannot actually provide."

"I'm not asking for the mechanism," he said, and found, saying it, that this was mostly true. "I'm asking for whatever you can honestly give me."

She considered that a long moment, the specific ancient stillness of someone deciding how much of an enormous, only partially understood thing could be safely handed to another mind without breaking something in the handing.

"The deepest folds Formicans ever built do not keep time the way a surface world does," she said finally. "This much I can tell you plainly, because it is closer to established fact than to speculation. Ren Wuji went deeper than any human before or since — deeper, I suspect, than even he fully understood at the time, in pursuit of an education he believed his student required and could not otherwise reach. What he carried out of that depth, he carried alone, for longer than either of us can comfortably describe. I do not know the precise relationship between the years he experienced and the years the surface world counted while he experienced them. I am not certain anyone still living does. I am not certain the question has an answer stated the way you would prefer to receive it."

He sat with that.

He had expected, somewhere in the part of himself that still worked the way it had five years ago, methodically, hungrily, wanting every gap closed before he allowed himself to move past it, to feel frustration at an answer this incomplete. He found, instead, that he did not.

*Observation 520,* he wrote, slowly, and understood, writing it, that this entry would be one of the last of its kind — not because the questions had stopped, but because he had finally, at considerable cost, learned something about which questions actually required closing. *I do not fully understand the relationship between Ren Wuji's four hundred years and Veth-Karun's forty million. I am no longer certain full understanding is the point, or was ever the point, of anything this deep or this old. What I understand instead is this: a man went further than anyone else has ever gone, alone, carrying an education for a student he could not save and a legacy for a stranger he had not yet met, and whatever the true shape of the time he spent doing it, he spent all of it, in the end, in service of people other than himself.*

"That's not an answer," Axis-7 said, watching him write.

"No," he agreed. "I don't think it needs to be, anymore."

She studied him a long moment, something in her ancient, careful attention settling into a shape he had never quite seen from her before — not satisfaction exactly, something adjacent to it, the particular quality of a very old mind watching a much younger one arrive, unassisted, at a conclusion she had privately hoped for and never once dared to promise.

"I would like to see what comes after," she said again, softer this time, less an open question than a simple, honest statement of intent. "I meant it the first time. I mean it considerably more now."

He held her gaze, and did not try, this time, to close the last remaining gap between them with words. Some things, he had learned, across everything the morning had already given him, were better left exactly as open as they honestly were.
