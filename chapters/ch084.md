# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Eighty-Four — Elder Kuo's Report

The report reached the Northern Peak Court's Elder Council on a Thursday, which was six days after Elder Kuo Tianming had obtained Lin Mushi's name and begun the enhanced investigation.

The report was thorough, precise, and — in the way of thorough and precise documents — alarming to the people who understood what they were reading.

Lin Mushi did not know the report's exact contents. He knew of the report's existence through Chen Yufei, who knew through a contact in the Northern Peak Court's administrative office who owed her a professional courtesy and understood that professional courtesies were how information moved in institutions that didn't share information officially.

What she could tell him: the Council had classified him as a Priority Acquisition Target. A senior operative had volunteered to assist with the acquisition. The operative's name was Lady Shen Baoling.

"Who is she?" he asked.

"The Northern Peak Court's most accomplished active practitioner. Rank equivalent to a third-generation elder, which is significant — the Court has a lineage system that doesn't promote quickly." A pause. "She is also, by reputation, the practitioner who located and acquired the last unaffiliated Qi source the Northern Peak Court acquired. That was eleven years ago. The practitioner in question is still in the Court's *secure cultivation environment.*"

He noted that she said *secure cultivation environment* with the same flat inflection he'd used when he repeated it. A shared recognition that the euphemism was not doing useful work.

"What does she want specifically?"

"The report mentions an unknown notation system. The Council's interest is in the notation — they believe it's a key to a Qi source they haven't been able to access. Lady Shen's interest—" Chen Yufei paused. "Lady Shen reads acquisition reports carefully. She will have noticed the geological survey accuracy. She will have noticed the self-taught efficiency. She will understand, faster than the Council does, what she's looking at."

"She'll want the Codex."

"She'll want what the Codex gives access to."

"How did Kuo find me to begin with?" he asked. "The dead node's in your Court's territory, not theirs."

"The report traces it to two data points, six months apart." Chen Yufei said it the way she'd clearly rehearsed saying it, plainly, because plainness was the courtesy. "Last spring, something drew briefly on a minor flow out past the western district — three hours, then a clean stop, the kind that reads as controlled use rather than leakage. Northern Peak still monitors that flow under the old intelligence-sharing arrangement with us, even though nobody's claimed it in decades. Kuo flagged it and did nothing with it for months. Then the node under your store died in his own Court's general monitoring radius, in the same district, and he connected the two."

He thought about the wrist. The frozen edamame. The warmth that had followed his attention the way water follows a slope.

He hadn't known the flow was watched. He hadn't known anything was watched.

He wrote it down anyway, because a thing being unintentional didn't make it not a cause.

He thought about the report Elder Kuo had filed. *Self-taught, technically strange, and surprisingly sophisticated.* He had read Kuo's unintended transmission of the authorization language. He had been building a picture of the investigation from the fragments available.

Lady Shen Baoling had read that report and volunteered the same day.

He wrote:

> *Lady Shen Baoling: Northern Peak Court senior operative. Practical cultivation level: high, specific level unknown. Motivation: not institutional — personal interest in the Codex and what it accesses. Acquisition: eleven years, one successful, previous subject still detained.*
>
> *Her transit time from Lhasa: 2–3 days flight and overland. On current timeline she could be in Chengdu by the weekend.*
>
> *Revised timeline: days, not a week.*
>
> *Response: not reactive. The reactive option — moving, hiding, changing routines — is what she'd expect and what she'd be calibrated for. An established, stable position is harder to displace than a mobile one.*

He looked at this last entry.

"The Strata," he said.

"What about it?"

"If I have a stable presence in the Strata — not just access, a *presence* — it changes what the Northern Peak can acquire. They can acquire a practitioner. Acquiring access to a space that has its own occupants and its own diplomatic status is a different proposition."

Chen Yufei was quiet for a moment. "You're thinking about the Keeper."

"The Keeper answered the door of the library. The Strata has its own authority structure that predates the Courts by a considerable margin." He looked at her. "I need to understand what that authority structure is, and what standing I have within it, before Lady Shen gets to Chengdu."

"And you think the Keeper will tell you."

"I think the Keeper has been waiting for someone with sufficient comprehension to ask." He closed the notebook. "We're going in tonight. I need you inside when I have that conversation."

"Why me?"

"Because whatever the Keeper says, I need a witness who can testify to it in terms the Courts understand." He paused. "Also, your Qi signature registered differently on the floor's recognition protocol. I want to know what that means."

She looked at him with the expression that had become familiar over the past few days: the slight recalibration, the adjustment to a model of him that was always being updated.

"When?" she said.

"Midnight. Dress for walking. The Strata is cold at depth."

She looked briefly like she wanted to ask why he knew that. Then she didn't.

"I'll be there," she said.
