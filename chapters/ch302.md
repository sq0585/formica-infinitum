# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Two — Teaching Amber-0

The hardest part, he discovered within the first week of training the system, wasn't the technical work. It was the sudden, uncomfortable requirement to explain decisions he'd been making for months without ever once having to justify them to anyone but himself.

"Why did you route the surplus Qi to the residential wing instead of the workshop district last Tuesday?" Meen-4 asked, the question arriving in the middle of an otherwise routine training session, Amber-0's early pattern-recognition loops parsing his historical routing decisions for the underlying logic they were supposed to represent.

He opened his mouth to answer and found, to his mild surprise, that he didn't have a ready explanation. "I felt like the residential wing needed it more."

*That is not sufficient input for a training pattern. Felt is not a variable Amber-0 can learn from.*

"It wasn't a calculation. It was — the elder Mrs. Tan's room ran cold that week, and two of the workshop's projects were between phases and didn't need the extra flow yet, and I just knew, without stopping to formalize it, that the residential wing was the better use of the surplus."

*Then formalize it now,* Meen-4 signaled, patient in the specific way she reserved for teaching. *You are describing at least three distinct variables — occupant comfort thresholds, project phase status, and comparative urgency. You held all three simultaneously through intuition built from months of direct sensing. Amber-0 has none of that sensing. It has only what you can name.*

He sat with the discomfort of that for a long moment, understanding, slowly, exactly what the exercise was actually asking of him — not simply to teach a system, but to make visible, piece by piece, an entire body of judgment he'd built so gradually that he'd stopped noticing he was making it.

---

The sessions continued through the following weeks, each one requiring him to reconstruct, in explicit and defensible terms, decisions that had felt, in the moment he'd made them, closer to instinct than reasoning. He found himself keeping a running list — comfort thresholds, project phase weighting, resident priority during maintenance windows, the specific hierarchy he apparently used, without ever having written it down, when two legitimate needs competed for the same limited Qi flow.

"This is strange," he admitted to Chen Yufei one evening, the two of them reviewing his day's training notes over dinner. "I've been making these decisions for months. I didn't know I had a hierarchy until Amber-0 forced me to write one down."

"That's not strange," she said. "That's just what expertise looks like from the inside. You don't usually have to explain your own judgment. You just use it."

"It's uncomfortable, though. Some of the reasoning doesn't hold up as well as I expected once I actually look at it directly. There's a bias toward the residential wing I hadn't noticed — I favor human comfort over Formican efficiency more consistently than I'd have guessed, if I'm being honest about the pattern."

"Is that wrong?"

He considered the question seriously rather than deflecting it. "I don't know yet. It might just be a preference worth having. It might be something I need to correct. But I wouldn't have found it at all if I hadn't been forced to formalize the decisions instead of just making them."

---

Meen-4 noted the same thing, three weeks into the training, with the particular directness she brought to observations she considered important.

*You are becoming more consistent,* she signaled, reviewing a comparison between his early-training decisions and his more recent ones. *The variance in your routing choices has decreased by approximately eighteen percent since we began. This was not the explicit goal of training Amber-0. It is, nonetheless, a genuine effect of it.*

"Because I have to explain myself now."

*Because explaining forces examination, and examination tends to correct drift that intuition alone does not notice.* She paused, her attention settling on him with unusual steadiness. *This is, I believe, a useful side effect worth naming directly. Amber-0 is not yet capable of anything resembling independent judgment. But the process of building it toward that capability has already improved your own.*

He thought about that for a while after she'd returned to the substrate work, turning the observation over with the same careful attention he gave most unexpected gifts this world kept handing him — not the tool he'd set out to build, but something adjacent to it, a discipline of self-examination he suspected would outlast Amber-0's own eventual capabilities by a considerable margin.

He wrote a brief note in his journal that evening, tucked between the day's more technical entries.

> *Teaching Amber-0 is teaching myself, mostly. Every pattern I have to explain out loud turns out to have edges I hadn't noticed while it was still just instinct. I don't know yet what Amber-0 will become. I already know what building it correctly has started to make of me.*

He closed the notebook and returned to the substrate the following morning, the training sessions continuing steadily, patiently, each explained decision another small brick laid in a foundation neither he nor Meen-4 yet fully knew the eventual size of.
