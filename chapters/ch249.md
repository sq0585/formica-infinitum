# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Nine — Gou Ping's Alignment

The news reached him through Lady Shen, this time, rather than through Chen Yufei's network — a small detail that told him, before he'd even read the message, how much her relationship to information about her own former subordinate had changed since the censure.

*Gou Ping has formally left whatever remained of his standing within Northern Peak,* her message read. *I had already suspended him pending review. He's preempted any further proceedings by walking away entirely. My sources indicate he's aligned himself with what's left of Iron Meridian's hard faction.*

Lin Mushi read it twice, turning the implications over the way he turned most developments in this slow, grinding conflict.

Gou Ping, expelled in all but formal name from the institution he'd spent his career serving, finding common cause with the remnant of an Iron Meridian Court that had just lost sixty percent of its membership to a single catastrophic gambit. Two coercive factions, each diminished, each carrying genuine grievance against the world that had diminished them, finding each other at exactly the moment when smaller and harder might prove more dangerous than larger and more cautious had ever been.

He called Chen Yufei to confirm.

"It's accurate," she said, once she'd checked her own sources. "Gou Ping brought roughly a dozen loyal followers with him — cultivators who'd backed his original assault on the Strata and never fully accepted the censure that followed. Combined with what's left of Iron Meridian's hard faction, you're looking at a coalition of maybe twenty to thirty committed members."

"Smaller than either institution was on its own."

"Smaller, and considerably more focused. These are the people who already believed force was the only remaining option, drawn from two institutions that have both now tried negotiation and lost. There's no remaining moderate influence left in that group to argue for caution."

He thought about the assault on the Strata months ago — fourteen cultivators, disoriented and ultimately harmless, contained through architecture rather than confrontation. He thought about the demolition, the catastrophic institutional collapse it had triggered, the particular desperation of people who'd already lost everything they'd built their identity around.

"What do you think they're planning?"

"I don't know yet. But I think the maze technique that worked against Gou Ping's first attempt won't work as cleanly a second time — not against people who've already experienced it and have had months to think about how to counter it. And I think a faction built entirely from people who've already lost their institutional standing has very little left to lose by escalating further."

He sat with the weight of this, the particular cold clarity that arrived whenever a threat's shape sharpened from vague concern into something he needed to actively prepare for.

"I should talk to Fang Ruxue," he said. "The Collective's growing fast enough now that it's becoming a visible target in its own right, not just something orbiting around me personally. If Gou Ping's faction decides the Collective itself is the next thing worth attacking, I want defensive thinking in place before they act, not scrambling to respond after."

"That's a reasonable instinct."

"I'd rather be wrong and overprepared than right and caught flat."

He ended the call and sat for a long moment with his notebook open, the familiar weight of a gathering threat settling into the careful, methodical planning that had kept him and everyone he cared about alive through every crisis the past two years had handed him.

He wrote the date at the top of a fresh page, and beneath it, plainly, the question he suspected would define whatever came next.

> *Gou Ping and Iron Meridian's hard remnant, combined. Twenty to thirty committed members, no moderate influence remaining among them. What does a coalition with nothing left to lose actually do?*

He didn't have an answer yet. He suspected he wouldn't have to wait long to find out.
