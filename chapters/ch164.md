# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Sixty-Four — The First Twenty-Four Hours

Twelve responses had arrived by the time he checked his phone at 2 PM, and by midnight there were thirty-one.

He read them in order, the way he'd read the first document's replies four months earlier — methodically, without skimming, giving each one the attention its sender had presumably given the document itself.

The first that mattered: an Iron Meridian senior practitioner he didn't recognize, writing through an intermediary because he didn't have Lin Mushi's direct contact. *Our foundational text says something specific about the fourth meridian gate. Your translation of the Northern Peak's foundational text describes what should be a completely different tradition, yet the underlying notation is identical to ours, just transposed. How is that possible, if our traditions developed independently over eight centuries?*

It was the right question — the same kind of question Wei Jianming had asked four months ago, the question of someone engaging with the content rather than the politics of its arrival.

He drafted a careful answer: both traditions had derived from the same Formican source material, transmitted through different historical channels — one through a fragment that had reached the Iron Meridian's founders directly, one through a more circuitous route that had reached the Northern Peak's. The notation similarity wasn't coincidence. It was shared ancestry, obscured by eight centuries of independent, isolated development that had each preserved different portions of the same original system.

He sent it.

He kept reading.

A Verdant Stream Collective elder, brief and direct: *I have suspected this for thirty years. I am relieved to have it confirmed rather than relieved that I am right.*

A junior Celestial Compass School practitioner, more agitated: *If this is true, what does it mean that we have been teaching incorrect cosmological alignments to new students for two generations? What do we tell them?*

This one he sat with for a long time before answering. He wrote back: *You tell them the truth, the same way I'm telling you. The technique they learned still works on the corrected framework — what changes is the explanation, not the practice itself. The new students inherit something more accurate than what you were given. That's not nothing.*

He kept reading through the evening.

Not every response was engaged or grateful. Several were silent in a way that felt deliberate — formal acknowledgment of receipt with no further comment, the institutional equivalent of a closed door. A few were hostile, accusing him of fabrication, of attempting to destabilize institutions for personal gain he hadn't yet revealed.

He answered the hostile ones with the same care he gave the engaged ones. Not to win them over — he didn't expect to win all of them over — but because the principle held regardless of the recipient's reaction: respond honestly, offer evidence, don't escalate.

By midnight he had answered nineteen of the thirty-one.

He wrote in his notebook:

> *First 24 hours: 31 responses. Roughly 60% engaged with content. 25% silent/formal. 15% hostile.*
>
> *The engaged percentage is what matters. People are asking the right questions — not 'why did you do this' but 'how is this possible' and 'what do we do now.' That's the response I was hoping the structure of the document would produce.*
>
> *The hostile responses don't surprise me and don't particularly worry me yet. What worries me is the silent 25% — institutions assessing quietly, which usually means they're deciding how to respond strategically rather than honestly. That's where the political maneuvering will come from.*
>
> *Elder Kuo hasn't responded yet. I expect to hear from him tomorrow, and I expect it won't be gentle.*

He was right about that.
