# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Forty-Five — The Emergency Response

He reached the anchor point with just under ten hours remaining, and spent the first hour of those ten simply understanding what he was looking at.

The structure was unlike anything he'd worked on during the Strata's reconstruction — older, larger, carrying a weight of accumulated connections that made his own modest dimensional repairs look, by comparison, like patching a single brick in a load-bearing wall. This anchor didn't just tether one section of the Chengdu Strata to the surface. It was woven into the broader network's coherence in ways he was only beginning to map as he worked.

Liang Jingzhi's structural data helped. It didn't make the problem smaller.

He understood, by the second hour, that he couldn't prevent the demolition itself — not without intervening directly in a way that crossed lines he wasn't willing to cross, confronting a construction crew that had no idea what they were actually about to disturb. What he could do, what the remaining hours actually gave him room for, was reinforce the anchor's dimensional architecture from below, deep enough and thoroughly enough that the surface disruption — however destructive to the parking structure itself — wouldn't propagate down into the fold's actual coherence.

It meant holding active Weaving at the anchor's full scale, continuously, for as long as the surface demolition lasted.

He sent the alert to Chen Yufei, Wei Canglan, and two senior Collective members he trusted with the work, and by the fourth hour, all four of them had arrived to help maintain secondary reinforcement around the structure's outer edges — not capable of the central work he was doing, but enough to ease the load at the periphery, buying him margin he badly needed.

"How long can you hold this?" Chen Yufei asked, somewhere around the sixth hour, her own hands steady on the secondary reinforcement she'd been maintaining without rest.

"I don't know," he said honestly. "Longer than I've ever held anything. I'll find out exactly how long somewhere past the point where it stops being comfortable to find out."

She didn't argue with that. She simply settled deeper into her own work, the four of them holding a structure together through the small hours of the morning while, somewhere above them, a construction crew began arriving for a job none of them understood the actual stakes of.

---

By the ninth hour, he could feel something he hadn't felt before — not pain exactly, but a kind of depletion at the edge of his perception, the particular sensation of an interface being asked to sustain more than it had ever sustained for this long without rest.

He kept holding.

Demolition began at the scheduled hour, the surface disruption arriving as a series of concussive shocks that propagated downward through the anchor's architecture, each one a fresh stress test against the reinforcement he was maintaining. He felt each impact as something close to physical pressure, his own Weaving absorbing and redistributing the shock before it could reach the anchor's actual coherence.

It worked. Hour after hour, impact after impact, it worked.

He held through the tenth hour. He held through the eleventh, the structural data confirming what his own strained perception was already telling him — that the anchor's coherence was holding, degraded but intact, the demolition's violence absorbed rather than allowed to propagate into genuine collapse.

He kept holding past the eleventh hour, into territory the warning's original timeline hadn't accounted for, because the demolition crew above, encountering structural resistance they couldn't explain through any conventional engineering, simply kept working longer than anyone had predicted they would.

Somewhere in the fourteenth continuous hour, his vision narrowed at the edges, his hands — steady for so long he'd stopped noticing them — beginning, finally, to shake.

He kept holding anyway, because the alternative, somewhere above him, was a structure with no one left to hold it at all.
