# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-Five — Chen Yufei and Lin Mushi, Acknowledged

They were sitting on the floor of the pocket dimension laboratory at the end of a long day, too tired to make the walk home feel like anything but an unreasonable additional task, the fourth Array's calibration finally settled, the day's accumulated exhaustion sitting heavy in both of them in the particular way that made honesty easier than it usually was.

Chen Yufei leaned her head back against the desk behind her, eyes closed, and said it without any preamble at all.

"We've been not-saying something for about six months."

He considered this, the accuracy of it landing without surprise, the way a fact lands when you've already known it for longer than you've been willing to say it out loud.

"Yes," he said.

"I think we should say it."

"Okay."

She opened her eyes, looked at him, and he found, meeting her gaze across the small distance between them, that there wasn't actually very much left to figure out. They'd already done the figuring, slowly, across six months of shared work and shared danger and shared quiet evenings exactly like this one — meals eaten standing over a sink, careful trust built crisis by crisis, the particular intimacy of being the only two people who fully understood the shape of each other's impossible daily life.

What was left wasn't discovery. It was simply acknowledgment.

He reached over, and she met him halfway, and it happened the way most genuinely important things in his life had happened — not with drama, not with a speech he'd never have been able to give anyway, but quietly, plainly, the natural conclusion of something that had been true for longer than either of them had said it.

Afterward, sitting together against the desk with the laboratory's faint amber light settling around them, she laughed softly.

"That's it," she said. "Six months, and that's it."

"What did you expect?"

"I don't know. Something more complicated, maybe. You're not exactly known for emotional efficiency."

"I'm efficient about most things. I don't see why this should be different."

She laughed again, fuller this time, and leaned into him, and he found, sitting there in the quiet, that what he felt most clearly wasn't excitement or nervousness or any of the things he might have expected from a moment like this.

He felt relieved.

Six months of carefully not naming something that both of them had clearly understood, six months of careful professional distance maintained around a feeling that had never actually been professional, six months of a particular kind of effort that he hadn't fully registered as effort until it finally, simply, stopped being necessary.

"I'm tired," he said, after a while.

"I know. So am I."

"Not the kind of tired sleep fixes. The kind of tired that comes from holding something carefully for longer than you needed to."

She was quiet for a moment, considering this, and then nodded against his shoulder.

"Yes," she said. "That kind."

They sat together for a long while after that, neither of them in a hurry to move, the day's exhaustion finally settling into something closer to peace than to depletion, six months of careful unspoken accumulation resolved, finally, into something simple enough to need no further explanation.

He thought, somewhere in the quiet, that this — exactly this, ordinary and unhurried and entirely without spectacle — was precisely the kind of moment the past two years of impossible discovery had been protecting all along.

He held onto it, for as long as the evening allowed.
