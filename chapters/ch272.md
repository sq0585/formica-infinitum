# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Seventy-Two — What Was Lost

He and Chen Yufei spent a quiet evening compiling the accounting neither of them had wanted to do but both understood needed doing — not a celebration of what the Collective had built, but a careful, honest inventory of what the Courts' dissolution had genuinely cost.

"Iron Meridian's breath regulation sequences," Chen Yufei said, working through her own notes from nine years of practice. "The specific rhythmic patterns, developed across generations of practitioners refining timing and emphasis through pure accumulated trial and error. The Formican source material describes the underlying mechanism cleanly, efficiently — but it doesn't describe the particular cultural weight of a technique passed master to student for two hundred years, refined by hundreds of individual practitioners who each contributed something small and specific to how it eventually sounded, how it eventually felt in the body."

"That's not nothing," Lin Mushi said.

"No. It's not nothing. Document 4's notation will teach someone the efficient version faster than nine years of Iron Meridian training ever could. But the efficient version doesn't carry the particular texture of generations of human refinement, the cultural inheritance of exactly how a technique gets taught from one specific person to another, the relationships built inside that transmission."

He thought about his own teaching of Wei Canglan — framework first, technique built independently — and understood, turning it over, that even his deliberately different methodology was already beginning to create its own particular texture, its own cultural inheritance distinct from anything the old Courts had built.

"What else?" he asked.

She worked through it carefully, methodically, the same precision she brought to everything that genuinely mattered to her. The Celestial Compass School's seasonal observation rituals, built around astronomical events that carried meaning independent of whether the underlying theory had been correct. The Stone Hall Sect's physical discipline traditions, genuinely effective conditioning regardless of the flawed theoretical framework wrapped around them. Northern Peak's particular emphasis on institutional stability and mutual obligation, imperfect in execution but built around values that had real worth even when the specific authority claiming to embody them had been built on misunderstanding.

"None of these traditions were frauds," she said. "That's the part I think people are going to forget, once enough time passes and the Collective's framework becomes simply how things are done. The Courts were wrong about almost everything important — the theoretical foundation, the source of their authority, the specific mechanisms underneath their techniques. They were also human beings' honest attempt to understand something real, sustained across centuries by people who genuinely believed they were preserving something worth preserving."

"And that has value," Lin Mushi said, "even in failure."

"Even in failure," she agreed. "Maybe especially in failure. There's something worth honoring in two thousand years of people getting the explanation wrong while somehow, through patient observation and accumulated practice, still managing to get the actual technique mostly right. That's not a small achievement, even if it turns out to have been built on a misunderstanding."

He sat with this, the particular weight of the accounting they'd built settling into something he understood he needed to carry forward, not as guilt exactly, but as a kind of respectful memory — the discipline of not letting the new structure's success erase the genuine, if flawed, human effort that had preceded it.

"I want this documented properly," he said. "Not buried in a working group's technical notes. Something the Collective actually preserves — what the old Courts got right, what they built that's worth honoring even now, alongside the correction of what they got wrong."

"I'll talk to Fang Ruxue about it," Chen Yufei said. "I think she'd agree. I think she'd want exactly this kind of honest accounting built into the Collective's own foundational documents, so the new structure doesn't simply repeat the old one's mistake of forgetting where its own knowledge actually came from."

He nodded, and the two of them continued working late into the evening, building a careful, honest record of two thousand years of human effort — wrong about almost everything that mattered, and worth remembering anyway.
