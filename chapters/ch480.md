# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 480 — Liang Jingzhi's Answer

She took three more days after the council, and Lin Mushi didn't press her, understanding, the same way he'd learned to understand every difficult waiting period this extraordinary life had handed him, that some decisions simply required exactly as long as they required.

She found him on the fourth morning, in the small geology office where the whole conversation had started, her expression carrying the particular settled clarity of someone who had genuinely finished deciding rather than merely grown tired of deliberating.

"I have my answer," she said, without preamble, the same directness she'd brought to everything since the beginning.

"I'm listening."

"I have spent my entire career reading the record that rocks keep of things that no longer exist," she said, echoing her own words from days earlier, though something in the repetition now carried the weight of a conclusion rather than an argument. "Extinction events without witnesses. Civilizations that left nothing but a chemical signature in a sediment layer, and no one left to say what they'd actually been. The dissolution zone is exactly that kind of gap — forty million years with no coherent witness. I want to be the witness. That is, when I strip away every technical justification and every careful hour of council deliberation, simply what I do. It's what I've always done. This is just the largest version of it I'll ever be offered."

"That's not sacrifice," he said quietly, understanding it even as he said it.

"No," she agreed. "It's vocation. I need you to hear the difference, because I think it matters enormously which one people believe they're watching. I am not giving something up. I am becoming more completely what I already was."

He sat with that, feeling the specific, settled rightness of it — not relief exactly, not yet, but something adjacent to it, the recognition of a decision that fit the person making it so precisely that arguing against it would have meant arguing against her entire character.

"Then I'll build it with you," he said. "Properly. With everyone's full attention, not rushed."

"That's all I asked for," she said, and something in her voice, for the first time since the whole conversation had begun, carried a genuine, unguarded warmth. "Properly. Not quickly."

---

The general volunteer network reached its own conclusion that same week, running its course alongside Liang Jingzhi's more private decision the way two rivers run parallel before finally meeting. The screening process Dr. Shen Yue and Meen-4 had built together worked through every volunteer methodically, clearing most, redirecting some toward support roles that mattered without carrying the same risk, turning a small number away entirely, gently, for their own safety. The orientation phase that followed gave everyone cleared a genuine, monitored preview of the commitment's actual scale, and some — a carpenter who'd volunteered with real enthusiasm and withdrew with real honesty once he'd felt the size of it, others with quieter, more personal reasons — chose, cleanly and without shame, not to continue. Lin Mushi made certain, every single time, that withdrawal cost no one anything in his estimation of their character.

The final count arrived the following Thursday, delivered by Meen-4 with the particular formal stillness she reserved for numbers she considered genuinely significant.

"Three hundred and twenty-three," she said, setting the completed roster down on the table between them. "That is the final figure, following screening, following orientation, following every withdrawal that came after either. Three hundred and twenty-three people who understood, completely, what this asks of them, and chose to remain anyway."

Lin Mushi looked at the number for a long moment, feeling its particular, specific weight settle into him — not a target deliberately reached, but the exact, ungoverned outcome of hundreds of individual, careful decisions, each one costing whoever made it something real to actually decide.

"It's not a number I chose," he said slowly. "I never set a target."

"That is, I believe, precisely why it matters the way it does," Meen-4 said. "It is the honest residue of a process built to filter for genuine, informed willingness rather than raw enthusiasm."

He gathered his five Anchors that evening, the roster in one hand and, sitting beside it, the far smaller, far weightier document Meen-4 had drafted describing exactly what Liang Jingzhi's own commitment would structurally involve.

"This is who's coming with us," he said, setting the roster down first. "Three hundred and twenty-three people, distinct from the five of you, choosing knowingly to hold a piece of this together." He set the second document down beside it. "And this is Liang Jingzhi, who isn't one of the three hundred and twenty-three at all. She's proposed something else entirely, and she's decided, and I want all of you to understand clearly that what she's offering is not the same category of gift as anyone else's."

Chen Yufei reached across the table and took Liang Jingzhi's hand, saying nothing for a long moment, the particular wordless solidarity of two people who had each, in their own different ways, learned exactly what it cost to give something permanent to this project.

"Then we build it properly," Chen Yufei said finally. "All of us. Starting tomorrow."

*Observation 481,* Lin Mushi wrote, later, alone, closing the day's entry the way he closed every entry that mattered most — plainly, without ornament. *Five Anchors. Three hundred and twenty-three dissolution-zone anchors, chosen knowingly. And Liang Jingzhi, who is not counted among either number, because what she has offered does not fit inside a count at all. Tomorrow, we begin building what she asked for.*
