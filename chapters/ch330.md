# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Thirty — The Incomplete Record, Resolved

The memory surfaced two nights later, uninvited, while he sat in quiet meditation working through the Anchor Protocol's now-daily discipline — three Anchors held steady in his awareness, Chen Yufei's absence a deliberate gap he'd learned to hold without strain.

He thought of Ren Wuji, the way he sometimes did during quiet moments, unprompted, and found himself returning, for reasons he couldn't immediately articulate, to a record he'd first encountered nearly two hundred chapters of his own life ago — a fragment from the Intermediate Archive, deep in his first year in this world, describing Ren Wuji's own history as a student at the Amber Lattice School, and a single, frustrating gap in the record beyond it.

*A notation that he had a student — the record is incomplete. The student's name is illegible.*

He'd carried that fragment for a very long time, filed away as one of many unresolved mysteries this world had handed him early and never circled back to explain. He hadn't thought of it in months.

He opened his eyes, and understood, with a certainty that arrived fully formed, that he needed to look at that record again.

---

He found the original entry in the Archive within the hour, the notation exactly as he remembered it — Ren Wuji's own thirty years as a student, followed by a later entry describing his return to the School as a teacher, and beneath that, the frustrating gap: a student record, deliberately obscured, the notation system used to blur the name distinct from everything else in the surrounding archive.

He extended his sensing toward it, drawing on everything the Planetary Architect threshold had given him — the geological-timescale comprehension Liang Jingzhi had spent six weeks teaching him, the full weight of a mind now capable of holding a planet's field coherently.

The obscured notation had resisted every previous attempt at reading, the blur too deliberate, too carefully embedded, for his earlier capability to penetrate. He tried again now, patiently, the same careful discipline he brought to every difficult perception, letting his attention settle into the notation's structure rather than forcing his way through it.

Slowly, the blur began to resolve.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. Simply, gradually, character by character, the deliberate obscuring losing its grip against a level of comprehension it had never been built to withstand.

He read the name as it finally settled into legibility, and felt the floor of his understanding shift beneath him.

*Veth-Karun.*

---

He sat perfectly still for a long moment, the Archive's quiet stone corridor pressing in around him, the full weight of the connection arriving in pieces too large to process all at once.

Ren Wuji's student. The name he'd read months ago, clinical and distant, in the Deep Archive's account of the Formless Scale event — a Formican cultivator who had reached Formless Scale through power without comprehension, dissolved in less than a second, leaving behind three untranslated symbols and forty million years of careful, quiet warning.

Ren Wuji's student.

He thought of Ren Wuji's voice in the recordings, dry and precise and warm underneath the precision, the man who had spent four hundred years carefully preparing a transmission for someone he'd never meet, who had died in a convenience store with his hand closing around Lin Mushi's own, passing on a Codex built with thirty years of deliberate, patient care.

That same man had taught Veth-Karun. Had watched, presumably, whatever came before the dissolution — the same accumulation of power without comprehension that had ended in 0.7 seconds and three untranslated symbols.

He didn't move for a long while, the Archive around him utterly silent, the specific vertigo of the discovery settling into his chest with a weight he didn't yet have language adequate to hold.

He thought of Ren Wuji's careful, patient thirty years building a transmission format. He thought of the obscured record, deliberately blurred, hidden not by the Isolationists but — he understood this now, with sudden clarity — by Ren Wuji himself. A man who had spent four hundred years carrying something he'd never once mentioned in any recording, any note, any careful, warm piece of guidance he'd left behind.

He'd taught Lin Mushi everything. Cultivation, Compression Ratios, the patient discipline of careful, methodical progress.

He had never once told him about this.

Lin Mushi closed the Archive record slowly, his hands steadier than his chest felt, and sat with the discovery a moment longer before he trusted himself to stand.

He didn't tell anyone that night. He didn't yet have words adequate to the weight of what he'd found, and some instinct, careful and self-protective, told him this needed to settle properly before he tried to share it with anyone else.

He walked back to his room through the Strata's quiet late-night corridors, past the elder Mrs. Tan's dark window, past the workshop district's silent, resting machinery, and understood, with a clarity that felt both terrible and inevitable, that everything he thought he knew about the man who had given him this entire impossible life had just, quietly, irrevocably, become considerably more complicated.
