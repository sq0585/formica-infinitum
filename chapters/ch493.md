# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 493 — The Decision

He sat with the inventory a long while after finishing it, the tea gone properly cold now, the Sunday light shifting slowly toward evening, and found himself reaching for the notebook a second time, understanding that the accounting alone hadn't quite finished saying what actually needed saying.

*Observation 510,* he wrote, and then set the pen down, and simply sat, for a while, letting the actual shape of the moment arrive before he tried to capture it.

He thought of everything the past year had built, methodically, one careful threshold at a time — Stellar Scale's comprehension, earned through eight patient weeks at the edge of the solar system. His mother's extension, and the truth that had made it honest. The Pelagic Archive's cold, patient chamber, and a letter that had taught him, at real emotional cost, the single mechanism that might finally let him survive what Veth-Karun hadn't. Three hundred and twenty-three people, distributed across a wound forty million years old, holding steady beside him.

He picked the pen back up.

*I have what I need,* he wrote, and found the sentence arrived plainly, without the vertigo he might have expected from a claim this large. *I know what went wrong before. Veth-Karun's account gave me that, at a cost I don't think I'll ever fully stop feeling. I know why it went wrong — solitary preparation, accumulated knowledge held alone, a mind that mistook understanding for adequate readiness and discovered, in the last 0.7 seconds of its existence, exactly how incomplete that readiness had actually been.*

*I know what to hold, and how to hold it. The Anchor Protocol was never merely scaffolding. It was always the answer, built years before I understood the question it was answering. I did not need to invent anything new. I needed only to trust what I had already, instinctively, built.*

*I have people who know to hold me. Five who have carried me through every threshold since Colony Link itself. Three hundred and twenty-three more who chose, knowingly, to extend themselves into the largest wound I know of in the whole of everything I can currently perceive. A mother who finally knows everything, and loves me anyway, exactly the same as she always has.*

He paused, the pen hovering, and let himself write the next line honestly, resisting the pull to soften it into something more comfortable.

*I do not feel ready.*

*I have learned, across five years of careful, repeated thresholds, that I will not feel ready, and that this is correct. Readiness was never going to arrive as a feeling — I understand that now, plainly, the way I understand most of the things that have actually mattered in this life. Readiness is a preparation. It is built, patiently, one careful decision at a time, by a person who keeps choosing connection over isolation, honesty over comfortable omission, discipline over impatient curiosity, again and again, until the preparation itself becomes the only readiness that was ever actually available.*

*The preparation is complete.*

He read that line back twice, feeling its particular, settled weight, and understood, sitting with it, that he had just written the truest and most difficult sentence of the entire notebook's four and a half years.

*What comes next is the work.*

He closed the entry there, without further elaboration, understanding that nothing he could add would carry more weight than the plain, complete honesty of exactly those words.

He sat for a while longer in the Sunday evening's fading light, the notebook closed now, the tea entirely cold, and found, in the quiet that followed, something he hadn't quite expected to feel at the end of an entry this large.

Not fear, though fear was somewhere present too, honest and unhidden. Not triumph, because nothing about this felt like triumph yet, and he suspected it wouldn't, not until considerably more of the actual work was finished.

Simply readiness, understood correctly, at last, exactly the way he'd just written it — not a feeling, but a preparation, complete, patient, built across four and a half years by a boy who had once been considerably more curious than prepared, and had become, gradually, carefully, both.
