# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 476 — The Call Goes Out

Fang Ruxue's transmission went out through every channel the Cultivation Research Collective maintained, and Lin Mushi spent the day it launched doing something he rarely allowed himself: waiting, without working, simply sitting with the specific discomfort of having asked something enormous of people he hadn't yet met.

The request, in its final form, read plainly, exactly the way he and Fang Ruxue had spent a week carefully shaping it.

*A call for volunteers to become distributed anchor points in a Void Lattice repair effort spanning a dissolution zone forty million years old and thousands of light-years across.*

*What is being asked: extension of your own Colony Link anchoring to stellar scale, sustained indefinitely, anchored directly against the residual entropy of a Formless Scale failure. You will feel yourself present here, on Earth or wherever you currently stand, and simultaneously present as part of a zone whose scale exceeds ordinary human comprehension. This is not metaphorical. It will feel exactly as large as it sounds.*

*What it costs: an unknown quantity of sustained attention, likely for the remainder of the repair's duration, which may extend considerably longer than any of us currently know how to predict. The risk profile is not fully established. We will screen every volunteer carefully, and turning down a volunteer for safety reasons reflects nothing about their character or commitment.*

*What it means: you will become part of the specific mechanism preventing another failure like the one that created this wound in the first place. You will not be doing this alone. None of us will be.*

He read it through one final time before it launched, and found, reading it, that he'd stopped trying to make it sound smaller than it actually was somewhere around the fourth draft.

"You're not going to sleep tonight," Chen Yufei observed, finding him still at the small table hours after the transmission had gone out, the notebook open in front of him though he hadn't written anything in over an hour.

"I keep thinking about the specific weight of asking," he admitted. "Not the technical risk — Dr. Shen Yue and Meen-4 will screen for that properly. The other weight. Asking people I've mostly never met to extend themselves into something this vast, on the strength of a request I wrote in a week."

"You didn't write it carelessly," she said, sitting beside him. "I watched you and Fang Ruxue work through every line of it. You made it as honest as language could make it."

"I know. I still don't know how many people are going to read it and feel obligated, rather than genuinely willing. That's the part I can't control from here."

She considered that for a moment, the particular thoughtful quiet she brought to questions that actually mattered. "Then that's what the screening process is for," she said. "You built careful thresholds into every other part of this. Trust that the same care will catch obligation before it becomes commitment."

He nodded, though the specific unease didn't fully settle, not that night, not the following morning when the first responses began arriving faster than he'd expected — dozens within the first day, the number climbing steadily as the request spread outward through the Cultivation Research Collective's careful, patient network of researchers, Strata residents, and the wider cultivation community the disclosure protocol had, slowly, brought into contact with each other.

Meen-4 brought him the running count each evening, her own expression carrying something between professional caution and genuine, quiet wonder at the response's scale.

"Sixty-three by the end of day two," she reported, on the second evening. "Not all of them will pass screening. Not all of them fully understand yet what they're volunteering for. But sixty-three people read what you asked, and sixty-three people said, in some form, yes."

He sat with that number, feeling the particular weight of it — far more than he'd dared hope for on the first day, and rather less, he suspected, than the dissolution zone's actual scale would eventually require.

"Keep the count coming," he said. "I want to know exactly who's answering, and exactly what they understand before they do."

Meen-4 nodded, and left him with the notebook, the numbers, and the specific, unfamiliar sensation of watching a project grow considerably larger than the shape he'd originally imagined it taking.
