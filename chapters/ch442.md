# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 442 — What the Records End With

Cataloguing, it turned out, required a different kind of attention than reading had — slower, more exhaustive, the discipline of making certain nothing got missed rather than the discipline of simply following a thread wherever it led. He spent two full days on it, working through the flagged section's final weeks entry by entry, building the careful, complete account he'd promised Meen-4 and the wider Collective he would provide.

The departure log, when he finally isolated it properly from the surrounding entries, was more formal than the fragments he'd already read — a structured record rather than a diary's shorthand, the kind of document, he suspected, that Formican protocol had required regardless of how informal the crew's day-to-day notes had become by that point.

*Expedition roster confirmed. Full complement, no substitutions. Departure vector locked to solar position 7.4-N. Estimated approach duration: eleven days at standard transit rate. Remote installation systems to maintain full operational status throughout, per standing protocol.*

He read it twice, the specific formality of it landing harder than the earlier fragments' exhausted brevity had, precisely because it sounded so entirely ordinary — the kind of log entry any careful expedition might file before any reasonably cautious journey, written by people who had, evidently, expected to file a matching entry on the other side of it.

Eleven days later, by the installation's own patient internal clock, a second entry appeared, shorter, clearly transmitted rather than logged locally — a signal sent back across the distance separating the expedition from the installation they'd left running behind them.

*Approach confirmed. Anomaly boundary reached. Instrument readings consistent with preliminary remote observation, within tolerance. Proceeding to direct investigation as planned. Next transmission scheduled at standard interval.*

*Observation 404,* he wrote, and found his hand, again, not entirely steady. *This is the last confirmed transmission from the expedition. The installation's own logs show no subsequent signal received, at the scheduled interval or at any point afterward, across the full remaining duration of the archive's operational history — which continues, uninterrupted, for a considerable time after this point, faithfully recording forty million years of stellar observation from a station that never received another word from the people who had built it that curriculum.*

He sat with the sentence a long while, the command chamber's cold settling into him in a way he'd stopped attributing purely to temperature days ago.

*Rule,* he wrote, finally, because the pattern, once he'd assembled every piece of it carefully and completely, resolved into something he could no longer avoid stating plainly. *The installation's crew departed to investigate solar position 7.4-N. They confirmed safe arrival at the anomaly's boundary. They proceeded to direct investigation. They never transmitted again. The installation itself continued functioning perfectly, exactly as instructed, faithfully maintaining and expanding its own archive for tens of millions of years afterward, waiting for a crew that was never coming back to collect it.*

He closed the notebook and sat, for a while, simply with the weight of the sentence — not the mystery of it, which curiosity would ordinarily have seized on immediately, but the specific grief underneath it, an entire team of researchers, careful and patient and thorough enough to build a curriculum this disciplined, walking deliberately toward something they had prepared for as well as anyone could, and simply not returning.

He thought of Ren Wuji, briefly, dying in a convenience store with a Chitin Seed in his hand and a message already prepared for whoever came next. He thought of how much preparation, in the end, had actually mattered, and how much hadn't, and found he didn't have an answer that satisfied him either way.

*Query for Meen-4,* he wrote, finally, turning the thought toward something he could actually act on. *Does the wider Archive — the one accessible from Earth, not this installation — hold any record of this expedition? A manifest, a list of names, anything beyond what this station's own logs preserve? I would like to know who they were, if that information exists anywhere.*

He sent the query through the relay chain along with his completed catalogue, checked the connection's stability one final time, and allowed himself, before Meen-4's reply could arrive, a long, unhurried moment simply sitting with the quiet, patient grief of a record that had ended exactly the way its final formal entry had promised it wouldn't — no substitutions, full complement, and, in the end, nothing more.
