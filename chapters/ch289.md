# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty-Nine — The Archive, Deep Access

The offer came the way Meen-4's most significant offers usually did — folded into an otherwise ordinary conversation, delivered with so little ceremony that he almost missed the size of it.

They'd been discussing the residential wing's water reclamation system, a mundane engineering problem he'd been working through with her for the better part of an hour, when she signaled, without transition, *I am extending your access to the Deep Archive. Effective immediately.*

He set down the schematic he'd been holding. "The Deep Archive."

*Yes.*

"You've mentioned it exists. You've never described what's in it."

*I have not, because access was not previously warranted.* She turned from the water schematic entirely, her full attention settling on him now with a weight he'd learned, across two years, to take seriously. *The general Archive you have used contains cultivation technique, spatial theory, translated fragments of historical record deemed relevant to a carrier's ordinary development. The Deep Archive contains the Formican Empire's political and historical records in their unredacted form. Governance structures. Conflicts. Decisions made and their consequences. Accounts the general Archive has, until now, filtered out of what you were shown.*

"Filtered for what reason?"

*Because a carrier who has not yet demonstrated sufficient judgment should not be handed a civilization's full and unflattering history and asked to draw conclusions from it unsupervised.* A pause. *You have now demonstrated sufficient judgment.*

He felt the weight of the sentence settle somewhere just beneath his sternum — not pride, exactly, though pride was somewhere in it, but something closer to the specific gravity of being trusted with something that could not be untrusted once given.

"What changed your assessment?"

*The residential wing,* she signaled. *Not the technique required to build it. The choices you made about what to build. A civilization's history is, in large part, the accumulated record of what its most capable individuals chose to do with capability once they had it. I have spent four hundred years watching what you choose. I am satisfied the record will be useful to you rather than merely dangerous.*

---

He entered the Deep Archive that evening, alone, the chamber Meen-4 led him to unlike anything else he'd encountered in the Strata — smaller than he'd expected, close and quiet, the walls lined with dense columns of Formican script that seemed to shift faintly at the edge of his vision, the way text sometimes does when a language is still settling into comprehension rather than fully arrived.

*The material is extensive,* Meen-4 signaled, standing at the threshold rather than entering with him. *I will not accompany you through the initial review. Some of what you find should be encountered without an observer present.*

"That sounds ominous."

*It is simply accurate. History is easier to sit with honestly when no one is watching your face while you read it.*

She left him to it.

He spent the first hour on governance structures — the loose, ancient federation the Formican Empire had operated under before its own long dissolution, the careful distribution of authority across regions and disciplines that read, in places, uncomfortably close to systems he recognized from his own world's failed attempts at the same problem. He took notes without fully processing them, the specific discipline of reading first and understanding later carrying him through material dense enough that immediate comprehension wasn't realistic.

The second hour, he found the Amber Lattice School's destruction — a bare administrative record at first, a location, a date, a designation change from *active* to *dissolved*, offering nothing about cause. He flagged it for deeper investigation and moved on, the entry's clinical emptiness itself a kind of signal that something larger sat underneath it, waiting.

He found the third thing almost by accident, three hours into the session, tired enough that his attention had begun to drift toward surface pattern rather than deep reading — a designation he didn't recognize, cross-referenced against half a dozen other records in a way that suggested significance without stating it directly.

*Veth-Karun.*

The name meant nothing to him. He almost passed over it, the way he'd passed over dozens of unfamiliar Formican names across the evening's reading. But the cross-reference pattern caught his attention — records from radically different sections of the Archive, political history, cultivation theory, a section he hadn't yet identified the purpose of, all converging on the same three syllables with a consistency that felt less like coincidence than architecture.

He sat back from the reading surface, the chamber quiet around him, and understood, with the specific certainty he'd learned to trust across two years of finding exactly this kind of pattern, that he'd found something the general Archive had been built, very deliberately, to keep him from finding early.

He didn't open the deeper record that night. It was late, and Meen-4's warning about encountering things without an observer present had settled into him with more weight than he'd initially given it credit for.

Instead, he marked the reference, closed the session, and walked back through the Strata's quiet corridors toward the residential wing, past the elder Mrs. Tan's dark window, past the training hall where Wei Canglan's evening practice had long since ended, carrying a single unfamiliar name that he suspected, even before he understood why, was going to change the shape of everything he thought he knew about how this world had come to be quarantined in the first place.
