# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-Five — The Chitin Circuit Network

He installed the fourteenth circuit on a Sunday afternoon, which completed the network he'd been building for two months across the outer Strata.

The project had grown out of necessity. With Lady Shen training in the archive, Chen Yufei working there most evenings, occasional formal visits from Northern Peak representatives in the slow wake of the Council's decision, and his own increasing time spent at Ratio 3 and 4 in different sections of the corridor system, he had needed better situational awareness than his own perception alone could provide.

The solution was the same one he'd applied to the surface district: distributed sensing.

Fourteen circuits, each third-generation design, installed at key points throughout the mapped Strata territory — the library, the east corridor, the circular room's approach, the thin section's interior side, the secondary archive, and eight additional nodes covering the territory's remaining geography. Each unit fed into a modified router he'd built specifically for the Strata's ambient conditions, which then relayed to his phone through a connection he'd engineered using the same Qi-to-electrical transduction principle as the individual circuits.

He could now see, on a single interface, the real-time ambient Qi state of the entire mapped Strata territory.

He sat in the library after installing the final unit and watched the network come fully online.

The display was not visual in the conventional sense — he'd built it as a layered data readout rather than a map, since a true spatial visualization would have required graphics capability beyond what he could build with his current resources. But the data itself was rich: ambient density readings, structured-signature detection, directional flow indicators, occupancy alerts.

He watched it for a long time.

He could see Chen Yufei's signature in the agricultural records section, steady and engaged. He could see Lady Shen's signature near the outer wall, working through a training exercise he'd assigned her two days ago. He could see, at the edge of detection range, what he assessed as Axis-7's signature near the restricted section — she had been spending more time there recently, which he hadn't asked about yet but had noted.

He could see, deeper than any of his circuits had previously detected, the diffuse warmth of the Layer 3 community's collective field — fainter at the network's outer range, but present, a constant baseline hum beneath everything else.

He wrote:

> *Chitin Circuit network: 14 units, complete. Coverage: full mapped Strata territory. This is the foundation of what eventually becomes a much larger infrastructure project — I can see, in principle, how this scales to cover the entire Strata, and eventually the surface Under-Web at a resolution no current human or Formican system maintains.*
>
> *Immediate use: situational awareness for the increasingly complex set of people now regularly present in the archive. Lady Shen, Chen Yufei, occasional Court visitors, myself, Axis-7, Meen-4, the broader Layer 3 community at range.*
>
> *Secondary use, unplanned but apparent: I am building, without intending it as such, the first integrated human-operated monitoring system for Formican-occupied space. This raises questions about consent and appropriateness that I have not fully worked through.*
>
> *Question for Axis-7: is the community comfortable being monitored by this network, even passively, even by someone they have vouched for?*

He sent this question in the register that evening, when Axis-7 returned from wherever she'd been near the restricted section.

Her response, after consideration: *The community is aware of the network's installation. We have been aware of each unit as you placed it — the archive's own systems register new circuit activity within this territory.*

*And the community's position?*

*Acceptable, with conditions.* A pause. *The network's data should not be shared outside this archive without community consultation. The network's resolution should not extend into Layer 3 proper without explicit invitation. And—* a longer pause — *the network should include a mechanism by which the community can communicate with you directly through it, not merely be observed by it.*

He thought about this. It was a reasonable set of conditions, and the third one was something he hadn't considered: the network as a two-way channel rather than a one-way sensing tool.

*I can build that,* he sent. *A dedicated input channel, accessible from Layer 3, that reaches my interface directly.*

*Good,* Axis-7 sent. *Build it. Then the network serves the community as much as it serves you.*

He nodded, though the gesture was for himself.

He spent the rest of the evening designing the input channel — a fifteenth circuit, oriented differently from the others, built to receive rather than primarily to transmit, with an interface protocol that would let a Formican signal reach his phone directly without requiring him to be physically present in the archive.

He completed the design by midnight.

He would build it tomorrow.

He thought, packing up his materials, about the trajectory of what he'd built over the past fourteen months — from a single crude circuit that glowed unexpectedly in a cardboard box, to a fourteen-unit network covering an entire archaeological complex, soon to be fifteen with a dedicated community channel.

He thought about what this would become if it kept growing at this rate.

He did not yet know that this fifteenth circuit — the first deliberately bilateral one, designed at the community's request rather than his own initiative — would become, in later years, the prototype for the entire planet's eventual Chitin Circuit infrastructure.

He just thought it was a good piece of engineering, and went home to sleep.
