# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 380 — The Human Ally Revealed

The third session was held, at the Orthodoxy's formal request and the Surveyor's neutral consent, with an observer present — the first time any party other than Lin Mushi and the Surveyor's own consensus body had been permitted inside the review's live proceedings.

He learned who it would be only an hour beforehand, through a brief notation transmitted by the Witness Node, and he read the designation twice before the shape of it fully landed.

*Observer, admitted under Collective standing: Shen Baoling, human, Zone QZ-7, submitted by petition of the Containment Orthodoxy.*

He sat very still with the notebook open in front of him, the pen unmoving in his hand, and felt the last month rearrange itself with the particular vertigo of a puzzle whose missing piece had, it turned out, been sitting across the table from him the entire time.

*She matches someone who left it,* Lady Shen had said, weeks ago, describing an ally the Orthodoxy had recruited. *Someone who understands the cultivation world intimately.* He had run through the short list of people that description could fit, and he understood now, with the cold clarity of hindsight, that he had never once considered the most obvious name on it, because the most obvious name on it had been the one bringing him the warning.

He did not confront her before the session. There was no time, and some part of him — the part that had learned, over three years, to trust procedure when emotion threatened to overwhelm judgment — recognized that whatever this was, it deserved to happen in the open, not in a hurried, furious exchange before the formal proceedings began.

She appeared in the chamber precisely as the session opened, composed as she always was, and met his gaze without flinching, without apology, without anything that could be mistaken for shame.

"You already know," she said quietly, before he could speak. "I can see it on your face."

"You told me about her," he said, careful to keep his voice level in front of the Surveyor's silent, patient attention. "You described her like she was someone else."

"I described her accurately," Lady Shen said. "I never claimed she was someone else. You assumed that, and I let you, because I was not yet ready to have this conversation, and because I wanted the review's own machinery to reach this moment before I forced it."

The Surveyor's notation arrived then, unhurried as ever, opening the formal session as though the personal weight of what had just been revealed carried no more or less significance than any other procedural matter before it.

*Session three is now in progress. The Observer's standing has been noted and accepted under Collective protocol. Proceedings will continue.*

He had prepared answers for technical questions, for philosophical ones. He had not prepared for the specific discipline of sitting across a formal review from someone who had, for months, occupied two positions at once — the source of every warning that had helped him survive this process, and simultaneously, apparently, an active party working to end it.

He got through the session on procedure alone, answering the Surveyor's questions with the same care he had brought to every prior exchange, aware the entire time of Lady Shen's stillness beside the chamber's edge, watching him with an expression he could not fully read — not triumph, not guilt exactly, something more complicated that he did not yet have the vocabulary to name.

It was only after the session closed, the Witness Node falling quiet, that he finally turned to her directly.

"Why," he said. Not accusatory. Genuinely asking.

"Not here," she said. "Not while I am still deciding how much of the answer I am willing to give you. But I will give it to you, Lin Mushi. I owe you that much, and considerably more besides. Soon."

She left the way she always left, without ceremony, and he stood alone in the chamber that had, an hour earlier, held the most consequential conversation of his life, trying to reconcile the version of Lady Shen Baoling who had brought him warning after warning with the version who had, it seemed, spent that same span of time quietly working against everything those warnings claimed to protect.

He thought of her words from weeks ago, the strange, specific tenderness underneath them. *Fear wearing the shape of betrayal is still, underneath it, fear. It deserves to be understood before it's condemned.*

She had been talking about herself the entire time, and he had not heard it, because he had never once considered that the person delivering the warning and the person the warning was about could be the same woman, standing in the same room, choosing, for reasons he did not yet understand, to hold both truths in front of him without letting either one show completely.

He picked up the notebook, and wrote a single line, because it was the only honest thing he had to say about it yet.

*Lady Shen is the Orthodoxy's human ally. I don't know why. I need to find out before I decide what it means.*
