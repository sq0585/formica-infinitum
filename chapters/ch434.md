# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 434 — The Formican Installation

It did not look like he'd expected, and he had, over five years, learned to distrust his own expectations enough that the difference didn't unsettle him. It simply corrected him.

He had imagined something closer to the Strata — organic, chitin-ribbed, grown rather than built. What resolved out of the dark at the end of the ninth segment was more geometric than that, a structure roughly three kilometers across at micro-scale, its surfaces faceted in the same disciplined economy he recognized from Formican architecture generally, but arranged with a precision that suggested purpose rather than growth. Not a home. An instrument.

*Observation 352: structure intact. No visible degradation across any external surface, despite an estimated operational age of forty million years. This should not be possible for any material I understand.*

He approached slowly, cataloguing before touching anything, the old discipline holding even here, this far from anything that had ever needed him to be careful before. The station's outer hull carried a faint, steady pulse of Veritonal activity — not the installation itself producing new energy, he realized after several minutes of careful measurement, but something closer to maintenance: small, patient Circuits, embedded throughout the structure, running the same self-repair function they'd presumably been running since whoever built this place had last walked away from it.

*Rule: this is not a ruin. It is a machine that has never stopped working. The distinction matters more than I initially gave it credit for.*

The entry yielded to the same kind of careful, patient effort that had opened every Formican lock he'd encountered since the Codex first taught him the grammar of them — not force, never force, but recognition, the structure responding to a signature it had presumably been built to eventually recognize, even across a gap of forty million years and however many light-years separated whoever had built it from the small, curious human now standing at its threshold.

Inside, the air — if it could be called air, in a structure that had never needed to accommodate breathing lungs — held the same faint temperature signature he remembered from the Strata's oldest chambers: cooler than expected, deliberately so, the specific coolness of somewhere built to preserve rather than to live in.

He moved through it methodically, room by room, the same procedure he'd used on every unfamiliar Formican space since the very beginning. Observe. Test. Record. The station's architecture organized itself, once he understood the pattern, into three distinct sections, and he catalogued each before allowing himself to linger in any of them.

The first: a vast archive of instrumentation and recorded data, structured in the same notation Meen-4 had spent years teaching him to read, its scale immediately, overwhelmingly apparent even before he'd translated a single entry. Forty million years of continuous stellar observation, by the look of the timestamps at the archive's oldest visible layer — a project of a duration that made every human institution he could think of look, by comparison, like something built yesterday and abandoned by lunch.

The second: smaller, quieter, sealed behind a lock with a different signature than the first, one that took him considerably longer to coax open. Inside: something that read, once he'd examined enough of it to be confident, as a cultivation archive rather than a stellar one — technique, notation, fragments of instruction, older by every measure he could apply than anything in Chengdu's own Deep Archive. He did not touch any of it yet. He simply confirmed it existed, logged its presence, and moved on, the discipline of five years insisting that cataloguing come before consumption, every time, without exception.

The third space was smaller than either of the others, almost incidental by comparison, tucked into what he eventually understood was the installation's original command chamber. A single, isolated data structure, distinct in its notation from everything surrounding it, addressed — he could tell this much before he'd translated a single symbol, from the deliberate, careful way it had been isolated from the rest of the archive — not to a system, not to a future observer generally, but to someone specific.

He stood in front of it a long moment, feeling the particular weight of a room built by hands — or whatever the Formican equivalent of hands had been, this far back, this far from anywhere he understood — that had known, with some certainty, that someone would eventually stand exactly where he was standing now.

*Observation 359,* he wrote, and found his hand was, faintly, not entirely steady. *Isolated data structure located in command chamber. Notation style distinct from archive proper — deliberate, personal, addressed rather than merely recorded. I have not yet opened it. I am recording this fact before I do, because I suspect, once I have read whatever it contains, I will not be the same kind of careful about it that I currently am.*

He checked the relay chain — stable, Amber-0 confirmed, Chen Yufei's last check-in four hours old and calm — and allowed himself thirty seconds of stillness before he reached, finally, toward the message the installation's last crew had left waiting, patiently, across a gap of forty million years, for exactly this moment.
