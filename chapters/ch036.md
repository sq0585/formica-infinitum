# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Thirty-Six — Due Diligence

He spent a week being skeptical of himself.

This was deliberate. He had developed, over six weeks of progressive perception and escalating documentation, a working model of the world that was increasingly elaborate and internally consistent. Internal consistency was not external validity. The model could be wrong. He could be wrong in ways that felt right from the inside, and the only partial protection against this was to apply the same systematic skepticism to his own conclusions that he applied to any other hypothesis.

He called this week's project *due diligence*, which was a term he'd borrowed from a business article he'd read once and found unexpectedly useful.

---

The tests he ran:

*Test 1: Can my perceptions be verified by an independent method?*

The wall crack — verified by contractor. The cold room — effect persisted for weeks, neighbor's plant still doing better (he'd checked twice). The wrist healing — he couldn't re-injure his wrist to retest this, but the baseline healing time for a comparable sprain was 48–72 hours and he had healed in approximately three hours. These were not definitive but they were consistent with real phenomenon.

The EM field sensitivity from channel two: he verified this by testing it against a gauss meter his friend Gao would have called an eccentric purchase but was in fact forty-seven yuan on an online platform. The meter registered EM fields. His perception of the same fields, run simultaneously, showed a correlation coefficient he estimated at around 0.85. Not perfect. Not zero.

Result: perceptions are consistently corroborated by external methods where external methods exist.

*Test 2: Is there an alternative explanation for what I'm perceiving?*

For the structural perceptions and the EM sensitivity: the gauss meter data ruled out pure hallucination as an explanation. For the Qi filament network visualization: he couldn't rule out that the visual layer was self-generated by his nervous system in response to genuine non-visual input — that he was *seeing* something that was in fact *felt*, with vision as the interpretive interface. He wrote this down because the distinction might matter later.

For the architectural depth perception: he could not test this independently. He could only note that its internal consistency was high (four surveys, consistent geometry) and that it corresponded structurally to the Codex schematic he'd received afterward (which could indicate either genuine correspondence or that the schematic was based on the same self-generated model — unclear).

Result: alternative explanations cannot be fully ruled out but are increasingly strained. The most parsimonious explanation is that the perceptions are accurate.

*Test 3: Have I been assuming conclusions rather than reaching them?*

He reread his notebooks from days one through forty-two, looking for moments of motivated reasoning. He found three.

Day eight: he had described the depth sensation as "not frightening" at a time when he may have found it frightening and was managing that by labeling it as not frightening. He corrected this entry with a note: *was probably frightening. The non-frightening framing was productive but possibly inaccurate.*

Day twenty-three: in the dark energy / Veritonal Energy hypothesis, he had written the evidence column under "for" and the counter-evidence column under "against" with noticeably different lengths, which was a tell. He reviewed and added three items to the "against" column that he should have included before.

Day thirty-one: he had described channel two's EM sensitivity as "confirmed" before he'd run the gauss meter test. Confirmation was premature. He corrected *confirmed* to *strongly suggested.*

Result: three instances of motivated reasoning, all minor, all correctable. Not a pattern of systematic bias, but worth monitoring.

---

He wrote the due diligence summary on a Saturday afternoon, sitting at his desk with all the windows open despite the cold because he'd needed air.

> *Due diligence week summary:*
>
> *The model survives external testing where testing is possible. Internal consistency is high. Three minor motivated-reasoning corrections identified and noted.*
>
> *Conclusion: I am not systematically deluding myself. The perceptions are real. The framework is the best available explanation for the data I have.*
>
> *I am going to keep going.*

He read this back.

He thought: six weeks ago I watched a man die and found an app on my phone and didn't know whether I was having a stress response. Now I have corroborating data, a rigorously tested framework, a cross-section schematic of a four-layer subterranean structure, and three open meridian channels.

He thought: the appropriate response to this is not awe. The appropriate response is to continue working.

He closed the due diligence notebook — he'd started a dedicated one, which felt slightly absurd and was also necessary — and picked up the notation notebook.

Due diligence complete. The model survived.

Seven weeks remaining.

He kept working.
