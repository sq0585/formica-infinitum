# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 411 — Other Quarantine Zones

The disclosure arrived unexpectedly, attached almost as an afterthought to what should have been a routine quarterly reporting exchange — the Surveyor, apparently, had decided that a certain category of information no longer needed to be withheld from a zone that had successfully completed its formal review.

*For context relevant to Zone QZ-7's ongoing development,* the notation read, *this body notes that Zone QZ-7 is one of nine active Monitored Development Zones currently under Collective observation. The other eight zone designations are transmitted herewith, for informational purposes only.*

Lin Mushi read the list twice, feeling something shift in his understanding of Earth's place in the wider Collective — a shift he hadn't fully anticipated, despite months of learning to think in terms of civilizations and zones and consensus bodies spanning distances he still struggled to hold in his mind at once.

"Earth isn't unique," he said slowly, to Meen-4, who was studying the same list with an intensity that told him she was finding something in it he hadn't yet spotted himself.

"No," she agreed. "It never was, in the strictest sense — I told you, months ago, that dozens of Quarantine Zone planets exist, each holding the remnants of a civilization too close to Veth-Karun's original event horizon. What is new here is the specific detail that nine of those zones have already progressed to Monitored Development status. Earth is not merely one civilization among dozens under quarantine. It is one of a smaller, more advanced group that has already begun the transition out of it."

He looked back at the list, the eight unfamiliar zone designations sitting alongside QZ-7's own, each representing, he understood now, an entire world's worth of history he knew nothing about — other civilizations that had, in their own ways, survived the same kind of scrutiny Earth had only just endured.

"Do we know anything else about them," he asked. "Their histories. What they're like."

"Very little, currently. Zone designations alone do not carry that context, and the Collective does not typically share detailed information between zones without specific cause." Meen-4 paused, her antennae angling toward a particular section of the notation. "But I want you to look at this part carefully. Two of the nine zones — designated here only by number, not name — have recently shown Qi activity spikes similar in pattern to Earth's own development trajectory."

He felt the shape of the implication settle into him slowly, the way the most consequential realizations often did. "You mean they're developing the way we developed. Before all of this started."

"Yes. Which means, if the pattern holds, they will eventually face something very similar to what Earth just survived — a Surveyor's arrival, a formal review, the same threshold argument the Containment Orthodoxy raised against you, quite possibly the same Orthodoxy itself, still arguing the same case against a different civilization that has not yet had the chance to build a defense as strong as yours."

He sat with that for a long moment, feeling something that was not quite obligation and not quite kinship settle into the shape of a decision he hadn't expected to be making today. He thought of the fifty-three residents who had chosen to leave the Strata rather than risk what he was risking. He thought of Dr. Shen Yue's revoked tenure, Liang Jingzhi's chaotic, honest disclosure, the months of careful, costly work that had, eventually, earned Earth its narrow, hard-won survival.

"If they're facing the same review eventually," he said slowly, "I don't want them to have to build the defense from nothing, the way I did. Not if I can help it."

"What are you proposing," Meen-4 asked, though something in her tone suggested she had already guessed.

"I want to offer to share Earth's review documentation," he said. "Not the sensitive internal details — the Strata's specific residents, anything that would compromise our own privacy or safety. But the strategic shape of it. What worked. What the Orthodoxy's argument actually was, and how we answered it. If two other worlds are heading toward the same reckoning we just survived, I don't want them walking into it blind, the way I did."

Meen-4 was quiet a long moment, something careful and considering in her stillness. "That is a generous offer," she said finally. "It is also, I suspect, going to draw attention — not all of it welcome. But I do not think that will stop you from making it."

"No," he admitted. "It won't."

"Then let's draft the transmission," she said, "and see what the Collective makes of a zone that survived its own review, and immediately turned around to help the next ones facing it."
