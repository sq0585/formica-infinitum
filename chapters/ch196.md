# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Ninety-Six — Meen-4's Assessment

Keeper Meen-4 delivered her assessment of the recent events three days after the confrontation, in the same flat, precise register she brought to everything, with no apparent concern for whether the content would be comfortable to receive.

She found him in the archive, going through the annotation exchange work that had continued, almost incongruously, throughout the entire crisis — the unglamorous documentation labor that didn't pause just because the surface world had been in upheaval.

*You have destabilized the human cultivation structure,* she sent, without preamble. *You have brought combative cultivation technique into the Outer Library, deployed at containment intensity. The collective authority was required to intervene directly, which has not occurred in this station's operational history within my tenure.* A pause that he had learned, by now, to read as her version of emphasis. *I find you to be extremely high-maintenance.*

He almost laughed, despite the gravity of what she was describing.

*That's a fair assessment,* he sent back.

*It was not offered as commentary requiring agreement,* she replied. *It was offered as fact.*

*I understand. I agree with the fact anyway.*

She regarded him for a moment with the particular stillness that, across months of working together, he'd learned indicated she was deciding something.

*The Layer 3 community's collective intervention was not punitive,* she continued. *It was protective — of the Archive's stability, not of you specifically. You should understand this distinction clearly, the way Axis-7 explained it to you previously.*

*I understand,* he sent. *I did not assume the intervention was an endorsement.*

*Good.* Another pause. *Despite the disruption, the community's broader assessment of your conduct across the past sixteen months remains favorable. You have not attempted to exploit the Archive's resources for personal advantage beyond what is reasonably necessary for your own development. You have shared corrected information honestly, at real cost to yourself and to people connected to you. You have, on the whole, behaved in a manner consistent with what the Inheritance Protocol's design intends, even when your methods have been — as previously noted — high-maintenance.*

He sat with this, the assessment's bluntness somehow more meaningful than a warmer version would have been.

*Thank you,* he sent.

*You are welcome.* She paused, then added something he hadn't expected. *I have decided to extend your access to a section of the Intermediate Archive you have been attempting to unlock for approximately one month. The technical materials on advanced Chitin Circuit network design — the section beyond what our exchange arrangement has previously covered.*

He felt genuine surprise at this. *Why now, specifically?*

*Because your handling of the recent crisis demonstrated capability and judgment consistent with someone who can be trusted with more advanced material,* she sent. *Despite, or perhaps including, the high-maintenance quality of how you arrived at that demonstration.*

He thought about this — criticism and continued cooperation, delivered in the same brief exchange, without contradiction. It was, he was realizing, characteristically Meen-4: she did not soften her assessments to make them more palatable, and she did not let her assessments prevent her from acting on the underlying trust that justified them.

*I'll try to be less high-maintenance going forward,* he offered.

*I do not believe that is realistic,* she sent. *I have adjusted my expectations accordingly.*

He laughed, this time without trying to suppress it.

She had already turned back to her own work by the time the sound left him, the conversation apparently concluded on her terms, the new archive access already logged and waiting for him at his usual table.

He went to look at it.

The work continued, as it always did, regardless of how much the surface world above had just shaken.
