# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Seventy-Nine — Axis-7's View

He brought the question to Axis-7 the next day, in the archive, framing it as plainly as he'd written it the night before.

She listened — or rather, processed, the register signal he sent her arriving in the careful, complete form he'd developed over months of practice. When he finished, she was quiet for longer than her usual consideration pauses.

*You have done,* she sent finally, *what humans always do when they find something true. You have made it political.*

He held this. It was not quite an accusation, in the flat quality of her communication style, but it carried an edge he hadn't encountered from her before.

*Is that a criticism?*

*It is an observation.* A pause. *The truth does not become less true because of what you do with it. The notation either correctly describes the technique, or it does not. Whether five human institutions fracture, reorganize, or collapse entirely as a consequence does not change the notation's accuracy by even a fraction.*

*But the consequences are real,* he sent. *People are losing homes. Losing standing. Losing the structures that organized their lives.*

*Yes.* Another pause. *This is what humans do with true things. You did not invent this pattern. Every civilization that has encountered correction to a foundational belief has gone through some version of what your Courts are currently experiencing. The Formicans went through their own version of this, in our own history, more than once.*

He sat with this — the idea that this wasn't a uniquely human failure, or a uniquely his-fault catastrophe, but a pattern that any sufficiently complex society encountered when its foundational beliefs were shown to be incomplete.

*Were you wrong to release the documents?* he asked.

A longer pause than usual.

*Wrong? No.* A pause. *Careful? Not particularly.*

He almost smiled, despite the weight of the conversation. It was, he was learning, characteristic of her — a kind of affectionate contempt that managed to be both genuinely critical and genuinely without malice.

*What would careful have looked like?* he asked.

*I do not know precisely. I have observed two previous Carriers, neither of whom attempted what you have attempted — releasing corrected information at this scale, this quickly, to this many institutions simultaneously. Neither precedent tells me what 'careful' would have produced instead.* A pause. *What I observe: you released true information. The institutions are responding according to their own internal logics, which include both genuine engagement and political maneuvering, both honesty and self-protection. This is not a failure of your action. It is simply what happens when truth meets institutions built partly on the absence of truth.*

He thought about this.

*The structural question I've been sitting with,* he sent. *What replaces what the Courts were providing — training, dispute resolution, protection for vulnerable practitioners. Do you have observations on that?*

*I have one,* she sent. *The Amber Lattice School existed for six centuries doing exactly this — training, in a structure that did not require the kind of exclusive, hierarchical authority your Courts have built. Human and Formican students together, taught according to demonstrated capability rather than institutional rank, with disputes managed by appeal to demonstrated technique rather than political position.*

He held this.

*You're suggesting the model already exists.*

*I am suggesting you have already been standing inside it for fifteen months and may not have recognized it as a model rather than simply as your own personal circumstance.* A pause. *The archive does not require exclusive belonging. It requires demonstrated comprehension. Anyone who develops sufficient capability gains sufficient access. This is, functionally, what your Courts claim to provide and largely do not.*

He sat with this for a long time.

He thought about Wei Canglan, methodically cataloging agricultural records while he found his footing. About Chen Yufei, who had built more genuine capability in ten months outside institutional structure than nine years inside it had given her. About the assembly he'd been imagining without naming.

He thought: the Amber Lattice School. Not as a metaphor. As an actual precedent, six centuries of working structure, sitting in the historical record he'd already partially read.

*Thank you,* he sent. *I think you've just given me the shape of something I've been circling for weeks.*

*You are welcome,* she sent. *Now work. The Dimensional Lock practice will not advance itself while you have philosophical conversations.*

He almost laughed.

He went back to work, but the question's shape had changed, and it would not stop developing in the back of his thinking for the rest of the day.
