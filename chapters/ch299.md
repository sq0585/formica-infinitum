# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Ninety-Nine — The Deep-Space Signal

The ping came through the Chitin Circuit network on an otherwise unremarkable Tuesday, buried in the routine diagnostic sweep Lin Mushi had taken to running every few days now that the network had grown large enough to require active monitoring rather than casual attention.

He almost missed it. The sweep's output was long, mostly routine confirmations — nodes reporting healthy, Qi flow within expected tolerances, the accumulated small business of a system that had grown considerably beyond what any one person should reasonably be expected to track by feel alone. It was Meen-4 who caught it first, flagging a single anomalous line in the output with the flat, precise urgency she reserved for things that actually mattered.

*This entry does not match any known Circuit node.*

He pulled up the flagged data, the coordinates attached to it taking a moment to fully register.

"This isn't Earth," he said.

*No.*

The coordinates, once he'd converted them into something his own frame of reference could parse, placed the signal's origin somewhere in the outer solar system — distant enough that the specific location took him a long moment of cross-referencing to even identify with confidence.

"An installation," he said slowly, reading through the decoded fragment Meen-4 had already begun assembling. "There's an installation out there. Formican."

*Yes.* Something in Meen-4's signal carried a weight he hadn't heard from her in some time — not alarm exactly, but the specific gravity of an old certainty being disturbed. *I was not aware any Formican infrastructure existed beyond this world's boundary. The Archive's records on off-world installations are sparse, and what exists is centuries out of date even by the Archive's own admission.*

"What's the signal actually saying?"

*A status ping. Automated, not composed — the kind of transmission a dormant installation sends periodically to confirm it still exists, rather than a deliberate message from an active operator.* She worked through the decoding as she spoke, the fragment slowly resolving into something more legible. *Designation code, partially corrupted. Operational status: degraded. That is the entirety of the content. No request for response. No indication of what the installation's original purpose was.*

He sat with the sparse, strange fact of it — something Formican, built long enough ago that even the Archive's ancient records had lost track of it, sitting quietly in the outer dark of his own solar system, occasionally announcing, to no one in particular, that it was still there.

"How long has it been transmitting?"

*I do not know. This is the first instance the Circuit network has been sensitive enough to detect. It is possible the installation has been signaling for millennia and we have simply, until now, lacked the infrastructure to hear it.*

---

He brought the discovery to Liang Jingzhi that evening, less because she had any specialized knowledge of off-world Formican installations and more because he'd learned, over the past year, that her particular gift for holding vast, indifferent scale steady in her hands without flinching made her one of the few people he trusted to think clearly about something this large.

She listened to the full account without interrupting, then sat quietly for a moment, turning the coordinates over on her tablet with the same careful attention she gave to geological survey data.

"It's outside Earth," she said finally. "Which means it's outside anything you've been responsible for until today."

"I know."

"Are you going to investigate it?"

He considered the question honestly. "I don't know how, yet. Everything I can do — the Colony Link, the Dimensional Weaving — is built around this planet. I don't have a way to reach something in the outer solar system, even if I wanted to."

"Then it's not an immediate problem." She said it plainly, without dismissing the discovery's weight, simply placing it accurately within the scale of what actually required his attention right now. "It's a fact you now know that you didn't know yesterday. Sometimes that's all a discovery is, for a while — not a task, just a fact, sitting there until circumstances change enough to make it actionable."

"That's a more patient response than I expected."

"I spend most of my working life studying things that move on geological timescales," she said, something dry in her voice. "A dormant installation transmitting a degraded status ping is, relatively speaking, urgent by comparison. But urgent isn't the same as immediately solvable. I've learned to sit with facts I can't yet act on without letting them become a crisis before they've earned it."

He found the observation settling into him with real usefulness — a discipline he suspected he'd need considerably more of, the further this world kept expanding past the boundaries he'd originally understood it to have.

---

He wrote the entry into his notebook that night, brief, the way the entries that mattered most to him had increasingly become.

> *Off-world Formican installation. Outer solar system. Degraded, dormant, transmitting a status ping that says almost nothing except that something is still there. I don't know what it means yet. I don't have a way to reach it yet. I'm noting it because Liang Jingzhi is right — some facts just need to be held until the moment they become actionable, and I'd rather hold this one honestly than pretend I understand its shape before I do.*

He closed the notebook, the quiet fact of the signal settling somewhere at the edge of his awareness alongside Veth-Karun's untranslated symbols and the Pelagic Archive's unopened door — one more piece of a world that kept, patiently and without apparent urgency, revealing itself to be considerably larger than anything he'd originally signed up to understand.
