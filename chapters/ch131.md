# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Thirty-One — The Negotiation

Chen Yufei arrived at 2:58 AM.

She came through the threshold with the ease of someone who had done it forty times — four minutes from entry to the library door, which was her personal record. She took in the scene in the doorway with a practitioner's quick assessment: Lin Mushi at the table, Lady Shen Baoling across from him, no visible tension, both sitting rather than standing. She came in.

Lady Shen Baoling looked at her.

"Chen Yufei," she said. "I had heard you were involved."

"Lady Shen." Chen Yufei pulled a chair from the end of the table and sat at the corner position — not across from Lady Shen, not beside Lin Mushi, but available to both. "You made good time on the Compression work."

"Your Iron Meridian breathing corrections were useful, as it turned out." Lady Shen said this without inflection. "Unofficial practitioner communication. Someone in your network shared a posture and breathing correction several months ago that improved my cultivation efficiency significantly."

Chen Yufei looked at Lin Mushi.

He looked at the table.

"I may have shared some cultivation correction notes more broadly than the immediate situation required," he said.

"You shared them with someone who wasn't me," Chen Yufei said. "That person shared them further."

"It appears so."

Lady Shen was looking between them with an expression that suggested she was revising her model of the situation in real time. "You corrected Iron Meridian technique and distributed the correction through informal networks."

"I corrected a specific error in Chen Yufei's practice," he said. "She mentioned the effect to a colleague. The colleague found it significant and passed it along." He paused. "I didn't authorize the distribution. I also didn't object when I found out about it, because the correction is accurate and the people who received it benefited."

Lady Shen was quiet for a moment.

"The Northern Peak breathing technique," she said. "The efficiency improvement I noticed three months ago."

"Probably a version of the same correction," he said. "The Northern Peak's atmospheric cultivation has a related suppression error. If the correction propagated through interCourt practitioner networks—"

"It propagated," she said. Her voice was flat. Then, after a moment: "The Northern Peak Court has noticed a cultivation efficiency improvement across its senior practitioners in the past three months that our internal training doesn't account for."

"Yes," he said.

"That was you."

"That was accurate information about how the technique actually works," he said. "Distributed by people who found it useful."

Lady Shen looked at him.

He looked back.

Chen Yufei was watching this exchange with the expression of someone who had been present for many significant exchanges in the past six months and was reading this one as different in a way she was still categorizing.

"The document," Lady Shen said.

He said: "Which document?"

"The one you're preparing. The comparative analysis of Formican source material against the Courts' foundational texts." She paused. "I have been monitoring informal practitioner communication networks for three months. I know you're preparing something."

He thought about this.

"The document is complete," he said. "It goes out tomorrow."

A silence.

"To whom?" Lady Shen said.

"Forty-seven people. Selected by Chen Yufei based on their known intellectual honesty and their history of engaging with contradictions in their traditions." He met her gaze. "The selection was designed to reach people who could receive the information as information rather than as a political attack."

Lady Shen was very still.

"I'm not on the list," she said.

"No."

"Why not?"

He considered how to say this accurately. "Because three months ago you were here with an acquisition mandate and I didn't have a basis for assessing your response to the document as distinct from your institutional role." He paused. "You're here now outside your institutional role. That's different information."

She looked at him.

"Add me to the list," she said.

He looked at Chen Yufei.

Chen Yufei looked at him. A brief, very clear communication in their three months of working together: *your call.*

He turned back to Lady Shen.

"I'll send you the document directly," he said. "Not on the list — a separate transmission, with context that the list recipients won't have." He paused. "Because you have access to the Strata now and you need to understand what the document says in relation to what you can see here."

Lady Shen looked around the archive. She looked at the shelves, the sealed containers, Axis-7 visible at the east wall still reading — she had been reading through all of this, with the absorbed attention of someone who had been in this archive for 180 years and had not changed her behavior because a human dispute was happening at the table.

"What does the document say about Northern Peak technique?" Lady Shen asked.

"That the atmospheric cultivation system is a derivation of Formican Stage Two breathing protocols," he said. "That the derivation preserved approximately sixty percent of the original technique's function and lost forty percent through transmission errors over a period of approximately eight hundred years. That the practitioners who built on those techniques were working with a real phenomenon and produced real results and that the explanatory framework their tradition developed was incomplete rather than wrong." He paused. "That the correct mechanism is the one in this archive."

Lady Shen was quiet for a long time.

Then: "What would I need to do to access the archive's cultivation materials?"

He told her the truth: Compression to Ratio 1.8 minimum, communication register fluency sufficient for the dimensional lock mechanism, Chitin Seed integration — she almost certainly had latent Seed structure, most serious practitioners did, but she had not activated it.

"I can help with the activation," he said. "It's the same mechanism as the efficiency correction — understanding the actual mechanism rather than the tradition's explanation for it." He paused. "It will take months. Probably six to eight, based on Chen Yufei's timeline."

Lady Shen looked at Chen Yufei.

Chen Yufei said, with the plainness of someone stating a fact: "He's correct about the timeline. And the process is worth it."

Another silence.

"I would need to understand," Lady Shen said carefully, "what you expect from me."

"Nothing," he said. "Or: the same thing I told Elder Zhao. Honest information. Advance notice if your Court plans something that affects me. And—" he paused — "I'd like to understand what you've told the Courts about the Strata. Not to control the information. To understand what they know."

She looked at him.

Then, unexpectedly: "I told them very little."

"I know. You protected your access."

"Yes." She said this without embarrassment. "I'm a practical person."

"So am I," he said.

She looked around the archive one more time. Her Qi field, at the low-resolution Colony Link reading, had changed quality since she'd first sat down: the *braced-for-leverage* quality was gone. What remained was more complex, with a specific layer he read as something like *unexpected relief* layered under *extensive recalculation.*

She had come here expecting a different kind of conversation.

He had offered her an honest one.

She was deciding, in real time, whether she wanted to take it.

"Send me the document," she said. "I'll read it tonight."

"Yes," he said.

She stood. She looked at the archive one last time.

"Tomorrow," she said, which was not quite a question and not quite a statement.

"I'll be here," he said.

She walked out.

He and Chen Yufei sat at the table for a moment.

"She expected to negotiate," Chen Yufei said.

"I know."

"You didn't give her anything to negotiate over."

"I gave her everything," he said. "There's a difference."

Chen Yufei thought about this. "The document goes out tomorrow."

"Yes. Her copy and the forty-seven go out simultaneously."

She stood, stretched — they had both been in the Strata for several hours. "How do you think they'll react?"

He thought about forty-seven people opening a document that said, with precise sourcing, that the traditions they had dedicated their lives to were built on a real foundation with an incomplete explanation.

"Some will be angry," he said. "Some will be relieved. Some will ask questions. Some will go quiet for a long time and come back later." He paused. "The ones who ask questions are the ones I'm hoping for."

"And Lady Shen?"

He thought about her field — the *extensive recalculation*, the relief.

"She's going to be one of the ones who asks questions," he said.

"How do you know?"

"Because she came here on her own time," he said, "when she had institutional permission not to."

Chen Yufei looked at him.

Then she picked up her bag.

"Let's go home," she said. "Tomorrow is going to be complicated."

It was.
