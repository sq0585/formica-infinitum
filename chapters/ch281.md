# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Eighty-One — The Anchor Consideration

He brought the question to Chen Yufei first, the way he'd brought every significant decision to her since the night they'd finally stopped not-saying what both of them had known for months.

"I'm considering asking Dr. Shen Yue to be an Anchor," he said. "Eventually. Not immediately — she's only just crossed the threshold, and I don't want to rush something this structurally significant before she's had time to understand what she'd actually be agreeing to."

Chen Yufei considered this with the particular careful attention she brought to anything involving the Colony Link's deeper architecture, the structural weight she herself had chosen to carry now woven permanently into how she thought about every subsequent designation.

"She'll say yes if you ask her correctly," Chen Yufei said, with a certainty that surprised him slightly given how briefly she'd known the physicist.

"What makes you confident of that?"

"I've watched two sessions of her work, secondhand through what you've described, and one afternoon of her actually crossing the threshold and immediately reaching for a notebook instead of simply standing there in shock. She's the type who would. Someone who treats extraordinary access as an obligation to engage seriously with, not a spectacle to simply enjoy."

"Is that a recommendation?"

"It's an observation," Chen Yufei said, something dry entering her voice. "Also yes. I think she'd be a good Anchor, if you decide the timing is right. I think her particular kind of rigor would bring something useful to the structure — Liang Jingzhi's clear, practical reasoning, my accumulated trust, and now potentially Shen Yue's relentless, careful precision. Three very different foundations, all genuinely solid."

He sat with this, the particular comfort of having Chen Yufei's clear-eyed assessment to weigh against his own instinct.

"I don't want to rush it," he said again. "I want her to have proper time with this world before I ask her to carry structural weight inside it."

"That's the right instinct," Chen Yufei agreed. "But I don't think you need to second-guess the eventual yes. I think you already know what her answer will be, the same way you knew with me, the same way you knew with Liang Jingzhi. You're not actually asking me whether she'll agree. You're asking me whether you should ask at all."

He considered this, recognizing the accuracy of it with the particular discomfort of being read this clearly.

"Should I?" he asked.

"I think so," Chen Yufei said. "I've watched what carrying this alone, without enough Anchors, cost you during the anchor demolition — fourteen hours, a six-hour dormancy, a body that simply ran out before your judgment did. I think the more carefully chosen, genuinely committed people holding part of your continuity, the safer you actually are. Not just structurally. Practically."

He thought about the dormancy, the strange, ordinary quiet of sitting on the cold ground with nothing but his own unaugmented eyes, the particular fear he'd carried for Chen Yufei working above him while he could do nothing to help.

"I'll give it time," he said. "A few more weeks, at minimum, before I bring it up with her directly. I want her relationship to this world to develop on its own terms first, not shaped from the beginning by the prospect of a structural designation."

"That's wise," Chen Yufei said. "And for what it's worth — I think you're handling this exactly the way it should be handled. Careful. Consultative. Never assuming the answer before you've actually asked the question properly."

He nodded, the weight of the consideration settling into something he could carry patiently now, rather than urgently — one more careful, deliberate piece of the slow, accumulating architecture he'd been building, person by person, choice by choice, across two years of learning exactly how much this world could ask of him and exactly how much he was willing to ask of the people who chose, again and again, to stand inside it with him.
