# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-Two — The Strata, Reconstruction Begins

He started with the outer library, because it was the section he understood best and the section most people actually used.

Two collapsed alcoves near the eastern wall had sat untouched since before he'd first crossed the threshold, their dimensional architecture sagged and partially folded in on itself the way a damaged tent sags when a pole has cracked. He'd avoided them out of caution more than disinterest — a collapsed fold was, as far as he understood the mechanics, not simply empty space but space that had failed in some specific, possibly hazardous way, and he hadn't trusted his own skill to work near the failure without risking a wider collapse.

Dimensional Weaver stage changed that calculation.

He spent the better part of a week studying the collapsed sections before touching them, mapping the failure pattern the way he'd once mapped unfamiliar notation — methodically, without assuming he understood the shape of the problem until the evidence actually supported the assumption. The collapse, once he understood it, wasn't damage so much as neglect: anchor points that had drifted slowly out of alignment over centuries of inattentive maintenance, the architecture sagging not from any single catastrophic failure but from the patient accumulated weight of nobody correcting small errors before they compounded.

He began the actual work on a Tuesday.

Rebuilding a sagged fold required something closer to surgery than construction — identifying which anchor points had drifted, calculating the correction each one needed, applying the adjustment slowly enough that the surrounding architecture could settle into the new geometry rather than fracturing under sudden change. He worked for six hours that first day and recovered perhaps a third of the smaller alcove's original volume.

Meen-4 observed without comment.

She had taken, over the past week, to positioning herself at the edge of his working radius during the reconstruction — not interfering, not offering guidance, simply present in the way she was present for most things that interested her, her attention precise and unreadable. He'd grown accustomed enough to her silences that he no longer found them unsettling. He'd even, somewhere in the past months, started to find them companionable.

She broke the silence on the seventh day, after watching him complete the smaller alcove's full restoration.

*Your anchor placement is slightly wrong on the eastern wall,* she signaled.

He stopped, surprised less by the correction than by the fact that she'd offered it unprompted. Most of what she'd given him over the past year had been theory — Archive access, translated context, the occasional pointed question. This was the first time she'd commented directly on his construction technique.

"Slightly wrong how?"

*The anchor's load distribution assumes uniform density in the surrounding rock. The eastern wall's density is not uniform — there is a mineral seam running diagonally through the section you have not accounted for. The fold will hold. It will require more maintenance than necessary if the seam is ignored.*

He checked the notation he'd built for the eastern anchor and found, once he looked specifically for it, the exact discrepancy she'd described.

"You've been watching the whole time."

*I watch most things that occur within my territory.* A pause, the particular cadence he'd learned to associate with something close to amusement. *You have been doing acceptable work. I would not have corrected an error that did not matter.*

It was, he understood slowly, watching her turn back toward the inner sections without further comment, the closest thing to a compliment she had ever offered him.

He corrected the anchor placement before he went home that evening, adjusting for the mineral seam the way she'd indicated, and found, when he tested the fold's stability afterward, that it held with noticeably less effort than the version he'd built without her correction.

He wrote it in his notebook simply, without elaboration, the kind of fact that didn't need more weight added to it than it already carried on its own.

> *Meen-4 corrected my anchor placement today. First unsolicited construction guidance she's offered. I think that means something. I'm choosing not to overanalyze what.*
