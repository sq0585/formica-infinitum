# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Forty-Three — The Biochemist's Email

He wrote the email on a Tuesday afternoon, which gave him something to do while waiting for channel seven to stabilize before attempting eight.

The Standard Protocol specified a minimum settling period between channel openings — the rhythm markers indicated something translating as *allow integration before proceeding*, a phrase that had the tone of a warning. He had been pressing the pace, opening channels faster than the original schedule, and the settling period was the one instruction he was not going to compress further. Six channels in parallel needed to run stably before a seventh could be reliably added. He had learned this the hard way the previous week when a premature attempt at channel seven had produced an hour of headache and no channel.

So: Tuesday afternoon, waiting, and the biochemist's paper open on his screen.

---

The lead author was a Dr. Huang Wei at a biochemistry department in Wuhan, which the university website described as focusing on quantum biology and neural architecture. She had three papers on quantum coherence in biological systems, two grants for the same, and the standard academic photo of someone who had been too busy doing the research to arrange a good photo.

He thought about what to write for a long time.

The problem was that the question he wanted to ask required context he couldn't provide. He wanted to ask: *if quantum coherence in neural microtubules can sustain near-lossless energy transmission along specific pathways, is there a mechanism by which a practitioner could deliberately extend or expand those pathways through focused attention and specific breath regulation?*

He could not ask this as written without sounding either medically concerning or plainly strange.

He needed to ask the underlying physics question, stripped of the context that made it specifically relevant to him.

He wrote:

---

*Dear Dr. Huang,*

*I read your 2019 paper on coherent proton tunneling in neural microtubule networks with interest. I have a theoretical question that may be outside the paper's scope, but I haven't found it addressed elsewhere.*

*If the coherent transmission pathways you describe are partially dependent on protein alignment in the microtubule structure, is there a mechanism by which sustained alignment-stabilizing inputs — whether biochemical, physical, or otherwise — could progressively extend the coherent zone? In other words: can the pathways be trained longer, and if so, what maintains the extended coherence against thermal decoherence?*

*I'm approaching this from a theoretical biology angle, not a clinical one. Happy to give more context if helpful.*

*Best,*
*Lin M.*

---

He read it back. He sent it.

He had no particular expectation of a reply. Academic email at this level of obliqueness got filed under *interesting but no bandwidth* approximately ninety percent of the time. What the email had done was force him to articulate the theory with precision, which was useful regardless of whether anyone else read it.

He printed the email out and filed it in the biological substrate notebook.

He wrote in the margin: *The question is: can the pathways be trained longer. The answer, based on my own experience, appears to be: yes. The mechanism: I don't know. The Codex Standard Protocol describes the practice without explaining the biochemistry. I need both explanations to build a complete framework.*

He paused. Added: *Writing this email showed me I have a complete enough model to ask a precise question of someone outside my framework. That means the model has reached a certain level of maturity.*

He looked at this last note for a moment.

Then he turned back to the settling period and waited for channel seven.

The email was never answered.

The question it contained remained the most important question he was sitting with.

He would find the answer through other means.
