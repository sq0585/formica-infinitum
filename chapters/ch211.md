# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Eleven — Teaching Wei Canglan

Wei Canglan found him in the outer hall on a Thursday evening, with the particular hesitation of someone who'd rehearsed a request and was now discovering the rehearsal hadn't covered every possible way it could go wrong.

"I want you to teach me," he said. "The corrected framework. Not whatever Iron Meridian or Northern Peak taught me — the actual version. What it's supposed to be underneath."

Lin Mushi set down the notebook he'd been working from. "Why now?"

"Because I'm tired of practicing techniques I don't understand the reasons for." Wei Canglan's jaw tightened slightly, an old habit Lin Mushi had noticed before, the kind of tell that showed up whenever the younger man was admitting something that cost him to say out loud. "I left everything to be here. I'd at least like to know what I'm building instead of it, instead of just copying whatever you do and hoping it works the way it works for you."

It was, Lin Mushi thought, a more honest request than most people managed to make on their first attempt.

"I'll teach you the framework," he said. "Not the techniques. Not directly."

"What's the difference?"

"The framework is why something works — the actual mechanics underneath, the Veritonal interface geometry, the reasons your old meditation produces the effect it produces and where it wastes effort doing it. The techniques are what you build once you understand the framework well enough to build something specific to your own body and your own needs." He paused, choosing the next part carefully. "I could hand you my techniques directly. They'd probably even work, approximately, the way Chen Yufei's old Iron Meridian forms worked approximately. But you'd be in the same position you're in now — running something you didn't build and don't fully understand, just with my name on it instead of the Court's."

Wei Canglan considered this, and Lin Mushi watched the calculation cross his face — the appeal of a faster path weighed against the cost of repeating the exact dependency he'd just walked away from.

"Learn why it works," Lin Mushi said. "Then develop the techniques yourself."

"That's a harder way."

"Yes."

"You're not going to make it easier even though you could."

"No," Lin Mushi said. "Because the easier version produces someone who can copy what I've already done. The harder version produces someone who can do things I haven't thought of yet. I'd rather have that, even if it costs both of us more time."

Wei Canglan was quiet for a moment, and something in his posture shifted — not relaxing exactly, but settling into a decision he'd apparently expected to have to argue for and now didn't need to.

"Fine," he said. "Where do we start?"

"With the actual Veritonal interface geometry. Not the meditation framework Iron Meridian taught you to visualize — the real architecture underneath it, the one your old technique was approximating without knowing what it was approximating." He pulled a blank notebook from the shelf and slid it across the table. "You'll want to take your own notes. I'm not going to give you mine. Building the understanding in your own hand matters more than having access to a clean copy of someone else's."

Wei Canglan picked up the notebook, opened to the first blank page, and looked up with an expression Lin Mushi recognized — the particular mixture of frustration and gratitude that seemed to define most of his relationship to this entire arrangement.

"You make everything harder than it needs to be," Wei Canglan said.

"I make everything as hard as it actually is," Lin Mushi said. "The version that looks easier just hides the difficulty somewhere you'll find it later, usually at a worse time."

Wei Canglan didn't argue with that. He pulled a pen from his jacket, settled into the chair across the table, and began, for the first time since he'd lost everything to be standing in this room, to actually build something of his own.
