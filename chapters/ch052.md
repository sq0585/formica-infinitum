# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifty-Two — The Forum Post Revisited

He had forgotten about the forum post.

Not entirely — he had saved the URL in his notebook, noted *ShenyueLab: anomalies in unpublished data, saved images* — but the note had been filed and not retrieved, because the priority had been immediate and the forum post had felt like something that might matter later. Later had arrived without announcement.

He had checked the thread once, in the first week after posting, found only the dismissive reply, and stopped looking. He had told himself he would return when he had something useful to say. He had not returned.

He returned to the thread on a Sunday afternoon, approximately six weeks after he'd originally posted.

---

The thread had four replies.

The first was dismissive: *probably a rendering artifact in whatever app generated this.* He had expected this and moved past it.

The second was *ShenyueLab*'s original reply: *Structure resembles anomalies in unpublished data I've been working with. Saving your images.* Still eleven words. No follow-up.

The third and fourth were also *ShenyueLab*, posted on consecutive days two weeks after the first reply:

*The hexagonal geometry matches the angular power spectrum anomalies we've been seeing in FAST telescope dark energy mapping at 1.4 GHz. Do you have higher-resolution images? And what software generated this?*

Then, the next day: *The notation structure is different from anything in standard mathematical physics but the spatial relationship between nodes follows a pattern I recognize from the anomalous variance in our dark energy density maps. Specifically the non-uniform distribution at what should be homogeneous scales. I'd like to discuss this if you're willing.*

Lin Mushi read both replies twice.

Then he looked at the posting date on the second one. It had been sitting there for four weeks, unanswered. Because he had stopped checking the thread after the first week, assuming silence. He had not been silent — he had simply not known there was a reply to respond to.

The distinction felt thin. The effect was the same.

*ShenyueLab* was using the FAST telescope. China's Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope, in Guizhou, the largest radio telescope in the world. The user had a dataset from it on dark energy. The dataset had anomalies. The anomalies matched the notation geometry.

He thought about his hypothesis from chapter twenty-three: *Veritonal Energy and dark energy are the same phenomenon, observed from different methodological positions.*

He thought about what it meant that a researcher with FAST telescope data was seeing the same patterns he was seeing in a Formican interface app on his phone.

He did not reply.

He was not ready. The connection was real and the implication was significant and he did not yet know enough about either the Codex system or the implications of bringing someone else into contact with it to make a responsible decision about replying. Bringing a physicist who had no context into contact with a phenomenon they weren't equipped to process — that was not an introduction, that was an ambush.

He saved the thread. He noted the replies.

> *ShenyueLab: FAST telescope, dark energy mapping, anomalies at homogeneous scales matching Codex notation geometry. 2nd and 3rd replies waiting four weeks — I did not see them because I stopped checking. They are actively researching something that appears to be the same phenomenon from the other side.*
>
> *Do not reply yet. Reason: I cannot responsibly bring an outside researcher into contact with this until I understand the full scope of what 'this' is.*
>
> *But this person matters. They are seeing the structure from the physics side. They may be the bridge I need when I'm ready to build it.*
>
> *I know about these replies now. The decision not to reply is a choice I am making, not an oversight. I am choosing to wait until I have something accurate and responsible to say. That is different from forgetting.*
>
> *Save. Return when ready.*

He closed the tab.

He thought about the researcher on the other end of those replies — whoever was behind *ShenyueLab*, sitting with a dataset of FAST telescope anomalies that matched a pattern they'd found in a forum post from a stranger, having asked their questions and received only silence.

He thought: they deserve an answer. When I have one worth giving.

He put the phone down. He opened the geological survey notebook.

He had a contract site assessment at 9 AM.

The forum post could wait. But he knew, now, that it was waiting.
