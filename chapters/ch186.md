# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty-Six — The Confrontation

She was standing in the middle of the outer hall when he arrived, looking at the shelves with the particular intensity of someone who had spent three weeks building toward exactly this access and was now taking the full measure of what it had cost her to get here.

She turned when she felt him approach.

"You've reached something close to Ratio 3," he said, reading the jagged quality of her field even at this short distance. "That's not stable capability. That's forced."

"It is sufficient for tonight's purpose," she said, and he understood, from the slight flatness underneath her usual controlled tone, that she knew exactly what it was costing her to hold it and had decided the cost was acceptable. "I assume you know about the Council's override."

"I do."

"Then you understand why I'm here on different terms than my previous visits." Her voice was controlled, formal, carrying a quality he hadn't heard from her in months — the institutional register she'd used on her very first visit, before the months of genuine engagement had softened it. "The Northern Peak Court maintains that the Archive falls under collective Court jurisdiction, under an agreement dating back fourteen hundred years, establishing shared authority over any site of significant cultivation resource discovered within the historical territory the five Courts collectively recognize as their domain."

He held her gaze.

"The Archive predates your agreement by millions of years," he said. "A document signed fourteen hundred years ago by five human institutions cannot retroactively claim jurisdiction over a Formican site that existed before any of those institutions had names."

"That is a philosophical objection," she said. "It is not how institutional law functions. Precedent and continuous practice establish jurisdiction regardless of the underlying site's age. The Courts have, by their own internal records, considered any site of this nature to fall under collective agreement since the agreement's founding."

"I'm not arguing institutional law," he said. "I'm telling you the actual authority structure here doesn't answer to institutional law, yours or anyone's. The archive's authority is Formican. You met that authority once already, several months ago. You retreated."

"I retreated from a confrontation I was not prepared for at the time," Lady Shen said. "I am prepared now."

He felt the shift in her Qi field — the close-range Colony Link reading carrying something he recognized immediately as combat preparation, the specific quality of someone settling into a stance rather than continuing a conversation.

"Lady Shen," he said. "I don't think this is actually what you want. I think you're acting on the Council's override, against your own better judgment, because the institutional position you've held for thirty years requires you to act on its behalf even when you privately know better."

For just a moment, something flickered in her field — recognition, maybe, of exactly how accurately he'd named what she was doing.

It didn't change anything.

"Whatever I privately believe," she said, "I have an institutional obligation, and the Council has determined the path forward. I am not here to negotiate the philosophy. I am here to establish, through demonstrated capability, that the Archive's resources are not solely under your control."

"They were never under my control," he said. "That's the entire point I've been trying to make for fifteen months."

"Then you will not object to my establishing equal access," she said, "by whatever method proves necessary."

He understood, in that moment, that the conversation had effectively ended several sentences ago, and that what remained was no longer a negotiation.

"I'd prefer this didn't become a fight," he said.

"So would I," she said. "I don't believe that's currently a choice either of us has."

She moved.
