# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Seventy-Five — What the Strata Shows Now

He went back into the Strata on a Saturday, two days after the tea shop meeting with Chen Yufei.

The meeting had not resolved anything. It had begun something — she had agreed to a follow-up, and she had taken photographs of the notebook pages she found most immediately relevant, and she had not reported his existence to the Iron Meridian Court, which he took as provisional evidence that she was operating with some degree of independent judgment. But she was still a Court member, and the Court had institutional interests that didn't align with his, and the arrangement was fragile in ways he could not yet fully assess.

He needed what was in the Strata before the surface situation became acute. He needed the presence to the north.

He entered through the threshold at 2:30 AM and stood at the edge of the illuminated hall and looked north.

---

The hall was different.

He had been inside it nine times now and knew its quality at Ratio 1 comprehensively. Tonight, with Ratio 2 stable — he had been maintaining it for thirty-second stretches as practice, extending the duration gradually — he opened the compression and looked at the hall at the finer scale.

The material of the walls resolved into its actual structure: not stone, not metal, not any composite he recognized from surface geology. A matrix of crystalline elements in a protein-analog substrate — the same hexagonal geometry at the molecular scale that the surface showed at the architectural scale, the same system operating at every resolution. The walls were maintaining themselves. The material had active processes running through it, slow but real, something like biological metabolism at the mineral scale.

The Strata was alive. Not sentient — he didn't have evidence for that. But not merely structural either. The maintenance was ongoing.

And to the north, at the edge of his sixty-meter perception range, the living presence he'd been tracking across nine visits had moved.

It was closer.

---

He walked north for the first time.

The floor lit under his feet in the ten-second lag pattern he'd come to expect. The columns passed him at their regular intervals, each one a load-bearing element in the vault's geometry. The ambient Qi field was richer fifty meters in than at the threshold — the heart of the network closer, the density increasing.

At ninety meters from the threshold, he stopped.

The presence was twenty meters ahead.

He could see now what it was.

Not easily — it was at the intersection of two columns, in the shadow between the vault's illuminated sections — but he had Ratio 2 active and at Ratio 2, the ambient Qi signature was clear enough to read structure rather than just location.

Formican.

Not human. The metabolic signature, the Qi field structure, the biological architecture — all of it was consistent with the records in the Codex's biographical sections, which included physical descriptions of the Amber Lattice School's practitioners. Smaller than a human, proportionally different in mass distribution, with a Qi field that moved through its biology in a pattern that was related to but distinct from the channel system he'd developed.

An ant-person, if he was going to use that phrasing, which felt simultaneously accurate and insufficient.

It had been in this hall.

It had been waiting.

It was looking at him now.

He stood very still.

Then he did the only thing he could think of that was appropriate: he opened his notebook and he wrote. Not about it specifically — he wrote what he was there to write. Observations, questions, the note about the wall material he'd been building toward.

He wrote because that was what he was, and what you were was the first thing you showed.

At the edge of his perception, the presence took a step toward him.

Then another.

Something was about to begin.
