# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Fifty-Six — Elder Kuo's Request

The formal request came through Chen Yufei rather than Kuo directly, which was itself information: the Northern Peak Court was choosing to route its diplomatic overture through someone who had functioned, for months now, as a recognized bridge between Lin Mushi and the cultivation world.

She brought it to him on a Wednesday, a formal document with the Northern Peak Court's seal, which she read aloud rather than handing over, because — she explained — Elder Kuo had specifically asked that it be communicated in person, not simply delivered as a piece of paper.

"The Council requests an audience with the acknowledged Carrier," she read, "not as an investigation, not as a negotiation for access, but as a formal introductory meeting between the Northern Peak Court's leadership and the individual whose work has materially affected the Court's practice and standing." She looked up. "Kuo wrote that himself, I think. It doesn't read like Council prose."

"What do they want from the meeting specifically?"

"Nothing specified beyond the meeting itself. Kuo told me separately: it's an overture. The Council wants to establish that contact is possible on a respectful basis, ahead of whatever the eventual formal delegation structure becomes."

He thought about this.

"Time and place?"

"Yours to choose. That was explicit in the request."

He considered this for a moment, then said: "The tea house on Wulin Street. The one with the planter circuit."

"You want to monitor the meeting."

"I want to be in a position where I have information they don't have to ask for. Not as leverage — as preparation. If the meeting goes somewhere unexpected, I'd rather have context than be caught flat."

Chen Yufei nodded. "When?"

"Next week. Give me a few days to think about what I actually want to say, beyond simply receiving them."

She made the arrangement. The meeting was set for the following Tuesday, mid-afternoon, at the tea house.

---

He spent the intervening days thinking carefully about what an "introductory meeting" with the institution that had begun this entire situation — Elder Kuo's original investigation, nearly a year ago now — should actually accomplish.

He talked it through with Chen Yufei twice. He asked Axis-7 what the archive's position was on formal human-institution contact, separate from the individual relationships he'd already built. He thought about Lady Shen's trajectory — from acquisition mandate to genuine engagement — and what had made that trajectory possible.

What he kept returning to was a simple framework: he was not negotiating on behalf of the archive, because the archive was not his to negotiate for. He was not seeking anything specific from the Northern Peak Court. What he could offer was accurate information about what he was, what the archive was, and what kind of relationship was actually possible — given honestly, without an agenda beyond the agenda of things being correctly understood.

He wrote in his notebook, two nights before the meeting:

> *The meeting with Kuo and the Council leadership: what do I actually want from it?*
>
> *Not recognition. Not protection. Not an agreement.*
>
> *What I want is for the most powerful institution that has been part of this situation to understand, clearly and without distortion, what is actually true. The Strata exists. The Formican community has its own authority. I have a relationship with that community built on honesty, not control. The archive's access terms are not mine to grant or withhold. The corrected technique in the document is accurate and freely shared.*
>
> *If they understand this clearly, whatever they decide to do next will at least be a decision made with accurate information. That's the only thing I can actually contribute to this meeting.*
>
> *Everything else — what they decide, how the Council's internal politics resolve, what the forty-five percent who voted against engagement choose to do — is not mine to determine.*

He read this back.

He thought about Ren Wuji's principle, the one that had been guiding him since the first audio file: understand fully before acting, and when you act, act on what's true rather than what's convenient.

He felt ready for the meeting.

He arrived early on Tuesday, as was his habit, and checked the circuit network's readings.

Clear. Quiet. The tea house in its ordinary mid-afternoon rhythm.

He ordered tea and waited.

Elder Kuo arrived precisely on time, accompanied by two senior Council members Lin Mushi hadn't met before — both older than Kuo, both with the specific bearing of people who had spent decades inside an institution's highest levels.

He stood as they approached.

The meeting began.
