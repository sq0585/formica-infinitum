# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Forty — First Encounter — Strata

Two weeks after Lady Shen's first training session, she arrived in the archive and found it occupied.

Not by Lin Mushi.

He was there, at his usual table. But across the table from where she had sat during the training sessions — not in the visitor's chair, in Axis-7's occasional position — was a Formican he had not seen before.

Not Axis-7. Not the archive worker who had given him the notes. A third individual, larger than both, operating at a Ratio he assessed as somewhere between 2 and 3 — which made them approximately knee-height, standing, with the same chitin-composite exoskeleton as the others but with striations that ran in a different pattern entirely, thicker along the spine and shoulders than along the limbs.

The Formican was looking at Lady Shen.

Lady Shen was looking at the Formican.

Lin Mushi was watching both of them.

He sent in the register, carefully: *She is a surface practitioner. She is here at my invitation, beginning training. She is working in good faith.*

The Formican's response was at the clearest register he'd received from any Formican other than Axis-7: *I know who she is. I know her capability level. I know she was here on a Court mandate before she was here on her own.*

He held this.

*She changed her approach,* he sent. *She disclosed the change. She is working toward access, not appropriation.*

A pause. Then: *We have been watching. The assessment of approach is ongoing.*

The Formican turned its compound eyes from Lady Shen back to him.

*You vouched for her,* it said.

*Yes.*

*The weight of a vouching falls on the voucher,* it said. *You understand this.*

He understood it. *Yes.*

The Formican looked at Lady Shen for another long moment. Then it turned and walked to the library door — not the door back to the main hall, but a door in the north wall that he had noted but not yet approached.

It stopped at the door. It looked back.

It sent: *She may begin with the outer containers. The same tiers as any new visitor.*

Then it went through the door.

Lady Shen was very still for approximately ten seconds.

Then she said, in a voice that was entirely controlled: "What was that?"

"A member of the Layer 3 community. I don't have their designation. They haven't introduced themselves to me directly." He paused. "That's the first time I've seen one come to the library rather than Axis-7 coming to meet me."

She absorbed this.

"They vouched for my access," she said.

"You were given outer-tier access. Equivalent to what any new practitioner gets." He met her gaze. "I vouched for you. If you do something that violates that vouching, the weight falls on me. I understood that when I said yes to helping you."

She was quiet for a moment.

"That's a significant commitment," she said.

"You've been thinking carefully about this for three days," he said. "I made my assessment."

She looked at the door where the Formican had gone.

"The Layer 3 community," she said. "They observe the archive."

"They maintain it. The Keepers are surface-layer staff. The community below is where the authority structure actually sits." He paused. "The network ping Axis-7 sent three weeks ago — it reported to the broader network that this station had an active Carrier. The community in Layer 3 has known everything that's happened here. Their monitoring is constant."

"They knew about the document release."

"Yes."

"And their assessment was?"

He thought about the quality he'd read at Ratio 4 in the Colony Link field two days ago. "Interested. Not alarmed." He paused. "They've been watching this planet's surface civilizations for a very long time. The document is a development, not an emergency."

Lady Shen looked at the shelves. She picked up the container she'd been working with in the previous session — outer-tier material, historical records, accessible without the dimensional lock mechanism.

She opened it.

She began reading.

He watched her read for a few minutes, then returned to his own work.

It was, he noted, becoming a reasonably normal thing: to be in an archive below Chengdu at 2 AM, with a former Northern Peak Court operative reading Formican cultivation history, and a member of the underground Formican community having briefly stopped by to assess the situation and pronounce it acceptable.

Normalcy was relative.

He was comfortable with relative normalcy.
