# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 468 — Veth-Karun's Account — Begun

The document's opening structure resolved faster than he expected, and slower than he wanted, the particular tension of translation work that mattered too much to rush and too much to leave incomplete.

*Timestamp notation,* he identified first, cross-referencing the structure against everything Meen-4 had taught him about Formican temporal recording. *Duration: 0.7 seconds. This entire document was composed in less than a single second.*

He sat with that fact a long moment before proceeding, the specific vertigo of it settling slowly. Whatever followed had not been written with the luxury of time — not decades of careful revision, the way the installation's own crew had built their stellar curriculum, not even the unhurried hours he himself spent shaping a single notebook entry. Less than one second, composed by someone who had known, with absolute certainty, that it was the only second remaining.

*Observation 467: this is not a document written in the aftermath of an event. This is a document written during it — Veth-Karun's own account, composed in the final instant of Formless Scale dissolution, transmitted outward before whatever remained of the original consciousness ceased to exist as a coherent, transmitting entity.*

He worked through the opening lines carefully, the translation resolving word by word, the specific grammar considerably more personal, less formal, than anything he'd encountered in official Formican record-keeping.

And then, three symbols in, he stopped entirely.

He recognized them immediately — the same three symbols he had first encountered years ago, in Arc III's earliest study of Veth-Karun's residual signature, symbols that had resisted every attempt at translation across three subsequent years of careful, patient work. Meen-4 had cross-referenced them against every Formican notation system she possessed. He had tested them against every framework he'd since acquired, at every scale he'd since achieved. They had remained, stubbornly, the single largest unresolved fragment in his entire understanding of Veth-Karun's story.

He checked them now against the Pelagic Archive's own grammar — older, more complete, carrying context the fragmentary residual signature had never offered — and felt something in his chest go very still as the translation, finally, resolved.

*Observation 468,* he wrote, and found his hand entirely unsteady this time, no longer performing the discipline of careful documentation but simply, genuinely, needing somewhere to put the weight of what he'd just read. *The three symbols are not a name, a location, or a technical designation, as I had assumed across three years of failed attempts. They are Veth-Karun's own personal notation — informal, intimate, the kind of shorthand a person develops for a single specific relationship rather than for general use.*

*They translate, as precisely as I can render it, to a single word.*

*Teacher.*

He sat with the translation a long while, the Archive's cold, patient silence settling around him, the Surveyor's presence quiet and formal beside him, offering nothing beyond its steady, unintrusive witness.

Three years. Three years of studying Veth-Karun's dissolution, of building his own technique in the shadow of a failure he'd never fully understood, and the single most persistent mystery in all of it had simply been this — not a technical term, not a location, not anything requiring elaborate translation machinery. A student, in the last 0.7 seconds of their existence, reaching for the word every student eventually reaches for, when everything else has stopped mattering except the person who first taught them to see.

*Query for Meen-4, later,* he wrote, still working to steady his own hand. *Does the Archive's grammar suggest who this teacher was? Is there a name attached anywhere in the surrounding notation, or does the account simply address them the way Veth-Karun addressed them — informally, personally, as "teacher" and nothing more?*

He did not yet know the answer. But he understood, sitting in the ancient chamber with the translation finally, fully resolved, that whatever followed in the account's remaining structure was not going to be a technical record.

It was going to be a letter.

He thought, unbidden, of his own notebook — five years of entries, some of them technical, dispassionate, careful records of Circuit nodes and Veritonal density. But others, scattered throughout, had never really been observations at all. They had been the same thing this document was turning out to be: a person, in an urgent moment, reaching for someone who had shaped them, needing to say something before the chance to say it disappeared.

*Rule,* he wrote, finally, underlining it with more care than the sentence strictly demanded. *Whatever comes next, I read it the way it deserves to be read — not as data to be extracted efficiently, but as the last words of someone who spent 0.7 seconds choosing exactly what mattered enough to say. I owe them that much attention, forty million years late as it is.*

He steadied himself, checked the relay's thin, faithful thread one final time, and turned, carefully, to what Veth-Karun had actually written.
