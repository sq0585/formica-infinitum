# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Fifty-Three — The Defensive Technique

It took six more days, and he finished it the way he finished most things — not with a single triumphant breakthrough, but with the accumulated weight of a great many small corrections finally arranging themselves into something that worked.

He called it the Spatial Anchor Array, the name arriving less from inspiration than from simple accuracy: a Dimensional Weaver technique that, once activated within a defined area, caused any combat technique deployed inside its boundary to collapse into a small, stable pocket rather than reach its intended target.

Not a wall. Not a deflection. The incoming force simply found itself folded into a contained space, harmlessly, the way water poured into a bottle stops being a flood and becomes, instead, a bounded, manageable thing.

He tested it first on himself, in the pocket dimension laboratory, throwing his own modest Compression-level techniques at a target within the Array's boundary and watching each one collapse into a small, glowing pocket the size of a fist, the technique's energy contained and slowly dissipating rather than ever reaching anything it might have harmed.

It worked.

He tested it against Chen Yufei's significantly stronger Compression-stage techniques next, his stomach tight with the genuine uncertainty of whether the principle scaled — and watched, with the particular relief that arrived whenever careful work finally proved itself sound, the Array hold against her full strength exactly as cleanly as it had held against his own.

"It's purely defensive," he told her afterward, both of them studying the small, contained pockets still slowly dissipating where her attacks had landed. "It doesn't hurt the person deploying the technique that gets contained. It doesn't hurt anyone standing inside the boundary. It just... stops things from connecting."

"What's the limitation?"

"It requires my active maintenance. I can't simply set it and walk away — the boundary needs continuous attention, the way Compression needs continuous attention, which means I can protect one location at a time, fully, for as long as I'm physically present and conscious to maintain it."

"That's a real limitation."

"It is. But it's a limitation I can work with, and work around, in ways that mattering more than the alternative — which was nothing, or worse, the crude improvisation that broke someone's arm because it wasn't precise enough to do better."

He deployed it for the first time three days later, around the Collective's next working group meeting, the same kind of modest administrative gathering that had cost Fang Ruxue the use of her left arm for the foreseeable future. He stood at the boundary's edge for the full four hours of the meeting, maintaining the Array's geometry with the same patient, unbroken attention he'd once given an eleven-second pocket dimension fold, and felt, somewhere in the third hour, the particular satisfaction of watching nothing happen at all.

No attack came that day. He hadn't expected one — Gou Ping's faction operated on its own timeline, not his — but the absence of incident didn't diminish what the Array represented.

He'd built an answer to violence that didn't require becoming violent himself.

He wrote it into his notebook that evening, the careful weight of six days' work finally settling into something he could trust.

> *Spatial Anchor Array, functional. Tested against Chen Yufei's full Compression-stage output — held cleanly. Requires my continuous active maintenance, which limits deployment to one protected location at a time. Not a complete solution. A genuine one, as far as it goes.*
>
> *This is my answer to the violence problem. Not strength matched against strength. A structure that simply refuses to let harm land, for as long as I'm able to maintain it. I think that distinction matters more than almost anything else I've built in this entire world.*

He closed the notebook, the day's quiet, uneventful vigilance settling into something that felt, for the first time in nearly two weeks, like genuine progress rather than reaction to someone else's escalating violence.

It wouldn't be enough on its own. He understood that clearly, even sitting with the relief of a working technique finally in hand. But it was a real answer, built carefully, exactly the kind he'd been determined to find since the night a young scholar's arm had broken in a room that should never have needed defending at all.
