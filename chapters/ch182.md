# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Eighty-Two — The Assembly

He called the first assembly two weeks after his conversation with Axis-7, the Amber Lattice School precedent having had time to settle into something closer to a plan.

Not a summons — an invitation, sent carefully through Chen Yufei's network to everyone who had engaged with the documents in good faith over the past several months: Wei Jianming, the Iron Meridian practitioner who'd asked the first careful question; the Verdant Stream elder who'd said he'd suspected this for thirty years; Lin Wenqing, whose institutional break had cracked Lady Shen's coalition; three of the Iron Meridian practitioners who had quietly verified the document and were now bearing a quiet institutional cost for it; Wei Canglan; and Elder Zhao. Chen Yufei would be there as well, by default rather than invitation — this was, after all, as much her project as his.

Twelve people in total accepted and made the journey to the thin section's parking structure entry point on the appointed evening.

He met them at the surface and walked them through, one at a time where necessary, helping those whose Compression capability wasn't yet sufficient with a temporary assist he'd developed for exactly this purpose — a Chitin Circuit configuration that could briefly lower the effective threshold for someone accompanied by a practitioner who'd already crossed.

They gathered in the outer hall, twelve people in total — drawn from five different traditions, Wei Canglan and Chen Yufei among them — standing in a space that, by any reasonable accounting, should not have existed and certainly should not have contained this particular gathering of people who, eight months earlier, would have had no occasion to ever stand in the same room.

He watched their reactions — the specific stillness of people encountering scale they hadn't been prepared for, the same reaction he'd had fifteen months ago, the same reaction Chen Yufei had had, the same quieter version Wei Canglan had shown.

He let them have the moment before he spoke.

"Thank you for coming," he said, when the initial absorption had settled enough for words to land. "I didn't call this assembly to propose anything specific yet. I wanted, first, for the people who have engaged with this honestly — who asked real questions, who verified rather than simply believed, who paid real costs for choosing truth over comfortable institutional position — to actually meet each other. To know you're not alone in this."

Wei Jianming looked around the hall slowly. "There are others like us," he said. Not quite a question.

"There will be more," Lin Mushi said. "This is twelve. I expect, over time, there will be considerably more than twelve."

"What happens now?" the Verdant Stream elder asked. "What structure are you proposing?"

"I don't have a complete answer," Lin Mushi said honestly. "I have a starting point, which an old archive precedent gave me: a structure built around demonstrated capability and genuine community rather than institutional hierarchy and exclusive belonging. A place where people who want to understand this discipline properly can learn it, regardless of which Court they were born into or whether they were born into any Court at all. Where disputes get settled by appeal to what's actually true, rather than by appeal to whoever holds the most institutional power."

He looked around the room.

"I don't want to build a sixth Court," he said. "I don't think a sixth Court solves the problem the first five Courts have. I want to build something different. I don't fully know its shape yet. I'd like to figure it out with the people who are already here, rather than handing down a structure from above."

The room was quiet for a moment.

Then Elder Zhao spoke, her voice carrying the specific weight of someone who had spent thirty years inside institutional structure and was now considering what came after it.

"Then let's talk," she said. "All of us. Properly. Tonight."

He nodded.

They sat — twelve chairs gathered from wherever they could be found, a loose circle in the outer hall, the amber light holding steady above them.

The conversation lasted five hours.
