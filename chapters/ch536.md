# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 536 — Two Distant Signals

The report reached him through the Witness Node in Meen-4's careful formal register, the specific structure she reserved for updates she considered genuinely significant rather than merely routine.

*Quarantine Zone QZ-3,* the notation read, *approximately forty years behind Earth's developmental trajectory by the review body's own comparative metrics. Quarantine Zone QZ-9, approximately twenty years behind. Both zones have now received Earth's full review documentation, and both have, independently, initiated contact with their own resident Formican communities.*

He sat with the report a long moment, understanding, reading it, the specific quiet weight of what it actually meant.

He had known, distantly, since the Collective's review had first revealed the number to him years ago, that Earth was not alone — one of nine active Quarantine Zone planets, a fact that had once, in a much younger and more overwhelmed version of himself, landed as a kind of vertigo, the sudden understanding that the entire, enormous story he'd been living inside was only ever one chapter of something considerably larger. He had carried that knowledge since, patiently, without ever quite having occasion to see it become real in front of him.

Now it had.

*Observation 536,* he wrote. *Two other worlds are beginning, in their own time, at their own pace, what Earth began six years ago. Neither one is yet fully in the Orthodoxy's sights the way Earth once was — a small mercy, I suspect, born of Earth's own review having gone as well as it did. I do not know these worlds. I will likely never meet the people currently standing where I once stood, uncertain, curious, holding a Codex they did not yet fully understand. But I find, reading this report, that I am glad, in a way that surprises me with its intensity, that they are not standing there entirely alone.*

Meen-4's presence arrived alongside the report, carrying something he had learned, over the years, to recognize as her considered satisfaction — rare, precise, never overstated.

*I have filed something else today,* she said. *A formal, permanent reclassification of the Strata's human-Formican cohabitation arrangement. No longer provisional.*

"After eight thousand residents and two years of an uneventful cohabitation," he said, "I'd have thought that classification changed itself."

*Formican institutional process does not work that way,* she said, something almost like dry humor threading through the formality. *A provisional arrangement remains provisional until someone with sufficient standing formally declares otherwise, regardless of how self-evidently successful it has become in practice. I have now declared otherwise. The Strata is, as of this filing, a permanent, recognized model of cohabitation — the first the Formican civilization has approved in forty million years.*

"What finally convinced you it was ready?"

*Nothing sudden,* she said. *I have been observing the pattern for some time. The humans ask too many questions. This was, in the earliest years, a source of some administrative frustration for me personally — every settled Formican process, questioned, examined, occasionally politely rejected in favor of some human alternative that took considerable explanation to justify.* She paused, and he felt, underneath the formal register, something genuinely warm. *I have concluded, after careful review, that the questions are, on balance, useful. They have made every institution in this city better than it would have been left unquestioned. I did not expect, forty million years into a very long life, to still be capable of revising an opinion this fundamental. I am pleased that I was.*

He held her report and the news of two distant, waking worlds together, side by side, and understood, closing the notebook that evening, that the world he'd helped build was no longer merely surviving.

It was, quietly, patiently, beginning to teach others how to survive it too.

He thought, before he set the notebook down for the night, of the nine worlds the Collective's original review had first revealed to him — Earth one chapter among them, a fact that had once felt like a diminishment and now felt, considered properly, like something closer to companionship. Six worlds still unaccounted for in tonight's report, still somewhere earlier in their own uncertain trajectories, still waiting, whether they yet knew it or not, for whatever version of a night-shift convenience store clerk their own history would eventually hand a Codex to.

He did not know their names. He suspected, in time, he might come to. For now, he simply held the knowledge that they existed, patiently, the way a person holds the knowledge of family members they have never met but feel, nonetheless, some obligation to hope well for — and found, extinguishing the lamp beside his desk, that he slept easier for the holding of it.
