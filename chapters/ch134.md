# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter One Hundred and Thirty-Four — The Council

Elder Kuo called at 4 PM.

Not Chen Yufei. Lin Mushi directly, which meant the number he'd given Kuo at the tea house meeting two months ago had been kept and was now being used.

"The document," Kuo said.

"Yes."

"I have received five messages about it in the past four hours. None of them were from me to my Council." A pause. "Which means it propagated through informal channels faster than I could brief formally."

"Yes," Lin Mushi said. "That was part of the design."

A silence that was Kuo deciding what to do with this honesty.

"The Northern Peak Court's Elder Council is convening an emergency session," Kuo said. "Tomorrow morning. They'll want a formal response."

"What kind of formal response?"

"That depends on what the session decides. At the moment: I cannot predict." Another pause. "I want to tell you something that I am telling you as a practical person to another practical person and not as an institutional representative."

"Yes," Lin Mushi said.

"The document is accurate. I have verified the specific translations I can verify against materials the Northern Peak Court possesses. The derivation analysis is correct." He paused. "That is going to matter for how some members of the Council respond. Others—"

"Will respond to what it means for the Court's authority," Lin Mushi finished.

"Yes." A pause. "The session will be — contested."

"I expected this."

"I know you did. I want you to know that there are people on the Council who will receive the information as information." A pause. "And people who won't. I cannot tell you which group will be larger."

He thought about this.

"Elder Kuo," he said. "What did you tell the Council about the Strata in your investigation report?"

A silence.

"I told them there was an anomaly consistent with a significant underground structure in the Wuhou vicinity. I did not specify what I had inferred about the nature of the structure." A pause. "Lady Shen told them considerably less."

"Why did you protect the information?"

"Because I didn't understand it yet." Another pause. "I try not to brief things I don't understand."

He thought about this. It was an interesting quality in an institutional representative.

"The document says what the Formican system is and how the Courts' techniques relate to it," Lin Mushi said. "It doesn't say anything about the Strata directly. The Strata is a separate conversation."

"Yes. But the document implies the Strata."

"Only to people who know enough to make the inference."

"Your forty-seven were selected to be people who know enough," Kuo said.

"Yes."

A pause. "The Council session tomorrow. I would like to know if you'd be willing to address it. Not in person — in writing. A statement of your position and intentions."

Lin Mushi thought.

He said: "What I can give you is a brief statement of what I'm doing and why. Not a negotiating position. Not an offer or demand. Just an accurate account of what's happening."

"That may be sufficient," Kuo said. "Or it may be insufficient but true, which is also a useful thing."

"I'll send it to you by tonight."

After the call, he sat at his desk for twenty minutes drafting the statement.

He wrote it the same way he wrote everything that mattered: plainly, accurately, without defensiveness or conciliation, saying what was true and what he intended and nothing else.

He read it back.

He sent it.

Then he went downstairs and bought noodles for dinner, the good ones, because tomorrow was going to be complicated and the evening before complicated things should, where possible, be simple.
