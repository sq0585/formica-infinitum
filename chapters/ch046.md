# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Forty-Six — Apartment 4B

The arguments started at midnight and ran until approximately 2 AM.

Not every night. Every other night, roughly, which made them hard to tune out because the pattern created an expectation that was either met or disappointed rather than becoming background. His downstairs neighbour — apartment 4B, directly below his 5B — conducted them with the particular volume of someone who was not shouting but not not-shouting, the voice rising and falling through registers of frustration that came up through the floor with uncomfortable clarity.

Lin Mushi heard the words *you never listen* on a Thursday, clearly enough to parse individual syllables, and understood that the argument was with someone who was not present in the apartment. The volume was not directed at another person in the room. The volume was the argument with something larger than a person in a room.

He had noticed, in the past week, that his emotional Qi sensitivity had extended its range from close contact to approximately three metres. He had not tested it systematically. He was testing it now, involuntarily, through the floor.

---

The man in 4B was desperate.

Not sad — desperate, which was a different quality. He could feel it through the floor the way he felt atmospheric pressure through walls: not a single emotion but a field, a sustained state, with the specific texture of someone who had been carrying too much for too long and was no longer sure the structure would hold.

Lin Mushi lay on his back in his bed and stared at the ceiling and listened and perceived simultaneously and felt, with the particular discomfort of involuntary knowledge, that the person directly below him was in serious distress.

He had not asked for this information. It had arrived because his channels were open and people in distress produced Qi fields that were legible at close range once you had the sensitivity to read them.

The question was what to do with it.

---

He had three options, as he saw it.

First: nothing. The man's distress was not his business. He had not been asked to help. Intervening without invitation in a neighbour's private crisis was a presumption.

Second: knock on the door and ask if the man was alright. This required him to explain why he was asking, which he couldn't do honestly, and a lie felt wrong here.

Third: something that was not intervention and not nothing.

He got up at 2:17 AM and found a piece of paper in his desk drawer and wrote on it in neat characters:

*The noodle shop on the corner has good soup. Open 24 hours.*

He went downstairs in his socks and slid the note under the door of 4B.

He went back upstairs.

He made himself tea. He sat at the desk. He could still feel, faintly, the quality of the field through the floor — the desperation that was also exhaustion that was also something underneath both that he didn't have a word for and didn't need one.

At 2:43 AM he heard the door of 4B open and close.

At 3:10 AM it opened and closed again.

The field through the floor was slightly different when the man came back. Not resolved. Not fixed. But slightly less alone-feeling, which was a real quality distinct from any other.

---

He wrote in the morning:

> *Apartment 4B. Involuntary emotional Qi perception through floor, range ~3 metres. Neighbour in significant distress. Left note under door — practical suggestion, noodle shop, nothing requiring explanation.*
>
> *He went. He came back. The field changed: less acute.*
>
> *This is not a cultivation matter. This is just a person downstairs.*
>
> *But the channels made it legible. The channels put me in a position of knowing something I didn't ask to know and had to decide what to do with.*
>
> *This is the Colony Link dimension. Not just perceiving the Under-Web — perceiving people. The distributed field of human emotional state, visible at close range once the sensitivity exists.*
>
> *Question I haven't answered: what is the ethical position of a person who can perceive another person's emotional state without consent? I didn't ask for this. He didn't consent to it. I used the information.*
>
> *Used it for a small kindness. But the structural question remains.*
>
> *File. Return when I have more to think it through with.*

The man in 4B did not come to thank him. He did not expect this. He had not left the note to be thanked.

He had left it because the information arrived and he was not the kind of person who could have the information and do nothing with it.

He needed to decide, going forward, what kind of person that made him.

He turned to channel ten.
