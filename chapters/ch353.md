# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifty-Three — The Visit

He arrived in Leshan on a focused-attention protocol Meen-4 had helped him design specifically for this trip — minimum ambient Colony Link, the vast planetary field reduced to the smallest sliver of awareness he could sustain without risking the coherence the Anchor Protocol depended on, everything else deliberately quieted so that three days could belong, entirely, to his mother.

She met him at the small bus station with the same unhurried warmth she always had, taking his bag before he could protest, already narrating the week's small domestic developments before they'd even reached the car.

"The neighbor's dispute is finally settled," she said. "Properly settled, not just quiet. She admitted the fence line was wrong all along. Took her three months to admit it, but she got there."

"That's a victory."

"It's a small victory. I've learned to take the small ones seriously. They add up." She glanced at him sideways, the same practical assessment she always ran on him the moment they were face to face rather than separated by a phone line. "You look better than you sounded on the phone last week."

"I feel better. Three days without half my attention somewhere else does that."

---

They spent the first day cooking, the specific unhurried ritual that had become, across two years, one of the few constants his impossible life had never once disrupted. She taught him a new dish, patient with his mistakes in the way she'd always been patient, correcting his knife work without making him feel corrected, the kitchen filling with the familiar, grounding smell of a meal built slowly rather than efficiently.

"You're better at this than you used to be," she observed, watching him work through the vegetables with more confidence than he'd shown even a year earlier.

"I've had practice. Chen Yufei and I cook together most evenings, when we can manage it."

"Good. A person should know how to feed themselves and the people they love. Everything else is negotiable. That isn't."

He smiled at the specific, unshakeable certainty in her voice, the particular quality of wisdom that had nothing to do with cultivation or cosmological review clocks and everything to do with a lifetime spent understanding what actually mattered.

---

The second day, they worked in her garden, and it was there, kneeling beside her in the warm afternoon light, his hands in the same soil she'd tended for longer than he could easily remember, that he first noticed something had changed.

He didn't say anything immediately. He simply worked alongside her, pulling weeds, turning soil, letting his attention rest on the small, ordinary satisfaction of physical labor that asked nothing of his cultivation, nothing of the review clock, nothing beyond the simple, present fact of dirt under his fingernails and his mother's steady presence beside him.

"You've gotten quiet," she said, eventually, not looking up from her own work.

"Just tired. Good tired. The kind that comes from doing something with your hands instead of your head, for once."

"I like having you here," she said, simply, without elaboration, the specific plainness of the statement carrying more weight than anything more elaborate could have managed. "You don't visit as often as I'd like. I understand why — whatever this life you've built actually is, I know it takes most of you most days. But I like having you here, in the dirt, being just my son for a few days instead of whatever you are the rest of the time."

He felt something settle in his chest at that, the specific ache of being loved simply, uncomplicatedly, by someone who had no idea what "the rest of the time" actually meant, and had never once demanded to know.

"I like being here too," he said. "More than I think I let myself remember, in between visits."

---

The third day passed too quickly, the way the good days always did, and he found himself, on the bus back toward the city, deliberately holding onto the specific, grounded texture of the visit rather than letting his attention slide immediately back toward everything waiting for him.

He wrote about it that evening, once he'd returned, the entry unhurried in a way his recent entries hadn't often managed to be.

> *Three days in Leshan. No review clock, no Colony Link beyond the smallest sliver I could manage. Cooking, gardening, my mother's steady, uncomplicated love asking nothing of me except that I show up and let myself be fed. I noticed something in the garden today — I don't fully understand what yet. I want to look more closely before I write about it. For now, I just want to hold onto how it felt to be, for three whole days, simply someone's son.*
