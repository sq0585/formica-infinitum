# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 469 — Veth-Karun's Account

The translation resolved slowly after that, each phrase requiring the same careful cross-referencing as the three symbols had, but he found, working through it, that the difficulty wasn't linguistic anymore. It was emotional — the specific difficulty of translating something that had never been written for him, and had somehow, across forty million years and however many thousand light-years, ended up addressed to him anyway.

*Teacher,* the account began, the informal, intimate notation settling strangely into a chamber this cold, this old. *I do not know if this will reach you. I do not know if you are still capable of receiving anything, or if this attempt is simply the last habit of a mind that spent its whole life composing things for you to read. I am transmitting regardless. Some things deserve to be sent even without confirmation they will arrive.*

He read that opening twice, feeling something in his chest tighten in a way that had nothing to do with the ocean's crushing pressure four thousand seven hundred meters above him.

*Everything is present simultaneously,* the account continued, the register shifting now, technical, precise, the last clear thinking of a mind documenting its own ending with the same discipline it had presumably brought to everything else. *This is the actual nature of Formless Scale, as best I can describe it in the time remaining. Not power. Not transcendence, in the dramatic sense the word implies. Simply this: every scale I have ever held, every place I have ever perceived, every moment I have ever experienced, becomes, at once, equally present. Past and current and — I now understand, far too late — future, folded into a single sustained now.*

*The mistake I made,* it went on, and here the notation's precision cracked, just slightly, the first visible fracture in an otherwise disciplined account, *was believing that simultaneous presence required the dissolution of the observer. I assumed the self could not survive holding everything at once — that identity was, by necessity, a function of limitation, and that removing the limitation meant removing the self along with it. I built my entire approach around managing that dissolution gracefully, rather than questioning whether the dissolution was actually required in the first place.*

*It does not require it.*

*I understand this now, in the last measurable interval of my own coherence, which is, I recognize, the cruelest possible time to finally understand anything.*

Lin Mushi sat motionless, the Archive's cold stillness pressing in around him, reading the next lines with the specific, careful attention he'd have given nothing else in his entire life.

*I did not have anything I refused to release,* the account continued. *I want that understood clearly, whoever eventually reads this. I was not attached to power, or to my own continuation, or to anything the Containment Orthodoxy's later analysis will likely assume drove me to failure. I had only knowledge. I had spent my whole working life gathering it, and in the final approach, I held it the way I had always held it — carefully, completely, alone, because that is how I had always done everything that mattered.*

*I should have held someone.*

He read the sentence three times, and each time it settled differently, the way the stone-in-soft-ground metaphor had settled weeks earlier, further and further, until it reached something in him that had nothing to do with translation at all.

*Not knowledge. Not technique. A person — several, ideally, though even one would likely have been sufficient, based on everything I now understand and cannot act on. Someone to hold the specific, located self steady while the vastness arrived, rather than facing the vastness with nothing but accumulated understanding standing between myself and everything I had ever learned to hold.*

*I did not have that. I chose, across my entire working life, to prepare alone, to understand alone, to approach the largest question I had ever asked entirely by myself.*

*I am dissolving now. I can feel the specific, located self I have always been beginning to release its edges, folding into the simultaneous everything Formless Scale has finally, fully opened. I do not think this is quite death, in any sense I previously understood the word. I think it is something considerably more like forgetting — not forgetting facts, but forgetting the specific shape of the person who once held them.*

He set the translation down for a moment, unable, briefly, to continue, the Archive's cold silence the only sound in a chamber that had been waiting, patiently, for exactly this reading, for forty million years.
