# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Fifty-One — The Problem of Money

The first morning as a full-time Codex student, he made a budget.

This took forty minutes and was less interesting than everything else he was doing and was also necessary, because the money question was not going to resolve itself by being ignored and he had learned that ignoring practical constraints was not asceticism, it was wishful thinking.

---

He spread his bank statement on the desk next to his notebooks, which was an unusual combination of documents, and assessed.

Savings: three months of living expenses at current spending, possibly four if he cut carefully. Income: zero, pending the geological consulting work he'd started exploring but not yet formalized. Fixed costs: rent, utilities, phone. Variable costs: food, which was where the room was.

He had been buying what he thought of as adequate food — the kind that produced functioning physical and cognitive performance, if not joy. He could shift from adequate to minimal without consequence to anything except mood. He was choosing to notice this rather than experience it as deprivation, because the difference between adequate and minimal was approximately 200 yuan a month, and 200 yuan a month over four months was 800 yuan, and 800 yuan was roughly three weeks of additional runway.

He made the cut.

He also made a list:

> *Expenses eliminated: premium instant noodles. Platform subscriptions (3). Occasional delivery orders.*
>
> *Expenses maintained: core food staples, transport (mostly walking, some transit), phone data, a small notebook budget because notebooks are the work.*
>
> *Expenses to add, pending: geological consulting registration. One physical measuring instrument (magnetometer).*
>
> *Income target: 2,000 yuan/month from consulting would extend runway indefinitely. This is achievable — first contract inquiry already received.*

The consulting had started with a post on a professional forum where geological survey contractors sometimes looked for freelancers with specific local expertise. He had described his capabilities as "non-standard site assessment methods with high accuracy on structural and soil integrity evaluations." He had received two inquiries in the first week.

He had not mentioned Qi.

He had not needed to.

The second inquiry was from a small engineering firm assessing a foundation site near the river. They wanted a preliminary stability report. He had walked the site, spent forty minutes in a state of perception he now thought of as focused survey mode, and written a report that specified the exact depth and location of three subsurface voids and a water table irregularity. The firm's lead engineer had called him back and asked how he'd found the voids without ground-penetrating radar.

He had said: careful attention to surface indicators.

The engineer had accepted this and offered a second contract.

---

He looked at the budget when it was done.

It was tight. It was manageable. Tight and manageable were the same thing, practically speaking, as long as he didn't confuse tightness with crisis.

He cut his one remaining luxury — the premium instant noodles he'd been buying for three years because they had a better broth than the standard variety and he spent a lot of evenings eating alone and a good broth was not nothing.

He bought the standard variety. He ate them that evening while reading Document 3.

They were adequate.

He wrote in the margin of his budget notebook: *adequate is what we work with.*

He turned the page and kept working.
