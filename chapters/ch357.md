# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Three Hundred and Fifty-Seven — What Communities Do

He found himself returning to the resolved dispute over the following days, turning it over with more attention than its small scale seemed to warrant, until he understood, finally, exactly why it had settled into him so persistently.

He raised it with Meen-4 during one of their now-regular review preparation sessions, the conversation drifting, briefly, away from Collective governance and toward something closer to genuine reflection.

"I keep thinking about the workspace dispute," he said. "It wasn't dramatic. It wasn't even particularly interesting, as disputes go. But I think it might matter more to the review case than almost anything else we've assembled."

*Explain your reasoning,* Meen-4 signaled, her attention sharpening with the specific curiosity she brought to any observation that surprised her.

"Everything else we've built for the case is impressive on purpose," he said slowly, working through the thought as he spoke it. "The Ring repair. Dr. Shen Yue's research. The careful governance framework itself. All of it demonstrates capability, deliberately, because we built it knowing the review was coming."

*And the workspace dispute?*

"The workspace dispute wasn't built for anyone to see. It's not impressive. It's genuinely mundane — two research groups arguing about scheduling, resolved slowly and imperfectly through a process that took two weeks to produce a compromise nobody was fully satisfied with." He met her attention directly. "I think that's exactly what makes it significant. It proves the Strata City isn't a performance. It's not a demonstration built to impress a reviewing body. It's an actual place, with actual ordinary friction, resolved through actual ordinary structure, entirely regardless of whether anyone important was ever going to be watching."

*That is a genuinely valuable insight,* Meen-4 signaled, something in her cadence carrying real consideration. *I believe you are correct. A community capable of handling its own small, unglamorous disputes without requiring extraordinary intervention demonstrates something a carefully curated showcase of impressive achievements cannot. It demonstrates that the achievements are load-bearing — that they support an actual, functioning society, rather than existing merely as evidence assembled for an audience.*

---

He brought the observation to the full preparation group the following week, watching it land with the same weight it had carried for him.

"I want us to include this," he said, laying out the resolved dispute alongside everything else the group had assembled. "Not as a headline achievement. As evidence of something quieter and, I think, more important — that this city functions as a real community, capable of resolving its own ordinary friction, without needing me, or any single authority, to hold it together."

Fang Ruxue considered the proposal carefully, her governance instincts immediately recognizing its value. "I think that's exactly right. I'd go further, actually — I think we should include several examples like this, not just the one. Small disputes, patiently resolved. Ordinary daily governance, functioning quietly. It's less dramatic than the Ring repair, but it might be more persuasive, precisely because it's unglamorous."

"Why more persuasive?" Wei Canglan asked, his careful, methodical attention already working through the reasoning.

"Because anyone can build something impressive for an audience," Fang Ruxue said. "It's considerably harder to fake ordinary, sustained, unglamorous functioning. If the review is genuinely evaluating whether this world's development represents careful, trustworthy judgment, I think the boring, everyday evidence might matter more than any single dramatic achievement."

---

He sat with the group's agreement settling into place, understanding, with a clarity that felt both simple and significant, exactly what this movement's quiet, unglamorous chapters had actually been building toward — not spectacle, not a crisis to manage personally, but proof, patient and cumulative, that the Strata City had become something real enough to survive its own ordinary difficulties.

He wrote about it that evening, the entry carrying the specific satisfaction of a realization finally settling into full clarity.

> *The workspace dispute wasn't a small thing after all. It was proof that this city doesn't need me to be a real place. Fang Ruxue's right — anyone can build something impressive for an audience. What we've actually built is something that works quietly, ordinarily, even when no one important is watching at all. I think that might be the strongest evidence we have, and I almost missed it, because it never once felt like evidence while it was happening. It just felt like an ordinary community, being ordinary, exactly the way I'd always hoped it eventually would.*
