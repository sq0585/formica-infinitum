# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter 445 — The Controlled Crossing

The pocket dimension laboratory looked exactly the way he'd left it eight weeks ago, which struck him, stepping back into it after so long spent in the installation's cold, patient dark, as its own small kind of miracle — the same worn table, the same shelf of notebooks stretching back years, the same faint warm light he'd built into the space specifically because sterile rooms had never once helped him think clearly.

Chen Yufei was already there when he arrived, and didn't say anything at first, simply crossed the room and held him for a long moment, the particular quiet of five years of shared danger doing more work than any greeting could have.

"You look thinner," she said finally, pulling back to actually look at him.

"I ate. Amber-0 kept records. I just also spent eight weeks thinking about very large things, and apparently that shows on a person's face regardless of diet."

The others arrived within the hour, in the particular unhurried order that had, over years, become its own kind of ritual. Liang Jingzhi first, carrying nothing but her usual careful attention, settling into the chair she'd claimed as hers years ago without ever formally announcing it. Dr. Shen Yue next, arms full of monitoring equipment she set up methodically along one wall, murmuring instrument calibrations to herself the way other people might hum. Wei Canglan arrived precisely on time, as he always did, with a folder of Strata business he'd promised, apologetically, to only mention if something actually required Lin Mushi's attention before this was finished. Meen-4 came last, the way she often did, her presence settling into the room with the specific weight of someone who had witnessed considerably more thresholds crossed in this exact space than anyone else present.

Five people. Five Anchors. The whole accumulated weight of five years of trust, gathered deliberately, on purpose, in the same room, for the first time in the novel's history, before rather than after whatever came next.

"Talk us through it," Chen Yufei said, once everyone had settled. "What are you actually going to do."

"Work through the classification data again," he said, spreading the notebook open on the table between them. "The same material I've already absorbed, at the installation. I'm not trying to learn anything new — I think the comprehension is already complete, or close enough that reviewing it properly should be sufficient to trigger the crossing on its own. I just need to hold my attention on it long enough, carefully enough, for the Chitin Seed to recognize the threshold's actually been reached."

"And if it doesn't trigger," Liang Jingzhi asked, practical as always, "what then?"

"Then we've spent an evening together for nothing more dramatic than tea and old notes, and I try again tomorrow." He found, saying it, that the possibility didn't actually worry him. Five years had taught him that comprehension arrived on its own schedule regardless of how badly he wanted to command it, and he'd made his peace with that particular truth a long time ago.

Dr. Shen Yue finished her instrument calibrations and nodded once, satisfied. "Monitoring's active. Whatever happens, we'll have data on it — coherence readings, Veritonal density, everything I can capture from this side. If something goes wrong, we'll know faster than instinct alone would tell us."

He began working through the material an hour later, the room's warm light steady around him, five careful, familiar presences arranged nearby — not hovering, not anxious, simply present, the way people who trusted each other completely could simply exist together without needing to fill the space with reassurance.

He read through the resonance principles first, then dampening, then amplification, letting each one settle the way it had settled at the installation, feeling for the particular texture of full comprehension rather than the surface familiarity of information merely remembered. The solar field's edge, when he reached toward it from here, resolved more clearly than it ever had from Earth before this journey — narrower now, the gap he'd spent eight weeks closing reduced to something he could almost, almost see the far side of.

He kept reading. The room around him grew quieter, not through anyone's deliberate effort but through the particular stillness that settled over people watching someone they loved approach something enormous, unwilling to break the concentration with even the smallest unnecessary sound.

Somewhere past the second hour, working through the amplification principle's final, most difficult section — the specific mechanics of how a stellar field, under the right conditions, could genuinely reshape itself around a compatible neighbor — he felt something shift.

Not dramatically. Not with any warning that announced itself as separate from the work itself. Simply a sense, sudden and complete, of a door that had been standing slightly ajar for eight weeks finally, quietly, swinging the rest of the way open.

The Chitin Seed, resting where it always rested, warm and constant against whatever part of him it had long since stopped being separate from, pulsed once — and the room, the table, the five careful faces watching him, all of it began, gently and without violence, to fall away.
