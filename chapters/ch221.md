# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-One — The Dimensional Weaver Threshold

He didn't notice the threshold the moment he crossed it, which was, he would later decide, exactly the right way for it to happen.

Forty days since the shoebox-sized fold had first held for eleven seconds. Forty days of incremental practice — wardrobe-scale, then larger, then variable shapes instead of simple boxes, then anchoring a fold to a fixed physical location instead of holding it freehand in the air. He'd stopped tracking the work as a sequence of breakthroughs somewhere around the third week, the way any skill eventually stops feeling like a series of events and starts feeling like a baseline.

He was building a storage alcove off the outer library — a modest project, nothing he'd have called significant in advance — when he noticed his hands had stopped shaking.

They'd been shaking, faintly, for every large-scale fold since the wardrobe breakthrough. A small, persistent tremor that he'd attributed to the sustained concentration required, the same way a muscle trembles under load it isn't yet accustomed to carrying. He looked down at them now, steady around the half-formed geometry of the alcove, and understood, with the particular clarity of noticing something only after it had already become true, that the load no longer felt like load.

He could create pocket dimensions of arbitrary size now. He could anchor them to physical locations with the same unconscious ease he used to anchor his own Compression to his body. He could navigate between folds reliably, the spatial bookkeeping that used to require deliberate attention now running somewhere beneath conscious thought, the way walking ran beneath conscious thought once you'd done enough of it.

He sat down in the half-built alcove and held still for a long moment.

He thought about Document 7's calibration table, the seven ratios of Compression he'd read about a year ago and felt the particular vertigo of someone who had been operating at two of seven levels without knowing six others existed. Dimensional Weaving had its own threshold, its own quiet line between practitioner and apprentice, and he had apparently been standing on the wrong side of it until some moment in the past forty days he couldn't precisely locate.

He opened his notebook.

> *Dimensional Weaver stage achieved. I don't know exactly when — somewhere in the last week, probably, given how the work has started to feel. No single breakthrough moment. Just forty days of incremental practice arriving, eventually, at a stable baseline.*
>
> *What this means practically: I can make rooms out of nothing. Arbitrary size, arbitrary shape, anchored wherever I choose, navigable without the careful spatial tracking I used to need. The Strata's reconstruction stops being a theoretical project and becomes an actual one.*
>
> *Will need to think carefully about what I put in them. A capability like this doesn't come with built-in judgment about how to use it. That part's still mine to figure out.*

He closed the notebook and looked around the half-finished alcove — walls that hadn't existed an hour ago, space carved out of nothing, holding steady around him without the smallest tremor of effort.

It should have felt larger than it did. He'd expected, somewhere in the back of his mind, that crossing this particular threshold would announce itself the way the first eleven-second hold had announced itself — a moment he could point to, a before and after he could mark clearly on a calendar.

Instead it had simply arrived, quietly, the way most real changes in him seemed to arrive these days. Not a door bursting open. A door he'd been walking through for so long he'd stopped noticing the frame.

He finished the alcove before he went home that night. It seemed like the right way to mark the occasion — not a celebration, just the next room, built a little more easily than the last one had been.
