# FORMICA INFINITUM

## Chapter Two Hundred and Ninety-Three — The Laboratory Expanded

The old laboratory had been built for one person working alone, and it showed — a single workstation, a single set of shelving, instrumentation crowded together in whatever configuration had made sense at the time rather than any configuration that made sense now. Lin Mushi stood at its threshold with Fang Ruxue beside him, the two of them surveying the cramped space with the specific dissatisfaction of people who had outgrown something without quite noticing until the outgrowing became a daily inconvenience.

"Eight workstations," Fang Ruxue said, reading from the plan she'd drafted. "Precision Qi measurement array along the east wall, shared clean room adjoining, and enough open bench space that people aren't working elbow to elbow the way Dr. Shen Yue currently is."

"Currently is generous," he said. "Currently she's working elbow to elbow with a shelf of reference texts that keeps threatening to fall on her."

"Then we're overdue." Fang Ruxue made a note on her tablet, the same careful administrative precision she brought to everything since taking on the Collective's governance work. "I'd like to formally propose Dr. Shen Yue gets her own dedicated station rather than shared bench time. Given the volume of independent work she's producing, sharing space has become an active inefficiency."

He thought of the manuscript she'd shown him three days earlier, still sitting somewhere in a submission queue, and felt the proposal land with an obviousness that made him wonder why it hadn't happened sooner. "Agreed. Put her at the array end — she'll want proximity to the precision instruments."

---

The expansion took two weeks of steady, unglamorous work — Dimensional Weaving to carve the additional space, ordinary human labor to fit it out, a careful negotiation with Liang Jingzhi over where the new clean room's ventilation could safely route without disturbing the geological monitoring equipment two corridors over. He found himself enjoying it in a way that surprised him, the specific satisfaction of watching a space transform not through crisis-driven necessity but through patient, deliberate improvement.

Dr. Shen Yue arrived on the first morning the expanded laboratory was operational, stopped in the doorway, and didn't say anything for almost thirty seconds — long enough that he began to worry he'd misjudged something in the design.

"You built me an actual station," she said finally.

"You've been working out of a shared bench for eight months while producing enough data to fill three careers. Fang Ruxue pointed out the inefficiency. I agreed."

She walked the length of the new array slowly, running her fingers along the edge of the precision instrumentation with the specific reverence of someone encountering exactly the tool she'd needed and had learned, through long habit, not to expect. "This is calibrated for dark energy density measurement. Not general Qi sensing — this is built for exactly the readings I've been chasing."

"I asked Liang Jingzhi what specification you'd need, based on the manuscript's methodology section. She helped with the geological baseline calibration."

Something in Dr. Shen Yue's expression shifted, the careful professional composure she usually maintained giving way, briefly, to something plainer and more affected. "You read the methodology section closely enough to know what instrumentation it implied."

"I read the whole paper closely. Several times."

She didn't respond immediately, occupying herself instead with the array's controls, testing calibration in a way that gave her hands something to do while whatever she was feeling settled. "Thank you," she said eventually, without looking up. "I don't say that as often as I should. I default to analysis instead of gratitude. It's a professional habit I've never fully corrected."

"You don't need to correct it. The gratitude was obvious in how you looked at the door before you said anything."

---

Fang Ruxue found him later that afternoon, reviewing the laboratory's completed layout with the satisfied efficiency of someone checking a finished project against its original plan.

"Combined output's going to increase substantially," she said, gesturing at the eight stations, five of them now occupied by Collective researchers working through afternoon sessions with the quiet, focused hum of real institutional research finally underway. "I ran rough numbers based on Dr. Shen Yue's current pace and the additional capacity. If this holds, we'll be producing more publishable material in the next year than the previous two combined."

"Is that good?"

"It's inevitable, and inevitability isn't the same question as whether it's good." She said it without alarm, the same practiced neutrality she brought to most institutional realities. "More output means more attention. Dr. Shen Yue's paper is only the first of what this laboratory is going to produce. You should think about that now, while the volume is still manageable, rather than waiting until it isn't."

He looked out over the laboratory — eight stations, five occupied, the quiet productive hum of genuine research finally given room to breathe — and understood, watching it, that Fang Ruxue was right in the specific, unglamorous way she usually was: that what he'd built today for comfort and efficiency was also, quietly, building toward something considerably larger than a nicer workspace.

"I'll think about it," he said.

"Good." She made one final note on her tablet, the practical part of the conversation apparently concluded. "In the meantime, it's a good laboratory. You should be proud of it independent of what it eventually leads to."

He found, standing there in the expanded space, that he was.
