# FORMICA INFINITUM
## Chapter Two Hundred and Twenty-Seven — The Assault on the Strata

The Circuit network caught them eight minutes before they reached the boundary.

Lin Mushi was in the outer hall, working through the final adjustments to the defensive geometry he'd spent three days preparing, when his phone's monitoring display lit with a pattern he'd hoped he wouldn't see this soon — fourteen distinct Qi signatures, moving together with the coordinated, purposeful pace of a group that had stopped pretending to be subtle.

Not surveillance. Approach.

He sent the alert to Chen Yufei with three words — *they're moving, now* — and felt the familiar cold clarity settle over him, the same procedural calm he'd learned to recognize from every genuine crisis the past two years had handed him.

Eight minutes.

He used them.

---

Gou Ping's fourteen cultivators reached the convenience store's threshold with the loose, confident formation of a group that expected resistance and had prepared for it — combat stances, defensive techniques already half-raised, the particular tension of people braced for a fight they believed they understood the shape of.

What they found, crossing into the outer hall, was not a fight.

It was a hallway that didn't end where it should have ended.

Lin Mushi had spent the eight minutes — and the three days before them — restructuring the Strata's entry passages, building on a principle the Codex's Dimensional Weaver curriculum had described in careful, theoretical terms he'd only now had occasion to apply: a fold's geometry didn't need to be consistent for every observer crossing it. Two people entering at the same point, a half-second apart, could emerge in different locations entirely, if the architecture beneath the fold was built to route them that way.

The first cultivator through emerged in the outer library, fifty meters from where he expected to be.

The second emerged in a storage alcove, alone, the rest of his formation nowhere in sight.

The third found himself walking, with growing unease, down a corridor that curved back on itself in a way that should have been geometrically impossible, arriving, after several minutes of increasingly confused navigation, back at a junction he was certain he'd already passed.

Fourteen cultivators, prepared for direct confrontation, found themselves instead scattered across a space that refused to hold still — a maze with no malice in its construction, simply an architecture being actively rewritten faster than any of them could map it.

---

Lin Mushi watched the chaos unfold through the Circuit network's distributed sensors, adjusting the geometry continuously, never allowing any group of more than two or three cultivators to remain oriented toward each other for long enough to coordinate.

He felt, faintly, a presence joining his work partway through — a precision he recognized immediately, moving through the Strata at a scale too small for him to perceive directly, adjusting dimensional anchor points in ways that compounded his own restructuring without either of them exchanging a single word about how to coordinate it.

Axis-7.

He didn't pause to acknowledge it. There wasn't time, and some part of him understood, working in parallel with her without needing to discuss the method, that acknowledgment would only slow what was already working.

Forty minutes in, three of Gou Ping's cultivators had found their way back to the original entry point, shaken and disoriented, and chosen to withdraw rather than continue navigating a space that no longer behaved like space was supposed to behave.

By the hour mark, nine had withdrawn.

By ninety minutes, the remaining five — Gou Ping among them, his Qi signature distinct in its frustrated, escalating intensity — had stopped trying to advance and started, instead, trying simply to leave.

Lin Mushi let them.

He hadn't wanted to fight fourteen cultivators. He'd wanted, from the moment Chen Yufei's call had landed, to make the fight itself impossible to have — to change the terms so thoroughly that the confrontation Gou Ping had organized simply couldn't occur in the shape he'd planned it.

It hadn't.

No injuries beyond the disorientation itself. No direct confrontation. No moment where Lin Mushi had needed to face fourteen committed combatants with anything resembling force.

Just a space that had quietly, patiently, refused to be what they needed it to be, until they'd had no choice but to leave it the way they'd come.

He sat down in the outer hall once the last signature had withdrawn past the boundary, the geometry settling back into its ordinary configuration around him, and found his hands, for the first time in ninety minutes, entirely steady.

He picked up his notebook.

> *Fourteen cultivators. No injuries. No direct confrontation. The Codex was right — don't fight at their scale. Change the scale of the space they're fighting in, and the fight stops being a fight at all.*
>
> *This worked today because I had eight minutes of warning and three days of preparation. It won't always work that cleanly. I should assume the next attempt accounts for what just happened.*

He closed the notebook and waited, with the particular patience he'd learned over two years of discovering this world one consequence at a time, to find out what Lady Shen would do once she learned what her own faction had just attempted without her knowledge.
